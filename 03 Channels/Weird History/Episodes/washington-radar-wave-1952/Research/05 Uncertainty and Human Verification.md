## Legal, ethical and historical flags

### Misinformation and extraordinary claims

The main risk is epistemic rather than defamation: a radar return can be real while the object identity remains unknown. The episode must not turn a historical observation into proof of aliens, a secret weapon or a cover-up.

### Living people and privacy

The principal 1952 witnesses and officials are deceased, but later descendants, private records and remembered testimony still warrant respectful handling. Do not publish personal details beyond what is needed to identify the public record.

### Historical military language

The records contain Cold War air-defence terminology and “flying saucer” language. Quote only when it establishes the document or public frame, and identify whether the speaker is a controller, pilot, Air Force official, CIA historian or later commentator. Do not turn “White House,” “Capitol,” “7,000 mph,” “cover-up,” secret-technology or extraterrestrial language into settled fact.

### Archive and rights risk

NARA catalogue record 25738 identifies the Samford film, but its full audiovisual content was not independently reviewed and its rights are “Copyright Not Evaluated.” The ROSA P CAA report is marked public domain. NICAP/CUFON-hosted scans reproduce archival records but are not themselves the National Archives catalogue. No image, film or clipping is approved for future production use.

## Mandatory human-verification gate

Before any material claim advances, a human must:

1. Compare S2–S4 with original NARA Project Blue Book holdings and transcribe dates, time zones, redactions, names and printed page numbers manually. The accessible catalogue guides identify the relevant series but did not expose item-level F-94/pilot records in this revision.
2. Separate the 19–20 and 26–27 July events into distinct source-backed tracks, preserving source notation (EST, EDT or report-time) before any normalization.
3. Inspect CAA Report 180 Table I separately from its August track-analysis sections; record July tabulations/weather correlations without presenting the August conclusions as a complete July verdict.
4. Reconcile radiosonde/weather records for both July weekends with the inversion hypothesis and record uncertainty in the ledger.
5. Read the complete 29 July press-conference minutes if obtainable and review NARA film record 25738; preserve both the official explanation and its acknowledged limits.
6. Retrieve contemporaneous newspaper pages and classify reporting, scepticism, satire, official statements and later memory separately. The S2 clipping index and secondary reproductions are leads, not original-page verification.
7. Have a specialist review the wording around radar physics, unsupported 7,000-mph calculations, White House/Capitol geography, Truman involvement, cover-up allegations, secret technology and extraterrestrial hypotheses.

Until these checks are complete, this pack remains in **Research** and must not advance to scriptwriting, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.
