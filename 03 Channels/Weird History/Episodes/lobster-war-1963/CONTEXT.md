## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the 1961–63 Brazil–France lobster dispute, provisionally titled “The Lobster War: When Brazil and France Sent Warships over Fishing Rights.” It is a research pack, not a script, title package or production brief.

The central question is how an unsettled legal category—whether spiny lobsters counted as sedentary resources of Brazil’s continental shelf or as mobile high-seas fish—became a sovereignty dispute and then a naval coercion crisis without a recorded exchange of fire.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification gate and evidence-bounded conclusion.
- Distinguish Brazilian and French legal positions from adjudicated law, contemporary reporting from later synthesis, and documented naval movements from claims about “war.”
- Keep the 12-nautical-mile territorial-sea question separate from Brazil’s claim over resources associated with the continental shelf beyond that limit.
- Record the 1964 settlement as a negotiated later outcome, not as proof that either side received a simple legal or military “victory.”
- Stop for explicit human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `lobster-war-1963` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “Lobster War” only as the period/media label. The safest factual description is a 1961–63 diplomatic and fisheries dispute that escalated into naval deployments and coercive signalling; no located source establishes a battle or declaration of war.
- Treat “swim” versus “jump” as a shorthand for the legal category of “sedentary species,” not as a complete account of the dispute or a settled zoological ruling. The memorable kangaroo analogy is preserved only as a later attribution pending primary verification.
- Anchor the legal record in Brazil’s 1950 Decree No. 28,840 as reproduced in the official Itamaraty legal-opinion volume, the 1958 Geneva Convention language, the French note described in the 1962 opinion, and the 1963 French arbitration communication.
- Keep the Cold War as period context and a possible diplomatic frame, not as a proven cause. A 2021 naval dissertation explicitly studies the crisis alongside the Cuban Missile Crisis, but that framing is not evidence of superpower direction or intervention.
- The authorized six-file research sync is `01 Source Index.md` through `06 Conclusion.md`; the fifth file is named `05 Uncertainty and Human Verification.md`.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not say Brazil “declared war,” that the countries fought a naval battle, or that anyone was killed unless a primary casualty record is found. The located institutional summary calls the episode armed persuasion, not combat.
- Do not collapse the continental shelf into territorial waters. Brazil’s 1950 decree and the legal opinions distinguish resource jurisdiction over the submarine platform from navigation in the overlying waters.
- Do not present “France said lobsters were fish and Brazil said they walked” as a verbatim diplomatic exchange without verifying the original French and Brazilian records. The legal texts use the categories “sedentary species,” seabed contact and high-seas fisheries.
- Do not state that the French aircraft carrier *Clemenceau* confronted Brazil. The evidence supports a French task force in or near West African waters and the detachment of *Tartu* to the Brazilian coast; reports about a larger force moving toward Brazil were disputed.
- Do not force a single withdrawal date. One Brazilian Navy institutional page gives 24 February 1963, while a detailed retrospective account records *Paul Goffeny* and four fishing vessels leaving on 10 March after the French announcement of 8 March. The original naval and diplomatic records must settle what each date marks.
- Do not call the 1964 settlement a Brazilian legal judgment. The located secondary legal account describes an ad hoc arrangement allowing 26 French vessels to fish for up to five years subject to a return/tribute arrangement; retrieve the original accord before quoting its terms.
- Avoid attributing political stress, anti-colonialism, U.S. commercial influence or Cold War strategy as causes unless the cited source and wording make the attribution explicit. Preserve these as hypotheses or source-attributed interpretations.
- Historical actors are mostly deceased, but reputational and diplomatic claims still require careful attribution. “Illegal,” “plunder,” “aggression” and “victory” are positions or rhetoric unless supported by a competent legal finding.
