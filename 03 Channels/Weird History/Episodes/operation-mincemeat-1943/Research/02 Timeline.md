## Evidence sequence

The strongest current chain is: an obvious Sicily target → a planned cover story about Greece/Sardinia → a fabricated officer and personal life → submarine release near Huelva → Spanish custody and access by German intelligence → German assessment and defensive activity in the eastern Mediterranean → British confirmation and later public disclosure.

The chain is strongest for design and German reception. It is weaker for the precise Spanish handoff, the forensic details, and any numerical claim about lives saved or the invasion’s causal dependence on the ruse.

## Timeline items not cleared for narration

- The exact Twenty Committee approval date and who first proposed each element.
- The exact sailing and release coordinates, and whether “near Huelva” should be narrowed to a specific coast or estuary.
- The fisherman’s identity, Spanish naval/judicial chain of custody, autopsy wording and burial date.
- Whether the German appreciation was based on direct Abwehr access, Spanish intermediaries, or both at each stage.
- Which German troop or naval movements were caused by Mincemeat rather than existing Balkan expectations or other deception plans.
- Whether the body was Glyndwr Michael beyond the best-supported released-record case, and how to present the HMS *Dasher* alternative.
