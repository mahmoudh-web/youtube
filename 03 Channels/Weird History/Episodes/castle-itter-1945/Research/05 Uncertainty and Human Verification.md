## Scope and stop condition

This note is the research stop for the Castle Itter pack. It is not a script, title, thumbnail, visual treatment, legal opinion or production asset. No material claim should advance to narration or packaging until a human editor checks the primary records, confirms translations and records the final support in [[03 Channels/Weird History/Episodes/castle-itter-1945/Research/03 Claim Ledger]].

The story involves Nazi detention, concentration-camp administration, armed conflict, possible execution fears, political detention and the postwar rehabilitation of Wehrmacht memory. These are historical-sensitivity issues even though the principal actors are deceased; do not turn a disputed episode into an exonerating institutional narrative.

## Legal, ethical and historical flags

### Nazi detention and concentration-camp context

Relative privileges do not make Itter benign. It was part of a coercive Nazi detention system connected to Dachau. Avoid “comfortable prison” language unless a prisoner account is quoted and the comparison is explained. Do not use the presence of famous detainees to eclipse ordinary camp prisoners or the system’s forced labor and murder context.

### Wehrmacht institutional responsibility

The episode’s unusual cooperation is an individual and local wartime event, not evidence that the Wehrmacht was separate from Nazi criminal policy. USHMM’s institutional summaries document Wehrmacht participation in the Holocaust, mass violence and the war of annihilation. The claim ledger must keep C18–C20 adjacent to the episode claims.

### Individual conduct and motive

Gangl’s resistance association is supported by Austrian and local sources. His personal motivations, the motives of his men and the conduct of every castle guard are not fully documented in this pass. Do not infer altruism, repentance or ideological conversion from the outcome alone. Conversely, do not assign a specific Nazi crime to a named individual without a direct source.

### Prisoner identity and political complexity

The detainees included rivals and people with different wartime positions. Some were former Vichy officials or associated with right-wing/fascist movements; others were anti-Nazi, republican or labor figures. Avoid collective labels such as “heroes,” “innocent hostages” or “collaborators” without individual attribution and context.

### Violence and casualty language

The source set establishes armed combat and deaths. It does not yet provide a reconciled casualty table. Do not use “bloodless,” “zero casualties,” “only one death,” “massacre,” or a settled attacker death toll without the underlying record. Distinguish killed, wounded, missing, captured and unknown.

### Image and rights risk

S17 is a museum catalogue for a contemporary newspaper object and links an IIIF manifest; DÖW provides a memorial image. These are source leads, not automatic licenses. Check the scan, caption, provenance, privacy/cultural context and jurisdiction-specific reuse terms before any future visual work. No visual asset is approved by this pack.

## Mandatory human-verification gate

Before any material claim advances, a human must:

1. Read the 23rd Tank Battalion AAR for 4–5 May 1945, the 142nd Infantry action report and the 103d Infantry Division G-2 report.
2. Inspect the Texas scrapbook scan at image level and correct every OCR-corrupted name, time, weapon and casualty figure.
3. Retrieve the Dachau/Itter administrative records and verify whether “subcamp,” “special detention facility” or another term is most accurate in English.
4. Build the 5 May roster from the camp register and prisoner diaries; distinguish French detainees, women, castle guards, Wehrmacht personnel, Austrian resistance members and later relief troops.
5. Verify the 17th SS attribution, attacker estimate, gun count, SS command and whether the attack was linked to a written execution order.
6. Compare Gangl’s death description across the U.S. casualty report, Austrian sources, prisoner accounts and any German record.
7. Reconcile relief times (about 15:00 versus 16:00), casualty counts and the claim that no Americans/French were injured.
8. Obtain specialist review of Wehrmacht institutional responsibility, Gangl’s biography, local Austrian resistance memory and the detainees’ individual political histories.
