## Scope and method

I independently read and checked the six-file pack: `01 Source Index.md`, `02 Timeline.md`, `03 Claim Ledger.md`, `04 Counterevidence.md`, `05 Uncertainty and Human Verification.md`, and `06 Conclusion.md`. I checked every material ledger row and the conclusion against the cited source, with emphasis on names, dates, quantities, quotations, chronology, agency attribution, medical interpretation, causation and legal wording.

The validation is source-led, not an approval of the episode. The original Special Report 142 scan, the complete 1951 medical paper, the complete 1977 Lancet paper, the district-court record and operational files were not available in the pack. Where a source was inaccessible or only a catalogue/metadata record, that limitation is recorded rather than inferred away.

## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — suitable for human verification only.**

The pack correctly identifies the strongest defensible finding: a 1950 U.S. military vulnerability test over the San Francisco Bay Area, using biological simulants, is documented; an unusual Stanford *Serratia* case series followed; and the surviving record does not establish that the military strain caused the infections or Edward Nevin’s death. The pack is appropriately cautious about legal immunity, ethical judgment and causal proof.

It is not ready for script or production. Before any narration, correct the case-count conflict, reconcile the Paine-study date, label the 1979 newspaper’s pneumonia and “11 other cases” wording as disputed, and have a human reviewer inspect the missing primary records. Keep the episode In Review.

## Material corrections and conflicts

1. **Case count:** S5’s contemporaneous abstract says 11 total cases, one fatal. S13 says Nevin died and “eleven other cases” survived, implying 12 total. S14 says 11 people and then one death, which is compatible with 11 total. Treat S13’s “eleven other cases” as a likely journalistic/counting error or unresolved conflict; do not repeat it as fact. The safe wording is “the 1951 paper reported 11 cases, one fatal.”

2. **Paine exposure date:** S2, pp. 189–190, gives a 1946 experiment using strain 8 UK. S9’s full-text review gives 2 October 1945 for Paine’s Camp Detrick experiment. S4’s hearing reproduces the 1946 testimony/account. The pack should mark 1945/1946 unresolved and retrieve Paine’s original report before fixing the date. Do not present “1946” as settled.

3. **Test dates and counting convention:** S2’s reproduced abstract gives six trials during 20–27 September; S2’s trial chapter later describes Army spraying SM on 26 and 27 September; S13 reports a six-day cruise/spraying between 20 and 26 September. These may reflect different release-day or document conventions, but the pack cannot silently reconcile them. Keep “the report records six trials from 20–27 September” and flag exact day sequence for primary-file review.

4. **Coverage and population figures:** S2 reproduces the report’s population-level fluorescent-particle model, including 800,000 people and “similar dosage patterns”; S13 repeats 800,000 and six days. These are modelled/attributed estimates, not measured individual bacterial doses. Do not convert fluorescent particles into viable-cell dose or infection count. The 23-mile station result, 117-square-mile figure and 800,000-person estimate must not be merged until the original calculations are checked.

5. **Medical wording:** S5 supports 11 chromobacteria cases and one fatal bacterial endocarditis. S13 calls the episode a “serratia-related pneumonia outbreak” and says 11 other cases; that article is retrospective and conflicts with the primary medical abstract. Use the medical paper’s wording and avoid “pneumonia” unless the full cases support it.

6. **Report provenance versus report text:** S1 is a government technical-report bibliography and verifies title, author, date and report identifiers, not the report’s conclusions. S2 is a secondary book account quoting/reproducing passages and contains OCR errors/redactions. The primary report scan remains a required human check.

7. **S10 citation metadata:** The National Academies page identifies chapter 19, *Biological Warfare Scenarios*, by **William Patrick III** (not “Patrick et al.”); the site URL is `/chapter/20` because of the web navigation. Correct the source index citation. S10 is a retrospective technical chapter, not a substitute for Special Report 142.

8. **Legal wording:** S7 is a public-law reproduction of the Ninth Circuit opinion, not an official court-hosted scan. It supports the majority’s FTCA holding and the dissent’s statement about the district finding. It does not establish scientific exoneration, ethical acceptability or absence of exposure.

9. **Disclosure-date metadata:** S13’s page header says 16 September 1979 while its archive URL contains `/1979/09/17/`. Cite the article as a Washington Post retrospective and have a human verify the issue/page date before using an exact publication date.

## Unsupported, overstated or unresolved claims

- Unsupported as fact: direct military-strain causation of the Stanford cases or Nevin’s death; permanent ecological change; “one of the largest human experiments in history.”
- Overstated unless corrected: “11 other cases” in S13; a single six-day event; a settled ship or sole agency; any individual bacterial dose; “harmless bacteria”; court “clearance” of the government.
- Unresolved: Paine exposure date (1945/1946), exact release days, vessel and command chain, exact organism/tracer allocation by trial, original report’s fourth conclusion, coverage metrics, Stanford patient-level chronology, strain archive status from primary records, and district-court findings.
- Missing competing evidence to obtain: contemporaneous medical literature and safety memoranda, health-department/hospital notifications, original operational logs, full CDC/Farmer methods and sample frame, and the trial record underlying the dissent’s description.
