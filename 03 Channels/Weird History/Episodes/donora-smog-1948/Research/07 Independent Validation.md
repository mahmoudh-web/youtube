## Scope and method

I independently read the episode `CONTEXT.md` and all six research files: `01 Source Index`, `02 Timeline`, `03 Claim Ledger`, `04 Counterevidence`, `05 Uncertainty and Human Verification`, and `06 Conclusion`. I checked each material claim against the cited online source, prioritising the 1949 U.S. Public Health Service (PHS) record, official Pennsylvania and federal records, the presidential message, and the peer-reviewed follow-up literature.

This file records validation only. The researcher’s six files were not edited. Access date for this audit is **2026-09-16**. A source being cited or listed below is not clearance for quotation, archival reuse, narration, or publication.

## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS.** The pack’s central claims are responsibly bounded: the PHS count is kept distinct from later 21/22 variants; the meteorological mechanism is separated from pollutant source attribution; the PHS “no single substance” finding is preserved; and the policy language is qualified. The pack is suitable to send to a human verification gate after the corrections below are applied.

The load-bearing limitations remain material: the original NLM scan of Bulletin No. 306 was not inspected page by page; the full 1961 follow-up and 2018 review were blocked by PMC’s access check during this audit; the state reports listed in Carton 14 were not opened; and no 1951 court docket or settlement instrument was located. No script, narration, packaging, visual, production, upload, or publication approval follows from this validation.

## Source-quality and access assessment

### Strongest evidence

- **S1, EPA HERO record:** official bibliographic record and abstract for the PHS preliminary report. It is strong for the headline counts, symptoms, major contaminants, and “no single substance” conclusion, but it is not the full report.
- **S2, NLM Bulletin No. 306 scan:** the needed primary source. The 57 MB PDF was too large for direct browser retrieval in this audit, so page-level approval is still outstanding.
- **S3, NEPIS Appendix A:** reproduces the relevant PHS pages and the Weather Bureau material, with useful locators. It is an educational guide reproduction, not a substitute for inspecting S2; the OCR contains visible errors (for example “1048,” “zino,” and distorted section numbering), and the guide states that it was not formally reviewed or endorsed by EPA.
- **S5 and S10:** official state archival material and a primary presidential message. They are strong for the Bainbridge letter, state institutional memory, and national policy attention, but not neutral proof of pollutant responsibility or legislative causation.
- **S9:** official EPA statutory chronology. Strong for what the listed laws did and when; insufficient on Donora’s precise causal weight.

### Useful but limited evidence

- **S4:** confirms that Pennsylvania’s Carton 14 contains the state Donora reports, borough report, and petitions. It is a catalogue/guide, not the findings themselves.
- **S6, S7, S8, and S14:** institutional retrospectives and technical syntheses. They triangulate chronology, mechanisms, recommendations, and policy framing, but should not replace the original PHS or state records on disputed facts.
- **S11 and S12:** the 1961 primary follow-up and 2018 peer-reviewed review. PMC’s full pages were blocked by an access check; PubMed/search-index text confirms the study identity and broad conclusions, but adapted numerical results remain human checks.
- **S13:** EPA catalog record confirms the 1957 follow-up’s title, date, scope, comparison design, and 262-page length. It does not provide the full results.
- **S15, S16, and S19:** reputable retrospective, local historical society, and JAMA historical retrospective. They document reception and later retellings, including the 21/22 count variants, but are not adequate alone for legal findings or a reconciled mortality count.
- **S17 and S18:** primary Congressional Record material, but speeches and inserted newspaper text establish political memory and argument, not independent epidemiology. Direct PDF opens were unreliable; searchable GovInfo/Congress text exposed the cited passages.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory, or missing-source items

1. **Mortality scope remains unresolved.** The PHS 20, PHMC 19-in-24-hours, local 21, and JAMA 22 are all evidenced as different retellings or scopes, but no located source supplies a reconciled death list and inclusion rule. The wording gate must stay “the PHS report counted 20.”
2. **The 1951 litigation date is not supported by the cited Smithsonian passage.** Only the limited “later settled without assessing blame” statement is currently supported, and that rests on a retrospective source without a docket or settlement instrument.
3. **Steamboat significance is overstated in C14.** The reproduced report explicitly calls domestic heating and local steam locomotives significant; it includes steamboats in a recommendation, which is weaker evidence of source magnitude.
4. **C23 overextends the follow-up evidence.** The cancer comparison in the 2018 review is not the same as a comparison by original smog-affected status. Use named study populations and comparison groups.
5. **The original PHS scan and Pennsylvania Carton 14 reports remain missing primary checks.** The pack correctly flags these gaps; they must not be represented as completed verification.
6. **No acute-event pollution measurements were found.** Later sampling and atmospheric reconstruction must remain clearly separate from measurements taken during 27–31 October.
7. **The local 21-death page contains rhetorical and memory claims** (including “who knows how many more”) alongside its count. Treat it as community-history evidence, not an independent mortality audit.
8. **Policy causation is inferential.** The state division, Truman message, Congressional Record, and federal statute sequence establish attention and influence. They do not prove that Donora alone caused a statute, EPA, or the Clean Air Act.
