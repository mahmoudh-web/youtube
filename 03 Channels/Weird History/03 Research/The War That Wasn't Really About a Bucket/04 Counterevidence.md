## Standard story being tested

The popular version says that Modenese soldiers stole a wooden bucket from a Bolognese well, Bologna went to war to recover it, and the resulting bloodshed became the “War of the Bucket.” The evidence supports a real and serious conflict, but it does not support that causal sequence at equal confidence.

## Counterevidence to a bucket-centred cause

### 1. The Modena chronicle’s 1325 entry does not mention a bucket

The later Modena chronicle records that the Bolognese were defeated and fled at Zappolino, then describes a pursuit toward Bologna’s walls under Passerino and Azzone (S1, PDF pp. 187–188). The entry does not say that a bucket theft began the campaign. This silence cannot disprove a bucket episode, but it prevents the chronicle from serving as direct support for “the bucket caused the war.”

### 2. An official Bolognese-area account explicitly separates fiction from history

The Metropolitan City of Bologna’s Zappolino page says that in the fiction the war is triggered by the theft of a bucket, while historically the battle was one of repeated territorial and political clashes (S4, line 12). This is a modern institutional summary, not a medieval source, but it is a useful counterweight because it makes the distinction the popular story usually erases.

### 3. The literary source itself says the causal story is reputed

Tassoni’s “A chi legge” describes the bucket as the object “per cagion della quale è fama che nascesse tal guerra”—roughly, “for which it is reputed that the war began” (S7). The poem’s opening then elevates a low-value wooden bucket into an epic object. A seventeenth-century mock-heroic poem is evidence for reception and memory, not a neutral statement of 1325 causation.

### 4. Tassoni knowingly plays with chronology

The annotation to the poem says the 1325 conflict at Zappolino is narrated in the poet’s own way, while the bucket theft is made the occasion for the large army and battle of 1249; it calls that arrangement an anachronism forgiven by the poem’s comic goal (S8, lines 106–110). This is unusually strong internal evidence against reading the poem as a literal campaign diary.

### 5. Border politics supply a less theatrical immediate explanation

Carbonara’s academic border study describes the Panaro/Muzza frontier, repeated fortification and conflict, and the prominence of Zappolino in a long series of Bologna–Modena struggles (S5, pp. 59–61). The New Cambridge synthesis places the 1325 victory within the wider consolidation of Ghibelline influence (S6, around p. 493). These sources do not identify one sole cause, but they show that the conflict had strategic and political stakes independent of a bucket.

## Evidence that prevents overcorrection

### 6. A bucket tradition may preserve a real trophy event

The official Modena heritage account says a Modenese squadron took a common bucket from a public well and carried it home as a trophy; Carbonara repeats the trophy interpretation (S3, lines 95–99; S5, pp. 59–61). The careful conclusion is not “the bucket is entirely fictional.” It is: **the object may have been attached to the victory as a trophy or humiliation symbol, but the evidence located does not establish that it caused the war.**

### 7. The object’s current custody is not authentication

Modena’s heritage page distinguishes an original in the Town Hall from a copy in the Ghirlandina (S3, line 90). That is useful for present-day provenance claims, but a displayed object, civic tradition, or surviving chain is not by itself proof of identity, date, or battlefield custody. No dendrochronology, inventory chain, or medieval act authenticating the exact bucket was located in this pass.

### 8. “Guelph versus Ghibelline” is explanatory but incomplete

The factional frame is real and relevant, but S5 describes local border and water-control problems, while S11 emphasises shifting alliances and the complex political profile of Passerino. Do not substitute a two-colour faction diagram for the specific interests of communes, exiles, signori, allies, and frontier castles.

### 9. Later civic memory is evidence of importance, not of causation

The 2025 Zappolino700 programme treats the battle as a source of enduring identity memory and discusses alliances, military strategy, political tensions, and symbols (S11, lines 16–21). This confirms that the bucket functions as a powerful memory object today; it cannot adjudicate what caused the 1325 war.

## Working verdict

The bucket is best treated as a **reported trophy and later organising symbol**, not as an established casus belli. The war’s secure historical core is a Bologna–Modena military conflict culminating in a Modenese victory at Zappolino amid longer territorial and factional struggles. The bucket-centred frame became durable through local memory and Tassoni’s seventeenth-century comic poem.
