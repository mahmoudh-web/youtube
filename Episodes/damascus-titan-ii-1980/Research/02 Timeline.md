## Timeline with evidence status

|---|---|---|---|
| 1963 | Titan II missile sites are established in Arkansas, Kansas and Arizona; the system is described in Senate debate as about 18 years old in 1980 and designed for a ten-year life expectancy. | The missiles were an aging strategic system by the time of the accident. | **High** for the Senate’s description; age alone is not proof that age caused this accident. | 27 Jan 1978 | A previous oxidizer leak occurred at Launch Complex 374-7; civilians were evacuated and some experienced effects from vapours. | The same complex had a documented earlier propellant emergency. | **High** for the institutional summary; verify incident report and casualty/medical wording. S6, S8. |
| 1979–1980 | Congress required an Air Force study of Titan II condition and safety and debated civilian warning systems after earlier leaks and deaths. | Damascus occurred amid an existing safety debate, not in a vacuum. | **High** for congressional statements; the study’s full findings remain to be checked. S3, S4. |
| May 1980 | A Titan II weapon-condition and safety report was presented to the armed-services committees and described by members as generally reassuring. | The existence of a pre-accident safety assessment and later political criticism is established. | **Medium-high**; obtain the report and avoid calling it a “whitewash” except as Glickman’s allegation. S3, S4. |
| 18 Sep, about 6:30–6:36 p.m. | A maintenance team worked on the Titan II at Complex 374-7. A socket fell from a work platform and struck the missile’s first-stage fuel tank. | The accident began during routine maintenance and the dropped tool caused the reported puncture. | **High** for broad sequence. Exact time and socket weight/drop distance vary: NNSA gives about 65 feet; other accounts give about 70–80 feet and different weights. S1, S6, S7, S9, S11. |
| 18 Sep, 6:47 p.m. | An Air Force account reported the first indication of vapours at 6:47 p.m.; the control-room crew later saw substantial fuel-pressure loss. | The leak was not necessarily recognised at the instant of impact; detection followed the puncture. | **Medium-high** as reported in the Senate; verify against the full Air Force report. S1, S5. |
| 18 Sep, evening | Personnel exited or were ordered out as vapour concentrations rose. Automatic sprinklers ran; NNSA says about a quarter-million gallons of water were used, while the contemporary Washington Post report gives 100,000 gallons. | Emergency systems and evacuation were attempted, but the vapour hazard continued. | **High** for sprinklers and evacuation; **medium** for the volume because sources conflict. S1, S5, S11. |
| 18 Sep, about 9–10 p.m. | The complex was evacuated and nearby civilians were told to leave the immediate area. | Military personnel and residents were evacuated before the explosion. | **High** for the broad action; exact radius and notification sequence need the emergency log. S1, S6, S7, S10. |
| 18 Sep, late evening | Air Force, Strategic Air Command and contractor personnel debated whether to wait, open/vent the silo or take another action. | Contemporary reporting describes conflicting advice and uncertainty about the risk of explosion. | **Medium-high**; the complete hearing and command log are required before assigning individual blame. S11. |
| 19 Sep, about 2:13 a.m. | An emergency team entered in protective clothing to assess or vent the silo; vapour was described as dense and readings became unsafe. | The response continued after evacuation, but conditions remained dangerous. | **Medium-high**; NNSA’s summary gives 2:13 a.m.; Senate reporting gives a similar late-night/early-morning sequence. S1, S5. |
| 19 Sep, 2:50 a.m. or just before 3:00 a.m. | Fuel vapours ignited inside the silo. The missile was destroyed and the 740-ton closure door was blown away. | The conventional explosion occurred after roughly eight and a half hours of leak and response. | **High** for the broad timing and outcome; exact ignition minute differs between summaries and must remain qualified. S1, S2, S6, S7, S11. |
| 19 Sep, immediately after explosion | The re-entry vehicle carrying the nuclear warhead was ejected; the warhead was recovered essentially intact or with visible damage. | The warhead did not produce a nuclear detonation and was recovered after a field check. | **High** for recovery and no reported contamination in NNSA/DOE summaries; inspect the underlying response record for technical condition. S1, S2, S6, S7. |
| 19 Sep, later that day | Senior Airman David Livingston died of injuries sustained in the explosion. Twenty-one other Air Force personnel were injured. | One fatality and 21 injuries are the stable official-summary figures. | **High** for count; verify names, ranks, injury descriptions and sequence from the mishap board and medical records before narration. S1, S2, S6. |
| 19 Sep 1980 | Members of Congress reacted immediately, calling for an on-site investigation and questioning earlier assurances about Titan safety. | The accident triggered public congressional scrutiny. | **High** for the remarks; statements are not themselves investigation findings. S3. |
| 22–25 Sep 1980 | Senate and House reporting described the leak, evacuation, injuries and Air Force Secretary Hans Mark’s account of conflicting advice; Mark said the missile’s age was not the immediate cause. | The public explanation included both a dropped socket and possible response/communications failures, while disputing that age caused the accident. | **Medium-high**; use attribution and obtain the complete hearing transcript. S5, S11. |

## Chronology controls

- Do not collapse 18 September maintenance/leak and 19 September explosion into one daytime event.
- Use “about 6:30 p.m.” for the maintenance start, not a false exact time; the Air Force report’s 6:47 p.m. vapour indication is a separate point.
- Use “about eight and a half hours later” or “shortly before/around 3 a.m.” for ignition unless the original mishap report resolves the 2:50/3:00 conflict.
- Keep 21 as the injured-person count reported by NNSA/DOE; do not convert it into a count of all responders or civilians affected.
- A 19 September source date is not evidence that maintenance began on 19 September; the overnight sequence is explicit in S1, S5 and S7.
