## Central conflict

The episode has two separate questions:

1. **What happened physically?** A dropped socket and fuel leak are strongly established, but the exact ignition source, timing and contribution of design, procedure and response failures need the original mishap record.
2. **How close was the nuclear danger?** A nuclear warhead was present and was ejected, but the public record located here does not provide a defensible probability that it would detonate.

## Stable core versus disputed detail

### Stable core

- NNSA/DOE summaries, Congressional Record reporting and Arkansas institutional histories converge on the overnight date, Complex 374-7, dropped socket, first-stage fuel leak, evacuation, conventional explosion, one death, 21 injuries, warhead recovery and no reported radioactive contamination.
- The NNSA record describes the accident as a fuel-vapour ignition inside a closed silo; the DOE guide independently describes ignition about eight and a half hours after the puncture.
- The same records identify the re-entry vehicle/warhead outcome as essentially intact or visibly damaged but recovered.

## Response and systems evidence

- Congressional remarks show that civilian warning systems and earlier Titan leaks were already policy concerns before Damascus. This supports a systems context, not a claim that Congress or the Air Force knowingly accepted a specific fatal risk.
- Hans Mark’s reported 24 September account describes conflicting advice between Air Force personnel and Martin Marietta and a decision to wait. It is evidence that the public explanation included command uncertainty; it is not a complete command log and does not by itself prove negligence.
- Mark reportedly rejected the missile’s 18-year age as the immediate cause. Glickman and other members questioned the pre-accident safety report. Both statements should remain attributed; neither settles the engineering question.
- The existence of a December 1980 Eighth Air Force mishap board means the Air Force created a fuller investigative record. Until that report is read, no precise causal hierarchy should be presented.

## Nuclear-safety counterevidence

- **Against a nuclear-detonation claim:** the NNSA/DOE records report a conventional explosion, intact/essentially intact recovery and no radioactive contamination. No located source gives a nuclear release order or a detonation event.
- **Against “no danger at all”:** the warhead was physically present in a missile that exploded and was ejected; officials treated recovery as requiring a detailed field check. That establishes a serious nuclear-weapons accident without establishing a near-detonation probability.
- **Against sensational counterfactuals:** the University of Arkansas exhibit discusses what might have happened if the warhead detonated, but that is an exhibit’s hypothetical framing, not an official casualty model. Keep it out of settled claims unless an independently verified model is commissioned.

## Alternative narrative risks

- “A three-pound wrench caused a nine-megaton bomb to nearly go off” compresses disputed tool details and an unsupported counterfactual into a single claim.
- “The missile was old, so it failed” confuses age context with the reported immediate puncture and ignores Mark’s contrary public statement.
- “The Air Force hid a nuclear explosion” is unsupported. The public record describes a conventional propellant explosion and no radioactive contamination; secrecy about the weapon’s presence is a separate communications issue.
- “One hero saved Arkansas” is unsupported. Multiple crews, responders, officials and safety features contributed to the outcome, and the record does not quantify alternative outcomes.
- “Twenty-one people died” is false in the located official summaries. The stable figure is one fatality and 21 injured Air Force personnel.

## What would change the working answer

The provisional conclusion would need revision if the original mishap board, hearing transcript, emergency logs or radiological survey shows: a different initiating event; a verified ignition mechanism materially changing the chain; a different casualty or contamination record; a nuclear detonation or release; or a final official finding that materially changes the role of maintenance, system design or command decisions.
