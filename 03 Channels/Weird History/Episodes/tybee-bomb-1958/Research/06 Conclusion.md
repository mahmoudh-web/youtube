## What the record can support now

The strongest located evidence supports a limited conclusion. On 5 February 1958, a B-47 returning from a simulated combat mission collided with an F-86 near Savannah. After three unsuccessful landing attempts at Hunter AFB, the crew jettisoned a Mk 15 Mod 0 bomb into the Wassaw Sound area off Tybee Beach to avoid the conventional-explosive hazard of a crash landing. The B-47 landed safely. An intensive search by Air Force and Navy personnel ran from 6 February through 16 April 1958, covered roughly three square miles and did not locate the weapon. The official status became “irretrievably lost.”

The Air Force’s 12 April 2001 assessment, supported by the DOE/DoD historical summary and later official Air Force releases, says this specific weapon was in a transportation/training configuration without its nuclear capsule. On that account, a nuclear detonation was not possible, although the bomb still contained conventional explosive and uranium/heavy-metal components. The 2001 report’s estimate that an intact weapon might lie 5–15 feet below the seabed is conditional modeling, not a discovered position. It also says a modern search would have a very low probability of success and could create environmental, economic and personnel hazards.

The 2004–05 interagency investigation did not recover the weapon. The Air Force reported more than 20,000 radiation measurements and 12 seabed samples, no evidence that the private search group had found the bomb, and no evidence of man-made enriched uranium. Officials attributed the measured variation to naturally occurring mineral deposits and retained the 2001 recommendation to leave the weapon undisturbed and keep it categorized as irretrievably lost.

## What the record cannot yet support

The pack cannot safely conclude that a live, fully armed hydrogen bomb is still in the sound. Later reporting says a 1966 W.J. Howard letter to Congress described the device as a “complete weapon,” but the original letter was not inspected in this pass. That report conflicts on its face with the 1958 custody receipt and the 2001 Air Force/DOE configuration analysis. The conflict may reflect a genuine record error, a category distinction, or a different meaning of “complete”; it is not evidence by itself that a nuclear capsule was aboard the aircraft.

The pack also cannot say the weapon was destroyed, secretly recovered, found by private divers, or located at a confirmed coordinate. “Irretrievably lost” means the documented recovery effort failed and the government retained an unresolved status. It does not prove continued presence, destruction, movement or removal.

## Evidence-bounded answer

**The declassified search record supports “a weapon lost and never recovered,” not “a confirmed live hydrogen bomb waiting beneath a known patch of mud.”** The official Air Force/DOE conclusion is that the Mk 15 Mod 0 was carried without its nuclear capsule, that no nuclear explosion was possible, and that further recovery efforts offered little public-safety benefit while carrying environmental and conventional-explosive risks. The 1966 congressional document is the essential unresolved challenge to that conclusion and must be checked before any stronger statement is approved.
