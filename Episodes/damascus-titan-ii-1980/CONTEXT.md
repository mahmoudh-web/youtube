## Purpose

This episode folder holds the source-led research pack for the 18–19 September 1980 Titan II accident at Launch Complex 374-7 near Damascus, Arkansas. Its research question is how routine maintenance, a pressurised fuel leak and emergency decisions produced a major nuclear-weapons accident without a nuclear detonation.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, dated evidence sequence, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty and human-verification gate, and evidence-bounded conclusion.
- Separate contemporaneous government records from later institutional summaries, interviews and documentary reconstructions.
- Distinguish the conventional explosion and toxic-propellant accident from claims about a possible nuclear detonation; do not imply that detonation was inevitable or quantify an unsupported probability.
- Stop at research until an independent validator and human editor approve the evidence, wording and any archival material.

## Key Decisions

- Use `damascus-titan-ii-1980` as the episode slug.
- Treat 18 September as the start of the accident and 19 September as the explosion and casualty date; some official summaries label the accident only by 19 September.
- Use “dropped socket” and “fuel-vapour explosion” as the safe core wording. Socket weight, drop distance, ignition time and the precise ignition source are recorded as disputed or source-dependent.
- Treat the NNSA/DOE summaries as official retrospective records accessed through Governmentattic; verify the underlying release and page images before quoting them as a primary record.
- Treat the 24 September Air Force Secretary account as a contemporaneous reported congressional briefing, not a substitute for the full hearing transcript or Air Force mishap board report.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-16.
- Do not give operational instructions about nuclear weapons, propellants, silo entry or emergency response.
- Do not state that the warhead was “armed,” that a nuclear explosion was minutes away, or that one person alone “saved” the region. The public record located here establishes recovery and no reported radioactive contamination, not a quantified counterfactual.
- Do not use the NNSA summary’s 2:50 a.m. ignition and the University of Arkansas exhibit’s “just before 3:00 a.m.” as though they were identical timestamps; retain the uncertainty.
- Respect the deceased airman and injured personnel. Avoid graphic medical detail and verify names and ranks against the underlying record.
