## Purpose

This episode folder holds the source-led research pack for the 9 July 1958 Lituya Bay earthquake and landslide-generated impulse wave. Its central editorial question is how eyewitness testimony, tree-line destruction and geological measurement produced the reported run-up elevation of about 1,720 feet.

## Responsibilities

- Keep primary observations, later syntheses and model outputs distinct.
- Maintain a claim ledger with source URLs, precise passages or page/line locators, access dates, confidence and open caveats.
- Preserve the distinction between wave run-up elevation and wave height/amplitude.
- Stop at research and human verification; this folder is not a narration, visual brief or publication approval.

## Key Decisions

- Use `Research/` as the canonical episode research location for this pack.
- Treat Miller's USGS Professional Paper 354-C as the primary field-evidence anchor.
- State the 1,720-foot figure as the maximum mapped trimline/run-up elevation above mean sea level, not as the height of a vertical wall of water in open ocean.
- Keep single-rockslide and possible double-slide explanations as competing interpretations.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Every material claim must be traceable to a source passage or timestamp.
- A model result is not a direct observation; label assumptions, input data and output type.
- The initial 1,800-foot altimeter reading, the later 1,720-foot trimline measurement and the approximately 1,740-foot surge description must not be collapsed into one number.
- Human verification is required before any claim becomes narration-ready.
