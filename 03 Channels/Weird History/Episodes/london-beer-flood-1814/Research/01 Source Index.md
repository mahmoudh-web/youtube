## Research question

What can be established from contemporary press and inquest reporting about the Meux vat failure on 17 October 1814, how did the released porter move through the adjacent streets and houses, and who lived or worked in the affected part of St Giles?

## Episode boundary

This pack reconstructs the documented failure, the immediate urban geography, the eight deaths named in the inquest report, and the later disagreement over volume, mechanics, and an alleged ninth death. It does not draft narration, settle the authenticity of every later retelling, or treat a newspaper transcription as a substitute for the original archive item.

## Source-quality key

- **A — contemporary report / primary-era publication:** a report printed close to the event or a digitised scan of the period publication. It still contains reporter, witness, and transcription bias.
- **B — contemporary report reproduced or indexed by a later host:** valuable for traceable wording, but the original scan or archive metadata still needs checking.
- **C — scholarly or specialist secondary:** source criticism, brewing history, or urban history.
- **D — institutional / collection record:** useful for current site or archival guidance; not sufficient alone for disputed 1814 facts.
- **E — orientation only:** non-specialist retelling; never sole support for a material claim.

## Key entities and evidence-led visual opportunities

### People and institutions named in the evidence

- **Meux & Co. / Horse Shoe Brewery:** industrial site and owner-partnership named in the period reports; do not infer which partner made the maintenance decision without a source.
- **George Crick:** storehouse clerk and principal inquest witness; his evidence covers the fallen hoop, the letter to Mr Young, the vat dimensions, the cascade, and Ann Saville's recovery.
- **Mr Young:** partner and vat-builder named in Crick's testimony; the report does not establish his full name or whether he received the letter before the rupture.
- **Richard Hawes:** landlord of the Tavistock Arms; his evidence places Eleanor Cooper in the yard and describes the pub's cellar and tap-room damage.
- **John Cummins:** bricklayer and owner of houses in New Street; his evidence gives the recovery locations of several victims.
- **George Hodgson:** Middlesex coroner named in the *Morning Post* inquest report.
- **The eight inquest-listed dead:** Elenor/Eleanor Cooper, Mary Mulvey, Thomas Murry/Murray, Hannah Banfield/Bamfield, Sarah Bates, Ann Saville, Elizabeth Smith, and Catherine/Catharine Butler. Names and ages require scan verification.

### Visual evidence that can be verified before any creative treatment

- The period *Gentleman's Magazine* volume and newspaper pages: evidence of the report's language, measurements, street names, and victim list; scan pages must be cited rather than represented by invented newspaper graphics.
- Historic England Archive image **BL19305**: a later documentary view of the Horse Shoe Brewery site, useful for location/reception context, not as an image of the 1814 failure.
- UCL's reproduced Rocque/period-map discussion: a bounded Rookery between Great Russell Street, Tottenham Court Road, St Giles High Street and Dyot Street; a period map still needs to be obtained and checked against the named addresses.
- The inquest's street-level evidence: New Street house ruins, Great Russell Street/Tavistock Arms, inhabited cellars, furniture used for escape, and the water butt where Cooper was found. These are evidence locations, not a license to reconstruct an unverified camera path or wave height.

## Source-handling rules

1. Anchor the accident and inquest in S1–S5, but label S2/S4/S5 as reproduced newspaper wording until the original scans are inspected.
2. Keep the failed-vat quantity (**3,555 barrels**) separate from total beer lost (**7,664**, **8,000–9,000**, or modern litre/gallon estimates). These may measure different stages or use different reporting conventions.
3. Prefer “between five and six” or “about six” for the time until the original reports are compared. Do not force the 4:30/5:30/6:00 retellings into a false minute-by-minute sequence.
4. Use “St Giles Rookery” as a historical/local label, with UCL's geographic and demographic caveats. Do not turn a period stereotype into a description of every resident.
5. Treat the alleged ninth death, mass drinking, hospital riot, and later folklore as disputed reception claims, not part of the inquest-established death toll.
