## Reading rule

This timeline separates the **long crisis** (migration, land, border, economic integration and nationalist politics), the **football sequence** (June matches and public violence), and the **armed hostilities** (14–18 July). Dates are given at the confidence level the sources support; disputed dates are not silently normalised.

| Date | Event | Evidence and confidence | Causal reading / uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. 1920s–1960s | Salvadoran migration into less densely populated Honduras grows over decades. | Cable, pp. 658–659; Vieytez (S14); FRUS d644, p. 2. **Medium-high.** | Structural background. Exact migrant count and legal status vary by source. |
| 1958 onward | Central American Common Market links the economies while intensifying debates about trade, labour and industrial imbalance. | Cable, pp. 658, 665–671; López Bernal abstract. **Medium-high.** | Economic integration is context, not a single cause. |
| 1964–1969 | Honduras faces unemployment, land pressure and agrarian reform; Salvadoran migration becomes politically salient. | Cable, pp. 659–660; FRUS d644, pp. 2–3. **Medium-high.** | Keep Honduran peasant land demands distinct from anti-Salvadoran policy and from Salvadoran government strategy. |
| May 1967 | A serious border incident occurs, preceding the football matches by two years. | FRUS d644, p. 3. **High for the US record.** | Demonstrates that armed tension predated the 1969 qualifiers; exact incident details need border records. |
| Early 1969 | Migration and agrarian policy harden; the 1967 immigration arrangement is reported as not renewed. | Cable / Bar-On & Lewis (S17), citing archival and secondary records. **Medium.** | Verify with the bilateral text and Honduran legal record before narration. |
| 8 Jun 1969 | First World Cup qualifier: Honduras defeats El Salvador 1–0 in Tegucigalpa. | Honduras football results (S12); Bar-On & Lewis (S17). **High.** | Match is a public flashpoint, not evidence that football caused the interstate crisis. |
| 14–16 Jun 1969 | Violence against visitors and national symbols is reported around the second qualifier; anti-Salvadoran violence and property attacks follow. | IACHR Annual Report (S3) identifies violence on 14–16 June; IACHR *Acuerdos* (S2) orders investigations; FRUS d641/d644; Bar-On & Lewis. **High that violence occurred; medium on sequence and responsibility.** | Football and media helped accelerate nationalist hostility. The IACHR’s wording avoids assigning all claims to one side. |
| 15 Jun 1969 | Second qualifier in San Salvador: El Salvador defeats Honduras 3–0. | S12; RSSSF result record (orientation); S17. **High for match date/result.** | FRUS d644 refers to the aftermath beginning 16 June; distinguish match day from following violence. |
| 19 Jun 1969 | Honduran foreign minister protests reported mistreatment of Honduran visitors and national symbols. | Bar-On & Lewis (S17), citing the National Archives record. **Medium pending direct FCO file.** | Evidence of diplomatic escalation; do not quote the letter until FCO 7/1210 is inspected. |
| 26 Jun 1969 | El Salvador breaks diplomatic relations with Honduras and accuses Honduras of responsibility for conditions affecting Salvadoran emigrants. | FRUS d644, p. 2; FRUS d641. **High.** | Diplomatic rupture occurred before the deciding match and before armed hostilities. “Genocide” is an attributed Salvadoran charge, not an adjudicated finding. |
| 26 or 27 Jun 1969 | Deciding qualifier in Mexico City: El Salvador wins 3–2 after extra time. | S12 lists 27 June; RSSSF lists 26 June; S17 discusses 26/27 June and contemporaneous press. **High for result; medium for date.** | The playoff was symbolically powerful. Its causal role in the later war remains contested and should not be stated as a trigger. |
| 28 Jun 1969 | UPI headline calls the result “Soccer War Won by El Salvador, 3–2.” | Bar-On & Lewis (S17), footnote 127, links [UPI archive](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1969/06/28/Soccer-war-won-by-El-Salvador-3-2/3141560963046/). **Medium-high.** | Supports the early media frame; it does not show that the headline caused policy. |
| 4–10 Jul 1969 | IACHR subcommission visits both countries, interviews authorities and receives testimonies and government complaints. | IACHR *Antecedentes* (S1). **High.** | Human-rights documentation was underway before the war; later hostilities added a second phase of complaints. |
| 9 Jul 1969 | Kissinger reports the crisis may become armed conflict and recommends support for Central American mediation. | FRUS d642. **High.** | Decision pathway had moved into diplomacy and state policy, not merely stadium violence. |
| 14 Jul 1969, c. 18:00 | US memorandum reports Salvadoran air attacks on Honduran targets and ground attacks at border points; it also reports a Honduran strike, with some details unconfirmed. | FRUS d644, p. 1. **High for what the memorandum reported; medium for every operational detail.** | Start of armed hostilities in the diplomatic record. Attribution and “first attack” claims require both governments’ records. |
| 15 Jul 1969 | OAS Council appeals for cessation and restoration of the status quo ante; US report says El Salvador initiated the air attacks. | FRUS d644, pp. 1–2; d645. **High for OAS/US records.** | OAS becomes the principal mediation channel. |
| 16–17 Jul 1969 | OAS and Central American mediation continue; US instructions urge an immediate Salvadoran cease-fire; both governments in principle accept the proposal by 17 July. | FRUS d645, d647 notes. **High.** | A negotiated exit was already forming during the fighting. |
| 18 Jul 1969, midnight | OAS Council resolutions call for cease-fire, withdrawal within 96 hours, grounding of air forces, security guarantees and an end to inflammatory media campaigns. | FRUS d647, pp. 1–2; UN/OAS trail S9–S10. **High.** | The settlement mechanism itself recognised media mobilisation and civilian protection, not only troop movement. |
| 19 Jul 1969 | US embassy reports Honduran implementation of cease-fire. | FRUS d647 note 1. **High for the report.** | Does not settle whether all firing stopped everywhere at the same moment. |
| 21–23 Jul 1969 | Salvadoran government sets conditions for troop withdrawal, including guarantees for Salvadorans in Honduras, reparations and punishment; demonstrations and anti-OAS sentiment grow. | FRUS d649, §§2–6; d648. **High for the documents.** | Domestic pressure continued after the nominal cease-fire; football is no longer the operative policy issue. |
| 29–30 Jul 1969 | Thirteenth OAS Meeting of Consultation adopts resolutions; Salvadoran withdrawal is announced and implementation is negotiated. | OAS records route S9/S10; FRUS d652. **Medium-high pending the full acts.** | Avoid implying that 18 July ended every diplomatic or territorial dispute. |
| Aug–Oct 1969 | IACHR continues field activity and complaints concerning residents, property, life, liberty and occupied Honduran towns. | IACHR *Acuerdos* (S2), *Antecedentes* (S1). **High.** | Aftermath includes displacement and rights violations; exact totals remain unresolved. |
| 29 Apr 1970 | IACHR publishes its bilingual-relevant Spanish report, finding serious rights violations and noting insufficient remedies. | S1–S3. **High.** | Important primary evidence for civilian harm; not a final war-deaths count. |
| 30 Oct 1980 | El Salvador and Honduras sign the General Peace Treaty at Lima. | UNTS 21856 (S19). **High.** | Long aftermath; not the 1969 cease-fire. |
| 11 Sep 1992 | ICJ judgment resolves remaining land/island/maritime questions under the later legal process. | ICJ case 75 (S20). **High.** | Confirms that border questions remained unresolved after 1969; it does not prove they alone caused the war. |

## Date and duration cautions

- “Five-day war” / “100-hour war” refers approximately to 14–18 July, but the exact cease-fire, withdrawal and sporadic-fire chronology should be sourced to OAS and UN records.
- The second match was 15 June; violence is described by the IACHR across 14–16 June, while a US memorandum frames the aftermath from 16 June. These are not necessarily contradictory.
- The deciding match is listed as 26 June by some records and 27 June by the Honduran football list. Preserve the discrepancy pending original competition records.
