## Proposition under test

The strongest modern natural explanation is: a small delayed slab avalanche or snow collapse forced a rapid exit from the tent; severe weather prevented return; later snow loading/collapse in the ravine caused the major injuries; exposure killed most of the group.

This is a testable reconstruction, not the wording of the 1959 file. The evidence below supports parts of it and leaves other parts open.

## Evidence that supports the proposition

### 1. The scene is compatible with a sudden evacuation

The tent protocol records no bodies in the tent while clothing, food, packs and equipment remained. The closure records two inside cuts and a line of 8–9 tracks away from the tent. Those observations are compatible with an urgent environmental threat. They do not identify which threat.

### 2. The injury record is compatible with substantial later snow force

The autopsies record major rib and skull trauma in three people found in the ravine. The 2020 prosecutor account says a snow-filled hollow or collapse caused the injuries. Gaume and Puzrin’s dynamic simulations show that a small slab could, under their assumptions, produce severe but non-fatal thorax/skull injuries. This is convergence on physical possibility, not proof of the historical event.

### 3. The model answers several classic objections

The 2021 paper explicitly addresses four objections: no obvious avalanche debris after 26 days, a reported slope below the usual 30° rule of thumb, a delay after the tent cut, and unusual injuries. It proposes local geometry, a weak layer, the cut, and wind-driven deposition as a delayed-release mechanism. This makes the hypothesis more physically coherent than the unqualified claim “a normal avalanche swept the tent away.”

### 4. Human violence is not supported by the recorded scene

The 1959 closure says there were no traces of a struggle or other people, and it records valuables remaining with the group. That evidence weighs against a simple assault or robbery story. It does not prove every criminal theory impossible, and it does not identify the natural mechanism.

## Counterevidence and limits

### 5. The 1959 investigators did not record an avalanche

The closure uses “overwhelming force,” not “avalanche,” and its scene narrative describes tracks, the tent, bodies and a fire without naming avalanche debris. A contemporary search record that does not mention a small slab is meaningful negative evidence. It is not decisive after 26 days of wind, snow and search activity; the 2021 paper’s central contribution is precisely an explanation for why a small event might leave little visible trace.

### 6. The slope geometry is not settled

The 28 February tent protocol says the campsite was on a 30° slope. A different case-file summary describes the tent slope as about 18–20°. The 2021 study uses a locally steeper area around 28° in its terrain reconstruction. These may be measurements of different surfaces, but the pack cannot state “the slope was X°” without resolving the reference point, method and post-event snow cover.

### 7. The model depends on unobserved snow and wind conditions

The delayed-release mechanism assumes a weak snow layer, a cut in the slope, subsequent wind deposition, and a specific local geometry. The historical file does not preserve a direct measurement of those conditions at the tent on the night. The paper demonstrates that the mechanism can work under realistic parameter ranges; it does not demonstrate that each parameter existed that night.

### 8. The movement/medical sequence is still incomplete

The 1959 file says 8–9 track sets lead away from the tent, while three people later have major traumatic injuries. The 2021 paper says the simulated impact can be severe but non-fatal, but it does not prove how the injured people reached the forest/ravine, who moved whom, or when the injuries occurred. The 2020 account supplies a later snow-collapse scenario, but the public article does not provide the full expert record needed to audit it.

### 9. The weather record is indirect and disputed

The reproduced Burmantovo table is from a station 47 miles away and is expressly said not to belong to the case file. The 2020 prosecutor review uses a modern microclimate reconstruction and reports stronger local wind/cold conditions. The available pack therefore supports “severe weather was plausible and central to the later reconstruction,” not one precise wind-chill or visibility number.

### 10. The original closure and the 2020 conclusion answer different questions

The 1959 closure answered a criminal-investigation question and ended the case without identifying a culprit, using “overwhelming force.” The 2018–20 review sought a natural reconstruction and publicly selected an avalanche/slab explanation. A later official interpretation can be more specific without retroactively changing what the 1959 investigators wrote.

### 11. Radiation is a limited anomaly, not a causal explanation

The 1959 lab report documents beta contamination on selected clothing, reduced after washing, while analysed tissue is within natural levels and the emitter is not chemically identified. The prosecutor review attributes the clothing to prior visits to the East Ural radioactive trail. That is a plausible source attribution in the later review, not proof that radiation caused the deaths or that a weapon test occurred.

### 12. Competing extraordinary explanations remain unsubstantiated

The primary records located here do not establish a murder, Mansi attack, secret weapon, UFO or nuclear-test scenario. The absence of proof for those alternatives does not prove the avalanche model; it only prevents those theories from being used as factual narration. The correct editorial position is asymmetric: retain them as historical theories if needed, but label them unsupported and do not amplify sensational claims.

## Comparative assessment

| Explanation | What it explains | What it does not explain / status |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed slab + weather + later ravine snow collapse | Sudden exit, tent state, cold deaths, major injuries, and a physically modelled delay | Historical occurrence, exact movement sequence and all scene details remain unproven. **Best located natural hypothesis; medium-high provisional confidence.** |
| Generic large avalanche sweeping the group away | Sudden danger and force | Does not match the recorded tracks/tent/body distribution without substantial extra assumptions. **Too broad; do not use as shorthand for the model.** |
| Snowstorm/hypothermia without a slab | Exposure deaths and the 1959 “overwhelming force” wording | Does not independently explain the abrupt tent exit and major ravine injuries. **Possible partial account; incomplete.** |
| Human assault or robbery | Sudden evacuation and injuries in the abstract | No recorded struggle/other people, valuables remained, and no primary perpetrator evidence. **Not established.** |
| Weapon test / radiation event | Later conspiracy narrative and clothing anomaly | 1959 radiation report cannot identify source; prosecutor report says a rocket travelled elsewhere; no located primary causal record. **Not established.** |

## Working verdict

The evidence supports a constrained statement: **the 1959 file records a sudden, unexplained evacuation into lethal weather and traumatic deaths; the 2020 prosecutor review and 2021 paper make a delayed slab/snow-collapse sequence physically plausible, but neither source proves the complete historical sequence.**
