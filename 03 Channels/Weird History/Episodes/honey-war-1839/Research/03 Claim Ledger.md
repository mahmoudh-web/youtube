## Current safe wording

> The so-called Honey War was a Missouri–Iowa boundary crisis that escalated through tax collection, rival proclamations, an arrest and militia mobilisation. The 1839 federal file confirms the competing jurisdictional claims and the threat of force. Later accounts attach the name to bee trees cut in the disputed area, but they disagree on the count, and the original newspaper and court records still need checking. No battle is documented in this source pass. The Supreme Court later upheld the practical Sullivan line in 1849, with the boundary physically remarked in 1850–51.

This wording deliberately omits an exact bee-tree count, a named cutter, a definitive “only casualties were bees” claim, a fixed troop total, a claim that slavery alone drove the conflict, and any statement that the 1849 decision instantly ended all physical boundary work.
