## Purpose

This folder holds the source-led research pack for the provisional episode “Operation Mincemeat: The Corpse That Was Supposed to Win a War.” It examines what released records establish about the operation’s design, Spanish handling, German reception and the identity of the dead man used. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, dated timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification note and provisional conclusion.
- Separate the documented deception design from the later reconstruction of Spanish and German handling.
- Distinguish established fact, source-attributed report, interpretation and unresolved identity claims.
- Treat the effect on the Sicily campaign as contributory unless a military historian verifies a stronger causal claim from German operational records.
- Describe Glyndwr Michael as a person, not as a prop or punchline; avoid gratuitous corpse imagery and sensational language.
- Stop at human research verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key decisions

- Use `operation-mincemeat-1943` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat Montagu’s 31 March 1943 submarine orders as the clearest public description of the intended mechanism: a dead body, fabricated identity, briefcase and rubber dinghy released near Huelva to resemble an aircraft loss.
- Treat the 30 April 1943 launch as supported by the submarine’s operational report reproduced in Montagu’s account; verify the original report and archival page images before quotation.
- Treat German acceptance as directly evidenced by the 14–15 May intelligence material reproduced in Montagu’s memoir and cited to released files, while retaining that the memoir is a participant account and the German reports are mediated through British records.
- Treat “Glyndwr Michael” as the best-supported identification in the released-record tradition and Denis Smyth’s source-critical case, not as a claim cleared from the underlying files in this pass. Preserve the HMS `Dasher` alternative as disputed counterevidence.

## Constraints and invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not state that Mincemeat alone won the war, saved a specific number of lives, or solely caused German force movements. Sicily was an obvious target and German planning had multiple inputs.
- Do not describe the fictional identity as “Captain Martin” without noting that the planted papers identify him as Temporary Captain (Acting Major) William Martin, Royal Marines; sources and captions vary.
- Do not turn the Spanish autopsy/death-certificate story into a modern forensic certainty. The accessible account is mediated and the original Spanish records have not been inspected here.
- Do not present the “swallowed whole” message as independent proof of success; it is a British internal assessment, to be compared with German records.
- Archive catalogue references are verification routes, not proof of every file’s contents. A human must inspect WO 106/5921, ADM 223/794 and the relevant CAB 154 material.
- No body photograph, grave image, forged-document scan, map or declassified-looking recreation is approved for production. Rights, provenance, caption accuracy and dignity treatment require human review.
