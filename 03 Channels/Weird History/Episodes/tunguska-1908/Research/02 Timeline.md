## Evidence sequence

1. **Witness layer:** light, heat, sound, shaking, wind and later reports of fire or unusual night brightness.
2. **Instrumental layer:** seismic, barographic, magnetic and optical anomalies reported or connected after the event.
3. **Field layer:** radial/butterfly forestfall and burned trees documented from 1927 onward.
4. **Negative evidence:** no conventional crater and no proven macroscopic fragment after repeated searches.
5. **Model layer:** atmospheric breakup of a stony body best fits the combined field pattern in the cited modern models, but exact composition and any surviving fragment remain unsettled.

## Date and time cautions

- Russian 1908 records commonly use **17 June old style**, equivalent to **30 June new style/Gregorian**; verify each document rather than silently normalising it.
- “About 7:00,” “about 7:15,” and “soon after 8” come from different observers and locations. They are not a contradiction requiring one exact universal timestamp.
- The “impact” wording in later sources often means an atmospheric encounter/airburst. It must not be allowed to imply a confirmed ground crater.
