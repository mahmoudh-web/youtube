## Stop condition

This pack stops at research. No narration, title/thumbnail package, visual plan, archival download, edit, upload or publication decision is authorised by these notes. A human editor must inspect the primary records and approve any material claim before downstream work begins.

## Unresolved factual questions

- What do the Brazilian Navy logs and reports of proceedings say about the exact dates, positions, boarding authority, cargo and disposition of each 1962 and January 1963 vessel seizure?
- Does 24 February 1963 mark an initial French withdrawal, the removal of *Tartu*, or another operational milestone, and how does it relate to the 8 March announcement and 10 March departure recorded by later sources?
- Was the French carrier group merely exercising in/near West Africa, or were any elements ordered toward Brazil? What do French operational orders and Brazilian intelligence reports say?
- What were the exact original wording and legal status of the 1950 decree, the 1962 French Note Verbale No. 97, the 20 March 1963 arbitration communication and Brazil’s replies?
- Did the 1964 arrangement formally involve both governments, private fishing companies, a Brazilian industry group, or a mixed legal mechanism? What were the designated zones, vessel list, duration and compensation/profit terms?
- What exactly did the 1966 Rennes administrative decision decide, and was it a holding about French administrative liability, a summary of party positions, or a substantive ruling on international law?
- Where and when was the “kangaroo” line first recorded? Was it spoken by Paulo Moreira da Silva, reported by a journalist, reconstructed from a memoir, or later popularised by maritime writers?
- What do Brazilian fisheries records and French Breton fishing records establish about catches, depletion, prices, local employment and the interests of small versus industrial operators?
- Is there primary evidence for the contemporary suggestion of U.S. commercial influence, or is it only a newspaper interpretation repeated by later scholarship?

## Required primary-source checks

1. **Brazilian naval record:** request the Arquivo da Marinha files indexed under “Operação LAGOSTA,” including ship logs for *Ipiranga*, *Purus*, *Forte de Coimbra*, *Paraná*, *Pará*, *Pernambuco*, *Barroso* and *Tartu*-monitoring units.
2. **Brazilian foreign ministry record:** inspect the original notes and legal memoranda behind the FUNAG volume, including the French note, the 20 March 1963 letter and annexes, and the Brazilian replies.
3. **French diplomatic record:** use the French Foreign Ministry inventory to request the “Guerre des langoustes” and “Différend franco-brésilien relatif au droit de la pêche des langoustiers français” files.
4. **French parliamentary record:** inspect the full page surrounding the 1963 answer that says the dispute was submitted to arbitration; determine whether this was political shorthand or a procedural act.
5. **Settlement record:** locate the 10 December 1964 accord in Brazilian/French treaty or foreign-ministry collections; verify whether its language supports “26 vessels,” “five years,” “designated zones” and “tribute/profit share.”
6. **Legal background:** check the official UN 1958 Conference records, treaty signature/ratification status and the original 1966 Rennes decision before making any claim about the law’s final meaning.

## Language and translation checks

- Preserve Portuguese and French ship names exactly, including accents and historically variable spellings; confirm *Paul Goffeny/Gaufeny* and *Plomarc’h/Plomarch* against naval registers.
- Have a bilingual reviewer check translations of “plateau continental,” “plate-forme sous-marine,” “eaux territoriales,” “espèces sédentaires,” “pêche en haute mer” and “modus vivendi.”
- Do not turn a legal category into a biological claim. “Sedentary species” means the treaty’s harvest-stage test, not “an animal that never moves.”

## Legal, ethical and editorial-risk flags

| Risk | Why it matters | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| War inflation | “War” can imply formal conflict, combat and casualties not established here. | Use “Lobster War” only as attributed label; factual prose should say dispute/crisis/naval confrontation. |
| Unadjudicated illegality | Both governments disputed the legal basis for fishing and seizure. | Attribute “unauthorised,” “illegal” and “plunder” to the source or government using them. |
| Nationalist simplification | Brazilian institutional accounts may foreground sovereignty and naval resolve; French records foreground freedom of the seas/arbitration. | Present both legal positions and identify source viewpoint. |
| Cold War overreach | Period context can be mistaken for causation or proxy conflict. | Mark the Cold War reading as interpretation; require primary evidence for U.S./Soviet involvement. |
| Quotation provenance | The kangaroo line and “Brazil is not a serious country” circulate with competing attributions. | Do not quote until the original publication, speaker, date and translation are verified. |
| Local community erasure | “Brazil” and “France” can hide northeastern jangadeiros and Breton crews/armateurs. | Use careful group labels and seek fisheries/social-history sources before making livelihood claims. |
| Archival rights | Newspaper scans, ship photographs, maps and Navy images have separate access and reuse rules. | No asset is cleared by this pack; rights/provenance review is a later human gate. |
