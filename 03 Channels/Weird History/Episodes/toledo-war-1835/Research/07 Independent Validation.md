## Source quality and coverage assessment

### Strong sources

- The 15 June 1836 act ([5 Stat. 49](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large/Volume_5/24th_Congress/1st_Session/Chapter_99)) and 23 June 1836 propositions ([5 Stat. 59](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large/Volume_5/24th_Congress/1st_Session/Chapter_121)) directly support the legal settlement, subject to verification against official scans.
- The 26 January 1837 admission document is available as a National Archives primary-source transcript via [DocsTeach](https://docsteach.org/document/act-of-january-26-1837-5-stat-144-admitting-the-state-of-michigan-into-the-union/).
- The [National Archives Northwest Ordinance transcript](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/northwest-ordinance) independently confirms the foundational boundary language and includes an explicit Indigenous-rights provision that the episode’s current source pack does not follow through.
- The Ohio History Journal article is useful for chronology, contemporary correspondence references, force estimates, the Phillips Corners dispute, the Wood report, and the September court episode, but it is a 1985 secondary synthesis and should not replace the cited manuscripts.

### Limited or interested sources

- Michigan DMVA, Michigan Legislature, Michiganology, Clements Library, MSU, Ohio Memory, and Ohio History Connection pages are useful institutional summaries but carry state or regional framing. The DMVA page uses sports metaphors, calls the UP “compensation,” and declares an obvious winner; those are not neutral legal conclusions.
- W. V. Way’s 1869 book is a retrospective local history. It is valuable for locating affidavits and court material, but its selection and memory cannot settle injury severity or sole-casualty claims.
- The Toledo Lucas County Public Library page is primarily a retrieval guide. Its catalogue and collection listings prove that records exist, not what those records say.
- The LOC map catalogue and National Intelligencer object record are evidence leads; neither was inspected at image/page level in this validation.
- The Wikisource statute pages are readable transcriptions, not the official scans. Use the official Statutes at Large/Library of Congress scan in the human pass where exact legal quotation matters.

### Access limitations

The linked Michigan Legislature PDFs, LOC item page, GovInfo PDF URL listed as S13, LOC blog page, and Michiganology page were not directly retrievable through the browser on this pass. I used accessible corroborating pages or alternate primary transcripts where possible and have not treated inaccessible pages as independently verified primary evidence. The source index should update S13 to the accessible [DocsTeach/National Archives record](https://docsteach.org/document/act-of-january-26-1837-5-stat-144-admitting-the-state-of-michigan-into-the-union/) and add the official 5 Stat. 49/59 scans when retrieved.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradicted, or missing-source claims

- **High risk:** “sole casualty,” “only bloodshed,” “minor flesh wound,” or “Wood recovered” are later-account claims contradicted in severity by Mason’s contemporaneous “said to have been mortally wounded” report. They require the underlying affidavit, indictment, disposition, and medical/coroner evidence.
- **Medium risk:** “shots exchanged,” “battle,” and precise Phillips Corners force or shot counts are disputed by contemporaneous/near-contemporary reports. Retain only as attributed reports.
- **Medium risk:** the exact 29 August Mason-removal date and 11 December 1833 statehood-submission date were not confirmed from the cited primary records during this pass.
- **Medium risk:** exact Upper Peninsula fractions, “more than 9,000 square miles,” and resource-value claims are institutional summaries or later hindsight. Use qualified wording and map the 1835/1836 boundaries.
- **High risk:** any claim that the federal settlement was a cash payment, that Ohio “paid” Michigan, or that Congress used “compensation” is unsupported by the cited acts.
- **High risk:** the pack has no treaty, Indigenous-government, or community-history evidence. This is a coverage failure, not a minor caveat.
- **Source-index issue:** S6’s listed OCR/page locator and at least one footnote date require image-level checking; the article’s text at pp. 306–307 is useful but not a substitute for the underlying House Document, letters, or affidavit.
