## Executive verdict

**FAIL as a verified research pack; pass with corrections as a handoff to human verification.**

The pack's substantive conclusion is appropriately cautious: the episode and municipal response are well supported, while the name, exact scale, deaths, and cause remain disputed or unresolved. However, two citation/provenance errors must be corrected before the pack can be treated as source-checked:

1. Source S12 is attributed to Élisabeth Clementz, but the cited 2019 article is by **Christelle Ferraty**, *Alors, on danse !*, pp. 18–31. Clementz is the author of the separate catalogue chapter in S2.
2. The cited open copy for S11 is not Miller's *Dance Research* article. It is Dart Sebastiani, “The Rites of Saint Vitus,” in the 2012 *UCD Historical Studies Journal*. The Miller DOI is a separate source and must be cited separately.

The archival anchors are credible and repeatedly identified by the official Strasbourg exhibition materials, but the 25 July letter, 23 July commission/instructions, and 2 August register leaf were verified here through institutional reproductions and secondary transcriptions rather than against authenticated manuscript images and original-language readings. No exact death count is supportable from the checked evidence.

## Timeline and chronology audit

- **1515–1517 conditions:** supported in broad outline by Ferraty, pp. 24–25, but the same passage rejects calling Strasbourg itself a famine-stricken city because municipal grain reserves and other supplies mitigated scarcity. The pack's caveat is correct.
- **Mid-July to September:** supported by the official dossier, pp. 2–3, and Ferraty, pp. 23–24, at broad-window level.
- **14 July and 22 July:** source-dependent. The accessible evidence supports attribution to later chronicle/Brant traditions, not a single uncontested start date. Preserve both dates only with provenance.
- **23 and 25 July:** strongly anchored by the listed archival cotes and institutional transcriptions; not independently checked against manuscript images in this pass.
- **2 and 3 August:** the prohibition is anchored to the 2 August register leaf. The 30-shilling wording is a distinct secondary transcription dated by a calendrical formula; human review must reconcile the entries.
- **August–September decline:** supported as a broad trajectory, not an exact last-case date.
- **Paracelsus:** his later writings and Strasbourg presence/publication chronology are secondary-source territory. Drury, pp. 196–197, supports Strasbourg citizenship in December 1526, estimated 1526–27 and 1531 texts, and posthumous print transmission beginning in the 1560s. Do not describe him as a 1518 eyewitness or as having “investigated” every detail.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong or useful sources checked

- **Official Musées de Strasbourg exhibition materials:** strong institutional guide to the archival cotes, broad chronology, and source-critical framing. The smaller press dossier was accessible at <https://histoiresduniversites.files.wordpress.com/2018/11/fic3a8vre-de-la-danse.pdf>; the linked teaching dossier was indexed but too large for direct retrieval at <https://pedagogie.ac-strasbourg.fr/fileadmin/pedagogie/daac/Memoires__patrimoine__musees/musees_strasbourg/Dossier_enseignant_1518.pdf>.
- **ADBR 12 J 1495, AMS III 200/15, AMS 1 MR3 f. 72r:** the correct primary anchors. In this pass they were accessed through institutional catalogue excerpts, reproductions, and secondary transcriptions, not authenticated full manuscript inspection.
- **Ferraty, “Alors, on danse !”**: accessible full PDF at <https://numerabilis.u-paris.fr/ressources/pdf/sfhm/esfhm/esfhmx2019x04/esfhmx2019x04.pdf>, printed pp. 18–31. It is a medical-history communication published by the Société française d'histoire de la médecine. It is useful for synthesis and reproduces archival wording, but it is secondary and must be cited under **Christelle Ferraty**, not Clementz.
- **Clementz, “La danse de 1518 à l'épreuve des sources”**: catalogue chapter, 2018, pp. 33–64, at <https://archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/artdok/9773/1/Clementz_La_danse_de_1518_2018.pdf>. Heidelberg's PDF was blocked by an access-control page during validation; the official exhibition dossier and search-indexed metadata corroborate key points, but the PDF itself remains to be inspected by the human verifier.
- **Marillier & Godard (2023):** toxicology abstract at <https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1586845/1518-%E2%80%93-epidemie-de-danse-mortelle-a-strasbourg>. It is useful for showing that ergotism remains a debated hypothesis, but its abstract repeats several popular claims and is not primary evidence.
- **Gotman, *Choreomania: Dance and Disorder*:** OUP abstract at <https://academic.oup.com/book/5874>. Useful conceptual guardrail; it does not verify Strasbourg's numbers or archival wording.

### Source defects requiring correction

1. **S12 author error:** the PDF's title page and citation block identify Christelle Ferraty, not Élisabeth Clementz. Correct every S12 citation in the source index and claim ledger.
2. **S11 link mismatch:** the DOI <https://doi.org/10.3366/drs.2017.0199> is Miller's *Dance Research* article, but the linked open PDF <https://spl.cde.state.co.us/artemis/ucdserials/ucd5410internet/ucd54102012internet.pdf> is Dart Sebastiani's 2012 “The Rites of Saint Vitus.” It is a student historical essay, not Miller's article. Replace the open-copy link or label Sebastiani as a separate secondary source.
3. **S6 quality:** the Archives de Strasbourg LinkedIn post at <https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/archives-de-strasbourg_strasbourg-tournage-histoire-activity-7298381487148617731-nalr> is an official institutional summary, but it is a current social-media post, not an archival finding aid or original record. Use it only to corroborate the archive footprint.
4. **Etymology disagreement:** the official catalogue material and Ferraty describe different Greek derivations for “Troffea.” Do not present either etymology as settled without checking the original Paracelsian text and a specialist edition.

### Coverage gaps

- No original-language inspection of ADBR 12 J 1495, AMS III 200/15, or AMS 1 MR3 f. 72r was possible in this pass.
- Imlin, Duntzenheim, Rem, Specklin, Brant/Wencker and Paracelsian texts were not all checked in their original editions; the numbers and death wording therefore remain source-dependent.
- No municipal mortality, burial, hospital, or coroner-like record was located that can establish a death count or cause of death.
- The pack does include counterevidence against famine, exact deaths, named “patient zero,” a complete stage/music sequence, ergotism, and modern psychiatric diagnosis. That is a strength and should be retained after source corrections.
