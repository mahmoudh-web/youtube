## Source quality and coverage assessment

S2–S5 remain the strongest case evidence: contemporaneous Air Force material and an official CAA technical report. S2–S4 are external reproductions of archival scans, some image-only and difficult to read; S2 itself documents missing reports and incomplete interviews. S5 is authoritative for the radar-phenomenon study, but its direct Washington track conclusions are limited to August and its July material is tabulation/weather correlation rather than a complete case adjudication.

S1, S6 and S11 are authoritative archive guides or catalogue metadata, not case-level substitutes. S7 is useful but remains a secondary transcription pending comparison with official minutes. S8 is an institutional retrospective; its White House wording cannot prove Truman’s personal action. S9 is a useful cross-index, not primary evidence for exact times or causal claims. S10 is a retrospective. S12 is a reproduction/index and includes advocacy framing; it is a retrieval lead only.

Coverage is now adequate for a cautious research account of reported radar returns, reported lights, the two July episodes, the F-94 dispatch, the probable official inversion explanation, the CAA report’s distinct July/August scope and the non-extraterrestrial evidentiary limit. It is not adequate for exact normalized chronology, target identity, 7,000-mph/altitude calculations, White House/Capitol geography, a complete interceptor sequence, a definitive July atmospheric attribution, or public-belief claims.

## Held-back claims and counterevidence recheck

The corrected pack correctly keeps the following out of settled wording: objects over the White House or Capitol; 7,000 mph; jets chasing an alien craft; a cover-up; Truman personally demanding an explanation; secret Soviet/American technology as the cause; and extraterrestrial vehicles. The current sources support only that these were later or possible interpretations, not that they occurred or were true.

The counterevidence is preserved and materially adequate: strong radar returns are not proof of solid airborne objects; the B-25 highway-vectoring episode and Samford’s bird/ground-return discussion support ordinary-return alternatives; CAA’s formal July limitation is explicit; the missing reports weaken both confident debunking and confident extraordinary conclusions; and visual/radar correspondence remains an event-level hypothesis. No “all witnesses agreed” or “CAA proved the July case” wording survives in the corrected ledger/conclusion.
