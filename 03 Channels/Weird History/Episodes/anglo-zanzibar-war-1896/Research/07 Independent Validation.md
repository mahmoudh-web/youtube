## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS for transmission to human verification; FAIL for narration-readiness.**

The pack’s central discipline is sound: it separates fact, allegation, interpretation and uncertainty; preserves competing duration intervals; avoids calling the event bloodless; and stops before creative or production work. The Rawson reproduction does support a 09:00–09:40 firing interval, and Clowes supports a 09:37 cease-fire plus an approximately 500-person killed-or-wounded estimate. However, several citations are weaker or duplicated, some confidence labels are too high for the evidence actually opened, and the casualty category and legal wording remain materially unresolved. Human verification must therefore remain the gate.

### Coverage and independence findings

- The pack has strong source-led coverage of the Rawson report, treaty context, public duration disagreement, physical damage and the need for local/casualty records.
- It does not yet contain a directly inspected FO 107 item, Admiralty report of proceedings, naval log, Zanzibar Government casualty return, hospital/burial record, 2 Sep 1896 *Zanzibar Gazette* page, or German Consul account.
- S4 and S10 are duplicate evidential material. S13 is a catalogue lead, not event evidence. S15 and S16 remain inaccessible. The apparent source count therefore overstates independent corroboration.
- S3 should no longer be described only as an unverified treaty lead: the Hertslet scan contains the relevant succession clause at printed pp. 308–309. It is still a compilation requiring human archival comparison, but the claim ledger’s legal uncertainty should reflect this stronger published evidence.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory material

1. **“500 dead” versus “about 500 killed or wounded.”** S17 and S7 use a death count; S6 uses a combined category; S14’s abstract uses “slaughter.” These cannot be silently harmonised. The combined, attributed wording is the least overstated currently supported form.
2. **The 43-minute figure.** S7 repeats it but does not identify the underlying source. It remains a reception claim until its source is found.
3. **The 45-minute figure.** The pack says no independent 45-minute source was located, but Guinness World Records currently gives 09:00–09:45. This is not primary corroboration, but the duration ledger should acknowledge the source and its low evidential status.
4. **British casualty and ship-damage wording.** Rawson’s reproduced report names one seriously wounded petty officer and says no other material ship damage; Clowes says one seaman and reports that *Thrush* was hit more than a hundred times and *Racoon* and *Sparrow* were repeatedly struck. S17 says two British casualties. No final casualty or damage assessment is cleared.
5. **“Opposed Khalid’s accession” and legal authority.** The British preference and ultimatum are supported, and Hertslet Article V gives British Government approval over the Sultan’s nominated successor. That is stronger than the pack currently says, but it still does not establish unlimited authority, a complete local legal interpretation or the precise legal status of the bombardment.
6. **“Formal declaration of war.”** The pack has not shown one, but its non-location is not evidence that none exists. This is a research gap, not an established negative fact.
7. **Source independence.** S4/S10, S7, S14 and S17 are not independent primary confirmations of the central timing/casualty claims. The pack should not imply that later summaries multiply the strength of one underlying report.
8. **Loaded terminology.** “Usurper,” “enemy,” “slaves left to fight,” “surrender,” “victory,” “puppet” and “bloodless” require attribution or removal. The legal-sensitivity note correctly flags this; S17 itself demonstrates why the constraint is necessary.

## Required corrections and additional primary-source checks

Before any narration or packaging, the human verifier should:

1. Update the claim ledger’s S3 locator to Hertslet No. 42, printed pp. 308–309, and quote Article V only after comparing the scan with the English/Arabic agreement and the archival record.
2. Mark S4 and S10 as one underlying report; downgrade S4 from “primary” to “contemporary primary material reproduced by a secondary intermediary.”
3. Remove S13 from C02 support and label it a catalogue lead only.
4. Add the Guinness 09:00–09:45 entry as a modern reception record, while keeping it separate from primary timings; locate the source behind 43 minutes.
5. Retrieve FO 107/54 and FO 107/57, including Basil Cave’s 25–28 August telegrams, and record item/page or folio locators.
6. Retrieve Admiralty correspondence and reports of proceedings for *St George*, *Thrush*, *Racoon/Raccoon*, *Sparrow* and *Philomel*, plus the casualty and medical returns. Resolve first discharge, cease-fire, flag fall, ship damage and British casualty count.
7. Inspect the 2 Sep 1896 *Zanzibar Gazette* supplement and Zanzibar administrative, hospital, burial and damage records. Record whether each number means killed, wounded, missing or a combined estimate.
8. Inspect original newspaper page images for S8, S9, S11 and S16; preserve publication, page, dateline, wire/eyewitness status and OCR corrections.
9. Check the German Consul’s account and Khalid’s 2 Oct transfer in FO 107/57 and German diplomatic records.
10. Obtain specialist Zanzibar/East Africa review of “protectorate,” “succession,” “surrender,” “war,” “victory,” “annexation,” “colonial violence,” local agency and the casualty frame.
