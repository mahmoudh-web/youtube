## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS.** The pack is unusually careful about attribution, counterevidence, uncertainty and the no-script boundary. Its central finding is supportable as a provisional interpretation: the response combined a highly visible military signal with an operation planned to avoid a direct clash, followed by diplomatic changes in the JSA.

It is not ready for narration or production without the corrections below. The load-bearing unresolved points are the work-party composition and casualty breakdown, the assault duration, the total Operation Paul Bunyan force, which aircraft were actually overhead versus on standby, the exact guard-post outcome, and the Korean wording of the regret message. The pack is safe to send to a human verifier as a marked research file; it is not publication-ready and does not constitute approval.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong sources

- S1–S7 (FRUS) are contemporaneous U.S. diplomatic and policy records. They are strong for what U.S. officials knew, debated, ordered and misunderstood. They are not neutral adjudications of North Korean intent.
- S8 (Ford Presidential Library) is a primary scanned packet. Direct scan checks of pp. 3, 62, 111–113 confirmed the JCS artillery recommendation, the 20 August situation report, and the incident account. The scan contains redactions, OCR errors and mixed document provenance; scan-page references must be retained.
- S14 (U.N. Security Council document S/12263) is the right primary institutional record for the incident and JSA changes, but the PDF was blocked by the Digital Library’s access challenge during this pass. Search-index text was visible, but it is not a substitute for page-level inspection.
- S16 (U.S. National Archives) is an authoritative armistice transcript and adequate for background legal/institutional context, not for the detailed 1976 rules of the JSA.

### Useful but bounded sources

- S9 (National Archives of Korea) is an official 2006 Korean-language institutional summary, strong for identifying Korean state memory and useful chronology, but it compresses events and includes claims requiring source-chain and bilingual checking. Its force counts, “war plan” language and Kim Jong-il attribution should not be treated as settled.
- S10 and S12 are later U.S. Army institutional/commemorative accounts. They are useful for unit names and the Army’s memory of the operation, but they simplify totals and use celebratory language. S12’s 42-minute figure conflicts with S11’s 45 minutes.
- S11 is a professional retrospective, not a contemporaneous after-action report. It supplies the 813-person, four-minute and 45-minute figures but relies on later sources and conflicts with the Ford incident report’s five-to-six-minute estimate. It also reports twelve South Korean laborers, whereas the Ford packet reports five and S9 reports five.
- S13 is an excised Strategic Air Command history hosted by the National Security Archive. It is useful for SAC activity, B-52 sorties and Midway timing, but it is an edited extract and the host’s summary must not replace the underlying record.
- S15 is a commercial research article reproducing attributed excerpts/images from FBIS, the *Washington Evening Star* and the Congressional Serial Set. It is useful for the North Korean narrative and message wording, but the original scans should be checked before quoting or translating.
- S17 is a 2023 Army doctrinal interpretation. It supports an analytical frame, not independent proof of motives, causal effects or counterfactual prevention of war.

### Coverage gaps

The pack still lacks the complete operation order and after-action report; contemporaneous medical/personnel casualty records; the JSA rules in force on 18 August; aircraft sortie/load and Navy carrier logs; a bilingual check of the Korean and North Korean records; the complete 6 September JSA agreement and implementation record; and the full 1992 broadcast behind the Kim Jong-il allegation. The U.N. report must be retrieved and inspected directly. These are not cosmetic gaps: they affect numbers, legal wording, attribution and the causal interpretation.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory items

1. **Do not present 813 as the total force.** It is a retrospective figure whose perimeter is undefined and conflicts with the Ford packet’s component figures.
2. **Do not present four minutes, 42 minutes or 45 minutes as the sole settled duration.** The source discrepancy is real; the primary incident report adds a five-to-six-minute account for the assault.
3. **Do not use a fixed work-party composition.** Five laborers is repeated by the Korean archive and Ford packet, but the escort counts and the *Military Review* total differ.
4. **Do not call the later message an apology without bilingual review.** The strongest directly checked wording is regret/regrettable plus a self-defence warning.
5. **Do not describe KPA non-interference as proven surrender, fear or a causal “backdown.”** The absence of a second clash is observable; motive is contested.
6. **Do not claim the operation alone prevented war.** That is an unprovable counterfactual.
7. **Do not treat the Kim Jong-il claim as fact.** The located sources show a later allegation, not a verified order.
8. **Do not repeat “most heavily armed” as an objective historical superlative.** Attribute it to the 2011 Army article or explain it as title language.
9. **Do not infer nuclear weapons were carried.** The sources establish nuclear-capable platforms/context, not a nuclear load.
10. **Do not collapse UNC, U.S. Army and ROK forces into one institution.** The records describe an allied/command arrangement with distinct authorities and perspectives.

## Required corrections and additional primary-source checks

Before human approval of any later script, the researcher or human verifier should:

- retrieve the complete Ford/NARA packet and operation order, then reconcile the 813, 110 and 300 figures by defining the perimeter counted;
- obtain the contemporaneous incident, medical and personnel records to confirm the nine wounded and the U.S./ROK breakdown;
- resolve the four-minute versus five-to-six-minute assault account and the 42-versus-45-minute tree-cutting account;
- inspect the full 6 September JSA agreement, maps and implementation record, including the two-versus-four guard-post discrepancy;
- retrieve the U.N. S/12263 scan and the relevant MAC minutes directly;
- check the Korean text of the regret message and the original FBIS reports with a bilingual reviewer;
- verify aircraft, ship, unit and timing claims against SAC, Air Force, Navy and Pacific Command logs;
- check the complete 1992 broadcast and independent intelligence material before mentioning Kim Jong-il;
- retain the pack’s sensitivity language: two people were killed, others were wounded, and this was a serious Cold War military crisis, not comic material.
