## Research question

What do the 1908 reports, later eyewitness records and first expeditions establish about the Tunguska explosion, and which impact or atmospheric-explosion models remain plausible when there is no conventional crater or recovered macroscopic fragment?

## Conflict and episode promise

The event is well established as a powerful atmospheric explosion over the Siberian taiga. The unresolved question is the object's composition and exact failure path: the strongest current models favour a stony asteroid airburst, while cometary, surviving-fragment/Lake Cheko and other proposals remain part of the historiography with unequal evidential support. The pack keeps the eyewitness record separate from later scientific reconstruction.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or official:** contemporary report, archival document, first expedition publication, official scientific record, or peer-reviewed paper presenting original analysis.
- **B — institutional/archive synthesis:** museum, university archive, NASA historical synthesis, or curated catalogue that identifies or reproduces primary material.
- **C — scholarly secondary or contested interpretation:** review, response, or preprint used to map disagreement rather than settle it alone.
- **D — popular retelling:** lead only; not used for material claims.
