## Scope and method

I independently read the episode `CONTEXT.md`, the complete source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence, uncertainty/legal-sensitivity note and conclusion. I checked every claim in C01–C16 against the cited online source, then cross-checked load-bearing facts against the official Oregon Historical Society (OHS) collection record and a contemporaneous 1979 newspaper scan/OCR. I did not edit the researcher files.

The cited newspaper pages are readable transcriptions hosted by TheExplodingWhale.com and link to Google Drive scans. The transcription pages were accessible on 2026-09-15; the linked scans returned cache-miss errors in this review, so exact newspaper wording, typography and names remain subject to human image inspection. The KATU transcript is likewise a transcription/mirror, not a substitute for checking the preserved film and audio.

## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections.** The core account is well supported: a carcass later identified as a sperm whale was handled by the Oregon State Highway Division; George Thornton is identified as the field engineer in charge and as the person who signalled the blast; the intended disposal method and failed outcome are documented; and the KATU/OHS custody history is substantially corroborated. The pack appropriately distinguishes reported fact, retrospective attribution, interpretation and uncertainty.

Corrections are required before any claim is promoted into narration: remove the irrelevant S12 citation from the Navy claim; downgrade or qualify mirror/transcription sources; correct the footage-rights statement using the OHS catalogue; source or remove the towing/haul-out reference; preserve the 1970 newspaper transcription/date anomalies for scan checking; and keep the 1979 treatment as a resolved source distinction rather than an unexplained contradiction. No script, production, packaging or footage reuse is cleared by this report.

## Additional pack-level findings

### Chronology, names and quantities

- The 1970 sequence is coherent: beaching on Monday 9 November; notification on Tuesday 10 November; planned and completed blast on Thursday 12 November; follow-up reporting on 17 and 23 November. The weekday/date combination in S3 needs scan checking: its page heading says 11/13/1970 and “Friday” in the page note, while the article body says “Thursday, November 13.” The timeline correctly avoids repeating the weekday, but any quotation or date caption should use the scan.
- The 11 November transcription says “Wednesday, November 11, 1970,” which is calendar-consistent, but the page contains transcription errors (“distriact,” “expcetion” elsewhere in the set). Do not silently normalize quoted copy.
- Umenhofer is misspelled “Umenhaufer” in S4’s transcription; the page’s correction note gives “Umenhofer.” Use the corrected spelling only after the scan check. Avoid reproducing his street address, which S5 exposes unnecessarily.
- The arithmetic for twenty 50-pound cases equals 1,000 pounds. “Half-ton” is a U.S. short-ton conversion, not an independent measurement. S6 records Thornton’s uncertainty about the quantity, so describe the quantity as the reported load, not as a technically justified amount.

### Quotation check

- Thornton’s “It went just exactly right” assessment is present in S3, lines 59–60, but the same passage records the sand-funnel problem, debris toward the parking lot and subsequent burial. It must remain an attributed contemporaneous assessment, not a success finding.
- Linnman’s “the blast blasted blubber beyond all believable bounds” appears in the transcript S6, lines 47–48, and is reproduced by S8, lines 34–36. It is broadcast narration and should not be presented as a neutral technical description.
- Umenhofer’s “They’re just lucky they didn’t kill somebody” appears in S5, lines 30–34. It is a contemporaneous witness/claimant criticism; attribute it and do not turn it into an independently established engineering or legal conclusion.
- All quoted newspaper wording remains provisional until the linked scan images are inspected. The transcript’s wording should be checked against the preserved film/audio, with the OHS MI11196 shot-list timestamps used for any final edit or quotation.

### The 1979 comparison

The pack correctly identifies a source discrepancy but can make it more precise. S8, lines 37–38, summarizes the 1979 event as burial. The later ODOT employee account S9, lines 38–40, says State Parks burned and buried the whales. An independently checked contemporaneous University of Oregon newspaper scan/OCR, *Oregon Daily Emerald*, 21 June 1979, https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2004260239/1979-06-21/ed-1/seq-4/ocr/, reports that the carcasses “have been burned and then buried on the site” and that trenches were dug for burning and burying. The likely correction is to describe the 1979 comparison as burning followed by burial, while noting that the Oregon Encyclopedia uses the shorter “buried” summary. Human review should inspect the scan image and source the 18–19 June reporting if the comparison becomes load-bearing.

### Unsupported or missing-source references

- `04 Counterevidence.md` refers to “later ABC/ODOT retellings” for towing/haul-out, but no ABC URL, date, transcript or source ID appears in the source index. This is a traceability gap. Add the exact ABC source and passage, or remove towing/haul-out from the documented alternatives and retain it only as an explicitly unresolved possibility.
- The source index classifies S11 as A/C, but the item reviewed is a KATU retrospective reproduced on TheExplodingWhale.com. Under the pack’s own key it should be treated as C/D unless the original KATU page is cited directly. S10 is similarly a mirror/portal page (D) even though it describes a KATU production. S9 is an agency publication in origin but a D mirror in access; it remains a retrospective, not a 1970 authorization record.
- S12 is an official OHS event page and supports the collection description, Linnman identity and attributed viewing estimate, but not Navy consultation or the decision chain. Remove it from C06 and C05 where it is used for those propositions.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

Coverage is strong for the event’s broad chronology, operational decision-maker, stated disposal rationale, explosive quantity, public-safety outcome, property damage, later compensation and footage custody. Coverage is weak or unresolved for the formal approval chain, Navy advice, the exhaustive alternatives analysis, the exact 1979 agency sequence, and licensing terms.

The best sources are the contemporary Register-Guard reports (S1–S5, subject to scan verification), the preserved OHS item record for MI11196, the official OHS custody note (S7) and the ODOT employee publication (S9) for what the agency later claimed. The weakest links are the privately hosted transcription/mirror pages and later institutional summaries used for disputed or causal claims. The Oregon Encyclopedia is a useful institutional synthesis and points to underlying 1979 reporting, but it should not replace the contemporary scans.

- OHS Digital Collections, KATU news footage, https://digitalcollections.ohs.org/katu-news-footage: collection identity, research access, KATU as creator and KATU-TV copyright condition.
