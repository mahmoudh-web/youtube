## Research question

How was the Quebec strategic maple syrup reserve breached over 2011–12, how did the stolen syrup move through the market, and what do the Quebec and Supreme Court records actually establish about responsibility and punishment?

## Working answer and scope

The strongest court record supports a sustained extraction scheme rather than a single overnight break-in: syrup was removed from a Federation warehouse, transferred through trucks, tankers and pumps, replaced with water or inferior syrup, and resold through Vallières. The Supreme Court records Vallières’s convictions for theft, fraud and trafficking in property obtained by crime, his admission that $10 million in syrup-sale income passed through his hands, and the restoration of a $9,171,397.57 fine after the Quebec Court of Appeal reduced it. The record does not make every person arrested in the investigation a convicted participant, and it does not justify presenting police-era suspicions as findings.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary judicial or official record:** Supreme Court reasons, Quebec judgments, court filings, SQ activity report, or official producer release.
- **B — official explanatory or regulatory record:** Supreme Court case brief, Régie/Quebec government report, or statutory source.
- **C — reputable Canadian reporting:** Canadian Press, Global News, Radio-Canada/RCI and Quebec reporting; useful for chronology and public response, but not a substitute for a verdict.
- **D — research lead:** secondary synthesis or later feature used only to locate a primary record or identify a disputed detail.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use S1, S3 and S4 for judgments; use S5 and S6 as party filings and evidence leads, not as verdicts.
2. Use “police reported,” “the Federation said,” “the Crown argued,” or “the Court found” so the source status remains visible.
3. Keep the initial 10m-pound/$30m estimate separate from the court’s 5,935,250-pound/$17,805,750 figure and later $18m shorthand.
4. Do not use the names in 2012 arrest reports as perpetrators without a documented conviction or guilty plea.
5. “Reserve,” “cartel,” “black market,” “organized,” “ringleader” and “insider” need source attribution or narrower descriptive wording.
