## Stop condition

This pack stops at research. No narration, title/thumbnail package, visual plan, archival download, edit, upload or publication decision is authorised by these notes. A human editor must inspect the primary records and approve each material claim before downstream work begins.

## Unresolved factual questions

- What is the defensible event window: 25–31, 26–31, or 27–31 October, and what does each date mark?
- Can the original Weather Bureau observations and PHS figures establish the onset, peak stability, wind direction, humidity, inversion depth and dispersal day?
- What exactly do the Pennsylvania State Archives reports titled “Report of the Donora Disaster,” “Progress Report on Investigations as to Causes,” and “Report of Investigation, Borough of Donora” say about source attribution and deaths?
- Does the PHS report’s 20 include only Donora and Webster, and what time window does “during or shortly after” cover?
- Why do later sources report 21 or 22 deaths? Are these delayed deaths, wider geography, a different coroner list, or uncited repetition?
- Which industrial units were operating on each day, and what do plant logs, production records and emission-control records show about curtailed operations?
- What were the exact contemporaneous findings on fluoride, sulfur dioxide, sulfur compounds, particulate matter, carbon monoxide and other contaminants? Which results came from post-event sampling rather than the acute episode?
- What was the precise role of domestic heating, locomotives and river traffic relative to the steel and zinc works?
- What were the emergency response times, hospital admissions, oxygen distribution and evacuation advice? Which details come from contemporaneous logs versus retrospective memory?
- What court(s) heard the 1951 civil suits against American Steel and Wire, what claims were pleaded, and what did the settlement release or not decide?
- What is the full methodology and result set of the 1957/1961 mortality and morbidity follow-up, and what confounders did the authors discuss?
- Which legislative records show Donora’s influence on Pennsylvania’s 1949 division and the federal 1955/1963/1967/1970 sequence? Is “inspired the Clean Air Act” an archival claim, a later slogan, or a supported causal conclusion?

## Required primary-source checks

1. **Original PHS report:** inspect the NLM scan of Bulletin No. 306 (S2), especially the foreword, acute mortality tables, atmospheric-study methods, meteorological figures, discussion of cause, summary and recommendations. Record page numbers and any OCR corrections.
2. **Pennsylvania state records:** request or inspect the Carton 14 Donora Smog, 1948 folder listed by the State Archives (S4). Preserve file titles, dates, authors, recipients, and the exact wording of source-attribution conclusions.
3. **Weather record:** locate U.S. Weather Bureau daily observations and the report’s original figures. Resolve whether 25–31, 26–31 and 27–31 describe different stages.
4. **Contemporary press:** inspect 26 October–7 November 1948 issues of the Donora Herald-American, Pittsburgh Press, Pittsburgh Post-Gazette and other local/regional papers for death lists, plant operations, response chronology and public allegations. Treat newspaper reports as contemporary evidence, not automatically as verified cause.
5. **Plant and labor record:** locate American Steel and Wire/Donora Zinc Works production and safety records, plus worker or union responses, if using exact operating claims or the industry’s position.
6. **Litigation:** locate the 1951 court docket, complaints, settlement notices and any available releases. Do not call the settlement an admission, exoneration or liability finding without the primary record.
7. **Long-term follow-up:** read Ciocco and Thompson (S11) and the catalogued 1957 report (S13), then distinguish their study population, comparison groups, time window and limitations.
8. **Legislative chain:** check the original Pennsylvania administrative/legal records and federal statutory histories. Keep “policy influence” separate from the dates and legal content of each act.

## Language and health checks

- “Smog,” “pollution,” “illness,” “affected,” “severely affected,” “death during or shortly after,” and “long-term mortality” are not synonyms.
- Use “the PHS report counted” for 20 deaths and 5,910 affected people. Report 21/22 only as later variants with a source and unresolved definition.
- Use “major contributor,” “source category,” “associated with,” and “consistent with” where the report does not establish sole causation.
- Do not diagnose individuals from historical descriptions. Do not state that a named pollutant caused a named person’s death unless a primary medical record explicitly supports it and a human editor approves disclosure.
- Avoid graphic descriptions of respiratory distress, autopsies, animal deaths or dead bodies. The research note may identify that evidence for fact-checking without turning it into spectacle.

## Legal, ethical and editorial-risk flags

| Risk | Why it matters | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Defamation / corporate blame | American Steel and Wire and the zinc works are named in accusations, but the PHS report found multiple sources and the located litigation account says no blame was adjudicated. | Attribute accusations; avoid “killed,” “murder,” “guilty,” “cleared,” or “liable” without primary legal support. |
| Medical causation | The acute episode exacerbated illness in vulnerable residents; later studies are observational. | Separate acute findings, pre-existing disease, and later associations; no individual-level causal claims without records. |
| Mortality inflation | 20, 21 and 22 appear in different summaries. | Use the PHS count as the wording gate and display the discrepancy in the ledger. |
| Chemical overclaim | Popular accounts sometimes elevate fluoride or sulfur dioxide to sole-cause status. | Preserve the PHS “no single substance” finding and its multi-source analysis. |
| Community erasure | “The town” hides Donora, Webster, workers, families, Black residents and people outside the borough boundary. | Name the study area and affected communities; investigate PHS race, housing and geography findings before generalising. |
| Policy overclaim | Donora is remembered as the origin of clean-air law, but legislation followed a wider sequence. | Say “helped influence/publicise” unless a specific primary legislative link is documented. |
| Archival rights | Report scans, newspaper pages, photographs and local-history materials have different reuse terms. | This pack records research leads only; rights review is a later human gate. |
