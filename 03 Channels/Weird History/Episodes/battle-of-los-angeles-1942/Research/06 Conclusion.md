## Provisional answer

On 24–25 February 1942, Los Angeles experienced a real military emergency response without a confirmed enemy attack. Reports and a reported offshore radar contact led to a blackout; searchlights and anti-aircraft units then fired roughly 1,400–1,430 rounds. No bomb was dropped and no aircraft was confirmed or shot down in the accessible record. The event became a “battle” because the response was visible, loud, geographically broad, and followed immediately by incompatible official explanations.

The leading later explanation is a meteorological balloon or other small object triggering an already tense defense system, followed by shell bursts, searchlights, and expectation multiplying perceived targets. That explanation has support in the official Army history, the 1949 antiaircraft retrospective, and later institutional synthesis, but the initial radar contact, exact trigger, firing chronology, and casualty record are not settled at primary-record level in this pass.

## What can safely be said now

- The barrage, blackout, searchlights, and lack of a confirmed downed aircraft are high-confidence episode facts.
- The Army/Navy disagreement is itself a documented event and a strong narrative engine.
- “One to five,” “as many as fifteen,” and witness counts must remain attributed statements, not a chosen object count.
- The Ellwood shelling and EO 9066 are context, not proof of a causal chain from one event to the other.
- The balloon/war-nerves account is a leading interpretation, not a fully demonstrated reconstruction.
- UFO/alien claims belong to later mystery culture, not to the evidentiary core.

## What remains unsafe

- “The Army fired at nothing” is too absolute; use “no enemy aircraft was confirmed or shot down in the located record.”
- “Fifteen Japanese planes attacked” is unsupported by the accessible evidence.
- “A weather balloon was definitely the target” is premature until the radar, command, firing, and inquiry records are checked.
- Any exact casualty total, exact number of aircraft, exact radar distance/time, or claim based on the famous photograph needs source-level verification.
- “The raid caused Japanese-American incarceration” is historically and ethically unsafe; EO 9066 predates the barrage and its broader causes require their own evidence.
