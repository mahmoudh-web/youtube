## Fact / allegation / interpretation / uncertainty controls

- **Fact:** BSES imported a colony from Hawaii in June 1935, bred it at Meringa and released young toads around Gordonvale; further distribution was later prohibited by the Commonwealth.
- **Period claim:** Mungomery and contemporary Queensland press presented overseas evidence as support; Froggatt warned of pest potential; Bulcock defended the toad’s safety and usefulness.
- **Interpretation:** The policy transferred a biological-control success story across jurisdictions without demonstrating that the target beetles and ecological conditions were sufficiently comparable.
- **Uncertainty:** The complete authorisation chain, the exact scope of pre-release testing and the documents behind the 1936 reversal remain unlocated.

Do not change “BSES-led” into “the Queensland Government ordered” without an administrative record. Do not change “later science identifies” into “officials knew.” Do not change “warnings were raised and the ban reversed” into a simple motive claim that named people did not care.

## Open questions

1. Which BSES, Department of Agriculture and Stock, quarantine or ministerial record authorised the June import and August 1935 release?
2. Did BSES conduct an Australian feeding or field-effectiveness trial before release, and did it conduct any systematic environmental-risk assessment? If so, what were the methods and results?
3. What exact local-beetle evidence did Bell and Mungomery rely on, and how did they address the period warning that greyback beetles were not regularly ground-accessible?
4. What was the date, legal basis, scope and wording of J. H. L. Cumpston’s Commonwealth prohibition on further distribution?
5. What correspondence links Bulcock, Forgan Smith, Lyons, BSES and Cumpston to the September 1936 reversal, and who made the final decision?
6. What does the original Froggatt article say beyond the short warning quoted in the NMA/SLQ reproductions, and was there a contemporaneous BSES reply?
7. What do the original 1941 QAJ and BSES annual records show about actual crop damage and target-beetle control?
8. Which later ecological claims can be supported at species-and-site level, and which are popular generalisations that should be excluded?

## Legal, ethical and editorial sensitivity

### Defamation and blame

The main named actors are historical figures, but modern descendants, institutions and successors may still be affected by careless wording. Avoid “negligent,” “reckless,” “criminal,” “corrupt” or “ignored science” unless a qualified historical/legal source supports the precise claim. The evidence supports institutional overconfidence and an incomplete evidence base more readily than personal bad faith.

### Scientific and causal overstatement

Do not claim that the 1935 release alone caused every later ecological decline. Use the peer-reviewed pathway—susceptible predators ingesting toxic toads—and identify study species, place and date. Do not use current population estimates unless separately sourced and date-stamped.

### Indigenous and community impacts

Modern impacts may include damage to culturally important native food sources and places, but that claim needs specific, respectful sourcing and community context. Do not add it to narration from the generic parliamentary summary alone.

### Animal welfare and graphic treatment

The story concerns a deliberate animal introduction and later poisoning of native predators. Avoid grotesque or celebratory language, close-up toxin imagery or decontextualised animal suffering. A human editor must make any later visual/ethical decision; this pack contains no visual or production brief.

### Archival and copyright controls

Government and museum pages may reproduce period text or images under different terms. Public access is not a blanket licence. Verify the rights status of newspaper scans, archival photographs and any historical journal page before reuse, and record permissions separately from factual citations.

## Human-verification stop gate

Research stops here. Before any script or production material is made, a human must:

1. inspect the original 1935–36 newspaper and QAJ scans;
2. locate the BSES import, quarantine and release records;
3. identify the Commonwealth ban’s date, authority and wording;
4. verify the correspondence and decision behind the September 1936 reversal;
5. read the original Froggatt article and the 1941 QAJ effectiveness material;
6. approve the wording “BSES-led release” versus any stronger authorisation claim;
7. approve modern ecological claims with species-level citations and current rights/ethics treatment.
