## Timeline and chronology audit

- The date, afternoon timing, 14 finishers, Hicks’s 3:28:53, Lorz’s apparent first finish/DQ, and the broad sequence of Hicks’s late treatment are supported.
- The exact start time is not yet primary-source verified. Olympedia says 15:00; Smithsonian says 3:03 p.m.; the period programme/newspaper should decide.
- Lucas’s mile markers are not internally stable: the Fourteen-Mile House is described as thirteen miles from the stadium, while the medical narrative uses ten, seven, twenty and other markers. Keep “about” and do not plot an exact route from Lucas alone.
- Lucas’s dog account conflicts internally (one dog/one runner/almost a mile versus two dogs/both runners). The timeline must preserve that conflict.
- The sanction is unresolved: Lucas/Barnett report one year, while modern summaries report a life ban later commuted. Verify AAU records and the relevant contemporary reports before choosing one.
- The Genty article and its IOC correspondence are real and discoverable through the LA84 item record/search extract; the direct ISOH PDF timed out during this validation, so the article’s cited newspaper passages remain a human inspection requirement.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong or useful sources

- **S1 Lucas (1905):** period near-primary account with unusually specific pages, but written by Hicks’s handler who drove behind him and defended the support cars. It is strong evidence for what Lucas reported, not neutral proof of every medical, racial or causal assertion.
- **S3 Sullivan (1909):** period handbook/official-adjacent summary. The OCR directly verifies 29 starters and 14 finishers at printed p. 106/scan p. 121, but it is retrospective and not a race-day log.
- **S4/S5 Genty (2023):** specialist historical interpretation with a useful contemporary-newspaper trail and IOC correspondence. The LA84 metadata/search extract was accessible; the direct PDF was not reliably fetchable. Do not treat the article as a substitute for the newspapers it cites.
- **S13 IOC anti-doping overview:** useful institutional context for the absence of a located prohibition, but not the period rule text.
- **S12 NCBI/StatPearls:** suitable modern toxicology context. It confirms strychnine toxicity and historical performance-enhancement use, not the 1904 dose, symptoms or causation.

### Limited or orientation-only sources

- **S8 Smithsonian:** reputable explanatory reporting and useful source-finding, but claims about Sullivan’s intent, apples/nap, surgery and exact Lorz details remain secondary unless its linked primary sources are inspected.
- **S9 Olympedia:** useful cross-check for date, participants, result order and modern name forms, but its narrative is a synthesis and should not be treated as an original record.
- **S10 NWS:** authoritative about archive limitations and record provenance, but the opened pages do not settle the 30 August 1904 race-day observation.
- **S11 PubMed/Noakes:** the abstract supports the history of changing hydration guidance (2007, lines 233–237), not a diagnosis of 1904 runners or Sullivan’s motive.
- **S14 Extreme Weather Watch:** gives the useful counterpoint of 84°F/67°F/no precipitation for 1904, but is an unexplained aggregation and not a primary station log.
- **S2 LA84 catalogue notes, S6 Post-Dispatch scan and S7 LOC guide:** the catalogue/download and LOC guide were inaccessible or timed out in this audit. The Post-Dispatch page image was not independently inspected and cannot close the route, water or newspaper-evidence gaps.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory or missing-source claims

1. Sullivan’s deliberate dehydration intent is the most important unsupported causal claim. The evidence currently establishes restricted water and later interpretation, not motive.
2. “Only one water station” is over-specific. Lucas himself reports Carvajal using a six-mile water tower while also describing one fresh-water place at twelve miles; official versus incidental access is unresolved.
3. Ninety degrees is verified as Lucas’s observation, not as a reconciled meteorological fact. NWS archive limitations and the 84°F aggregation must remain visible.
4. The one-milligram injection account is not supported by the opened core source and conflicts with Lucas’s two oral one-sixtieth-grain accounts. “Rat poison” is explanatory shorthand, not the documented treatment wording.
5. Garcia’s haemorrhage is reported; dust erosion, a one-hour-to-death prognosis, surgery and a modern diagnosis are not independently established.
6. Carvajal’s peaches are in Lucas. Rotten apples and a nap are later traditions/secondary retellings and should not be treated as settled.
7. Dog interference is broadly supported, but the number of dogs, runner(s) affected and distance conflict within Lucas and across later summaries.
8. Barefoot running is not established; the opened Smithsonian account explicitly describes it as a fable and notes shoes in photographs.
9. Lorz’s conduct is well supported; his motive and sanction are not. “Joke,” “fraud,” “cheating,” one-year suspension, lifetime ban and commutation must not be collapsed into one unqualified statement.
10. Lucas says Hicks was caught after breaking the tape, not carried across it. The pack should not use the stronger carry image without a primary report or photograph that clearly establishes it.
11. Starter counts, route distance and exact mile/time markers remain inconsistent. Avoid “14 of 32,” “slowest ever,” exact pace, exact ride distance and precise plotted route until reconciled.
12. Historical names and classifications vary: Lucas uses forms such as Lentauw/Yamasani and colonial racial labels, while Olympedia uses Len Taunyane/Jan Mashiani and modernised spellings. Standardise names while preserving the original wording only as quoted source history.
