## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research pack for *Operation Sea-Spray: The Bacteria Cloud over San Francisco (1950)*. It is an evidence file, not a script, creative treatment, thumbnail brief, shot list, visual asset, edit plan or publication material.

## Central question

What did the U.S. military's 1950 San Francisco Bay vulnerability tests actually measure, what did officials believe about the organisms' safety, and can the later Stanford infections or Edward Nevin's death be causally linked to the release?

## Key decisions

- Use `operation-sea-spray-1950` as the episode slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat the event itself as documented by the 1951 Camp Detrick report record and the 1977 Senate record. Keep Army sponsorship, Navy delivery, and interservice participation distinct until the original operation file is reviewed.
- Use “biological simulant” for the organisms in the test. Do not collapse a simulated vulnerability test into a claim that the military released an operational weapon.
- Treat the Stanford cluster as an association in time and place, not proof of exposure or causation. The original medical report identified urinary-tract instrumentation as a likely route and did not identify the source.
- Do not state that Sea-Spray killed Edward Nevin. The court record confirms the family alleged that causal link; the Ninth Circuit record and later strain analysis do not establish a match.
- Treat “Operation Sea-Spray” as the established later label for this episode, while noting that the located 1951 primary report is titled *Biological Warfare Trials at San Francisco, California, 20–27 September 1950*.

## Constraints

- Research accessed 2026-09-16; source locators and caveats are recorded in `Research/01 Source Index.md`.
- This subject involves biological testing on an unwitting civilian population, illness, death and government secrecy. Human legal, medical and editorial review is required before any later narration.
- Do not reproduce procedural details that would enable biological dispersal. Archive the evidence at a high level only.
- Preserve disagreements over dates, agency responsibility, number of releases, coverage, and whether the Army or Navy should receive the operational label.
