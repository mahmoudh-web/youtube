## Allegations and interpretations that must remain labelled

- **“Greece invaded/aggressed”:** use as a source-attributed description; the Council’s adopted wording specifically concerns occupation of Bulgarian territory and Covenant violation.
- **“Bulgaria provoked the crisis” or “Greece used the incident as a pretext”:** these are competing interpretations and appear in partisan accounts; they are not cleared causal facts.
- **“The war was over a dog”:** a memorable explanatory label with uncertain provenance; the record supports a border crisis, not that single-cause explanation.
- **“The League punished Greece”:** avoid unless describing the adopted finding and compensation terms precisely; the League also managed withdrawal and enquiry.
- **“Bulgaria won”:** a political reading of the settlement, not a neutral description of every issue or later bilateral relationship.

## Not yet claimable

- That a Greek soldier definitely crossed while chasing a dog.
- That the initial encounter involved only one soldier and one dog rather than a wider exchange at frontier posts.
- That Petrich town was captured, besieged or spared at a specific minute.
- That the crisis lasted exactly ten days or was the “shortest war.”
- That 30 million leva and £45,000 are interchangeable without a period exchange-rate/source check.
- A final Greek, Bulgarian or civilian casualty total.
- That Pangalos deliberately manufactured the incident or that Bulgarian forces deliberately staged it.
