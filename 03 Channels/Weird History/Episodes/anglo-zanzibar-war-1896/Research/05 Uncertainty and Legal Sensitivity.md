## Legal, ethical and editorial flags

### Historical legal-status risk

The located treaty material supports a British protectorate context, but it does not yet establish the precise local succession authority exercised in August 1896. “Treaty enforcement,” “imperial intervention,” “protectorate,” “annexation” and “colonial violence” are not interchangeable descriptions. The 14 June 1890 agreement, its English/Arabic wording and the relevant 1896 official notice require human inspection before the pack makes a legal-status claim.

### Defamation and allegation risk

Hamad’s suspected poisoning and any claim that Khalid was responsible are unresolved historical allegations. They should not be presented as fact, motive or guilt. If the allegation is ultimately discussed, preserve the source, date, attribution and uncertainty in the claim ledger, and have a human editor review whether the wording is fair and necessary.

### Colonial violence and casualty risk

The short duration does not make the bombardment harmless. Available sources disagree over whether the commonly repeated figure means approximately 500 killed, or approximately 500 killed and wounded. Do not use “bloodless,” a settled death toll, or a triumphalist British-only casualty frame. The final wording must retain attribution and distinguish killed, wounded, missing and unknown.

### Local agency and source-balance risk

British dispatches, Admiralty material and internationally circulated newspapers dominate the located record. The *Zanzibar Gazette*, local administrative records and German material may change the balance of the account, but they have not yet been fully retrieved. Do not infer Zanzibari consent from European diplomatic instruments or treat Zanzibar as a passive setting.

### Imagery and rights risk

The British Library catalogue is a lead to photographs, not a reuse licence or proof that a particular image depicts the claimed moment. Rights, provenance, captions, cultural sensitivity and whether the image shows damage, aftermath or a later reconstruction must be checked separately. No imagery is approved by this note.

## Mandatory human-verification gate

Before any material claim advances, a human must:

1. Read FO 107/54 and FO 107/57, including Cave’s 25–28 August telegrams.
2. Inspect the 14 June 1890 Great Britain–Zanzibar agreement and the exact succession clause.
3. Verify the Rawson telegram against the original Admiralty/Foreign Office record and casualty return.
4. Compare the *St George*, *Thrush*, *Racoon/Raccoon*, *Sparrow* and *Philomel* reports or logs.
5. Retrieve the 2 September 1896 *Zanzibar Gazette* supplement and search local casualty, hospital, burial and damage records.
6. Locate the source behind 43 minutes and resolve whether any 45-minute rounding is independently sourced.
7. Check the German Consul’s account and Khalid’s 2 October transfer.
8. Obtain specialist Zanzibar/East Africa review of legal terms, political labels, casualty wording and colonial context.

Until these checks are complete, the pack remains in **Research** and must not advance to scriptwriting, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.
