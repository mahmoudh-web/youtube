## Fact / attribution / interpretation / uncertainty controls

- **Fact:** The 1958 directive named sparrows and called for nationwide mobilisation; People’s Daily reported mass campaigns.
- **Attribution:** The official reports claimed particular kill totals; the CAS institutional account says the 1959 report argued for seasonal/regional distinctions; Frank et al. estimate a contribution under a model.
- **Interpretation:** The campaign is best understood as a state-led ecological intervention whose measurable effects may have interacted with a fragile political food system.
- **Uncertainty:** The evidence does not establish a precise national sparrow population loss, a single nationwide pest outbreak, or a settled share of famine deaths attributable to sparrow killing.

Do not change “the paper estimates” into “the policy caused.” Do not change “official statistics reported” into “the country killed.” Do not change “one contributor” into “the cause.”

## Ethical and editorial sensitivity

### Human suffering and political sensitivity

The famine involved mass hunger, illness, death and family trauma. Avoid comic framing that makes famine victims the punchline of an animal story. The episode may discuss responsibility and state policy, but should distinguish historical evidence from moral verdicts and preserve uncertainty where the record is incomplete.

### Scientific integrity

Do not use the story as a morality tale in which “science” was uniformly correct and politics uniformly irrational. Zheng’s published position changed; scientists disagreed; the 1959–60 record shows policy adjustment rather than a single cinematic moment of enlightenment.

### Graphic material

Archival images may show dead birds, mass collection, weapons, exhausted participants or famine conditions. A human editor must approve whether any image is necessary, confirm rights, avoid gratuitous repetition and provide context. Do not use graphic suffering as a thumbnail hook.

### Copyright and translation

People’s Daily pages, archival documents, photographs, posters and academic PDFs may have different rights. Public access is not a blanket reuse licence. Record permissions and use short translated excerpts only after checking the original text and rights terms.

## Human-verification stop gate

Research stops here. Before narration, title/thumbnail packaging, visual generation, editing or publication, a human must:

1. Check the original 1956–59 newspaper scans, including the Chinese wording and page metadata.
2. Retrieve and read the original CAS report, attached scientific materials and meeting minutes.
3. Verify the March/April 1960 policy documents and whether “bedbugs” was the exact formal replacement at each stage.
4. Review the Frank et al. working paper’s methods, robustness and limitations with a qualified historian/economist.
5. Assemble provincial agricultural, pest, weather, procurement and mortality records before approving any causal sentence or number.
6. Decide whether the episode promise is the narrower policy reversal story or a broader ecological/famine story; do not imply the latter is settled.
7. Approve the human-sensitive visual treatment and document rights for any newspaper scan, poster, photograph or archival footage.
