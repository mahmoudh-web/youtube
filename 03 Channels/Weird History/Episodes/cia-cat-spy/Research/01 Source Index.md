## Research question

What did the CIA actually test when it explored a cat as a mobile listening device, what was approved or abandoned, and how did a documented research effort become the public story of a cat killed by a taxi?

## Conflict and episode promise

The popular version is a complete spy mission: the CIA surgically wired a cat, released it near Soviet targets, and watched it die under a taxi after spending an extraordinary sum. The strongest located record is narrower. CIA says it explored the idea beginning in 1964, implanted a microphone/transmitter/antenna system, ran limited field tests, found the cat uncontrollable and ended the program in 1967 without operational use. A surviving 1967 memo says cats could be trained to move short distances but the technique was not practical for specialized intelligence needs. The taxi death, exact cost and exact mission details come mainly from later testimony and press repetition and remain contested.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or official:** CIA public account, declassified CIA memo/FOIA record, official FOIA log or correspondence.
- **B — contemporary reporting:** reporting close to the 2001 declassification/public disclosure, useful for reception and quoted testimony but not a substitute for the redacted record.
- **C — participant interview / scholarly synthesis:** later interview or institutional analysis that can preserve testimony and context.
- **D — archive lead / secondary retelling:** useful for locating documents or mapping the legend, not sufficient alone for a material claim.

## Key entities and voices

- **CIA Directorate of Science and Technology:** institutional home described by CIA and archival summaries; specific office names are not fully visible in the redacted memo.
- **Victor Marchetti:** former CIA official whose later account supplied the taxi-death story; the account is quoted by the National Security Archive and 2001 press, but is contradicted by Robert Wallace and the CIA’s current account.
- **Robert Wallace:** former head of the CIA Office of Technical Services, quoted by Smithsonian as disputing the taxi death.
- **Bob Bailey:** animal trainer interviewed by Smithsonian; describes conditioning work and a cat-transmitter system but says relevant private records were destroyed in a 1989 fire.
- **Robin Michelson:** California otolaryngologist identified in the Smithsonian account as involved in the transmitter work; verify the role and spelling before foregrounding.
- **Jeffrey Richelson / National Security Archive:** researcher associated with the 2001 FOIA/declassification work and later archival commentary.
