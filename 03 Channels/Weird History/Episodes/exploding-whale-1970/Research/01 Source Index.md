## Research question

Who actually authorized the decision to use dynamite on the beached whale near Florence in November 1970, what alternatives were considered, and how did a local maintenance decision become a durable visual legend?

## Working answer and scope

The located record supports a narrow answer: the Oregon State Highway Division had responsibility for the beach problem, Assistant District Highway Engineer George Thornton was the named operational decision-maker, and the decision followed a short search for workable disposal options. Contemporary reporting documents burial and rendering as rejected or unavailable; the later KATU transcript and agency retellings add cutting/burying and burning as impractical. The pack does **not** yet establish a formal approval memo, a governor-level decision, or the exact content of any Navy recommendation.

The event is documented through contemporary reporting and surviving KATU film. This pack separates fact, allegation/attribution, interpretation and uncertainty. It does not draft narration or approve footage reuse.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary/contemporary or custodial record:** 1970 newspaper reporting, original broadcast/film, archival collection record, or agency publication.
- **B — institutional historical synthesis:** Oregon Encyclopedia or Oregon Historical Society interpretation that identifies the underlying record.
- **C — reputable later reporting:** useful for recollection and reception history; not sufficient alone for a disputed material claim.
- **D — access/transcription mirror:** useful for locating scans and readable text, but the underlying scan or custodial record must be checked before quotation.

## Decision-chain summary

1. A carcass washed up south of the Siuslaw River; the 11 November report says Port of Siuslaw manager Bill O’Conner called the Eugene district maintenance office.
2. The State Highway Division was the agency described as responsible for ocean beaches and cleanup.
3. Assistant District Highway Engineer George Thornton became the named field decision-maker. The 1994 ODOT employee account says the task fell to him after district engineer Dale Allen and others went hunting; this is retrospective recollection, not a 1970 order.
4. Sources report consultation with the U.S. Navy and/or munitions experts, but no written Navy advice or formal approval memo has been located in this pack.
5. Thornton gave the signal; Highway Division workers placed and detonated the charges. The contemporary report records a plan to direct the blast toward the ocean and later bury the larger remnants.

## Source-handling rules

1. Say “the Highway Division” or “Thornton, the assistant district highway engineer,” not “the Oregon government,” unless the sentence is explicitly about the agency.
2. Treat the Navy consultation as attributed institutional history until a contemporaneous or agency record identifies who was consulted and what was advised.
3. Treat the alternatives as a documented short list, not proof that every possible method was considered. Towing/haul-out is later-reported and remains open.
4. Use “sperm whale” for the final identification only with a note that the first newspaper and broadcast called it gray; do not silently erase the correction history.
5. Distinguish no reported human injury from the car damage, near-miss debris and contamination of spectators.
6. Treat KATU/OHS footage as rights-controlled archival material. The pack records provenance and possible visual opportunities, not permission to publish.
