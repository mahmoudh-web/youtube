## Historical uncertainty

- **Responsibility:** Documented actors are Luna Park officials, the local Edison Electric Illuminating Company of Brooklyn and Edison Manufacturing Company. Thomas Edison’s personal attendance, instruction or motive is not documented in the located sources. This is a bounded negative finding, not proof of a private conversation.
- **ASPCA/SPCA identity and decision:** Sources alternate between “SPCA” and “ASPCA.” The period organisation’s exact name, officer, intervention, attendance and written position must be checked in the 1903 report and correspondence.
- **Animal biography:** Birth date, origin, ownership, handlers and number of prior deaths vary across period press and later retellings. Do not use a precise biography unless independently triangulated.
- **Method and cause:** Newspapers report poison, rope and electricity, but do not establish which method caused death or whether the sequence was simultaneous. “Chiefly by electrocution” follows the Edison Papers’ summary, but final physiological cause remains a specialist/primary-record question.
- **Numbers:** 6,000 and 6,600 volts both appear; poison is reported as 460 grams in one reproduction and 460 grains in modern retellings; crowd estimates range from 1,500 to several thousand. Use only attributed figures.
- **Film timebase:** The inspected Internet Archive MP4 is 95.26 seconds and includes modern title/end cards. Period metadata lists 70 feet, often timed around 74 seconds. All timestamps in [[01 Source Index]] and [[03 Claim Ledger]] are transfer-specific and approximate until frame-audited.
- **Motive:** Luna Park publicity is supported by pre-event newspaper coverage and the film’s commercial catalogue language. An anti-AC purpose is a later interpretation; no located 1903 Edison instruction establishes it.

## Animal-cruelty and exploitative-imagery risk

The source footage depicts a real elephant being restrained, shocked, smoking at the feet and collapsing, followed by a prolonged view of the body. The subject is not a simulated death. Repeated viewing, slow motion, cropped thumbnails or sensational narration could turn historical documentation into exploitative spectacle.

Minimum editorial safeguards for human review:

1. Decide whether the footage is necessary to explain the episode; a catalogue still, newspaper scan or diagram may provide sufficient evidence.
2. If used, place a clear content warning before the excerpt and explain in the audio/visual itself why it is being shown.
3. Use the shortest excerpt that establishes the point; do not loop, zoom, colourise for shock or use the collapse/body shot as a thumbnail.
4. Avoid celebratory language such as “fries,” “snuff film,” “humane,” or “revenge.” Attribute period language and distinguish it from the narrator’s voice.
5. Consider blurring/obscuring the collapse or body if a shorter non-graphic segment can establish the film’s existence and construction.
6. Obtain a human sign-off from editorial, rights/QC and platform-policy reviewers before upload.

## Platform and rights flags

- YouTube’s current violent/graphic-content policy says graphic animal abuse intended to shock or disgust is prohibited, while documentary/educational context may be considered. It also says context must appear in the video itself, not only in the title or description. This footage is high-risk because it is real, central to the video and depicts animal suffering/death.
- A 1903 copyright registration and Library of Congress paper-print history do not automatically clear every modern scan, restoration, soundtrack, title card or third-party upload. Confirm the exact asset’s rights, credit and restrictions with the holding institution and source host.
- Avoid linking viewers to an uncontextualised copy of the execution as a “watch the shocking video” call-to-action; YouTube’s policy applies to external links and metadata as well.
- This note is a platform/compliance risk assessment, not legal advice. Jurisdiction, fair-use/public-domain analysis, privacy/publicity issues for identifiable descendants or performers, and monetisation treatment require human/legal review.
