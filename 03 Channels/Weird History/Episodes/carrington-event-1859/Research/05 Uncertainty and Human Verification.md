## Research stop condition

This is an evidence pack, not a script or production brief. No narration, title, thumbnail, visual treatment, simulation, archival-image treatment or publication work should begin until a human editor verifies the original pages, source chain, time conventions, observed/inferred boundary and modern-risk wording below.

## Observed versus inferred boundary

| Layer | Safe provisional content | Must not be silently added |
|---|---|---|
| Direct solar observation | Carrington and Hodgson saw a short-lived brilliant solar outburst on 1 September 1859. | A measured CME, exact plasma mass, speed or flare class. |
| Observatory record | Kew photographic magnetograms show a short disturbance near the solar observation and a larger disturbance beginning on 2 September. | A modern Dst value or a claim that the 1859 trace has one uncontested conversion. |
| Aurora reports | Contemporary reports place unusually bright aurorae at low latitudes and describe their appearance. | A precise global boundary, uniform brightness or controlled photometric measurement. |
| Telegraph reports | Selected lines and offices reported induced currents, interruptions, sparks, heating, shocks, fires and battery-free operation. | A total network failure, every-office fire or exact percentage of lines lost. |
| Modern physics | Later work interprets the sequence as a solar-terrestrial storm, commonly involving a CME/ICME. | Period-authentic CME language or a certainty that exceeds the named reconstruction. |
| Modern risk models | NOAA/NWS and Lloyd’s/AER describe possible impacts to grids, radio, GPS/GNSS, satellites and aviation; Lloyd’s/AER models conditional North American scenarios. | A guaranteed global blackout, internet collapse, fixed repair duration or universal economic loss. |

## Open uncertainty register

| ID | Uncertainty | Why it matters | Current handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| U01 | How should Carrington’s and Hodgson’s observation times be transcribed from the scan? | OCR errors can turn a five-minute observation into a false precision. | Inspect the page image and diagram; quote the printed wording only after checking. |
| U02 | Which historical time standard is used by Kew, Greenwich, telegraph offices and newspapers? | Mixing local, mean, astronomical and Greenwich time can create false causal timing. | Preserve each source’s convention; have a historian or space-weather specialist check conversions. |
| U03 | How should the 28 August precursor, 1 September solar flash/crochet and 2 September storm be narrated? | Collapsing them into one instant hides the documented sequence. | Use separate timeline entries and define “Carrington Event” as a later umbrella label. |
| U04 | Which telegraph incidents are direct operator reports, superintendent summaries, newspapers or Loomis’s later compilation? | Dramatic stories become overconfident when the copy chain disappears. | Trace the Boston, Pittsburgh, Washington, Springfield, Norway and European references back to originals where possible. |
| U05 | Did the Boston–Portland battery-free exchange last two hours, and what exactly was transmitted? | The episode’s signature anecdote is widely paraphrased. | Inspect the New York Times, Boston Traveler and any reprints; preserve “reported” until reconciled. |
| U06 | What did “fire” mean in each report? | Paper fire, sparks, scorched wood, hot coils and an office fire are not interchangeable. | Retain the exact object/location and avoid “telegraph offices burned” as a collective claim. |
| U07 | What modern physical quantity is being reconstructed? | Flare class, CME speed, Dst, magnetic-field change and induced electric field are different quantities. | Attribute each number to its study and record baseline, time standard and method. |
| U08 | How much of modern infrastructure risk is grid-specific versus satellite, radio, GPS or internet-specific? | A storm can affect separate pathways differently. | Use NOAA/NWS mechanism categories and Lloyd’s/AER only for the systems/model it actually covers. |
| U09 | How should outage duration, population and cost estimates be framed? | Scenario outputs can be mistaken for forecasts or global totals. | Label the 20–40 million, 16 days–1–2 years and $0.6–2.6T values as a 2013 North American model scenario. |
| U10 | How does the 1989 Quebec event compare? | It is a real precedent but not a duplicate of 1859. | Use it as an observed modern comparison with different storm/infrastructure conditions. |
| U11 | Which archival scans are public-domain, licensed or restricted for publication? | Research access does not equal reuse permission. | Do not create or package visuals in this task; production must perform a separate rights check. |

## Scientific and editorial sensitivity

- **Terminology:** “solar flare,” “CME,” “geomagnetic storm,” “aurora,” “induced current,” “radio blackout,” “grid collapse” and “internet outage” refer to different observations or mechanisms. Keep them distinct.
- **Historical interpretation:** Carrington recorded a coincidence and Stewart later developed the connection. Do not retroactively give the 1859 observers modern vocabulary or certainty.
- **Telegraph anecdotes:** shocks and fires are human-impact evidence, but local and source-specific. They should not be inflated into a universal network failure.
- **Modern counterfactual:** the safe form is “a comparable storm could disrupt [named systems], and models estimate [named scenario].” Do not say “would collapse the internet” without a source that explicitly models that outcome.
- **Infrastructure equity and public safety:** if modern impacts are discussed, distinguish grid customers, aviation, satellite operators, navigation-dependent industries and communications users rather than implying one uniform experience.
- **Archival presentation:** no faux 1859 footage, unlabeled simulation or unlicensed newspaper scan is approved by this pack.

1. [ ] Carrington and Hodgson page images checked against the OCR and diagram.
2. [ ] Kew/Stewart pages and plates checked, including historical time conventions.
3. [ ] 28 August, 1 September and 2 September sequence retained as separate events.
4. [ ] Original telegraph sources behind Loomis’s dramatic examples identified and classified.
5. [ ] Boston–Portland battery-free exchange checked against the newspaper scan and reprint chain.
6. [ ] “Fire,” “spark,” “hot coil,” “shock” and “communication failure” kept as distinct reported effects.
7. [ ] Any CME, flare-class, Dst, energy or arrival-time number attributed to a named modern reconstruction.
8. [ ] 1989 Quebec comparison labeled as a separate observed modern event.
9. [ ] Lloyd’s/AER population, duration and cost figures labeled as a North American model scenario, not a historical result or global forecast.
10. [ ] “Internet collapse” removed or explicitly marked as an unsupported analogy; modern wording names the systems and source.
11. [ ] Archival image provenance and publication rights checked by the production owner before any visual use.
12. [ ] Human editor approves final claim wording before scriptwriting.

## Research stop

Stop here pending explicit human research verification. The pack is sufficient for fact-checking and editorial decisions, but it is not approval to write narration, produce creative assets, generate visuals, edit or publish.
