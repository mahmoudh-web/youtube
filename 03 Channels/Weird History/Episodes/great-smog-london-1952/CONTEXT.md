## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Great Smog of London: Five Days When the City Disappeared,” about the severe London air-pollution episode of 5–9 December 1952. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, dated timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty and human-verification note, and provisional conclusion.
- Reconstruct the episode from meteorological observations, contemporaneous public-health records, Ministry of Health material, parliamentary records, the Beaver Committee report, and the Clean Air Act.
- Distinguish event-window deaths, registered excess deaths in different geographies, and later retrospective estimates that extend into 1953.
- Separate established fact, official estimate, later epidemiological estimate, interpretation, allegation and uncertainty.
- Stop for human source verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `great-smog-london-1952` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “5–9 December 1952” as the event window because it is supported by the Met Office, National Archives transcript and parliamentary records. Note that some contemporary medical analyses treat the dense fog as 5–8 December and track consequences afterward.
- Use “about 4,000 excess deaths in the immediate episode and following weeks” only with an explicit source and scope. Keep the 4,703 Greater London registrations for the week ending 13 December and the 2,484 administrative-county registrations in Scott’s table as different datasets, not competing interchangeable totals.
- Treat Bell and Davis’s approximately 12,000 excess deaths from December 1952 through February 1953 as a later model-based estimate, not a death-certificate count and not the acute five-day toll.
- Explain the mechanism as coal smoke and other emissions accumulating under cold, calm, inversion conditions. Do not describe the weather, coal burning, one pollutant, one industry or ordinary households as a sole cause.
- Describe the 1953 Beaver Committee, 1954 report, 1954 City of London legislation and 1956 Clean Air Act as a documented sequence of inquiry and policy response. Do not claim that the smog alone created all UK clean-air law.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this initial pack: 2026-09-16.
- The Ministry of Health report is catalogued by Wellcome and Google Books, but the pack does not rely on uninspected page quotations from the full 60-page report.
- Historical mortality numbers depend on geography, registration window, baseline and attribution method. Every number must retain its source and scope.
- The National Archives transcript records 4,703 deaths registered in Greater London for the week ended 13 December; Scott’s Public Health Reports article records 2,484 in the administrative county of London. These cannot be combined without resolving the boundary and dataset definitions.
- “Excess deaths,” “deaths registered,” “deaths attributed to the fog,” “respiratory illness,” “hospital admissions” and “long-term effects” are different categories.
- The pack contains no script, title/thumbnail package, visual-generation prompt, downloaded archival asset, rights clearance, edit, upload or publication recommendation.
