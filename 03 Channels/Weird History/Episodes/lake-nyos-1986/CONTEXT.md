## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Lake That Released a Deadly Invisible Cloud,” about the 21 August 1986 Lake Nyos disaster in north-west Cameroon. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification note and provisional conclusion.
- Separate the established gas, medical and geographic evidence from competing interpretations of the trigger.
- Keep the USGS/limnological interpretation and the Italian technical mission’s phreatic-explosion interpretation visible as distinct source positions.
- Record affected communities and the official Cameroon response without reducing the event to an exotic-science spectacle.
- Stop at research verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `lake-nyos-1986` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat “CO₂ release from gas-rich deep lake water” as the strongest current mechanism interpretation because chemical, isotopic, geological and medical evidence converge on it; do not present the trigger as solved.
- Treat the Italian technical mission’s phreatic-explosion explanation as a documented competing interpretation, not as misinformation or a settled historical error.
- Distinguish the source of the gas (magmatic CO₂ entering the lake system), the release mechanism (rapid exsolution and upward movement of gas-rich water) and the initiating trigger (unresolved; overturn is one proposed mechanism).
- Use attributed casualty counts: the USGS report says at least 1,700, the 2016 Cameroon ministry address gives 1,746, and the medical evaluation gives 1,700–2,000.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access dates in this pack are 2026-09-14.
- The event date is 21 August 1986. A few later web pages contain date errors; they are not used to override the USGS report or official Cameroon record.
- “Lake exploded” and “limnic eruption” are shorthand labels. The located evidence supports a sudden gas release and water surge, not a conventional magmatic eruption or ordinary flood as the cause of the deaths.
- “No wounds” or “no fire” must not be used as universal claims without the medical and field-source qualifier. Survivors had reported symptoms and some skin lesions whose interpretation changed across studies.
- The event caused mass death among people, livestock and wildlife. Avoid comic, sensational or graphic treatment and do not reproduce victim imagery as a research shortcut.
- No script, title/thumbnail package, visual brief, edit plan or production asset is authorized by this pack. Human verification remains the final gate.
