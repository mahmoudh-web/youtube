## Research question

Which Queensland and Commonwealth actors authorised or enabled the 1935 cane-toad release, what evidence and warnings were available at the time, and how did the policy continue after the first objections?

## Working answer and scope

The located record supports a narrower answer than “Queensland released toads with no evidence.” In 1932–35, Queensland sugar researchers had overseas reports and observations suggesting that *Bufo marinus* could reduce cane beetles. The 1935 Queensland journal published that case and also acknowledged a material local uncertainty: Queensland greyback beetles did not behave like the Puerto Rican beetles, so success depended on how long the beetles were accessible on the ground. The record located here does not show a completed Australian pre-release test of target-beetle control or a systematic environmental-risk assessment before the first field releases.

Operationally, the Bureau of Sugar Experiment Stations (BSES) imported a colony from Hawaii under assistant entomologist Reginald William Mungomery, bred it at Meringa near Gordonvale and released young toads around Gordonvale in August 1935. A Commonwealth Health Department ban followed later in 1935 after Walter Froggatt’s objections. Queensland Agriculture Minister Frank Bulcock, Premier William Forgan Smith, BSES and cane-grower interests then pressed for reversal; the ban was lifted in September 1936 after information was supplied to the Health Department. The exact original approval chain and administrative records remain open.

This pack does not draft narration, titles, thumbnails, visual briefs, edits or publication materials. Period sources and later science are separated throughout.

## Source-quality key

- **A — period primary/official:** government or departmental publication, contemporary newspaper, or period scientific article.
- **B — official/institutional synthesis:** government, museum, parliamentary or archival summary that identifies or interprets underlying records.
- **C — peer-reviewed later research:** useful for ecological consequences and methodological qualification, not proof of what officials knew in 1935.
- **D — secondary synthesis or access aid:** useful for triangulation and leads; not sufficient alone for a disputed material claim.

## Decision-chain summary

1. Queensland’s BSES had a long-running cane-beetle problem and had access to overseas claims about *Bufo marinus*.
2. Arthur Bell encountered the Puerto Rico evidence in 1932 and reported favourably; Reginald Mungomery studied the toad in Hawaii and brought a colony to Queensland in June 1935.
3. BSES prepared a quarantine breeding enclosure at Meringa near Gordonvale; thousands of young toads were released around Gordonvale in August 1935.
4. The period record already contained an applicability warning: Queensland greyback beetles did not regularly return to the ground in the same way as Puerto Rican beetles, and success required further observation.
5. Walter Froggatt objected; the Commonwealth Health Department prohibited further distribution late in 1935.
6. Queensland Agriculture Minister Frank Bulcock, Premier William Forgan Smith, BSES and cane-grower interests pressed the Commonwealth; the ban was lifted in September 1936 after further information was supplied. The exact documents and approvals remain open.
