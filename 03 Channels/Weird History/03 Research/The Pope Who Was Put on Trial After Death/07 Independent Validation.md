## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS.** The pack’s narrow core is supported: Formosus died in Rome in 896; a proceeding under Stephen VI removed his body from its tomb and treated it as deposed; near-contemporary and later sources preserve additional ritual details; and the controversy was rapidly contested and revisited. The pack is unusually careful about partisan sources and manuscript transmission.

It is not yet safe for script or production use. Correct the citation error and downgrade the wording of the Theodore/reburial, Tiber-agency, exact-date, and finger-count claims. ## Timeline, conclusion, and counterevidence checks

- The Bulgaria mission window is supported in Monroe, pp. 40–57 / online lines 3139–43 and the older [Catholic Encyclopedia Formosus entry](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06139b.htm), lines 10–12. The exact year is not a settled single date; the pack’s “c. 866–867” and medium confidence are appropriate.
- The 876–878 conflict and the Troyes date are supported by MGH’s contents, [*Concilia* 5](https://www.dmgh.de/mgh_conc_5/) lines 50–60, and Monroe, pp. 92–94 / lines 7175–87. The pack correctly avoids compressing this into permanent condemnation; the [Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06139b.htm), lines 19–23, also records later release/restoration.
- The 882–884 restoration to Porto is supported by Monroe, pp. 5–6 / online lines 581–92 and the Catholic Encyclopedia, line 23, but the underlying evidence is mediated and partly polemical. “Marinus later absolved/restored him” should remain medium confidence, not a directly documented papal act unless the cited primary text is checked.
- Formosus’s 6 October 891 accession and 4 April 896 end date are supported by the Holy See page. The pack appropriately treats the official Vatican page as a date source, not as evidence for the Cadaver Synod.
- The Arnulf sequence is strongly supported by Regino’s 896 entry and the Fulda continuation. The secret-invitation story is attested in the Fulda tradition as reported by Monroe, but Regino’s omission is not a refutation; the pack’s counterevidence wording is correct.
- The trial window is not completely settled. MGH’s contents place the relevant Roman material at “end 896/beginning 897,” Treccani gives January 897, and Monroe discusses alternative dating. Keep the pack’s broad window and do not promote January to certainty.
- The Stephen/Boniface succession and the sparse Fulda version are secure enough for the core narrative. The throne, deacon, mutilation, blood, and Tiber details remain source-layered and should carry attribution every time they appear.
- Stephen’s imprisonment and death are supported, but the pack correctly treats popular outrage as causal inference. Theodore’s intervention remains indirect and should be downgraded as above.
- The Ravenna rehabilitation is well supported as an 898 event, but the exact month and division of Roman/Ravenna material remain disputed. The pack’s warning against treating the dossier as a verbatim 897 transcript is correct.
- The later Sergius III revival is supported by Monroe, pp. 230–231 / online lines 16608–41 and 14171–76: in 904 he renewed the ordination dispute, but the source does not support merging that later controversy with the 897 corpse trial.
- The conclusion is substantively sound after the corrections above. Its phrase “rapid reversal” should remain a process description with Theodore’s role qualified, while John IX’s Ravenna rehabilitation can remain the better-attested later endpoint.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strengths

- The pack uses a useful layered-source model: a sparse near-contemporary annal, independent Regino context, annalistic expansions, pro-Formosan polemic, manuscript/catalogue evidence, and modern source criticism.
- The Holy See page is appropriate for current papal dates but is correctly not used as evidence for the trial.
- The Fulda text is a primary medieval annalistic witness in a modern critical edition scan, although the Internet Archive OCR is not a substitute for checking the printed page or manuscript tradition.
- Regino is an independent medieval chronicle for the capture/coronation context, but the Wikisource transcription is not itself a critical edition; the Latin should be checked against a scholarly edition before quoting.
- The Geschichtsquellen and Capitularia pages are strong scholarly cataloguing/manuscript resources. They support provenance and transmission claims, not every narrative detail.
- Monroe’s dissertation is a substantial scholarly secondary source and the strongest source-critical control in the pack. It remains an interpretation, not a primary witness; the ResearchGate copy is a repository mirror rather than an official Columbia-hosted edition.
- The Moore article is useful because it reproduces and compares medieval annal passages with page locators. It is still a modern secondary article and should not replace direct examination of the Latin.
- The Catholic Encyclopedia and Treccani entries are reception/synthesis sources. They are useful for comparison, but the former is old and church-oriented and the latter preserves some traditional chronology/motive claims that must be labelled as such.

### Access and coverage limits

- The dMGH [*Concilia* 5 page](https://www.dmgh.de/mgh_conc_5/) exposes the contents and dates but its BookReader requires JavaScript; I could not independently inspect the full pp. 416–445 critical text through that landing page. The validation therefore confirms the pack’s use of the MGH catalogue and corroborating scholarship, while leaving the requested Latin-edition check open.
- The dMGH [*Der Streit um Formosus* page](https://www.dmgh.de/mgh_qq_zur_geistesgesch_32/) and the direct ResearchGate page for [“A New Look at the Cadaver Synod”](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374697965_A_New_Look_at_the_Cadaver_Synod) were not readable in this access pass. Their relevant arguments are represented in the accessible Monroe dissertation and the pack’s other sources; do not treat the blocked pages as independently verified full texts.
- The pack does not yet provide a direct, page-level critical Latin check for every translation, especially *per advocatum suae responsionis*, the Ravenna capitula, and the precise Theodore/John IX sequence.
- The pack’s C14 locator is malformed and must be corrected before a human verifier can reproduce that check.

Overall coverage is strong for the core event and appropriately cautious for the dramatic details. It is incomplete for exact council chronology, agency, the Theodore episode, and the original Latin wording—exactly the areas the pack already flags for human verification, but which must remain blocked from downstream production.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory, or missing-source items

1. **Overstated:** C11 calls Theodore’s reversal/recovery “established,” but the cited dissertation calls the evidence “supposedly” and notes that the evidence for a Theodore synod is indirect.
2. **Overstated if narrated without attribution:** C06–C09. The throne, deacon, dragging, mutilation, blood, and river are reports from different annals/polemical traditions, not one surviving eyewitness transcript.
3. **Unresolved agency:** C08. The evidence does not establish whether Stephen ordered the Tiber disposal, whether it was a second removal, or whether later material separates tomb violators from the original synod.
4. **Unresolved number:** C07. Two and three fingers are both attested; no single number should be used without naming the source.
5. **Citation defect:** C14’s “pp. 2290–2292” is not a valid printed-page locator. Correct it before human sign-off.
6. **Date conflict:** C12/timeline. Scholarly catalogue and Monroe use May 898; Treccani gives July 898. The year and Ravenna location are secure enough; the month requires critical-edition confirmation.
7. **Missing direct source check:** the validation cannot yet certify the exact wording or manuscript placement of the MGH pp. 416–445 text. A Latin specialist must inspect it before any direct quotation or legal characterization.
8. **Causal caution:** the “popular uprising caused by the scandal” explanation for Stephen’s death is an inference, not a directly established causal fact.
