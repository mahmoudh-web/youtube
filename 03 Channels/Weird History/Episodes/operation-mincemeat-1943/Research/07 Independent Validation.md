## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — safe to send to human verification, not safe to advance to script, packaging or production.**

The pack is unusually disciplined about separating operational design, reproduced intelligence reports, later interpretation and unresolved identity evidence. Its central account is supported at the level of the accessible evidence: Montagu’s reproduced 31 March orders describe the body, identity, case, container and Huelva release; the reproduced *Seraph* report records the 30 April launch; and the reproduced German material records an assessment that the documents were “above suspicion” and pointed to eastern Mediterranean operations.

The principal correction is to remove or qualify the unsupported “Tom Martin” detail in C14. The cited HNN article does not contain that claim, while the Hansard debate documents John Melville as a publicly advanced alleged identity. The pack should also correct several source locators and keep the primary archive, Spanish records and German operational documents as explicit human-verification requirements.

This is an evidence audit, not approval. The pack remains a research stop and must not be treated as narration-ready.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong or useful sources

- Montagu’s 1953 account is a valuable participant source and reproduces the 31 March orders, the 30 April *Seraph* report and German intelligence material. It is retrospective, self-involving and edited, so reproduced records must remain distinguished from Montagu’s interpretation.
- Gottlieb and Lilly’s 2009 *Military Medicine* article is a peer-reviewed specialist source with useful forensic and Spanish-record discussion. It is explicitly interpretive, reports that the original autopsy no longer exists, and argues for the *Dasher* theory; it should not be treated as a neutral resolution.
- Smyth’s 2010 Oxford University Press monograph is substantial source-critical scholarship and provides precise archival references. The accessible copy is an unauthorised hosted reproduction, so the human verifier should consult the published book and cited files before quoting.
- The DHM page is an independent institutional synthesis that confirms the 10 July landing, 17 August completion and broad Sardinia/Greece deception context. It does not isolate Mincemeat’s effect.
- The UK Parliament answer is an official archival-route statement. It establishes record custody and references, not the contents of WO/ADM/CAB files.
- Hansard is primary evidence for what named parliamentarians and the minister said about HMS *Dasher*, John Melville and the government’s Michael identification. It is not forensic proof.
- The IWM guide and exhibition release are institutional summaries/object leads. The browser could not open the supplied PDFs/download, but indexed text confirms the broad plan and the Montagu/Cholmondeley attribution. They are not substitutes for collection records.

### Coverage gaps

The pack is well covered for the intended mechanism, the reproduced launch report, the reproduced German appreciation and the caution against claiming that Mincemeat alone won the Sicily campaign. It remains incomplete for:

- direct inspection of WO 106/5921, ADM 223/794 and the relevant CAB 154 files;
- the original German appreciation/report, translation provenance and intelligence chain;
- Spanish naval, judicial, autopsy, death-certificate, burial and briefcase-transfer records;
- exact Twenty Committee approval chronology and division of credit;
- independent German operational orders tying each defensive measure to Mincemeat rather than prior expectations or other deception;
- the released-record basis for Glyndwr Michael and the direct CWGC grave record;
- a balanced account of the *Dasher* theory, with John Melville retained as a public claim and unsupported candidate names removed;
- the exact origin and evidentiary status of the “Mincemeat swallowed whole” signal if it is used later;
- any numerical claim about lives saved, forces diverted or the operation’s share of the Sicily outcome.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory or missing-source items

1. **C14 / Tom Martin:** not supported by the cited HNN or Hansard pages. Remove the name or add a direct, reliable source and label it as a public theory.
2. **“The corpse won the war,” “saved 30,000 lives,” “saved thousands,” or any quantified effect:** unsupported by this pack. The NHHC summary stresses that Sicily was obvious and that rapid Allied preparation also mattered.
3. **Mincemeat alone moved German forces or caused the invasion to succeed:** overstated. Montagu’s chapter 13 is a participant interpretation, even when it cites contemporary documents. Keep “connected,” “influenced” or “contributed,” with wider deception context.
4. **Complete Spanish custody or official complicity:** not established from the mediated German report. Avoid “Spain handed the papers to the Nazis” unless specific actors and records support the wording.
5. **Definitive forensic identity:** unresolved at independent validation level. Gottlieb’s article argues for a *Dasher* body; Smyth argues for Michael; the original Spanish autopsy is reported destroyed. The best-supported identity may be stated only with its evidence basis and dispute.
6. **Exact post-mortem findings as settled fact:** the timeline’s 1–2 May dates are recoverable from Smyth, but the original Spanish report is not inspected and its reported loss must be explicit. Do not narrate medical details as definitive.
7. **The exact 4 February approval milestone, 19 April sailing record, release position and Spanish official names:** remain archive-verification items, not independently cleared facts.
8. **“Tripoli” for the Panzer move:** correct to **Tripolis**, the Greek town, to avoid conflation with Tripoli in Libya.
9. **Source locator mismatch for the 1–2 May row:** S12 and S13 do not establish the autopsy/burial dates. Cite Smyth’s pages only as secondary reporting, then require the underlying records.
10. **Catalogue and institutional summaries:** S1, S7, S8, S12, S14 and S15 are valuable retrieval/context sources but cannot establish the contents of uninspected archive files. The pack generally recognises this; downstream work must preserve the distinction.
