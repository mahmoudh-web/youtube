## Research question

Who was held at Castle Itter, who defended it on 4–5 May 1945, what can the surviving military and Austrian records establish about the battle, and what does the episode reveal—and not reveal—about the “clean Wehrmacht” myth?

## Conflict and episode promise

The documented event is a last-week-of-the-war defense of a Nazi detention site in Tyrol by a small, improvised force that included U.S. soldiers, Wehrmacht personnel linked to the Wörgl resistance, Austrian resistance members and some French detainees. The tension is not simply “Americans and good Germans versus the SS”: the Wehrmacht was an institution deeply implicated in Nazi crimes, the exact roles and motives of individual German soldiers vary, and the evidence is uneven between U.S. unit records, Austrian memory, French prisoner accounts and later narrative histories.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or near-primary:** contemporary unit report, wartime record, prisoner diary/memoir, archival object or contemporaneous photograph/newspaper.
- **B — institutional record or archival synthesis:** state archive, memorial, museum, official unit history or catalogue.
- **C — scholarly interpretation or researched secondary account:** useful for context and disagreement, not a substitute for the underlying record.
- **D — later popular synthesis:** use only to track reception or locate leads; not sufficient for a material claim alone.

No source in this pack establishes a complete, definitive roster of all defenders or a verified Waffen-SS order to execute the prisoners.

## Primary-source retrieval queue

1. Obtain the **23rd Tank Battalion After Action Report, 4–5 May 1945**, and the **Headquarters, 103d Infantry Division, G-2 Periodic Report, 6 May 1945**; compare their times, units, personnel and casualties with S3.
2. Retrieve the **142nd Infantry Regiment action report** and the 12th Armored Division unit diary/operations report for the relief column; resolve whether relief reached the castle at about 15:00 or 16:00.
3. Inspect Austrian local-resistance records and the Wörgl Stadtarchiv/Heimatmuseum material for Alois Mayr, Rupert Hagleitner, Hans Waltl, Gangl and the actual number of local fighters present at the castle.
4. Read the French prisoner diaries/memoirs identified in S16, especially Daladier, Reynaud, François-Poncet and Augusta Léon-Jouhaux, for their own account of the guards’ departure, arming and rescue.
5. Verify the prisoner roster and detention classification against Dachau/SS-Sonderkommando Schloss Itter records; do not call all detainees “POWs” without checking the record.
6. Verify the 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division attribution, the attacking force size, the claim that an SS sniper killed Gangl while protecting Reynaud, and the names/counts of German and Austrian casualties.

## Documentary visual-source opportunities (not a production plan)

- The S3 unit-report scan: a source image for the contemporaneous U.S. operational account, subject to legibility and rights review.
- The S17 newspaper object and IIIF manifest: release photograph/caption and immediate French reception, with provenance and reuse checked.
- DÖW’s Gangl memorial photograph and Austrian state-history PDF: local memory and official Austrian framing, not proof of every tactical detail.
- Wörgl memorial and city-chronicle records: evidence that local resistance and anti-fascist memory remain part of the story.
- Current castle photographs may establish terrain only; they cannot substitute for wartime damage, maps or after-action evidence.
