## Research question

What did the Commonwealth actually authorise in Western Australia in 1932, how did the operation perform, and how did a short pest-control deployment become the later “Emu War” story?

## Conflict and episode promise

The documented event is a military-assisted wildlife cull requested by Western Australian soldier settlers during drought and low wheat prices. The narrative tension is between that administrative record and the later “Australia went to war and lost to birds” legend. The notes preserve both the contemporary record and the later framing without treating the latter as a literal war report.

## Source-quality key

- **A — direct official/primary:** parliamentary record, archival record description, or contemporary newspaper report.
- **B — institutional/archival synthesis:** National Archives or Australian War Memorial explanation that identifies and contextualises records.
- **C — scholarly/curated secondary:** academic or specialist interpretation used for triangulation.
- **D — later reception:** later retrospective, folklore, or popular retelling; useful for legend history, not for proving 1932 facts.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use “military-assisted cull” or “operation” for the documented event. Use “Emu War” only as an attributed contemporary/later label.
2. Keep **Central Australia** in S2 separate from the Western Australian wheat-belt operation clarified in S3. The 8 Nov question is not by itself proof that the member had the location right.
3. Treat “fewer than 1,000” (S1), any newspaper interim figure, and any later exact total as separate claims with separate provenance.
4. The Hansard record supports machine guns, a deputation, shared costs and return of the guns; it does not support a formal declaration of war, human casualties, or a clean “victory/defeat” outcome.
5. Inspect the Trove page scans before using tactical anecdotes, ammunition counts, quoted jokes, or casualty/kill totals.
