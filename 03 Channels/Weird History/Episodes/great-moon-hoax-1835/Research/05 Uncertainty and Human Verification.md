## Legal, ethical and historical flags

### False attribution and living reputation

John Herschel and Richard Adams Locke are deceased, but the pack still must not state fabricated observations as Herschel’s work or reduce Locke’s motive to a proven fact. Keep “claimed,” “attributed,” “later said” and “documented” distinct.

### Historical language in the fabricated text

The serial’s descriptions of “bat-men” and other beings use nineteenth-century racial, gendered and anthropomorphic language. Quote only when necessary to establish the text, identify it as the newspaper’s fiction, and avoid reproducing dehumanising wording as the narrator’s voice.

### Reader-belief claims

Do not turn anecdotes into a population-level conclusion. Reprints, a Yale visit, a missionary story, sales claims, parody and later correspondence are different kinds of evidence. A human editor should build a small dated reception sample and label each item as belief, doubt, satire, commercial reuse or later recollection.

### Science and misinformation

The episode should not mock nineteenth-century readers for failing to know modern lunar science. Explain the real authority borrowed by the hoax—Herschel, Cape astronomy, technical detail and a foreign journal—while making clear that the report was fabricated.

### Image and rights risk

S3 is a Library of Congress object with a “no known restrictions” advisory; S14 has an archival catalogue record and a date range. These are source leads, not automatic licences for platform use. Check item-level rights, image provenance, attribution requirements and legibility before any future visual work.

## Mandatory human-verification gate

Before any material claim advances, a human must:

1. Read the original *Sun* issues for the 21 August teaser, 25–29 August and 31 August serial installments, and 16 September editorial; record stable issue/page/column locators.
2. Compare the original issues with S1’s 1859 reprint and mark editorial or typographic differences.
3. Verify the exact title and publication status of the *Edinburgh Journal of Science* and the invented “supplement.”
4. Retrieve Locke’s earliest available authorship/intention statement and distinguish his later account from contemporaneous attribution.
5. Check the Yale, Springfield missionary and circulation stories against contemporary records; remove any anecdote that remains uncorroborated.
6. Read the original Herschel correspondence and actual Cape-observation records; separate real astronomy from the hoax’s technical fiction.
7. Confirm which prints are contemporary, which are later reprints/translations and what each object can actually prove.
8. Obtain specialist review of the reception-history wording, the “first fake news” temptation and any quoted historical language.

Until these checks are complete, this pack remains in **Research** and must not advance to scriptwriting, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.
