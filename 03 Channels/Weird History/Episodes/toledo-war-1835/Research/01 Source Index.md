## Research question

How did an ambiguous Great Lakes boundary become an armed statehood crisis, and what can the records actually support about the “war,” the Wood–Stickney casualty, and Michigan’s supposed compensation in Upper Peninsula land?

## Conflict and episode promise

Ohio and Michigan Territory claimed overlapping jurisdiction over the strip containing Toledo. Rival surveys, competing statutes, arrests and militia movements made the dispute look like a war, but the located evidence describes a coercive boundary crisis rather than a conventional campaign. Congress resolved it through a conditional statehood/boundary settlement: Ohio retained the disputed strip, Michigan was admitted after assent, and the new boundaries included most of the present Upper Peninsula. The episode’s tension is the gap between the theatrical label and the legal record, with casualty and “trade” language kept qualified.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or near-primary:** statute, contemporary map, contemporary newspaper/document, affidavit or archival manuscript.
- **B — institutional record or archival synthesis:** government, library, historical society or museum source that reproduces or describes records.
- **C — scholarly or researched secondary account:** useful for interpretation and disagreement, not a substitute for the underlying record.
- **D — later popular synthesis:** lead-finding only; not sufficient for a material claim alone.
