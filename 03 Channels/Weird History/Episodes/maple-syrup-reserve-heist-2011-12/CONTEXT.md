## Purpose

This folder holds the source-led research pack for the Weird History episode concept **The Strategic Maple Syrup Reserve Heist**. The episode asks how 9,571 barrels were removed from a Quebec reserve warehouse over roughly a year and how the courts reconstructed responsibility and financial consequences.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve six linked Research notes: source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence, uncertainty/legal sensitivity, and provisional conclusion.
- Prefer Quebec court and Supreme Court records, Sûreté du Québec records, producer/Federation records, and Canadian reporting.
- Keep convictions and judgments separate from police allegations, charging language, media shorthand, and research inference.
- Keep the pack in Research until a human verifies French court records, source passages, translations, names, amounts and legal wording.

## Key Decisions

- Use the evidence-bounded slug `maple-syrup-reserve-heist-2011-12`; the event is also called the Great Canadian Maple Syrup Heist or *vol de sirop d’érable du siècle*.
- Treat the Supreme Court’s 2022 decision in *R. v. Vallières*, 2022 SCC 10, as the controlling appellate record for Vallières’s fine, not the earlier 2020 Quebec Court of Appeal amount.
- Use the court’s 9,571-barrel and 5,935,250-pound figures for the proven theft narrative; preserve other contemporary estimates as dated reporting or producer statements.
- Do not call every arrested or accused person a thief. Use “accused,” “suspect,” “charged,” “convicted,” “pleaded guilty,” or “found responsible” only when the cited record supports that exact status.

## Constraints and Invariants

- No narration, title, thumbnail, visual plan or publication decision is approved by this folder.
- Living people are named only where necessary and only with their procedural status attached.
- French judgments and French-language producer/police releases require human language and legal review before quotation or translation is treated as final.
- The Supreme Court case brief is an explanatory summary and not part of the Court’s reasons; cite the full reasons for legal conclusions.
- Police and Federation releases establish what authorities or the victim organization reported at the time; they do not, by themselves, prove guilt.
