## Purpose

This note keeps the strongest competing explanations visible. A hypothesis is not promoted because it is dramatic, official or sceptical; it must explain the specific radar, visual and operational evidence without silently filling documentary gaps.

## Hypothesis matrix

| Hypothesis | Evidence that supports it | Evidence or limits against it | Current handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: Temperature inversion/anomalous propagation produced many radar returns | The CAA report’s Table I tabulates July records and weather correlations; it reports firsthand analysis of 80 August targets and proposes secondary reflections from refracting areas. Samford’s officials presented inversion/reflected ground returns as probable. | The CAA’s formal motion-analysis conclusions focus on August 13–16, not the full July 26–27 sequence. The Blue Book case says Holcomb found only a slight inversion and did not think it explained the strong returns at that moment. | Strong explanation for a class of radar phenomena; not a complete adjudication of both July weekends or of visual lights. |
| H2: Ordinary traffic, ground returns, birds or radar artefacts were misread as objects | The case records a B-25 being vectored toward targets and reporting busy highways/intersections; Samford noted birds and reflected ground targets can give good returns; CAA report discusses known radar “ghosts,” “angels” and “pixies.” | Controllers distinguished known aircraft, checked equipment and reported some returns as unusually solid or erratic. Those statements may still be wrong, but they are evidence against a simple “nobody checked” story. | Test target-by-target. Do not dismiss trained operators wholesale or treat their confidence as proof of physical craft. |
| H3: The visual reports were independent misidentifications, lights or atmospheric effects while radar produced separate artefacts | The Air Force separated visual and radar explanations; CAA technical findings concern radar, while weather/optical effects could produce lights. Some pilots saw only lights, not structured craft. | Several reports were contemporaneously compared with radar positions, and the case includes different radar systems and professional observers. The correlation needs original time/position data before it can be accepted or rejected. | Plausible compound explanation; requires event-level reconstruction rather than a single cause. |
| H4: Secret Soviet or American aircraft/technology | Cold War air-defence concerns made this a live operational question; intelligence officials reviewed the possibility of a security threat. | CIA’s historical account says Air Force briefers rejected US/Soviet secret-weapon explanations for lack of evidence; the case has no recovered hardware, photographic proof or verified technical signature. | Unsupported by the current evidence. Retain only as historical context/an investigated possibility; do not present it as a candidate explanation with positive support. |
| H5: Extraterrestrial vehicles | The reports remained unidentified in the surviving case record, and some witnesses described rapid movement or lights that interceptors did not overtake. | “Unidentified” is an observational category. Missing interceptor reports, incomplete pilot interviews, no physical evidence and later institutional findings do not establish extraterrestrial origin. | Unsupported hypothesis. It must not appear as a conclusion, only as a clearly labelled claim the evidence does not establish. |

## Specific counterevidence and source limits

### The Air Force case file is not a complete dataset

Case 1661 says it could not collect all facts into one report. It says the official reports from two interceptor flights had not been received and that the commercial and CAA pilots reporting visual observations had not yet been contacted. That weakens both a confident debunking and a confident extraordinary conclusion.

### The two radar episodes are not interchangeable

The file’s own comparison says the 26–27 July returns were more haphazard than the early 20 July returns. A single narrative such as “the same objects returned exactly a week later” exceeds the record. Keep the nights, systems and witness groups separate.

### “Good, solid returns” do not prove solid airborne objects

Holcomb and the controllers used strong language about radar returns, but Samford’s press conference records that birds and reflected ground targets can produce good returns. Radar strength is evidence about the signal, not a direct measurement of mass, altitude or construction.

### The CAA report is strong but narrower than the legend

Report No. 180 is a primary technical report, not a generic “Air Force debunking.” It used interviews, official records and firsthand observations. Table I includes July Washington entries and weather correlations, while the direct motion-analysis/conclusion sections address Washington MEW observations beginning on 13 and 15 August 1952. A human editor must keep those layers separate before attributing the famous July incidents to it.

### Archival retrieval remains incomplete

NARA’s public guides identify the Project Blue Book case-file, Air Intelligence Report and motion-picture series, but the accessible catalogue did not expose item-level F-94 mission reports, complete pilot files or a synchronized original copy of Case 1661 during this revision. This does not establish that the records are lost; it establishes only that the pack has not verified them.

### Press-record and newspaper checks remain limited

NARA catalogue record 25738 confirms a 31 July Samford film record, but the full audiovisual content was not independently reviewed. The 29 July transcript remains a secondary transcription. The Case 1661 clipping index gives dated newspaper titles and pages, and secondary reproductions provide leads, but original 1952 newspaper pages were not independently checked. Therefore the pack can establish press coverage, not the exact public reaction or belief distribution.

### Visual/radar correlation must be rebuilt, not assumed

The first-weekend Air Force reports and the second-weekend case file record visual reports near radar activity. That does not establish that every light and every blip represented the same object. The required reconstruction is: witness, time, location/heading, radar system, target track, weather and whether an aircraft was asked to check.

### Press attention is not proof of public belief

Contemporary clipping references show that the story became national news. Headlines and later witness interviews prove circulation and memory, not the number of believers, the accuracy of every claim or the existence of a cover-up.

## Claims that should not pass review without new evidence

- “The objects hovered over the White House” as a universal description.
- “A jet chased an object at 7,000 mph.”
- “The radar proved the objects were physical craft.”
- “The temperature inversion explanation was disproved” or “proved” for the complete July case.
- “The Air Force knew they were extraterrestrial and hid it.”
- “Secret Soviet/American technology caused the sightings.”
- “All witnesses agreed on what they saw.”
