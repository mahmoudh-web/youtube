## Provisional answer to the research question

The source pass does not support the simple claim that Mexico and France fought over pastries. It supports a wider dispute in which French diplomats pressed indemnity claims, commercial access, exemption from forced loans and action against Mexican officials. Mexico disputed the government’s responsibility for losses arising from internal political disorder and resisted negotiating under naval pressure. The result was a blockade, the capture of San Juan de Ulúa, fighting at Veracruz and a March 1839 settlement.

The pastry story remains important, but mainly as a disputed grievance and public label. Mexican archival and later scholarly accounts associate the story with a French resident named Remontel in Tacubaya. Yet the account varies on whether the business was a fonda, restaurant or pastry shop; whether troops looted it, consumed goods without paying, or both; and whether the claim was 800, 30,000 or 60,000 pesos. The SRE history’s 800-peso note and the peer-reviewed discussion of press magnification make the familiar 60,000-peso version unsafe without the original claim file.

The 9 March 1839 convention is the clearest control against the pastry-only narrative. Its Article I settled French claims for pre-26-November losses at 600,000 pesos, paid in three drafts against Veracruz customs. The treaty does not say that the money was for cakes, does not name Remontel, and does not adjudicate each complaint. It also arranged arbitration for certain captured ships and later losses and granted interim most-favoured-nation privileges. This is a negotiated diplomatic settlement, not a judicial finding that every French claim was valid.

The working conclusion is therefore: a small or disputed Tacubaya grievance became an exceptionally durable Mexican shorthand for a serious French coercive intervention, but the documented conflict was built from accumulated claims, commercial and legal disputes, sovereignty concerns, naval pressure and the weakness of the post-independence Mexican state. The causal balance remains open until the ultimatum, French correspondence, original claim file and earliest press evidence are inspected.
