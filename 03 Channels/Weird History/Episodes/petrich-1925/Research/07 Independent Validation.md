## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — safe to send to human verification, not safe for script or production.**

The pack is unusually careful about disputed causation, casualty totals, the status of Petrich town and the dog anecdote. Its central sequence is supported by independent League summaries and a near-contemporary League Secretariat survey. However, the pack still has material corrections to make before a human can treat it as a clean evidence ledger:

1. The Commission is independently reported to have found that it was impossible to determine which soldier fired the first shot. “First shot unresolved pending the report” should be replaced with this more precise result, while the primary report remains required for quotation.
2. The chronology should distinguish Bulgaria’s 22 October appeal/telegram from its receipt by the League early on 23 October, and should say that orders were reported on 28 October while evacuation was completed on 29 October.
3. A League Secretariat survey reports £45,000 in reparations, payable within two months and later duly paid; the 2025 Bulgarian Telegraph Agency reproduction reports 30 million leva. These are source-specific currency presentations, not interchangeable figures until the original decision and payment records are checked.
4. S10’s indexed JSTOR URL is wrong: `https://www.jstor.org/stable/985596` resolves to “The Conditions of Occurrence of Phantom Limbs,” not James Barros’s article. The correct stable identifier for the 1964 article is `https://www.jstor.org/stable/985913`.
5. S3 and S4 are League Information Section summaries whose own front matter says they are not official League documents. They are valuable institutional secondary sources, but should not be labelled as primary records. S7 is a 2025 institutional reproduction of 1925 wires, not itself a contemporaneous source.

The full Commission report, Official Journal decision and Annex 815, Council statements, original national notes, and payment record were not available for direct page-image inspection in this environment. The human gate therefore remains mandatory.

## Timeline corrections

- **19 October:** independently supported as the initial frontier incident by S1 metadata, S7, S8 and S11. The pack should retain the 19–22 October range rather than treating 22 October as the first shot.
- **22–23 October:** the Bulgarian appeal was dated/sent on 22 October; S13 says it was received early on 23 October. The timeline should state this explicitly.
- **24 October:** S3 and S13 support a scheduled Greek attack/offensive that was countermanded after Briand’s intervention. This is stronger than the pack’s current “planned offensive” wording, but it remains a summary rather than a military order.
- **26–29 October:** the Council set the two-stage 24/60-hour mechanism on 26 October; orders were reported on 28 October and evacuation was reported complete on 29 October. “Withdrawal began/reported” on 28 October is less precise than “orders were given/reported.”
- **6–7 November:** the pack correctly marks this as medium confidence. S9 says the Commission was to leave Geneva on 6 November; S7 says it would meet/depart around 6–7 November. Retain as unresolved until the Commission report is inspected.
- **16–26 November itinerary:** remains a scholarly reconstruction, not independently verified here. Do not promote it to established chronology.
- **28 November and February 1926:** the report metadata and later publication are bibliographically plausible, but the report and Official Journal scan were not image-checked. Keep the existing human-verification flag.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strengths

- The pack separates the initial shooting, the Greek territorial advance, the League withdrawal mechanism, the Commission inquiry and the December settlement.
- It consistently labels Greek and Bulgarian narratives as competing government accounts rather than neutral fact.
- It avoids a final casualty count and does not claim Petrich was captured.
- Its legal wording is substantially sound when it attributes the Covenant finding to the Commission/Council record.
- Independent sources corroborate the institutional sequence and the broad settlement outcome.

### Material source problems

- **S10 is mislinked.** `stable/985596` is an unrelated JSTOR article on phantom limbs. Replace it with Barros’s 1964 article at `stable/985913`, or use the 1970 monograph record cited above.
- **S3 and S4 are not primary records.** Both are League Information Section publications and their front matter disclaims official-document status. Reclassify them as institutional secondary summaries (or A/C), while retaining S1/S2 as the primary retrieval targets.
- **S7 is retrospective.** The Bulgarian Telegraph Agency page is dated 2025 and reproduces historical wires. It is useful for provenance leads and the competing notes, but not a substitute for the original cables or Commission report.
- **S5 is a later UN legal study.** It is an authoritative locator and quotation source, but it is not the 1925 decision itself.
- **S12 is a search page, not an inspected newspaper page.** It cannot support an exact quotation, earliest dog-story provenance or page-level newspaper claim until the named issue/image is opened.
- **S11 is a secondary legal-history text hosted on a non-authoritative mirror.** Its citations are valuable, but claims should be confirmed against the cited Official Journal pages.

## Unsupported, overstated or unresolved claims

The following should not pass into narration without another check:

- “A Greek soldier definitely crossed while chasing a dog.”
- “The dog caused the crisis,” or any single-cause version of the episode.
- That the first shot was fired by Greece or Bulgaria. The Commission reportedly could not determine this.
- That Captain Vassiliadis’ death occurred exactly as the Greek verbal note states; the account is supported as an allegation and later summary, not yet as a primary neutral finding.
- That Petrich town was captured, besieged or reached at a precise time. The independently checked survey says Greek detachments entered Bulgarian territory near Petrich and that an attack on Petrich was scheduled; it does not establish capture.
- Any final Greek, Bulgarian or civilian casualty total.
- That 30 million leva and £45,000 are identical without explaining the period currency basis and exact decision wording.
- That £45,000 was or was not paid: S13 says it was duly paid, but the payment record remains uninspected and should be obtained.
- That the 16 December acceptance letter is a treaty or proves final payment; the pack’s catalog lead still needs retrieval.
- That Pangalos deliberately manufactured the incident, or that Bulgarian authorities staged it.
