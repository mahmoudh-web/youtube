## Executive verdict

**SUBSTANTIALLY SUPPORTED WITH OPEN HUMAN-VERIFICATION ITEMS.** The four repaired findings now have support at the level claimed: the 3 March Palmerston entry uses direct S19; the 17 January aftermath entry uses S14 while S13 remains December evidence; the route wording is limited to the supported Charleston–Cuba–*Trent* sequence and 7 November Havana departure; and the 30 November demand is supported by direct-copy source S18. The original Foreign Office file and Lyons's delivery record remain open checks.

The central account is supportable: on 8 November 1861 USS *San Jacinto*, under Captain Charles Wilkes, removed Confederate commissioners James Mason and John Slidell and their secretaries from the British mail packet *Trent*; Britain treated the removal as a neutral-flag and international-law grievance; forces were prepared; and the prisoners were released and transferred without an Anglo-American war. The legal dispute, military scale and causal weighting remain source-attributed or unresolved where evidence is incomplete. The pack remains research-only and must stay pending human verification; it is not narration-ready.

## Repaired findings and remaining chronology audit

1. **3 March 1862 is repaired.** S19, [Hansard, “Troops for Canada—Question,” 3 March 1862](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1862/mar/03/troops-for-canada-question), lines 21–24, records Palmerston saying that a very large force sent to Canada in mid-winter tended greatly toward a peaceful solution. This is a retrospective ministerial interpretation, not proof that force alone caused settlement and not a troop total.
2. **17 January 1862 is repaired.** S14, [Adams to Seward, 17 January 1862](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1862/d7), lines 7–16, supports favourable public-opinion effects, restored friendly relations and Adams's reported exchange with Russell. S13, [Judd to Seward, 14 December 1861](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1862/d445), lines 11–21, supports the earlier concern/rumour phase, not January relief.
3. **Route wording is repaired.** S18 lines 13–15 supports *Trent* leaving Havana on 7 November; S1/S3 support the broad Charleston–Cuba–*Trent* sequence. Nassau and intermediate dates remain unresolved.
4. **30 November demand is repaired.** S18 PDF pp. 1–2, lines 32–57, is direct documentary evidence of Russell's instruction as copied in the Lincoln Papers. The original Foreign Office file and proof of Lyons's delivery still matter for completeness, but the former retrospective-only defect is closed.
5. **21/25 December Cabinet chronology:** S16, NPS lines 34–38, says Cabinet voted on 21 December; S2 lines 90–99 says Lincoln called an emergency Cabinet on Christmas Day to consider Seward's 26-page response. This is a real source conflict, not a proofreading issue. Preserve both as attributed dates until Cabinet notes, Seward's draft and Welles's diary are checked.
6. **1/3 January release chronology:** S3's New Year's Day release/transfer and S2's 3 January departure are not contradictory if kept as separate operational events. Do not collapse “release,” “delivery into British protection,” “transfer to Provincetown” and “departure for England.”
7. **Prince Albert:** S2 lines 82–84 supports the institutional account that Albert helped soften the instructions and died on 14 December, but it is a later narrative. It does not establish that his intervention was decisive or settle the drafts' exact wording. Attribute cautiously pending Foreign Office/Cabinet papers.
8. **Hansard links and quotations:** The original S6/S7 Hansard URLs are valid official records, but direct fetching was inconsistent. The equivalent official endpoints confirm the relevant text. Hansard itself warns of typographical errors; any on-screen quotation should be checked against the printed parliamentary paper.

## Quotation and wording audit

- **S18:** the copy's operative wording is a demand for “liberation” and “delivery into British protection” plus a “suitable apology” (PDF pp. 1–2, lines 43–57). This supports the British demand, not a claim that the United States later issued a formal apology.
- **S19:** Palmerston says the winter dispatch “tended very greatly to the peaceful and satisfactory solution” (lines 21–24). This is retrospective and must not be strengthened to “forced” or “alone caused.”
- **S4:** Russell's legal text says the persons/despatches were not contraband (lines 26–33) and describes a neutral vessel under a neutral flag as protected (lines 43–48). These are British legal arguments, not an adjudicated holding.
- **S14:** Adams reports “complete restoration of friendly relations” (lines 7–16). This is his report/assessment, not proof that every hostile sentiment disappeared.
- **“One war at a time”:** the six-note pass still has no authenticated contemporary manuscript or diary for the exact wording. NPS and later histories are insufficient for a verbatim Lincoln quotation; retain only as an unresolved later attribution or omit.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong sources

- **S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S10, S13 and S14:** contemporary diplomatic despatches, parliamentary records, a Congressional printing and a contemporary manuscript. These are the strongest evidence for legal positions, formal demands, public institutional framing and diplomatic aftermath. They still represent the authoring government's or speaker's position and are not neutral verdicts.
- **S18 and S19:** S18 is a direct copy of the 30 November Russell instruction held in the Lincoln Papers; S19 is the direct official Hansard record of Palmerston's 3 March statement. Both support the corrected findings, subject to the source-specific limitations above.
- **S11:** the 1862 official correspondence volume is the essential primary retrieval route. The catalogue confirms a 15-page digitised pamphlet containing the Seward, British and French notes, but the researcher did not inspect the complete scan. No page from the uninspected scan should be quoted as independently verified.

### Useful but secondary or limited sources

- **S1:** retired Office of the Historian overview. It is a useful compact chronology but explicitly retired and not a substitute for underlying records.
- **S2:** Department of State museum synthesis. It is valuable for the Palmerston/Adams anecdote, financial context, Albert narrative, Cabinet date and seven-day window, but those details need primary confirmation.
- **S3:** official DANFS ship history. It supports the naval outline and New Year's Day marker, but is a later summary; the direct Wilkes report, Fairfax account, orders and deck log remain outstanding.
- **S9 and S16:** Library of Congress/NPS public-history gateways. S16's footnotes expose later and unpublished secondary sources behind several details; it should not carry the exact “one war at a time” quotation or precise Cabinet claims alone.
- **S12:** Naval History and Heritage Command collection page is a visual catalogue lead, not evidence for event details or a rights clearance.
- **S15:** one Richmond newspaper page is valuable reception evidence, but OCR, reprints and editorial selection require image inspection and comparison with official records. It cannot represent British, Northern, Southern or Canadian opinion generally.
- **S17:** official Canadian Army journal article, but a 1999 authorial synthesis whose 11,500/6,818 figures and “war seemed certain” language require reconciliation with War Office and transport records. Its own issue metadata says opinions are those of the author, not official policy.

### Coverage gaps that remain material

- Charles Wilkes's report, Lieutenant Fairfax's account, *San Jacinto* deck log and Navy orders: exact position, shots, boarding, resistance, papers and authority.
- The direct 30 November British instruction, Lyons's delivery record, Seward's original note and the full S11 scan: exact words for apology, disavowal, inadvertency, liberation and reparation.
- War Office, Admiralty, Canadian colonial and transport records: force categories, orders, sailing/arrival dates, Halifax versus Canada, and defensive versus coercive purpose.
- Lincoln Cabinet notes/diaries, Welles's diary and Sumner's papers: 21/25/26 December sequence and the provenance of “one war at a time.”
- Treasury/market records: whether financial pressure was causal or a later institutional summary of contemporaneous effects.
- A cross-country newspaper sample: British metropolitan and provincial press, Northern and Southern U.S. press, and Canadian papers, with original/reprint/editorial/rumour labels.
- A direct source for the mission's final failure to obtain British or French recognition, rather than inference from the mission objective and later non-recognition context.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory items

- The exact Nassau/intermediate route and dates remain unsupported and are correctly marked unresolved in the corrected notes.
- The 3 March Palmerston locator is repaired in S19; the statement remains a retrospective causal interpretation, not proof of sole causation or a troop total.
- The 17 January aftermath locator is repaired to S14; S13 is correctly retained only for the earlier December concern/rumour phase.
- “Thousands of troops at Halifax,” “11,500,” “6,818,” “3,000,” “8,000” and “10,000–12,000” are different source-attributed quantities or stages, not one validated total; S17 also gives 274 officers/6,544 men for a particular route. They must not be combined.
- “Britain had to back down,” “Seward surrendered,” “formal apology,” “Lincoln personally overruled the country,” “Britain was determined to declare war,” “diplomatic immunity,” “unanimous public opinion,” and verbatim “one war at a time” remain unsafe unless attributed and narrowed exactly as the six notes already advise.
- The source index's statement that S11's scan was not fully machine-readable is a valid limitation. It means the complete primary correspondence remains a required check, not that the catalogue record itself verifies every note in the volume. S18 now supplies direct-copy evidence for the dated demand, but not the complete original-file/delivery chain.
