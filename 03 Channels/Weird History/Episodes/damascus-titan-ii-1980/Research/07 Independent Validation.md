## Source quality and coverage

- **Strongest evidence:** S1 and S2 are official retrospective summaries of the nuclear-weapon accident record. They converge on the initiating socket impact, first-stage fuel leak, evacuation, roughly overnight duration, conventional ignition/explosion, one fatality, 21 injured personnel, recovery of the weapon/re-entry vehicle and no radioactive contamination. S1 is a reproduction with poor OCR and must be checked against the scan and FOIA release markings; S2 is an official DOE guide reproduced by an external host and compresses the chronology.
- **Primary contemporaneous context:** S3, S4, S5 and S13 are Congressional Record pages. They are primary records of what members said and what was reported to Congress, not themselves the full accident report, hearing transcript, radiological survey or engineering finding. S5 reproduces an Air Force account but remains a reported account.
- **Useful but secondary:** S6 is an Arkansas institutional synthesis; S7 is a university exhibit; S10 is an institutional collection write-up. They cross-check the broad sequence but contain unsourced or speculative framing. In particular, S7’s “almost destroyed Arkansas,” fallout and safety-switch passages must remain quarantined as hypothetical/interpretive material.
- **Interested/retrospective sources:** S9 is a PBS documentary transcript built from interviews and declassified material, useful for witness recollections but not a substitute for the board record. S11 is a valuable contemporary newspaper report of Hans Mark’s briefing, but it is not a hearing transcript and its page date conflicts with the URL path. S12 is a later author’s research-note apparatus: it is a lead to the primary report, not evidence for every report-size or witness-count detail claimed in the pack.
- **Institutional primary lead needing visual inspection:** S8, the Arkansas National Register nomination, is an official state record, but the accessible PDF has no usable text layer in this audit. Its extracted date/detail snippets should not be relied on until the relevant page images and underlying references are inspected.
- **Coverage gap:** The pack still lacks the original Eighth Air Force mishap board, the Titan II Weapon System Review Group Report, the full House Armed Services hearing behind S11, the emergency/command log, radiological survey and casualty records. Those omissions are honestly flagged in the researcher notes and remain load-bearing gaps.

## Corrections, conflicts and missing-source claims

1. Correct S11’s displayed date discrepancy: the Washington Post page header reads September 24, 1980, while the URL path is September 25. Describe it as a report of Mark’s testimony and verify the hearing date/transcript.
2. Remove or hold the “more than a thousand pages” and “92 witness statements” description attached to C18. The accessible S12 notes support the existence of the December board report and its three-volume description, not those two numbers.
3. Downgrade the provenance of “W-53” in C12. The official summaries checked here establish a nuclear warhead but do not name W-53; that designation is supported by S6 and must be confirmed in the weapon/recovery record. Yield statements in S4 and S7 conflict and should remain out of the pack.
4. Correct the retirement-announcement chronology in C19/timeline: S7 says September 1981, while official Air Force history says October 1981. Verify against the presidential or Defense Department primary record. Keep later final deactivation/removal distinct; official Air Force history records May 5, 1987 as the end of the Titan II operational era.
5. Keep water volume, socket mass/fall distance, precise ignition source, evacuation radius, exact time sequence, medical details and any individual blame unresolved. These are not safe to “average” across secondary sources.
6. Treat S7’s nuclear counterfactual as exhibit framing only. It does not establish a probability, launch sequence, fallout footprint or casualty model.
