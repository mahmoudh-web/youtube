## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Tanganyika Laughter Epidemic: What Did the Records Actually Say?” It examines the 1962–63 outbreak reported from Kashasha, Nshamba and nearby communities in the Bukoba district of Tanganyika (now Tanzania). It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification note and provisional conclusion.
- Reconstruct what Rankin and Philip’s 1963 medical report actually recorded: symptoms, spread, examinations, laboratory work, competing physical explanations and the limits of their conclusion.
- Separate the contemporary medical record from later interpretations using “mass psychogenic illness” and from popular claims about contagious laughter, continuous laughing, national paralysis or deaths.
- Preserve local terminology and reported community explanations without treating them as proof, curiosities or evidence of irrationality.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `tanganyika-laughter-1962` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “laughter epidemic” only as the historical/reporting label. The record describes recurrent attacks of laughing and crying, restlessness and occasional violence when restrained; it does not describe continuous laughter or a humorous event.
- Treat Rankin and Philip’s 1963 conclusion—“mass hysteria in a susceptible population” and “probably a culturally determined disease”—as a tentative, historically situated interpretation, not a diagnosis of individual pupils or communities.
- Use “mass psychogenic illness” as a later interpretive framework attributed to Hempelmann and related scholarship. It is not a settled explanation and must not erase the possibility of incomplete records or the limits of 1963 testing.
- The authorized six-file Research sync uses `01 Source Index.md` through `06 Conclusion.md`, with the fifth file named `05 Uncertainty and Human Verification.md`.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not diagnose the affected people, imply feigning, or use “mass hysteria” as an unqualified present-day label. Historical terms remain source-attributed.
- Do not portray Tanzanian communities as inexplicable, primitive or uniquely susceptible. The record includes local names and explanations, but the surviving medical account did not obtain relevant information from the initially affected Kashasha pupils.
- Do not say that people laughed continuously for months, that the whole country was affected, that the episode was caused by a joke, or that it was a contagious virus. These claims are either contradicted by the report or derive from later exaggeration.
- Do not state that no physical cause was possible. The 1963 work found no abnormality in its sampled tests and no food factor, but Hempelmann notes that a viral infection cannot be excluded on clinical grounds and the original sample was limited.
- Do not use the original report’s “no fatalities” as proof that every affected person was harmless or that no unrecorded harm occurred; phrase it as the report’s finding.
- “Endwara ya Kucheka”/“Enwara Yokusheka” and “Akajanja” are recorded spellings/translations from the sources. A language specialist should verify spelling, pronunciation and meaning before narration.
- No visual asset is approved. Archival scans, maps, school imagery and any depiction of affected children require provenance, rights, dignity and cultural review.
