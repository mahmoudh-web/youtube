## Chronology guardrails

- “Survival experiment” is an editorial framing, not the name of the event or a phrase in the 1905 report.
- The period account supports an afternoon race, dust, hills, restricted fresh water, visible medical distress and oral strychnine treatment. It does not by itself prove that Sullivan consciously designed the race as a dehydration experiment.
- The start count must remain source-labelled: Lucas 31 answering the clerk, Sullivan 29 starters in the later handbook, and modern databases commonly 32.
- The six-mile water tower and 12-mile well belong to later/reproduced accounts; Lucas's own wording says one fresh-water place at 12 miles.
- “Hicks was carried across the line” is stronger than the located primary wording. Lucas says Hicks was caught after breaking the tape and was too weak to accept the trophy; retain the distinction.
