| 9 Nov 1983 | Simulated initial nuclear use is delivered; SACEUR requests follow-on use later that day. | The nuclear use was exercise play and did not launch real weapons. | High for the SHAPE scenario; Soviet interpretation of these signals is uncertain. |

| 11 Nov 1983 | Exercise ends on schedule after simulated follow-on nuclear use. | SHAPE says the exercise did not end early; PFIAB says the Soviet alert was evidently withdrawn and East German flight training returned to normal. | High for scheduled end; Soviet normalization timing remains partly redacted. |
| 15 Nov 1983 | General Richard Stilwell and Major General Edward Atkeson discuss Soviet operational analysis. | The National Security Archive places this among the first U.S. efforts to understand unusual Soviet behaviour. | Medium-high; read the underlying memo for exact scope. |
| 16 Nov 1983 | Reagan records meeting George Shultz about a direct channel to Soviet officials. | The diary records concern about communication after the tense period. | High for the diary entry; the prompt and direct connection to Able Archer are unclear. |
| 18 Nov 1983 | Reagan writes that the Soviets are “so defense minded” and afraid of attack. | This is Reagan’s recorded perception, not an independent measure of Soviet intent. | High for the entry; avoid causal claims. |
| 1984 | U.S. intelligence produces a more sceptical SNIE on Soviet military-political activity. | The estimate, as quoted by Miles, judged Soviet actions not evidence of an imminent conflict. | Medium-high; retrieve the CIA/NARA record and inspect the full estimate. |
| 16 Oct 1988 | The “Able Archer” near-war story enters wide public circulation through a Sunday Telegraph article. | The National Security Archive says the name became common through this exposé. | Medium-high; verify the original article before making media-history claims. |
| 15 Feb 1990 | PFIAB completes “The Soviet ‘War Scare.’” | The report reviews intelligence and interviews, later concluding that Soviet forces may have prepared for an attack under cover of the exercise. | High for report existence and wording; its conclusions are retrospective and partially redacted. |
| 1997 | CIA publishes Fischer’s *A Cold War Conundrum*. | The official history presents genuine Soviet fear as plausible while documenting missing corroboration and contrary recollections. | High for publication; use it as an institutional interpretation, not as newly contemporaneous evidence. |
| 2013 | NATO and U.S. Air Force records are posted by the National Security Archive. | The releases clarify exercise dates, nuclear play, wider Autumn Forge/Reforger context and communications “slips.” | High for the released documents; Archive commentary must be separated from document text. |
| 2015 | PFIAB report is declassified after a long FOIA/declassification effort. | The public receives the detailed report and its stronger warning about intelligence underestimating Soviet concern. | High for declassification history; the report still contains redactions. |
| 2018 | National Security Archive publishes Soviet-side documents. | Documents from KGB, Politburo and General Staff contexts show war concern in parts of the Soviet system, while the curator explicitly says there is no evidence of an imminent launch. | Medium-high; translations, provenance and representativeness require human review. |
| 2020 | Simon Miles publishes a sceptical archival study. | Miles argues RYaN was not operational in the way the near-war narrative assumes and that Warsaw Pact archives show more sanguine observations. | High for the published argument; it is an interpretation of incomplete archives. |

## Date and nomenclature control

Some accounts use 2–11 November for the wider Autumn Forge/Reforger activity or for the alert period. The SHAPE historical summary and SAC after-action report date **Able Archer 83 itself to 7–11 November**. The episode must not flatten the command-post exercise, the live troop airlift and the wider exercise umbrella into one event.

## Research stop

Before narration, verify the original 10 November intelligence report, the SHAPE and SAC scans, the PFIAB pages 69–76, the Soviet documents/translation and the 1984 SNIE. No date in this timeline should be used to imply that a simulated nuclear decision was a real launch order.
