## Confidence summary

- **High confidence:** *Panorama* broadcast the fictional item on 1 April 1957; the setting was presented as Ticino/Lake Lugano; the surviving footage and narration staged a spaghetti harvest; Richard Dimbleby narrated it.
- **Medium confidence:** de Jaeger originated/filmed the idea, Wheeler wrote the script, Peacock approved a £100 budget, and the film lasted roughly 2½–3 minutes. These are supported by later production accounts and should be checked against BBC paperwork.
- **Medium to low confidence:** “millions” / “eight million” audience, “hundreds” of callers as an exact figure, the two-hour call period, and the proportion who believed the story.
- **Unresolved:** exact filming site, complete crew/approval chain, surviving viewer correspondence, original BBC response memo, and the provenance of the audience estimates.

## Verification queue

| Priority | Question | Required record | Safe interim treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the authoritative production record? | BBC Written Archives Centre *Panorama* file, running order, script, budget, camera/producer credits and correspondence for 1 April 1957. | Use attributed later accounts; do not present any one crew member as sole author. |
| 2 | What exactly aired? | Best-quality BBC film/audio, full timecode, opening/closing frames, on-screen date cue and item duration. | Describe only what the surviving clip shows; avoid reconstructed shots. |
| 3 | Where was it filmed? | BBC location paperwork, travel/expense records, Ticino local archive or contemporaneous participant testimony. | Say “Ticino/Lake Lugano area” and omit a named hotel/village. |
| 4 | How many people watched? | Original BBC audience research or BARB predecessor record, with definition and sampling method. | Say “millions according to later summaries” only with explicit attribution, or omit the number. |
| 5 | How many people responded? | BBC telephone-exchange logs, letters, internal memos, press office log and contemporary newspaper coverage. | Say “contemporary and later accounts report hundreds of calls,” not “the BBC received exactly X calls.” |
| 6 | What did the BBC tell viewers? | Original 1957 BBC press statement, newspaper issue, correspondence templates and any broadcast clarification. | Use the contemporary reproduced wording provisionally and mark source-recovery pending. |
| 7 | Did viewers believe it? | A dated sample of letters/calls, with response categories for belief, scepticism, humour and complaints. | Describe mixed documented responses; do not infer a national belief rate. |
| 8 | What was the food context? | 1950s UK retail/food histories, sales data or contemporary newspaper coverage of spaghetti. | Say “spaghetti was less familiar to many British viewers than today,” not “Britons did not know it.” |
| 9 | Can the material be reused? | BBC archive licence, YouTube/BBC platform rights, newspaper archive terms and any still-image restrictions. | Treat all film, audio, script and stills as research leads only. |

## Research stop condition

This research phase stops here. The current evidence is sufficient to support a carefully qualified research conclusion and an independent-validation handoff. It is not sufficient for a final script, audience-belief statistic, precise location claim, or approved visual plan.

## Legal, ethical and correction flags

- No living-person defamation issue is apparent from the historical subject, but individual crew-role and intention claims should remain attributed until records are checked.
- Do not frame the hoax as evidence that all broadcast journalism is deceptive or as a direct analogue proving modern “fake news.”
- Clearly label staged footage, fabricated narration and later recollections.
- Preserve corrections if new primary records contradict the current pack.
