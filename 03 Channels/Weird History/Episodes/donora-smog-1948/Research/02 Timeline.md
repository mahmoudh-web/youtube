## Date convention

The PHS meteorological section describes the episode as **25–31 October 1948** (S3, lines 6082–6105), while EPA’s later handbook begins the disaster on **27 October** (S7, lines 84–91) and Pennsylvania Heritage says the smog enveloped Donora on **26 October** (S6, lines 21–24). These may reflect different definitions of onset: early fog/smoke, the extreme episode, or the documented acute health crisis. The timeline preserves the variation instead of selecting one date without the original daily records.

| Date / period | Event or evidence | Status and source |
|---|---|---|
| 1901–1915 | Donora develops as a heavy-industrial town; the steel works and later Donora Zinc Works become central employers. | High confidence for industrial context; PHMC S5, lines 17–19. The exact opening/ownership chronology needs the plant records if used in narration. |
| Late Oct 1948 (onset disputed) | Weather pattern and fog begin to create the conditions later analyzed as a prolonged stable period. | Provisional. S3 calls the meteorological episode 25–31 Oct; S6 gives 26 Oct onset and S7 begins the disaster on 27 Oct. Check daily Weather Bureau records and original report figures. |
| 26–27 Oct | Dense fog/smog becomes an abnormal community event; later EPA summaries date the disaster’s start to 27 Oct. | High for the broad window, not for a single onset hour; S7 lines 84–91; S1 abstract. |
| 27–29 Oct | Pollutants accumulate in the valley as low wind, high stability, humidity/fog, topography and active sources combine. | High for mechanism as reconstructed after the event; S3 lines 6045–6118 and 6242–6329. No contemporaneous acute-event monitoring series was found. |
| 29–30 Oct | Residents develop respiratory and mucous-membrane symptoms; doctors and local responders face an expanding caseload. | High for symptoms and broad response; S1 abstract, S5 lines 13–14, S7 lines 90–95. Exact call logs and patient chronology remain to be checked. |
| 30 Oct | Fatalities peak. The PHS summary says 17 of 20 deaths occurred on day 3; PHMC describes 19 deaths within a 24-hour period. | High for the PHS count; the two statements use different scopes and must not be merged. S3 lines 6177–6194; S5 lines 13–14. |
| 31 Oct | Rain and a change in atmospheric conditions disperse the smog. Lois Bainbridge writes to Governor James Duff requesting smoke control and blaming the zinc works. | High for rain/letter date; S5 lines 35–64 and S7 lines 84–91. Her attribution is testimony, not a final causal finding. |
| Nov 1948–early 1949 | State and federal investigators collect household, clinical, atmospheric and meteorological evidence. | High for the investigation; S1 abstract; S15 lines 24–26. Exact fieldwork dates and surviving state file contents require archival review. |
| 1949 | The PHS preliminary report is issued. It records 5,910 affected (42.7%), 20 deaths during or shortly after, multiple contaminants, and no single responsible substance. | High, pending original scan check; S1, S3 lines 6145–6234. |
| 1949 | Pennsylvania establishes a Division of Air Pollution Control in response to the event and wider concern. | High as an institutional-history claim; S5 lines 27–29; S6 lines 27–28. Confirm the enabling order/legislative record. |
| 3–5 May 1950 | U.S. Technical Conference on Air Pollution meets. This is evidence of policy attention, not proof of sole legislative causation. |
| 1951 | Later reporting says civil suits against American Steel and Wire were settled without an adjudication of blame. | Medium; S15 lines 27–29. Locate the complaint, settlement terms, and any court docket before using dollar figures or saying “exonerated.” |

It is a retrospective political source, not an independent re-investigation. |
