## Purpose

This folder holds the source-led research pack for the episode hypothesis about the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops (“Ghost Army”) in Western Europe, 1944–45. The unanswered question is: **which deceptions demonstrably changed German expectations, and what can official and captured German records actually establish?** The editorial promise is to separate confirmed tactical effects, partial or mixed results, and attractive claims that remain unproven.

## Responsibilities

- Keep one traceable claim per ledger row, with a source locator, confidence, caveat, and safe wording.
- Distinguish wartime fact, retrospective official assessment, veteran recollection, enemy evidence, interpretation, allegation, and uncertainty.
- Preserve counterevidence, especially German records that complicate a total-success narrative.
- Keep this folder research-only. No narration, title/thumbnail package, visual prompts, edit plan, or publication asset belongs here.
- Leave the material awaiting human verification before scripting or publication.

## Key Decisions

- The pack treats Task Force Mason, Bettembourg, and Viersen as the strongest cases for localized or operational effect; Brest as tactically useful but strategically incomplete; Brittany as marginal; and Kodak as unresolved.
- The captured Viersen overlay and reported POW evidence are valuable enemy-record leads, but they are reported through U.S. histories and must not be presented as an unqualified direct German voice.
- Eldridge’s scholarly critique and the cited German microfilm records are retained as counterevidence: German intelligence was not uniformly or permanently deceived.
- Published lives-saved totals are retained only as attributed estimates. No exact total or causal counterfactual is accepted as established fact.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for the current pack: 2026-09-14.
- Primary source priority: NARA RG 407 records, original operation reports, captured German records, veterans’ papers/oral histories, and official U.S. Army records.
- The Ghost Army Legacy Project pages are useful mirrors and finding aids, not substitutes for inspection of the NARA originals.
- Do not say “fooled Hitler,” “won the war,” or “saved X lives” without stronger, claim-specific evidence and human approval.
- Historical participants and civilians must be treated precisely and without sensational or defamatory inference. Copyright and reuse rights for scans, photos, maps, and oral-history media remain open.
