## Claims not yet safe to use

- “The lake exploded” as if the event were a bomb, magmatic eruption or single underground blast.
- “Scientists know exactly what triggered it.”
- “A landslide definitely caused the disaster,” despite landslide evidence from Monoun and later popular accounts.
- “There was no earthquake,” unless a seismological source and the limits of the historical record are checked. Lack of reported shaking is not a complete seismic catalogue.
- “No one suffered burns” or “there were no injuries.” The medical literature needs to be represented accurately and respectfully.
- An exact toll of 1,746 as the only possible count; use the source-attributed range and definitions.
- “The cloud was pure CO₂.” Medical and chemical studies support CO₂ as the primary lethal agent but do not justify erasing all minor components.
- “The dam burst caused the deaths.” Dam failure is a separate future hazard in the USGS record.
- “The first limnic eruption” or similar superlative without a specialist source and definition.
