## Research question and promise

How did a resource dispute involving fishing limits, naval escorts, collisions, and trawl-wire cutting end through diplomacy? The answer must preserve the difference between three confrontations, legal positions, and later interpretations of who “won.”

## Source-quality key

- **A** — primary treaty, court record, parliamentary record, or official archival record/catalogue.
- **B** — contemporary institutional or first-party statement with a clear perspective.
- **C** — reputable retrospective, scholarly, or government synthesis that needs triangulation.
- **D** — tourism, exhibition, or other tertiary account used only for leads or attributed recollection.

## Key entities and voices

- Icelandic government and Althingi: conservation, coastal dependence, and jurisdiction policy; official political claims require attribution.
- Icelandic Coast Guard: vessels and enforcement records including Ægir, Þór/Thor, Óðinn and Týr; names and incident roles require log verification.
- UK government, Foreign Office, Parliament and Royal Navy: legal position, trawler protection, negotiation, and fleet policy; official claims are not neutral.
- British distant-water trawler industry and ports: operational and economic consequences; local testimony and industry records remain a gap.
- ICJ: legal framework and 1974 mixed holding.
- NATO and Norway: strategic and mediation context, not proof of a single-cause explanation.

## Source gaps

Ship logs, Coast Guard logs, incident reports, injury/death records, complete 1973 exchange notes, cabinet minutes, and bilingual Icelandic translations are not yet verified. No claim about a total number of wire cuts, collisions, or casualties should be treated as final.
