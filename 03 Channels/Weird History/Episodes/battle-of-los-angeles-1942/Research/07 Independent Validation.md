## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strengths

- S2 is an accessible reproduction of the official Army history and directly supports the central sequence, approximate 1,400-round figure, probable balloon first shot, and later Army/Navy split.
- S4 is a contemporary AP newspaper scan reproducing a WDC statement. It is the strongest located source for the 2:25 a.m. blackout, conflicting reports, no bombs/no planes shot down, 7:21 a.m. all-clear, and contemporaneously reported damage and deaths. It remains a wire-service reproduction rather than the underlying WDC release.
- S5 is contemporary UP/AP coverage that supports what Stimson and Knox were reported to have said, including the 1,430 figure and “enemy agents” theory. It does not prove their factual theories.
- S8 is a primary presidential document and securely supports the date and legal authority of EO 9066.
- S3, S7, S9, and S10 provide useful institutional or archival triangulation but are later summaries and must not substitute for command, radar, firing, casualty, or image records.
- S6 preserves a potentially important participant recollection, but its seven-year delay, memoir-like tone, and access-mirror hosting make it retrospective testimony, not an original inquiry finding.

### Coverage weaknesses and source-handling corrections

1. S1 is labelled “A” in the source index, but a 1964 official history is an official retrospective synthesis, not a contemporary primary record. It should be treated as authoritative secondary evidence (strong synthesis, not the original event record). The direct PDF was not retrievable in this audit; S2 was used for the relevant text.
2. S4’s underlying WDC release has not been retrieved. The LOC scan/OCR is accessible and supports the quoted public wording, but the release, local civil-defense log, and time standard remain open checks.
3. Exact radar range, radar-site corroboration, first-trigger identity, start/stop times, battery sequence, ammunition type, and total rounds are not established at primary-record level.
4. The 1,400 (Army history), 1,430 (Stimson report), and 1,433 (later LA Times retrospective) figures must remain separate. They may reflect different counting bases, but no located source proves that they do.
5. The pack underuses the casualty detail already visible in S4 and S9. Those accounts support reported indirect deaths and shell/property damage, but do not eliminate the need for a reconciled local record.
6. S9 is useful evidence of retouching and image provenance, but its author’s 2011 inspection is not an archival chain-of-custody report. Rights clearance and original-negative verification remain separate requirements.
7. S11 and S12 are catalogue/research routes only. No catalogue hit, search snippet, or listed archive lead should be counted as evidence until the record group, series, box/item, pages, and contents are inspected.

## Unresolved, unsupported, or contradictory points

- The original radar and command records were not located or inspected; “120 miles,” “1:45 a.m.,” “2:15 confirmations,” and the exact trigger remain unresolved.
- The Army history’s “shortly after 3:00 a.m.” first-shot timing conflicts with Droke’s recollection of a meteorological balloon at about 1:00 a.m. The pack correctly preserves this conflict; it must not be harmonised by inference.
- Stimson’s fifteen-aircraft theory, commercial-plane/enemy-agent theory, and the Army’s one-to-five assessment are official statements or later synthesis, not verified aircraft counts.
- The Navy’s “false alarm” position and the Army’s one-to-five position are genuinely competing institutional interpretations in the located sources. Neither should be silently promoted to the physical truth of what was in the sky.
- “Five casualties” is supported by later LA Times and Smithsonian summaries, while the contemporaneous LOC article exposes only some details on the accessible front page. The exact total and causation remain open.
- The photograph’s publication is verified; its object identity, original appearance, and production rights are not.
- No located 1942 source supports extraterrestrial craft. UFO/alien framing is later cultural interpretation, not evidence.
