## Research question

What do released records establish about Operation Mincemeat’s design, the way Spanish and German authorities handled the planted papers, and the identity of the man used as “Major William Martin”? How much can the operation’s effect on the Sicily campaign safely be separated from the wider Allied deception and military plan?

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary/released record:** wartime operational report, intelligence document or archival file inspected in its original or official digital form.
- **B — official institutional synthesis:** National Archives, IWM, government or museum account that identifies objects, files or the historical sequence.
- **C — specialist secondary analysis:** peer-reviewed or archival scholarship that compares primary material and makes an explicit argument.
- **D — public access/transcription mirror:** readable reproduction of a primary or published source whose original scan, edition or permissions still need checking; this includes a later participant memoir reproducing wartime documents.
- **E — general secondary/reception source:** useful for later identification or public memory, not sufficient alone for a disputed claim.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use S2–S6 to reconstruct the operational design and reported reception, but label Montagu’s interpretation separately from reproduced German material.
2. Use S1, S9 and S10 for the identity question; do not collapse the released-record case and the HMS *Dasher* theory into an unresolved “either/or” without weighing the evidence.
3. Treat Spanish autopsy, document access and burial details as mediated until Spanish records and the British file images are inspected.
4. Use S13–S15 to prevent a single-operation explanation of the Sicily campaign. “Influenced German planning” is safer than “caused the invasion to succeed.”
5. Do not use the title’s “supposed to win a war” as a factual claim. The pack supports a deception intended to protect the Sicily operation, not a quantified war-winning effect.
6. No visual asset is approved for production by this research pack.

## Visual opportunities (research leads only)

- **Operational-orders treatment:** a clearly labelled document-inspired reconstruction of the 31 March orders, using short paraphrase and archive reference rather than a faux original. Human review must clear Crown copyright, transcription accuracy and security context.
- **Evidence chain:** a map/timeline linking the *Seraph*, Huelva, the Spanish naval authorities, German intelligence and the later Sicily landing. Use an explicitly schematic map, not a claim that every handoff is independently documented.
- **Identity card / personal effects:** use the TNA/IWM collection leads only after provenance, licence, caption and dignity review. Avoid repeated corpse imagery.
- **German assessment:** a source-attributed graphic contrasting “genuineness above suspicion” with the possibility of a plant; preserve the uncertainty rather than making the document look like a definitive verdict.
- **Huelva grave:** consider a respectful, non-graphic memorial visual only after human confirmation of current grave details and image rights.
