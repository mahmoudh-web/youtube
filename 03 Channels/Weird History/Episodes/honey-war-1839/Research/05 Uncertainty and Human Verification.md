## Verification status

This is a source-led research pack, not script approval. The central legal/jurisdictional chronology is well supported by contemporary federal documents and later court records. The bee-tree episode, force totals, exact local sequence, casualty wording, slavery context and Indigenous land history still need source-by-source verification.

## Uncertainty register

### 1. “Honey War” as a contemporary name

The label is securely established in later institutional and historical sources. S7 reports a Palmyra Whig poem dated 26 December 1839, which is a promising contemporary naming lead, but the original newspaper has not been inspected. The 1839 federal message S1 does not use “Honey War” or mention bees. Use “the later-named Honey War” until the original newspaper and other 1839 press are checked.

### 2. Number and identity of bee trees

Several sources say three trees; NPS says four. Some later accounts say the cutter’s identity was lost, while others describe an unnamed Missourian or a tax-related act. Do not name a culprit, give an exact count or assert an official order until the county court record, local newspaper and relevant correspondence are located.

### 3. “Bloodless” and casualties

The located sources document threats, civil process and militia mobilisation but no battle. Lucas’s 5 November message, as quoted by S7, warned of possible “effusion of blood”; it was a prediction, not evidence that blood was shed. A complete casualty ledger has not been located. Safe wording: “No battle is documented in this source pass”; “later histories often call it bloodless.” Do not say “only the bees were casualties.”

### 4. Force totals and battlefield presence

Accounts range from nearly 800 Missouri militiamen to more than 2,000 gathering and NPS’s broad 600–2,500 range. Iowa is variously described as about 600 troops or a nominal force of 1,200, with only about 500 reaching camp in S7. These may refer to different stages or administrative categories. Treat every figure as source-attributed until muster rolls, requisitions, pay claims and orders are read.

### 5. Gregory’s sequence and custody

S7 dates Gregory’s arrest to 20 November 1839. S15 places a meeting on 24 October and describes Gregory’s return in November; S1’s official documents establish tax/jurisdictional claims but do not provide a complete local court chronology. Retrieve the arrest complaint, court minutes, sheriff correspondence and release record.

### 6. Boundary and legal endpoint

The 1839 record shows rival interpretations of the Sullivan line, Brown line and “rapids” language. S10 gives the Supreme Court’s 1849 holding; S11 records resurvey and monuments in 1850–51. Keep these separate. Verify the exact date and wording in the official U.S. Reports scan before narration.

### 7. Slavery and political motive

Iowa educational and autobiographical sources identify the difference between slave-state Missouri and Iowa Territory as part of the context; Lucas’s July letter in S1 alleges Missouri officials asked about slavery. The proclamations themselves centre on boundary and jurisdiction. Present slavery as context or a possible stake, not a proven single cause.

### 8. Indigenous title and territorial framing

The line originated in an Osage cession boundary, and the broader region involved Ioway, Sauk, Meskwaki and other treaty histories. S12 is only a catalogue lead. Before using “ownership,” “settlers,” “empty strip” or “land prize,” add the treaty texts and Indigenous-history scholarship and seek human editorial review.

## Legal, ethical and editorial risk

No living-person defamation issue is apparent. The principal risks are historical overstatement, false precision around the bee-tree count and troop totals, treating partisan proclamations as neutral legal findings, using “bloodless” as a casualty claim, and erasing Indigenous title or local communities from the map. Human editorial review is mandatory before title, thumbnail, script, archive-image reuse or publication.

## Stop condition

Stop research-to-script handoff until the bee-tree newspaper/court record has been checked, the Missouri boundary act date is resolved, the militia counts are separated by category, the 1849 versus 1851 legal/physical endpoints are verified, and treaty/Indigenous context has been sourced.
