## Main narrative tests

| Common version | Evidence for it | Counterevidence / limitation | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| “China declared war on sparrows in 1958.” | The 1958 central directive named sparrows, ordered mass participation and used war/conquest language; Beijing and Chongqing reports describe coordinated attacks. | The policy developed from earlier public-health and crop-protection campaigns, including Zheng’s 1956 article; “war” is a useful shorthand, not the exact legal name of one operation. | Say “the Four Pests campaign mobilised a war-like campaign against sparrows,” and explain the wider programme. |
| “Mao alone invented the policy and every scientist opposed it.” | Mao and central institutions were visible in the policy; the later reversal involved CAS research. | Zheng’s 1956 article supported active control; scientists disagreed and refined their positions over time. | Preserve the changing scientific record and do not make a unanimity claim. |
| “The campaign exterminated China’s sparrows.” | Official reports claimed local near-eradication; Beijing reports described very large kills; the new working paper models a major ecological disruption. | Official totals are incomplete; local depletion is not national extinction; independent population baselines are scarce. | Use “drove sparrows sharply down in many targeted areas” only when geographically sourced; avoid national extinction unless data are found. |
| “No sparrows meant locusts exploded everywhere.” | The ecological mechanism is plausible; CAS records discuss insect pressure and the 2026 paper tests crop effects. | The located primary sources do not yet provide a national insect census or a clean counterfactual; weather, pesticides, farming practices and reporting bias also matter. | Say “may have increased pest pressure in some places”; locate provincial crop-protection records before stronger wording. |
| “The campaign caused the Great Famine.” | The famine followed the campaign; the sparrow mechanism could affect yields; Frank et al. estimate a measurable contribution. | Peer-reviewed famine literature emphasises central planning, procurement, resource diversion, malnutrition, distribution, provincial response and weather; the new sparrow estimate is a working paper. | State that sparrow eradication is a contested possible contributor within a multi-causal famine, not the sole cause. |
| “Natural disasters were the cause.” | The 1981 party resolution includes natural disasters and Soviet contract rupture in its official explanation. | Other academic work finds policy and institutional mechanisms necessary or quantitatively important; weather is one factor among several. | Attribute the official formulation and place it beside peer-reviewed policy-focused findings. |
| “The 2.1 billion figure proves the campaign’s scale.” | A 1959 ministerial report printed the figure. | The source describes incomplete statistics and was produced in a success-reporting environment; it is not a population survey. | Use only as a reported official statistic, with the caveat in the same sentence or caption. |
| “The policy reversal happened as soon as the ecological damage was noticed.” | Mao circulated the CAS report in Nov 1959; meetings followed in Dec 1959–Mar 1960; the March 1960 instruction changed the target. | Circulation, research coordination and formal policy substitution were separate steps; dates and implementation need primary-document checks. | Present a sequence, not an instant reversal. |
| “The campaign ended in 1959.” | Scientific reassessment and the CAS report date to late 1959. | The located institutional account places the explicit “stop killing sparrows” instruction in March 1960 and formal wording in April 1960. | Use “the reversal was prepared in late 1959 and stated in March/April 1960.” |

## Famine causation: evidence map

### Stronger evidence for major non-sparrow mechanisms

- Meng, Qian and Yared find rigid, progressive grain procurement necessary for the observed regional mortality pattern and estimate it explained 32–43% of famine mortality in their model (S13; PDF pp. 4 and 34).
- Li and Yang attribute 61% of the output decline in their model to resource diversion and excessive procurement (S14).
- Houser, Sands and Xiao model central policy, provincial response and weather together rather than treating “natural” and “man-made” causes as mutually exclusive (S15).
- The 1981 official resolution itself identifies Great Leap Forward and anti-right-deviation errors alongside natural disasters and the Soviet rupture (S11).

These sources do not disprove a sparrow effect. They do establish why the episode must not turn one ecological intervention into a complete famine explanation.

### Evidence that keeps a sparrow contribution live

- The Four Pests directive and local reports prove a very large state-backed intervention against sparrows (S2–S7).
- The CAS archival account records that scientists considered seasonal/regional ecological effects and shifted policy (S8–S9).
- Frank et al.’s working paper reports geographic yield and mortality differences associated with sparrow suitability after eradication (S12).

This is evidence for a possible contribution, not a final national attribution. A human reviewer should read the paper’s identification strategy, robustness tables and data appendix before approving any number.

## Missing counterevidence to retrieve

1. Provincial crop-pest and locust records from 1958–60, including places with different sparrow suitability.
2. Agricultural yield and mortality series that can be compared before and after local campaigns.
3. The original CAS report and meeting minutes, including dissenting scientific views.
4. The full 1957 Zheng food-analysis article and contemporary critiques in *Wenhui Bao*, *Beijing Ribao* or provincial papers.
5. Independent demographic estimates and archival evidence on procurement, communal kitchens, labour diversion and weather.
