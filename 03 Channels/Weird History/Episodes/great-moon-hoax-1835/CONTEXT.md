## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Newspaper That Put Bat-Men on the Moon,” about the six-part 1835 *New York Sun* fabrication commonly called the Great Moon Hoax. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty note and provisional conclusion.
- Separate the newspaper’s fictional lunar observations from established facts about the newspaper, John Herschel, Richard Adams Locke and the story’s reception.
- Record source quality, exact page/line/image locators, access dates, confidence and unresolved caveats.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `great-moon-hoax-1835` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat the six installments as a primary-text object: describe what *The Sun* printed, but never present its lunar creatures or landscapes as observations.
- Prefer the 1835 text preserved in the 1859 Locke compilation, contemporary or near-contemporary newspaper material, institutional catalogues and original Herschel records; use later summaries to triangulate and identify leads.
- Keep the “Richard Adams Locke wrote it,” “Yale scientists were fooled,” circulation totals and September 16 admission claims attributed until their underlying contemporary records are checked.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- “Bat-men,” “unicorns,” bipedal beavers and lunar temples are elements of a fabricated story. They must always be labelled as claims in the hoax text.
- Do not claim that the hoax “fooled the whole world,” that all readers believed it, or that it was the first fake news story without defining the evidence and limits.
- Do not imply that John Herschel endorsed the story. His real Cape observatory and astronomical work are the factual control record.
- Images in the source index are archival leads, not approved production assets; rights and reuse terms require a separate human decision.
