## Conflicts and reconciliation rules

| Popular or early wording | Evidence that complicates it | Current handling |
|---|---|---|
| “$30m and 10m pounds were stolen.” | The Federation’s 30 Aug 2012 release called this the warehouse’s total holdings while the scope was still being evaluated; its December release used about 6m pounds; the court record uses 5,935,250 pounds and about $17.8m. | Use the judicial figure for the proven theft. Preserve $30m as an initial warehouse estimate only. |
| “Nearly 3,000 tonnes / $18.7m.” | Canadian reporting and public summaries round or convert the volume/value; the Crown factum gives 5,935,250 pounds/$17,805,750, and the SQ report says $18m. | Say “about $18m” only when attached to the reporting source; use exact court figures in the ledger. |
| “The theft was discovered on 24 August 2012.” | The complaint date is 24 August; official/SCC summaries say the robbery was discovered in July during a routine inventory. | Keep discovery/inventory and complaint as separate markers until the trial record resolves the sequence. |
| “10,000 barrels were stolen.” | Early reporting used a rounded estimate; the court record uses 9,571 barrels. | Use 9,571 in the central case narrative; label 10,000 as early shorthand if needed. |
| “Vallières was the mastermind/ringleader.” | The Supreme Court calls him one of the major players and describes Caron as in charge of warehouse operations; Canadian reporting uses “mastermind,” while the exact legal counts and role allocation belong to the lower-court judgments. | Use the role distinction in S1; avoid an unqualified superlative unless the French trial reasons support it. |
| “Everyone arrested was part of the theft.” | SQ and Radio-Canada records describe accused, arrested or sought people; investigation status is not a conviction. | Name only documented convictions/pleas after checking the relevant judgment; otherwise omit names or retain status language. |
| “Two-thirds of the syrup was recovered.” | Federation says about two-thirds apparently traced; SQ says 67% recovered; these may describe different dates or meanings. | Attribute the percentage and distinguish traced, seized, recovered and returned. |
| “There was a break-in.” | The judicial reconstruction emphasizes access to a rented warehouse, extraction logistics and replacement; it does not describe a forced-entry scene in the cited passages. | Say “the reserve was breached through sustained access and extraction” unless a trial exhibit proves forced entry. |
| “The black market explains the motive.” | The Court records unlawful purchasing/resale and the Crown’s case, but motive is not a single sentence in the final judgment. | Describe the market route and the regulatory context; label motive as interpretation unless the trial reasons are checked. |
| “The 2020 fine was the final sentence.” | The Quebec Court of Appeal reduced the fine; the 2022 Supreme Court allowed the Crown appeal and restored $9,171,397.57. | Use the Supreme Court’s 2022 disposition as final for this appeal. |
| “The fine was $9.4m.” | Canadian reporting rounds the 2017 amount; the final SCC amount is $9,171,397.57 after restitution. | Use exact final amount in legal notes and “more than $9m” in plain-language summary. |
| “The reserve is a government stockpile.” | Producer records describe a Federation-managed reserve supported by Quebec producers; the legal/regulatory relationship is more specific than state ownership. | Say “Federation-managed strategic reserve” unless a verified public-law source supports another formulation. |

## Negative evidence and absent records

- No written warehouse access log, audit worksheet, surveillance recording, police exhibit inventory, insurance file or complete producer inventory was located in this pass.
- No complete English translation of the Quebec Superior Court and Quebec Court of Appeal reasons was located through the accessible sources.
- The SCC reasons give a concise role reconstruction but do not list every search, witness, wiretap, bank record or co-accused disposition.
- The Federation’s releases are useful producer records but are advocacy/communications documents and changed their estimate as the audit progressed.

## Counterevidence handling

The safe documentary frame is not “one number and one mastermind.” It is: a reserve system existed for supply and price stability; a year-long extraction was later reconstructed in court; early public figures shifted as the inventory and investigation matured; Vallières’s convictions and final fine are settled judicial facts; the status of other named people must be checked individually.

Any script sentence that collapses a dated estimate, an allegation, a conviction and a later legal interpretation into one assertion should be rejected until rewritten.
