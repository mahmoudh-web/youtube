## Wording gate

Current wording that may advance only after human verification:

> The “Toledo War” was a boundary crisis, not a conventional campaign. Ohio and Michigan Territory backed rival readings of Great Lakes boundary language with arrests, surveys and militia mobilisation. A Michigan deputy sheriff, Joseph Wood, was reportedly stabbed by Ohio partisan Two Stickney—the best-supported injury, though the date and underlying record still need checking. Congress later made Michigan’s statehood conditional on a new boundary: Ohio kept the disputed strip, while Michigan entered the Union with most of the present Upper Peninsula. “Compensation” is a later shorthand for that settlement, not the word used by the federal statute.

This wording intentionally omits a fixed militia total, a definitive “sole casualty” statement, a cash-compensation claim, a simple winner, and an exact Upper Peninsula fraction.
