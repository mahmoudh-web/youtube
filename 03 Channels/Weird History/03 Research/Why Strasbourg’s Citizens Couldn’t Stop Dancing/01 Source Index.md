## Research question

What can be established from the surviving Strasbourg records and chronicles about the 1518 dancing episode, and which claims about scale, deaths, and cause are later interpretation rather than secure fact?

## Evidence rules

- **A — primary record:** surviving municipal or ecclesiastical record, or a clearly identified archival item.
- **B — near-contemporary / critical transcription:** a chronicle or later copy close to the event, with provenance and limitations stated.
- **C — later chronicle:** useful for reception and remembered sequence, not equivalent to a 1518 eyewitness record.
- **D — modern scholarship:** source criticism, medical history, or historiography; never substitute for the underlying record where a claim is disputed.
- A number is not safe merely because it appears in several later chronicles. Track the earliest source and the date of the witness.

## Source-handling rules

1. Anchor the episode in S3–S5; use S7–S10 to show how the story expanded in transmission.
2. Treat **14 July**, **34**, **400**, **15 per day**, and **deaths** as source-dependent claims, not a single verified dataset.
3. “Frau Troffea” is not a secure contemporary identity. The 25 July letter says only that the episode began with a woman; the name is associated with later Paracelsian tradition.
4. The 1518 physicians' “natural illness,” astral conjunction, heat and hot blood are historical explanations, not modern diagnoses.
5. Never state ergotism, mass psychogenic illness, conversion disorder, hysteria, or trance as the solved cause.
