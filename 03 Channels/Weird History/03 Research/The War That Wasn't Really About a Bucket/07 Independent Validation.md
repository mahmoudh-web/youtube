## Executive verdict

**FAIL — material corrections are required before the pack is safe to send to human verification.**

The pack’s central conclusion is substantially sound: the evidence supports a serious Bologna–Modena conflict and does not establish that a bucket caused it. Its strongest cautionary language should be retained. However, the critical Griffoni text was not actually represented in the pack’s evidence table, and it materially changes the chronology and framing that a narrator would otherwise inherit:

1. Griffoni’s critical edition dates the battle **16 November 1325**, while the Modena heritage page gives **15 November**. The discrepancy must be exposed, not presented as a settled exact date.
2. Griffoni records the pre-battle conflict, the treasonous capture of Monteveglio, and Bologna’s mobilisation against Modena; the account contains no bucket. This is stronger positive counterevidence than the pack currently states.
3. The source pack’s exact army/casualty caution is directionally correct, but the critical text does contain chronicler-reported figures: more than 1,000 Bolognese dead, more than 1,000 captured, about 800 imprisoned at Modena, and 26 notable prisoners. These must be labelled as a retrospective chronicle report, not suppressed or repeated as verified totals.
4. The poem’s publication history needs correction/precision: the 1622 Paris princeps is the standard dating used by the digitised text, while Treccani records printing completed in March 1621, a 1624 revised edition, and a 1630 last edition controlled by Tassoni. “First published in 1622” is usable only with that bibliographic caveat.

This validation does not approve the episode, write a script, or permit movement beyond the human In Review gate.

## Timeline cross-check

- **13th–early 14th century rivalry:** supported by S5, PDF pp. 68–70, including border, fortification, water-control, and factional context. S6 was not independently accessible at its supplied URL; do not rely on it alone.
- **Fossalta 1249 versus Zappolino 1325:** supported by S8, lines 105–10, and Treccani S10b, lines 55–56. Keep them separate.
- **Pre-November 1325 context:** strengthened by Griffoni S2, pp. 36–37: the Bolognese expedition, the capture of Monteveglio by treason, and the attempt to recover it are recorded before the battle. The “malicious plot” is Griffoni’s attributed report, not independently established motive.
- **Battle date:** unresolved between Griffoni’s 16 November and the Modena heritage page’s 15 November. The timeline must not call 15 November primary-level secure.
- **Pursuit and red *pallium*:** present in S1’s transmitted Modena account, but not in the same form in Griffoni. Attribute and keep the witnesses separate.
- **Bucket trophy:** supported only as later tradition/institutional memory; no checked 1325 primary text names the bucket.
- **1326 peace:** secondary synthesis only; treaty text and terms remain a gap.
- **c. 1411 Griffoni compilation:** supported by Treccani S2a, lines 45–50.
- **Tassoni composition/publication:** revise “1614–1617” to “composition began late 1614, with work continuing into early 1618”; distinguish the 1621 Paris printing/1622 conventional date, the 1624 revision, and 1630 last author-controlled edition. ## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong sources

- **Griffoni critical edition (S2):** the decisive checked source for the Bolognese account. It is retrospective and partisan/limited, but its exact passage supplies the missing chronology, Monteveglio context, casualties/captives as reported, and absence of a bucket. The Internet Archive text is OCR; a human should compare pp. 36–37 with page images before quoting Latin.
- **Modena chronicle edition (S1):** useful transmitted Modenese-side evidence for the 1325 defeat and pursuit, but its note incorporates Corio and explicitly compresses/confuses chronology. It is not an eyewitness report.
- **Tassoni text (S7):** direct literary evidence for the later framing and the *fama* hedge; it is not evidence of 1325 causation.
- **Treccani biographies (S2a/S10b):** authoritative scholarly provenance and bibliographic controls, but not substitutes for the chronicle or a critical Tassoni edition.

### Useful but limited sources

- **Carbonara thesis (S5):** good secondary synthesis for border history and the trophy/peace framing, but the relevant claims depend on cited older works and Besomi; check those works before treating them as primary fact.
- **Ecdotica (S9):** peer-reviewed textual-scholarship context for edition history; it does not independently establish what happened in 1325.
- **Modena and Bologna institutional pages (S3/S4/S11):** valuable for current custody, civic memory, and modern framing; insufficient alone for medieval causation, exact date, object identity, or treaty terms.
- **ISIME catalogue (S12):** confirms bibliographic control for the Bologna chronicle corpus, but a catalogue record is not evidence for the Zappolino passage.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory, or missing-source claims

1. **Overstated exact date:** 15 November is not reconciled with Griffoni’s 16 November. This is the principal correction.
2. **Understated primary counterevidence:** the critical Griffoni passage explicitly supplies a castle/political campaign context and reports no bucket; the pack currently leans too heavily on silence and modern institutional summary.
3. **Potentially misleading publication history:** “first published 1622” needs the 1621/1622 printing convention and later 1624/1630 editions stated clearly.
4. **Unsupported treaty precision:** the 1326 peace remains a secondary-synthesis claim until the diplomatic record is checked.
5. **Unverified material figures:** chronicle numbers are evidence of what a chronicler reported, not validated troop, death, or prisoner totals.
6. **Object identity gap:** “original” is an institutional custody description, not proof that the current object is the 1325 bucket.
7. **Missing direct control for S6:** the Cambridge claim is not independently verified from the supplied scan.
