## Ledger conventions

Confidence measures how safely a claim can be stated after source comparison. Every exact number or vivid anecdote needs its own source and caveat. “Later legend” means a real event has been simplified, personified or given a cleaner outcome than the records support.

## Not yet claimable

- “Australia declared war on emus” as a formal legal or military act.
- “The Australian Army lost a war” as an uncontested historical fact.
- “986 emus were killed,” “9,860 rounds were fired,” or any other exact total without the underlying Defence report and count method.
- “No soldiers were wounded” or “the army suffered zero casualties” without a complete personnel/medical record.
- “20,000 emus” as a verified population count; treat it as a contemporary estimate until sourced.
- The “medals should go to the emus” line as exact Hansard wording until the complete debate is verified.
- A claim that the AWM holds or displays a specific emu-war object unless an accession record is located.
