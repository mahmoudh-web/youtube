## Stop condition

This pack is safe for independent human verification and is not ready for narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC, or publication. Stop until the human verifier records dispositions for the items below.

## Open historical questions

1. **Radar and trigger:** What did the original Aircraft Warning Service/radar plot, operator log, and command-room record say about the reported contact about 120 miles offshore? Was the first visible object the same object later described as a balloon?
2. **Timing:** Reconcile the official history’s first shot shortly after 3 a.m. with Droke’s recollection of a meteorological balloon at about 1 a.m. Determine whether two balloons or two stages are involved.
3. **Firing total and duration:** Reconcile “about 1,400” in the official Army history with Stimson’s reported 1,430. Verify the start/stop times and ammunition type in 37th Coast Artillery Brigade records.
4. **Object count:** Keep one-to-five, fifteen, three, and other witness counts source-attributed. Do not select one number without a record-based reason.
5. **Casualties and damage:** Reconcile newspaper, police, coroner, fire-department, and military records. Distinguish deaths, injuries, shell fragments, spent ordnance, traffic accidents, heart attacks, and property damage. The “five casualties” figure in S10 is not independently verified here.
6. **Army/Navy conclusions:** Obtain the underlying reports that the 1964 Army history cites, and determine whether “one to five” was an initial report, a later estimate, or a final conclusion.
7. **Marshall memorandum:** Retrieve and inspect the 26 February 1942 Chief of Staff memorandum to Roosevelt cited as OCS 21347-86. Treat newspaper accounts of it as secondary reporting until the item is checked.
8. **Photograph:** Inspect the original Times negative/print, publication workflow, and any alteration notes. Confirm whether the apparent central object is a shell burst, glare, retouching artifact, or something else; do not infer identity from the image alone.

## Fact, allegation, interpretation, uncertainty

- **Fact:** blackout, searchlights, anti-aircraft fire, no bombs reported, no aircraft shot down, and an all-clear.
- **Official statement:** WDC’s conflicting-reports release; Knox’s false-alarm statement; Stimson’s fifteen-aircraft report; later Army history’s one-to-five summary.
- **Allegation/speculation:** enemy agents operating commercial planes, Japanese signaling, or a Japanese submarine-launched aircraft. Attribute and do not repeat as fact.
- **Interpretation:** weather balloon plus war nerves; shell bursts and searchlights multiplying perceived targets; later UFO framing.
- **Uncertainty:** exact radar contact, first trigger, total objects, exact casualties, and image content.

## Ethical and legal sensitivity

- The episode sits immediately after Pearl Harbor, the Ellwood shelling, and Executive Order 9066. Explain that fear and anti-Japanese racism formed part of the context without implying that Japanese Americans caused or deserved suspicion. The National Archives documents EO 9066 as authorising military exclusion areas; it does not establish that the barrage caused the order (S8).
- “Enemy agents” was a wartime theory reported by Stimson, not a proven accusation. Do not name private people or ethnic communities as conspirators without primary evidence and editorial necessity.
- If discussing arrests, signaling reports, or Japanese-American surveillance, use individual-level records and careful historical framing; otherwise omit them from narration rather than generalising.
- Civilian deaths and injuries require non-gratuitous, source-specific wording. Do not turn uncertain totals into spectacle or imply that every death was directly caused by anti-aircraft fire.
- The topic concerns historical public figures and institutions, but false claims about covert attacks, ethnic communities, or living descendants can still create reputational and ethical risk. Preserve attribution and uncertainty.

## Rights and production cautions

- The LOC newspaper scan, Times photograph, Space Force PDF photographs, and military-journal scan each have separate provenance and reuse conditions.
- A public archive URL is not a production licence. Record rights status, repository credit, crop/alteration history, and caption verification for every asset.
- Any reconstructed map, radar screen, or balloon diagram must be labelled as conceptual and must not resemble an archival record closely enough to imply that it is one.
- No image from UFO sites, “leaked memo” pages, or unsourced social-media posts is admissible evidence or automatically safe visual material.
