## Research question and promise

Which parts of the Borneo cat-drop story are documented, which belong to later retelling, and did DDT spraying actually cause the cat deaths and rat crisis that supposedly required the airdrop? The evidence must keep three linked but separate questions apart: the documented malaria campaign, the documented or reported delivery of cats to Bario, and the unverified ecological chain connecting them.

## Source-quality key

- **A** — primary government, institutional, contemporaneous newspaper or scientific record.
- **B** — official retrospective or a named archival reproduction of a primary record.
- **C** — peer-reviewed historical synthesis or scholarly interpretation.
- **D** — later reporting or popular retelling, used only to locate records or document reception.

## Entities and voices

- **WHO Sarawak Malaria Pilot Project:** institutional programme documented by S1 and S2; do not make it the sole actor in the 1960 delivery without a primary operational record.
- **Royal Air Force / No. 48 Squadron:** delivery actor in the March 1960 ORB account (S5), with the original page still requiring inspection.
- **Bario and the Kelabit Highlands:** the reported drop location. The Kelabit community should be treated as people affected by the intervention and recipients of aid, not as a comic backdrop.
- **Tom Harrisson:** Sarawak Museum curator and source of a 1965 personal account as traced by S4. His account is important evidence of story formation, not automatically proof of every causal detail.
- **Francois LaChance and Julian de Zulueta:** WHO authors of the 1956 malaria-control report (S1).
- **Malcolm McSporran/MacSporran and John Seal:** names associated with later accounts of the request and drop; spelling, role and firsthand status require primary-record checking.

## Research-use opportunities and gaps

The useful archival leads are the original No. 48 Squadron March 1960 ORB page, the four NLB newspaper scans, Sarawak’s DOL/12/1 malaria files, the full 1959 North Borneo annual-report scan and Harrisson’s 1965 text/correspondence. These are research sources, not a visual brief. Before any later image use, verify provenance, copyright/licence and whether an image depicts Bario, Sarawak, Sabah or an unrelated animal drop.

The central gap is not whether cats were ever dropped by RAF aircraft: the ORB account and contemporary newspaper trail make a single Bario delivery highly plausible. The gap is the causal chain. No source located here proves that Sarawak cats died from DDT biomagnification, that the Bario rats were created by the malaria campaign, that WHO ordered the airdrop, or that 14,000 cats were involved.
