## Uncertainty map

### High-confidence record statements

- A formal case was opened on 26 February 1959.
- The tent was found without bodies and with substantial equipment/clothing inside.
- The closure record reports cuts from inside, 8–9 tracks, and no recorded traces of struggle or other people.
- Bodies were recovered near the forest-edge fire, along the slope, and in a ravine near the cedar.
- Three ravine victims had major traumatic injuries in the translated autopsy/closure records.
- The 1959 radiation report records beta contamination on selected clothing and natural-level analysed tissue.
- A 2020 prosecutor review publicly selected an avalanche/slab-plus-weather explanation.
- A peer-reviewed 2021 paper models a delayed slab mechanism under specified assumptions.

### Medium-confidence or source-attributed interpretations

- The group left because of an immediate snow threat.
- The major ravine injuries came from later snow collapse.
- The group could not find the tent because of local visibility and wind.
- Clothing contamination came from earlier visits to the East Ural radioactive trail.
- A small slab could leave no obvious trace after 26 days.

These may be reasonable, but they depend on a later reconstruction, translated records, or model assumptions. Keep the source attached in every future script draft.

### Open or low-confidence claims

- Exact trigger, time of exit and time of death.
- Exact slope angle at the tent and above it.
- Exact weather and visibility at the tent; the nearest located station is remote and not a direct site measurement.
- Whether anyone was injured at the tent, in the ravine, or during a fall.
- Whether the four ravine victims were moved by living companions, snow, or both.
- Why the group moved downhill and whether that was deliberate shelter-seeking, disorientation, or a response to a specific threat.
- Why some clothing and footwear were distributed between bodies.
- The source and significance of the beta contamination.
- Whether the 1959 translation preserves all forensic and procedural nuance.

4. Obtain the full 2018–20 prosecutor review, expert reports, raw weather inputs, visibility method and legal disposition. The accessible material is a newspaper account and contemporaneous wire report.
5. Audit Gaume–Puzrin’s model inputs, sensitivity ranges and supplementary material. Mark every historical input inherited from another researcher rather than directly from the file.
6. Ask a forensic pathologist or medically qualified reviewer to check the translated injury terms and the wording “violent death.”

8. Check copyright/licence status for scans, photographs, maps and the paper’s figures before any visual reuse. A research URL is not a production licence.

## Legal and ethical sensitivity

### Deceased people and surviving families

The victims and their families deserve respectful treatment. Do not use autopsy photographs, exposed remains, or sensational thumbnails. Avoid gratuitous descriptions of missing soft tissue, eyes or tongue; those details are not necessary to answer the central question.

### Historical allegations and living people

Do not state or imply that named investigators, prosecutors, Mansi people, Soviet personnel, surviving researchers or living officials committed murder, fabricated evidence or concealed a weapon test without a primary source and legal review. Attribute criticism to the source and distinguish an error claim from proven misconduct.

### Forensic and legal wording

“Violent death” is a translated forensic conclusion about traumatic death, not a finding that a person was attacked. “Overwhelming force” is the translated 1959 closure wording, not a legal finding that an avalanche occurred. “Official conclusion” should be dated and attributed to the 2020 prosecutor review; it is not a court judgment.

### Conspiracy amplification

The case’s association with radiation, Soviet secrecy, rockets and Mansi geography creates predictable misinformation and stereotyping risk. Extraordinary claims should appear only as clearly labelled hypotheses with a primary-source test and a result. If no evidence is found, the pack should say “not established” rather than repeat a vivid allegation in the title or thumbnail.

### Translation and source-host risk

The main archive host preserves useful scans and translations but is not the State Archive of the Sverdlovsk Region. Web translations can contain awkward phrasing, altered dates or terminology. Human validation must quote the Russian original where wording changes the causal or legal meaning.

## Research stop condition

This pack is complete for research handoff but not ready for narration. Work must stop until an independent validator signs off the source chain, translation of material passages, geometry/weather assumptions, injury wording, radiation interpretation, copyright position and conclusion wording. No script, title/thumbnail package, generated visual or edit decision should be derived from an unvalidated claim.
