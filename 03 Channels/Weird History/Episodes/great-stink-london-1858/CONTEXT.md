## Purpose

This folder holds the source-led research pack for the provisional episode “The Great Stink That Forced London to Rebuild.” It investigates what MPs, engineers and public-health records document about the 1858 Thames crisis, and how that crisis translated into the sewer reforms associated with Joseph Bazalgette. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification stop gate and provisional conclusion.
- Separate the observed smell, Victorian sanitary arguments, parliamentary debate, engineering design, statutory change and later construction timeline.
- Distinguish established fact, attributed political or technical position, interpretation and uncertainty; do not turn the Great Stink into a single-cause origin story.
- Stop for explicit human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `great-stink-london-1858` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat the Great Stink as a political and administrative accelerator for a sewer project already developing through Chadwick’s 1842 report, the 1847–1855 sewer institutions and Bazalgette’s pre-1858 engineering work.
- Treat the 2 August 1858 Act as an enabling and financing measure that changed authority, borrowing and approval constraints; it did not complete the sewer system in eighteen days.
- Keep parliamentary speeches, Bazalgette’s retrospective account and period medical claims attributed. Do not state that smell caused cholera or that Parliament acted only because its own members were uncomfortable.
- Preserve the six-note Research structure: `01 Source Index.md`, `02 Timeline.md`, `03 Claim Ledger.md`, `04 Counterevidence.md`, `05 Uncertainty and Human Verification.md`, and `06 Conclusion.md`.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Initial source-pack access dates are 2026-09-13; the source index received a research/visual-controls review on 2026-09-14.
- The exact first use and duration of “Great Stink,” the predecessor-commission count, the full MCS/MBW record chain, the engineering chronology and some outfall details remain human-verification items.
- Do not convert Victorian miasma language into modern disease causation or modern wastewater-treatment claims.
- No archival scan, newspaper page, map, photograph, plan, quotation or reconstruction is an approved production asset. Rights, provenance, dates and captions require separate human clearance.
- No script, title/thumbnail package, visual brief, edit plan or publication decision is authorized by this pack.
