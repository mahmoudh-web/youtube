## Purpose

This folder holds the source-led research pack for the provisional episode “Australia’s Cane Toad Decision: The Pest Control That Backfired.” It examines the 1935 Queensland release of *Bufo marinus* as a biological-control measure against sugar-cane beetles, the evidence available at the time, the warnings and federal intervention that followed, and the later ecological outcome. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, dated evidence sequence, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/legal-sensitivity note and provisional conclusion.
- Reconstruct the decision chain without collapsing the Bureau of Sugar Experiment Stations, the Queensland Department of Agriculture and Stock, Queensland ministers, the Commonwealth Health Department and Prime Minister Joseph Lyons into one actor.
- Separate established fact, contemporaneous claim, later attribution, interpretation and uncertainty.
- Keep the 1935 evidence question precise: overseas observations supported trying the toad, but the located record does not show an adequate Australian pre-release test of target-beetle effectiveness or environmental risk.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `cane-toad-decision-1935` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat Reginald William Mungomery and the Bureau of Sugar Experiment Stations as the documented operational actors in importing and breeding the first colony. Do not call Mungomery the sole authoriser without a primary administrative record.
- Treat Arthur Bell’s 1932 Puerto Rico report and Mungomery’s 1935 Queensland Agricultural Journal article as evidence of the pro-introduction case, not proof that the toad would control Queensland beetles.
- Treat the federal release ban, the 1936 lobbying to reverse it, and later release locations as separate phases. The ban shows that warnings generated an institutional response; it did not end the policy.
- Use current ecological findings to describe later consequences, but do not project later knowledge backward as if it was known in 1935.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access dates in this pack are 2026-09-14.
- The scientific name used in the period sources is *Bufo marinus*; current sources may use *Rhinella marina*. Preserve the period name in quotations and explain the taxonomy if needed.
- “Released in 1935” is secure. Exact counts and dates vary between later summaries: the pack records 100 or 101 imported animals, a June arrival and August releases with a caveat rather than silently choosing one number.
- The 1935 journal records that Queensland greyback beetles did not regularly return to the ground like Puerto Rican beetles; this is a period-specific applicability warning, not proof that every official understood the eventual risk.
- “Ignored warnings” is too compressed. The record supports: warnings and objections were raised; the Commonwealth imposed a ban; Queensland industry and officials sought reversal; and the ban was lifted in 1936.
- The ecological harm is well documented in later science, but the pack does not claim that one named person intended harm, acted negligently, or could have predicted every later effect.
