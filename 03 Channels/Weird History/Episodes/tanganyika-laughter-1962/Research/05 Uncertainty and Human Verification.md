## Research stop condition

This is an evidence pack, not narration or a production brief. No title, thumbnail, script, visual treatment, reenactment, audio treatment or publication work should begin until a human editor verifies the original medical pages, terminology, place names, methods, source chain and dignity boundaries below.

## Legal, ethical and editorial sensitivity

- **People and mental-health dignity:** The affected pupils and villagers were people experiencing distress, not comic characters or “infected laughers.” Do not imply feigning, low intelligence, primitive psychology or conscious manipulation.
- **Colonial/postcolonial framing:** Do not turn a Tanganyikan community into a laboratory or use psychiatric language as neutral proof of what the community “really” was. Explain that the surviving medical account was produced through particular institutional and linguistic relationships.
- **Race and culture:** “Susceptible population,” “culturally determined disease,” “African mind,” “westernization” and “detribalization” must remain attributed to the historical source or later scholar. Never use them as narrator voice without critical context.
- **Local voice and translation:** Preserve the fact that local terms and fears were recorded, while acknowledging that the investigators did not obtain relevant information from the initially affected pupils. A Tanzania/Haya language specialist should review any translation, pronunciation or cultural explanation.
- **Medical uncertainty:** A normal result in the reported tests is not proof that the attacks were imaginary or that every organic cause was impossible. “No cause identified in this investigation” is safer than “doctors proved it was psychological.”
- **Minors and privacy:** The original subjects were mostly schoolchildren, and some family ages are listed. Do not identify, reconstruct or dramatise individual pupils/family members without archival and ethical review.
- **No graphic or comic imagery:** Avoid laughing-child stock footage, staged seizures, exaggerated “madness,” medical restraint imagery and sound design that makes distress entertaining.
- **Archival rights:** The existence of a journal page, map, newspaper or audio interview is not a reuse licence. Rights, provenance, scan quality and captions must be cleared separately.

## Human verification checklist

1. Inspect the original Rankin–Philip article (pp. 167–170), including the map, footnotes, sample descriptions, dates, wording and references; correct this pack against the scan.
2. Retrieve the complete Kagwa article and record which passages concern Bukoba versus Kigezi/Mbale and which are general theory.
3. Locate contemporary Tanganyika health, education, mission and district records for Kashasha, Nshamba, Ramashenye and Kanyangereka; document closures, attendance and any official correspondence.
4. Ask a Tanzania/Haya historian or language specialist to review place names, community terminology, translations and the framing of Bahaya/Haya identity.
5. Ask a medical historian or clinician familiar with functional neurological symptoms to review the distinction between historical “mass hysteria,” MPI and modern diagnostic language; retain the non-diagnostic boundary.
6. Trace every later figure—1,000 people, 14 schools, 18 months, 2.5 years, six months and any Uganda extension—to an identifiable source before deciding whether it can appear.
7. If using oral history, record consent, interview date, language, translator, provenance, whether the speaker witnessed events and whether anonymisation is required.
8. Re-run the claim ledger after every factual change; no material claim advances to narration without a source locator and caveat.

Until these checks are complete, the pack remains **Research only** and is not ready for scriptwriting, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.
