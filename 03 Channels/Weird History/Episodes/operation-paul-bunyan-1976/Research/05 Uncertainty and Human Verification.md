## Stop condition

Research is sufficient for an evidence-bounded answer but not for final narration or production. Stop here until a human reviewer checks the primary records, approves the classifications and wording in the Claim Ledger, and decides which disputed details are necessary. This pack creates no script, creative treatment, thumbnail, shot list, visual asset, edit plan or publication material.

## Unresolved factual questions

1. What was the complete UNC work-party composition on 18 August? Reconcile the 5 laborers + 3 officers + 7 security description with the Korean archival summary of 5 laborers and 11 UNC/ROK soldiers.
2. What were the exact JSA rules governing pruning, tree removal, guard-post positions and firearms on 18 August? Read the Armistice annexes and the relevant MAC/JSA supplements.
3. What is the authoritative sequence and timing of the attack, including first KPA arrival, reinforcement, “kill” command, deaths, evacuation and reaction-force arrival? Retrieve the complete incident report and medical/command logs.
4. Were four U.S. and five South Korean personnel the complete nine wounded, and what injuries were recorded? Avoid severity language until medical records are checked.
5. Was the attack centrally planned, locally initiated, or a planned confrontation that became lethal? Compare U.S. intelligence assessments, KPA orders/records if available, and later claims; do not infer from the fact that reinforcements arrived.
6. What was Operation Paul Bunyan’s complete order of battle? Reconcile the 813-person retrospective total, the 110-person cutting task force, the 300-person QRF and the named Task Force Brady/Vierra components.
7. Which aircraft actually flew overhead during cutting, which were on standby, and what did the *Midway* group do? Check SAC, Pacific Command, Navy and Air Force logs.
8. Did the cutting take 42, 45 or another number of minutes? Resolve S9’s “40-plus,” S11’s 45 and S12’s 42 against the operation log.
9. Was the three-minute notification symbolic, operationally required, or a later recollection? Verify the notice record and Korean/North Korean receipt.
10. What exactly did Kim Il-sung’s message say in Korean, and is “regret” or “apology” the defensible translation? Check the original FBIS and MAC records with a bilingual reviewer.
11. Did North Korea’s non-interference result from intimidation, prior diplomatic signaling, command restraint, Chinese/Soviet pressure, or some combination? Treat all causal explanations as interpretation unless a contemporaneous record supports them.
12. What did the 6 September JSA agreement legally change, and which guard posts were removed? Read the full U.N. document, maps and implementation record.
13. What did the classified/redacted U.S. contingency plans contain? Do not reconstruct omitted details from secondary sources as if declassified.
14. Is the Kim Jong-il responsibility claim supported by the full 1992 broadcast and independent records? Until then, label it allegation and keep it out of any essential factual spine.

## Required primary-source checks

- **Ford Presidential Library / NARA:** inspect the complete Box 10 “Korea — Operation Paul Bunyan” packet, especially scan pages 3–6, 62 and 112, plus any related opened documents and withdrawal sheets.
- **U.S. military records:** retrieve the 2nd Infantry Division/JSA battalion operation order, after-action report, Task Force Brady/Vierra records, medical reports, SAC sortie records, Navy *Midway* logs and Pacific Command messages.
- **U.N. and MAC records:** read the 18 August protest, 21 August meeting/message, 25 August discussions and 6 September supplement in full; check the operative wording and maps.
- **Korean records:** obtain the National Archives of Korea source chain, contemporary ROK Ministry of National Defense reports and Korean-language transliterations of people, units, dates and locations.
- **North Korean/FBIS records:** compare the 18, 21 and 23 August broadcasts with the translated reproductions in S15; mark propaganda framing rather than silently adopting it.
- **Casualty records:** confirm deaths and injuries from military personnel/medical records. Do not use graphic descriptions from later retellings without necessity.
- **Legal context:** have a qualified reviewer explain the JSA and Armistice rules without treating the 1976 incident as a conventional war or assuming later legal frameworks apply retrospectively.

## Language and naming checks

- Use “Korean People’s Army” or “KPA” for the North Korean force and “Republic of Korea” or “ROK” for South Korean institutions after first expansion.
- Keep “United Nations Command” distinct from the U.S. Army and from the South Korean military; the three worked together but were not interchangeable.
- Verify whether Bonifas and Barrett were styled captain/first lieutenant at the time; some later sources use posthumous or retrospective rank forms.
- Use “poplar tree” unless a primary record establishes the botanical identification and height; 40-foot and 100-foot descriptions vary.
- Say “message expressing regret” rather than “apology” unless the bilingual record supports the stronger word.
- Never translate “backed down,” “provocation,” “murder,” “self-defence” or “retaliation” as neutral descriptions when they originate in a national account.

## Legal, ethical and editorial flags

- Two people were killed and others wounded. Avoid comic framing, graphic imagery or a joke built around the tree.
- The incident was a serious military crisis in a nuclear-armed Cold War setting. Do not turn “heavily armed” into celebratory militarism.
- Alleged responsibility by named North Korean leaders requires a high evidence threshold and clear allegation labeling.
- “Murder,” “atrocity,” “provocation” and “self-defence” are source-position terms; identify whose language is being used.
- Do not claim the mission “prevented war” or that a single leader’s decision caused de-escalation without direct evidence.
- Do not reproduce classified/redacted details inferred from later summaries; mark the gap.
- Any future use of archival footage, photographs or broadcast recordings needs separate provenance, rights and sensitivity review.

## Research stop statement

The evidence supports a provisional explanation of coercive signaling plus operational restraint, with diplomatic follow-through. It does not yet support a settled troop total, duration, command-causation story, apology wording, or claim that the operation alone prevented war. Human verification is the required next gate.
