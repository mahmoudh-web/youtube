## Research question

How did the Four Pests campaign turn sparrows into a state target, what evidence prompted the 1960 reversal, and what can responsibly be attributed to sparrow killing amid the wider Great Leap Forward famine?

## Working answer and scope

The located record supports a narrow answer: the Chinese Communist Party and state made sparrows one of the four official targets, mobilised mass killing through central instructions and local campaigns, and later removed sparrows from the target list after Chinese Academy of Sciences research stressed their seasonal and regional ecological role. Contemporary official reports document the campaign’s scale and rhetoric, but their totals are incomplete and politically framed. The ecological backlash is plausible and supported by newer quantitative work, yet the famine was produced by interacting factors—production collapse, procurement and distribution institutions, mobilisation and political constraints, weather, and possibly pest pressure. This pack does not support “sparrows caused the famine.”

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary/contemporary:** official directive, contemporary newspaper, archival file description or contemporaneous statistical report.
- **B — authoritative institutional retrospective:** Chinese Academy of Sciences, party-history or official historical resolution material that identifies underlying records.
- **C — peer-reviewed academic:** published quantitative or historical research; methods and scope are recorded.
- **D — working paper / access mirror:** useful for current hypotheses, but not equivalent to peer-reviewed consensus.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use official newspaper figures as evidence of what authorities claimed, mobilised and celebrated—not as audited totals.
2. Preserve the difference between a campaign goal (“eliminate sparrows”), a local report (“we killed X”) and an independently estimated population effect.
3. Attribute the policy reversal to the 1959–60 scientific and political record; do not claim that the campaign ended immediately after the first complaints.
4. Keep famine causation multi-factor in every summary: sparrow-related pest pressure is one hypothesis within a wider Great Leap Forward crisis.
5. Use “may have contributed,” “the study estimates,” or “the source reports” where the evidence is not settled.

## Visual opportunities (research leads, not cleared assets)

- Scans of the 1956 People’s Daily article and the 1958 central directive can show how the policy was worded. Verify scan provenance, translation and reproduction rights.
- The 19–21 April 1958 Beijing campaign has contemporary newspaper photographs, captions and a reported newsreel. Use only after checking the archive, image rights and whether the image depicts dead birds or identifiable people.
- A restrained map or data graphic could compare documented local campaigns with the high-sparrow-suitability areas used by Frank et al.; this would be an analytical visual, not a claim that every mapped county experienced identical effects.
- A timeline graphic can show the lag between the 1958 directive, late-1959 CAS reassessment and March/April 1960 policy reversal.
- Avoid using famine suffering or mass-killing photographs as a thumbnail hook. Any human suffering image requires editorial necessity, rights and sensitivity review.
