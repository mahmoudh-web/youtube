## Executive verdict

**FAIL for narration, production or human approval in the current form; safe to send to human verification only as a flagged research pack.**

The pack is unusually careful about separating the 1959 record, the later prosecutor reconstruction and the Gaume–Puzrin model. Most core scene, chronology and forensic claims are supported by the cited pages. The central evidence-bounded conclusion is sound. It cannot yet pass the human gate because:

1. the Russian scan wording has not been independently certified by a qualified Russian-speaking legal/forensic reviewer;
2. the English “overwhelming force” wording is a consequential translation choice: the Russian closure reads `стихийная сила` (natural/elemental force), not the English phrase as an original quotation;
3. search, first-body and ravine-recovery dates conflict between the closure resolution, expedition summary and later reporting;
4. the 2020 prosecutor source chain is a Komsomolskaya Pravda republication and Interfax report, not the underlying review file, expert reports or raw inputs;
5. the geometry and weather inputs remain modelled, indirect or disputed; and
6. usage rights are not cleared for the archive scans, photographs, maps, newspaper material or third-party figures.

The pack may proceed to a human fact-checking decision with these blockers visible. It must remain in review and must not be treated as approved.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Primary 1959 record and translations

- **S1, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files` — usable navigation/provenance, not a state-archive citation by itself.** The index says the Foundation received an official copy from the Sverdlovsk State Archive in 2012 and that the case was transferred there in 1974. This is a provenance statement made by the host. Retain “archive-hosted copy of material said to derive from the state archive,” not “the state archive says” unless the archive itself is consulted.
- **S2, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-32-35` — useful case-file summary, but not a contemporaneous diary.** The page explicitly says the chronology was compiled from diaries, maps, witness testimony, developed film and belongings. Its English is awkward and its 18–20° passage conflicts with S3’s 30°.
- **S3, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-2` — direct scene protocol transcription.** The Russian page confirms the 30° wording, 300 m from the summit, eight pairs of skis, nine backpacks and no bodies in the tent. The page is a host transcription linked to scans, not an authenticated archival edition.
- **S4, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-1?rbid=17743` — direct opening resolution transcription.** It supports the 26 February opening date and investigative scope, not a cause of death.
- **S5, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-384-387?rbid=17743` and Russian `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-384-387-ru?rbid=17747` — strongest source for what the 1959 closure says.** The Russian page confirms the relevant dates, quantities, track count, injury summary and `стихийная сила`. The translation must not be treated as the original wording.
- **S6, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-341-343?rbid=17743` — direct ravine scene protocol transcription.** It is dated 6 May and describes four bodies approximately 50 m from the cedar, in water, under 2–2.5 m of snow. It does not by itself establish the 4 May recovery event date; that comes from S5.
- **S7–S9, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-349-351?rbid=17743`, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-352-354?rbid=17743`, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-355-357?rbid=17743` and corresponding `-ru` pages — strong for the recorded examination findings, limited for mechanism.** The Russian and English pages agree on the material injury conclusions. The translation of `насильственная` requires forensic/legal contextual review before being used publicly.
- **S10, `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-371-377?rbid=17743` and Russian `https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files-371-377-ru?rbid=17747` — strong for the 1959 laboratory report, limited for source attribution.** It documents beta contamination and inability to identify the emitter; it does not identify dose, radionuclide, pathway or cause of death.
- **S11, `https://dyatlovpass.com/burmantovo-weather-february-1959` — weak/indirect weather source.** The host expressly says the documents do not belong to the case files and that Burmantovo was 47 miles away. It is a lead, not site weather.

### Later official and scientific sources

- **S12, `https://dyatlovpass.com/prosecutors-investigation` — important but weak-to-moderate source chain.** It is a Komsomolskaya Pravda article/republication quoting Andrey Kuryakov and describing examinations. The page’s Russian version identifies the authors as Nikolay and Natalya Varsegova and states that all rights belong to Komsomolskaya Pravda. It is not the prosecutor’s complete file, a signed disposition, raw expert report or reproducible data release.
- **S13, `https://www.interfax.ru/russia/716956` — independent contemporaneous report of the public statement.** It supports what Kuryakov said on 11 July 2020, including avalanche, worsening weather, cold and snow-pressure injuries. It remains a news report, not the underlying evidence. Interfax’s page states that the material is for personal use and may not be reproduced/distributed without written permission.
- **S14, `https://dyatlovpass.com/prosecutor-generals-office` — useful for review initiation and scope.** It quotes prosecutor officials about the September 2018 start, 400-plus pages, natural-only priority versions and planned site/expert work. The linked official video was not accessible during this audit; the archive-host republication is not a substitute for the official review file.
- **S15, `https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-020-00081-8` / `https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-020-00081-8` — high-quality peer-reviewed model, not a historical finding.** The paper gives the 7.5–13.5 hour delay range, local 28° buried-layer geometry, wind deposition mechanism and impact simulations. It states that the work demonstrates plausibility and does not resolve radioactivity, post-exit behaviour, body locations or the whole mystery. It provides supplementary data at `https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.4302235` and code at `https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.4088052`; those should be audited before numerical graphics or quantitative narration.
- **S16, `https://dyatlovpass.com/louhi-dmitrievskaya-litvinova-ankudinov` — secondary, interested source-critical review.** It is useful for locating objections, including the 8/9 track testimony and searchers’ no-avalanche observations, but it combines author interpretation, interviews and reproduced quotations. It is not a neutral adjudication and its claims must not be elevated above the underlying case-file testimony or a qualified scientific review.

## Specific corrections, conflicts and unresolved issues

### Russian wording and translation

1. **Closure wording:** the Russian `стихийная сила` in sheet 387 should not be silently converted into an original English quotation “overwhelming force.” A reviewed translation may choose “natural force,” “elemental force” or another contextually justified rendering, but the original Russian must be shown in the validation record.
2. **Forensic wording:** `насильственная смерть` is rendered “violent death” in all three cited autopsy pages. This supports the translated wording, but not the modern legal inference “homicide.” The autopsies describe traumatic injury and possible mechanisms such as fall, compression, throw or impact; they do not identify an assailant.
3. **Translation quality:** the HTML pages contain obvious machine-like or editorially awkward phrases, including “overwhelming force,” “at low temperature,” “concussion,” “bath skin” and inconsistent anatomical wording. Keep the Russian page/scan locator beside every future quotation and obtain a qualified review.

### Names, dates and quantities

- The nine fatal hikers and Yudin are consistently identifiable across the case-file summary, closure and autopsy pages, but the expedition roster changed before departure. Use “nine proceeded from the 2nd Northern area” and do not call the preliminary 10/11-person route roster the final fatal group.
- The closure gives the case opening on 26 February, the tent discovery on 26 February, the first fire-site bodies on 26 February and the ravine recovery on 4 May. The expedition summary reports the first four bodies on 27 February, and S6’s inspection protocol is dated 6 May. These are not clerical details: they affect a chronological reconstruction and must remain attributed.
- The closure says the institute search group was sent on 20 February. Interfax’s background says searches began on 16 February. Do not state one as the uncontested first search date; obtain the search organization records, radiograms and witness testimony (case sheets 36–39 and relevant witness pages).
- The 1959 closure uses 1,500 m from the tent to the fire and 300/180/150 m along the route; the expedition summary uses 850 m, 150 m, 180 m and 300 m for the same chain. These are different summaries and possibly different reference points. A map/measurement source is required before visualizing distances.

### Forensic, radiation and causal language

- The 1959 record supports exposure/hypothermia findings and severe internal trauma. It does not establish whether trauma occurred in the tent, in the ravine, during a fall, during snow movement or by another mechanism.
- “Beta contamination” is the accurate narrow description. The report’s tissue conclusion is natural-level/K-40 comparison; its clothing result is selected spots, partly washed before testing, with no chemical or spectrometric identification of the emitter. The prosecutor’s East Ural radioactive-trail attribution is a later hypothesis, not a result of the 1959 lab report.
- S12’s snow-collapse account is a public reconstruction: it reports a 1–3 m snow movement/cover and a six-expert forensic examination, but the complete expert documents are not in the cited pack. Do not present the quantity as independently reproduced evidence.

### Geometry and weather

- Keep all three geometry descriptions visible: 30° in the tent protocol, about 18–20° in the expedition summary, and S15’s modern average/locally steeper buried-layer reconstruction (about 23° ±2° average and about 28° locally). The S15 value is not a direct 1959 measurement.
- S12 reports prosecutor-derived values such as an 11° ravine slope, a roughly 2 m bank and up to 3 m with snow, plus microclimate values. These are not reconciled with S3/S2 and were not supplied with raw data in the article. Keep them as attributed review inputs only.
- S11’s Burmantovo table is 47 miles away and expressly outside the case file. It cannot establish wind or temperature at the tent.
- S15’s wind-deposition and katabatic-wind mechanism is a model input/scenario. The paper does not prove that the required wind flux, weak layer, parapet or local geometry existed on the night.

### Prosecutor source chain

The accessible chain is: prosecutor office review announced in S14; Kuryakov’s 11 July 2020 statements reported by Interfax in S13; Kuryakov’s reconstruction republished by Komsomolskaya Pravda/Dyatlovpass in S12. The full legal review, signed final document, examination orders, expert reports, raw microclimate inputs, coordinate calculations and chain of custody were not supplied in the research pack. Obtain them, or downgrade all precise 2020 causal details to “reported prosecutor reconstruction.”

## Rights and usage position

The pack’s rights note is directionally correct but incomplete:

- The Dyatlovpass archive footer states site copyright and the case-file index identifies the Foundation’s claimed provenance. No clear reuse licence was located for the scans, transcriptions, photographs, maps or site graphics. **Do not assume that archival provenance or public access equals production permission.** Request permission or use independently created diagrams and properly licensed substitutes.
- S12’s Russian page states “all rights belong to Komsomolskaya Pravda.” Do not reproduce its text, screenshots, photographs or embedded video without permission. Cite it for reporting provenance only.
- S13’s Interfax page states that its material is for personal use and may not be reproduced or distributed without written permission. Do not reuse article text or screenshots.
- S15 is open access under **CC BY 4.0**, subject to attribution, a licence link, indication of changes and separate clearance for any third-party material identified in a credit line. The paper acknowledges permission to use original drawings and photographs from named contributors and the Dyatlov Memorial Foundation; those acknowledgements do not automatically license the underlying images for a new production. Check each figure and supplement credit line individually.
- S16’s page has no production licence established by this audit. Treat its quotations, photographs and embedded material as permission-required.

Rights status: **not cleared**. A research URL is not a licence. No visual reuse, screen capture, figure adaptation or narration-length quotation should be approved from this pack without a rights log.
