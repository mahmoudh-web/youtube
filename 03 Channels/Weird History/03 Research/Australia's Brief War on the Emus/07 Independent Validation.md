## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — the documented core is supported, but the pack is not narration-ready.**

The central account is sound: Western Australian soldier settlers reported crop and rabbit-proof-fence damage; Defence made Lewis guns and military personnel available; the first withdrawal was followed by a reported resumption; and the National Archives later summary gives fewer than 1,000 emus killed before abandonment. The pack also correctly resists the formal-war, surrender, exact-986, exact-ammunition and zero-human-casualty versions of the story.

Corrections are still required before human approval:

1. The timeline’s **October 1932** date for the deputation is not supported by the cited passages. The 9 November answer says only “recently”; keep the deputation date unresolved unless the underlying file is checked.
2. The 9 November cost wording should be reconciled with the fuller 22 November House answer: settlers paid ammunition, the State Government paid railway fares, military personnel received no extra pay, and there was no additional Commonwealth cost. The 22 November answer also says the Minister did not let settlers operate the guns; the crews were one officer and two other ranks with two guns.
3. S2 and S3 are readable, traceable **harvested Hansard transcriptions**, not the directly inspected official ParlInfo display. The official Parliament search index confirms the 8 and 9 November 1932 House records, but a human should compare the wording, page and speaker attribution against the current official record.
4. The “fewer than 1,000” figure is a modern NAA summary bound, not a retrieved 1932 count with a disclosed method. Keep it attributed and do not silently replace it with 986.
5. S15’s article metadata should be corrected to Pattrice Jones, “Provocations from the Field - Derangement and Resistance: Reflections from Under the Glare of an Angry Emu,” *Animal Studies Journal* 8(1) (2019), pp. 1–20. The supplied PDF is correctly useful for press-label and reception evidence, but it is interpretive secondary scholarship.

This validation is not approval, does not produce creative material, and does not move the issue beyond `in_review`.

## Timeline cross-check

- **After the First World War / early 1930s:** supported by the NAA summary, but exact settlement locations, numbers and loss scale need underlying Commonwealth or Western Australian records.
- **October 1932 deputation:** **not established.** S3 says “recently,” and S1 gives no month. Replace the exact month with “before the 8–9 November parliamentary questions” or leave the date unresolved.
- **Before 8 November:** supported that Lewis guns and personnel had been made available, but the exact deployment date, gun count and personnel count were not in S3. The 22 November answer supplies the later operational detail of two guns and a three-person crew.
- **8 November:** verified as a House question and answer, including the “Central Australia” wording and Defence-cost statement. The question’s location must remain distinct from the 9 November Western Australian clarification.
- **9 November:** verified in the harvested Hansard transcription as a government explanation of the Western Australian deputation, rationale, cost condition and return of guns to Perth. Current ParlInfo comparison remains required.
- **10 November:** the existence of the Western Mail reports “Machine Gun Offensive Against Emus” and “A THOUSAND BIRDS IN LUCK” is supported by Trove records. The latter’s accessible metadata describes open warfare at Campion as unsuccessful and mentions machine-gun jamming, but the scan/OCR should be inspected before tactical detail is used.
- **12 November:** S5 directly supports a reported resumed attack; S9’s Trove title supports the “Emu War Again” and “Machine Guns Re-Issued” framing. The S9 scan was not accessible here, so the 300 remains a headline/interim figure.
- **1 December:** S6 supports contemporary “The Emu War” language and alternative trapping methods at the rabbit-proof fence. It does not establish which method produced which kills.
- **November–December abandonment:** the broad sequence is supported by S1 plus the resumption reports, but the exact end date and phase totals remain unresolved.
- **1934, 1943 and 1948 requests:** S1 states that later requests for military assistance were refused. This remains a modern official summary claim pending underlying files.
- **5 July 1953:** S14’s title/date are supported by its Trove identifier and S15’s bibliography/PDF, but the page should be inspected before quoting the retrospective’s wording.

## Counterevidence and competing interpretations

1. The official material checked describes a cull/expedition, guns, personnel, cost allocation and withdrawal; it does not establish a declaration of war, legal enemy status or surrender. This is a bounded negative finding, not proof that no other record or metaphor exists.
2. The first return of guns to Perth cannot be treated as the end of all activity because the Argus reported a resumed attack by 12 November and the 22 November House answer discusses continuing operations.
3. The “fewer than 1,000” NAA bound, the 300 in a newspaper headline, and later exact figures are not interchangeable. The pack is right to keep them separate.
4. The “medals for the emus” anecdote has a secondary transmission trail but was not found in the checked 8–9 November passages. It remains unresolved, not disproved.
5. “The emus won” is a cultural or journalistic interpretation of limited effectiveness and withdrawal. It should not erase the real agricultural problem or be stated as an uncontested military result.
6. S15 is interpretive animal-studies scholarship and uses rhetorically loaded language. It is suitable for reception/source criticism, not for independently proving the operational chronology or the claim that government policy “mandated” a war.

## Unsupported, overstated or missing-source claims

1. The exact **October 1932** deputation date is unsupported by the cited passages.
2. S3’s “State and settlers jointly” wording is accurate as quoted, but the pack should include the 22 November breakdown rather than leave the cost distinction unresolved.
3. C03 should distinguish a request for guns from the later statement that settlers were not allowed to operate them.
4. “Fewer than 1,000” is supported only as an NAA summary statement; it is not a transparent primary count.
5. The 300 headline is an interim newspaper figure, not a final total; 986, 9,860 rounds, 20,000 birds and zero human casualties remain uncleared.
6. The medals anecdote remains secondary and unverified against the complete official Hansard record.
7. The claim that the story was “lost” by Australia/the Army is a later interpretation. Attribute it or use the narrower “limited cull effectiveness and withdrawal.”
8. The source index’s S15 bibliographic description needs the author, full title, journal, volume/issue and pages corrected as above.
