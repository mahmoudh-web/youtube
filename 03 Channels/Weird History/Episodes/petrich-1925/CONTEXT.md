## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the October 1925 Greco-Bulgarian frontier crisis at Demir-Kapu/Petrich, provisionally titled “The War of the Stray Dog: What Really Happened at Petrich?” It is a research pack, not a script, title package or production brief.

The central question is what the League of Nations’ official record establishes about the incident sequence, the dog story and the settlement, and where the popular “war over a dog” explanation outruns the evidence.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification gate and evidence-bounded conclusion.
- Prioritise League of Nations primary and official records, then use British, Bulgarian, Greek and scholarly material to expose competing accounts and source bias.
- Separate the initial frontier shooting from the later Greek incursion, the League-supervised withdrawal and the Council’s December settlement.
- Stop for explicit human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `petrich-1925` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat “War of the Stray Dog” as a later/popular label. The safest factual description is the 1925 Greco-Bulgarian frontier crisis and Greek incursion at Demir-Kapu/Petrich.
- Do not state that the dog story caused the crisis. The accessible League summaries describe a frontier incident, firing, troop movements and occupation; they do not establish a dog.
- Preserve the Bulgarian and Greek accounts of the first shots and the death of a Greek officer/sentry as competing claims. The League commission’s exact findings require inspection of the original report and Official Journal scan.
- Describe the December outcome as a League-supervised settlement that included withdrawal, a finding that Greece violated the Covenant by occupying Bulgarian territory, and compensation; verify the exact currency, amount, payment and any frontier-control measures from the primary decision.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not call the event a formal declared war or assert that Petrich was captured unless a primary record and exact date are located.
- Do not present “a soldier chased his dog” as an established fact. At most, identify it as a later/side-specific version pending provenance.
- Do not use “aggression,” “provocation,” “invasion,” “victory,” “punishment” or “guilty” as unqualified narrator language; attribute the term to the government, Commission, newspaper or later historian using it.
- Do not state a final casualty count. Reports vary and the primary Commission/field records must be checked.
- Historical actors are deceased, but nationalist and diplomatic claims remain legally and ethically sensitive because they can reproduce propaganda or assign intent without proof.
