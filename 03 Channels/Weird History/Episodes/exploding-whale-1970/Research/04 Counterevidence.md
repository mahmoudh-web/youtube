|---|---|---|---|
| Bury in place | Thornton told the Register-Guard that a carcass could be uncovered; the later broadcast repeats that rationale. | Burial was considered and rejected in the immediate decision process. | It does not show that burial was technically impossible, nor that every burial location was assessed. |

## Contradictions and corrections

### Species

The 11 November article and the original KATU narration called the animal a Pacific gray whale. The 12 November newspaper noted teeth and revised the identification to sperm whale; OHS and Oregon Encyclopedia use sperm whale. The pack should say “a carcass later identified as a sperm whale” if the initial misidentification matters to the story.

### “It went exactly right” versus the observed outcome

Thornton is quoted in the 13 November report saying, “It went just exactly right,” while the same article records debris in every direction, a damaged car, spectators running and larger pieces being buried. The quote is evidence of Thornton’s contemporaneous assessment, not proof of successful disposal. A safe treatment is: “Thornton said the plan had gone right except for the sand funnel; the observable result was a partial failure.”

### No human injury versus real exposure and property damage

The accounts say no person was hit, but they also record large pieces passing overhead, spectators running for cover, smaller particles landing on people and a car roof being crushed. “No one was hurt” is a bounded outcome claim; “harmless,” “bloodless” or “only a whale died” overstate what the sources support.

### Thornton as decision-maker versus “Oregon” as actor

Later coverage often compresses the event into “Oregon blew up a whale.” The contemporary report names the Highway Division and Thornton, while the 1994 ODOT article says Thornton received the task by default because district engineer Dale Allen and others were away. The latter is retrospective and may explain staffing, not legal authority. No located source proves a formal approval above Thornton.

### Navy advice

Oregon Encyclopedia, ODOT’s 1994 employee paper and later news reports say Navy or munitions experts were consulted. The located sources do not name the individuals, preserve their advice or show that the Navy approved the amount of dynamite. This distinction must remain visible.

### Footage and “viral” afterlife

The original film is identifiable: KATU news, Paul Linnman, Doug Brazil, 16mm color reversal with magnetic audio, later donated to OHS. Internet copies are degraded derivatives; the 2020 remaster is a later presentation using an OHS 4K transfer. “First viral video” and view-count superlatives are reception claims, not necessary facts about provenance.

## Counterevidence checklist for human review

- Compare the 11–13 November newspaper scans with the online transcriptions.
- Search Oregon State Highway Division/ODOT district files, commission minutes, maintenance logs and correspondence for a formal authorization.
- Identify the source behind the Navy-consultation claim and determine whether the advice was technical, informal or merely later recollection.
- Reconcile the exact 1979 treatment of the forty-one whales: Oregon Encyclopedia says burial; the ODOT employee account says burned and buried.
- Verify whether the original KATU film catalogue assigns rights to OHS, KATU, another party or multiple rights holders.
