## Executive verdict

**FAIL for clean validation clearance; conditionally safe to send to human verification with corrections.**

The central conclusion is well supported: the Great Stink was a political and administrative accelerator for a sewer programme already documented in sanitary reports, sewer institutions, engineering plans, and the 1855 Act. The parliamentary record, Bazalgette’s 1865 paper, the 1855 statute, the 1858 statute, Faraday’s 1855 observation, and the archive guide mutually reinforce that sequence.

The pack cannot receive an unqualified validation pass because:

1. **High severity — S2 is misidentified.** `https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/54646/pg54646-images.html` is *A supplementary report on the results of a special inquiry into the practice of interment in towns* (1843), not the 1842 general sanitary report described in the source index. The broad C01 proposition is supported by the correct Gutenberg transcription (`https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65090/pg65090-images.html`) and the Wellcome record, but the pack’s current S2 URL and line references are not reliable evidence labels.
2. **Medium-high severity — S18 is a catalogue record, not evidence for the detailed completion chronology.** The London Met page confirms a 1997 doctoral thesis exists and identifies its subject, but it does not itself establish “1859–1874,” a 1874/1875 completion milestone, or the individual construction dates. Obtain and cite the PDF pages and, preferably, MBW annual reports or committee minutes.
3. **Medium severity — several primary originals remain uninspected.** The 1842 scan’s page-level passages, the original *Times* issue for Faraday, the June 1858 MBW booklet, original British newspaper issues, and MBW annual reports/minutes are still needed before narration or production.

## Timeline and counterevidence audit

- The **1842 sanitary-reform precursor** is sound in substance, but the researcher’s S2 link must be corrected. The correct Gutenberg report title, 1842 presentation, and sanitation/drainage recommendations are independently visible at https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65090/pg65090-images.html, lines 0–43 and 5088–5102; the Wellcome catalogue confirms the primary scan and metadata.
- The **1847–1855 institutional transition** is supported by the London Archives guide and Bazalgette, with the seven/eight discrepancy correctly preserved. The guide also states that sewer connections were compulsory for new buildings in 1847, lines 39–44, a detail that should not be generalized into proof of universal compliance.
- The **1849 “116 schemes” and 30,000 cesspools** are supported only by Bazalgette’s 1865 retrospective account. They are usable as attributed figures, not as unqualified administrative totals.
- The **1855 legal baseline** is strongly supported by the official Act: MBW constitution (section XLIII), Thames-prevention duty and 1860 deadline (section CXXXV), and prior plan approval (section CXXXVI). The 1855 Act’s commencement on 1 January 1856 is explicit at PDF p. 96, section CCLI.
- The **1857–July 1858 deadlock and debate** is strongly supported by primary Hansard. The rhetoric (“utterly impotent,” “wrong end,” “pestilential”) must remain attributed; the debates also show disagreement over outfall, navigation, cost, capacity, and responsibility.
- The **newspaper disruption** is supported by the Newfoundland Express reprint’s indexed text, including the 21 July date and closed Commons windows. The direct PDF endpoint was not machine-openable in the web pass; the British source issue and comparison set remain outstanding.
- The **2 August 1858 Act** is supported by the official PDF text: prompt commencement, £3m borrowing ceiling, Treasury guarantee, 3d rate for 40 years, repeal of the specified approval provisions, 1863 deadline, and surviving navigation approval. It authorized and financed work; it did not build a completed network in 18 days.
- The **1859 construction start** is directly stated by Bazalgette in 1865. The later 1865/1874/1875 completion milestones are not adequately sourced by the current S18 catalogue citation and require direct thesis or MBW records.
- The **counterevidence section** correctly rejects the single-cause story and correctly separates smell, Victorian miasma reasoning, Snow’s waterborne argument, statutory change, engineering design, and construction. It should inherit the S2 correction and S18 chronology correction.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong sources

- **S5 and S14:** official statute scans; strongest evidence for legal powers, duties, finance, approval, and deadlines. The S14 PDF was directly text-extracted during validation although browser extraction intermittently failed.
- **S9–S13:** official Hansard records; strong primary evidence for what named speakers said and what issues were debated. They are not neutral audits or modern medical studies.
- **S7:** a near-contemporary engineering paper by Bazalgette; strong for his account of plans, institutions, quantities, engineering objectives, and construction start, but interested and retrospective.
- **S1:** Wellcome catalogue and scan route; strong for identification and primary-source access, but page-level claims still need scan citations.

### Useful but limited sources

- **S4:** current archival guide; strong for holdings, institutional boundaries, and access routes, not proof of historical performance beyond what it explicitly states.
- **S6:** first-person Faraday text hosted as a later transcription; strong for the content of the observation, weaker for publication metadata and final quotation clearance.
- **S15:** contemporary newspaper reprint; useful period witness, but overseas reproduction and unavailable original British issue require comparison.
- **S16 and S17:** museum/heritage syntheses; useful orientation and triangulation, but their compressed causal narratives and medical/engineering summaries should not replace primary records.
- **S18:** doctoral-thesis catalogue page; confirms the thesis and subject only. It is insufficient for the detailed construction/completion claims currently attached to it.
- **S2:** currently unusable as labelled. Correct the URL to Gutenberg 65090 or cite the Wellcome scan directly.
