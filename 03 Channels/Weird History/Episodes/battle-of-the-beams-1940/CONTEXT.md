## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Battle of the Beams: Britain’s Invisible War over Nazi Navigation,” about German radio-navigation aids and British detection, deception and jamming in 1940–41. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/legal-sensitivity note and provisional conclusion.
- Explain the beam systems in plain language while separating Knickebein, X-Gerät and Y-Gerät rather than treating “the beam” as one device.
- Reconstruct detection through signals intelligence, technical examination and an RAF search flight, then distinguish jamming, spoofing, meaconing and physical attacks on transmitters.
- Keep the operational meaning of “battle” qualified: the phrase is a later umbrella for an evolving electronic contest, not a single engagement with a settled start and end.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `battle-of-the-beams-1940` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat 21 June 1940 as the key located detection flight for a Knickebein signal, while preserving the preceding intelligence trail and the possibility that earlier tests or observations are described differently in classified records.
- Treat “Bromide,” “Aspirin,” “Ruffian” and related names as source-specific code words requiring reconciliation. The RAF Museum timeline and later RAF Historical Society account do not align cleanly on which German system “Ruffian” denotes or when Bromide was operational.
- Treat the British “beam-bending” story cautiously. The later RAF Historical Society paper says ordinary Knickebein jamming used unsynchronised Morse dashes and that deliberate beam aiming was not the normal method; any stronger claim needs primary operational records.
- Treat the Coventry raid as a revealing test case, not proof that one jammer single-handedly saved Britain. The located account records an ineffective early BROMIDE setup, later modification, weather/moonlight, marking and wider air-defence factors.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not call the beams radar. They were radio-navigation systems; the British detection story combines radio interception, direction finding, technical intelligence and airborne signal search.
- Do not state that Churchill personally invented, directly operated or single-handedly won the countermeasure programme. The sources support high-level approval and intervention in continuing the investigation, alongside specialist work by the Air Ministry, RAF, TRE and No. 80 Wing.
- Do not collapse “jamming worked” into “the signal disappeared.” The later RAF Historical Society account argues that some jamming mainly created distrust and fear of interception; this is an interpretation requiring attribution.
- Do not present the Coventry casualty figures, beam accuracy, frequency/power figures or claims about later German abandonment as narration-ready without checking the cited pages and underlying wartime records.
- No declassified-looking document, archive catalogue entry, technical diagram, map or photograph is an approved production asset. Rights, classification history, legibility and context require human review.
