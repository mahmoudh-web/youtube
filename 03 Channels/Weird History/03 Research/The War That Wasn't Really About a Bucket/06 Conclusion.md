## Narrowest defensible conclusion

The Battle of Zappolino was a real 1325 Modenese victory over Bologna within a longer pattern of territorial, political, and factional conflict. The evidence located does **not** establish that a stolen bucket caused the war. A bucket is preserved in Modena’s civic memory as a reported trophy, but its exact timing, provenance, and relation to the battle remain unproven at primary-source level.

The bucket became the event’s dominant public label through local memory and, above all, Alessandro Tassoni’s seventeenth-century mock-heroic poem *La secchia rapita*. Tassoni’s own wording says the causal story was reputed, and later annotations openly acknowledge that the poem rearranged material from the 1249 and 1325 conflicts for comic effect. The safest interpretation is therefore: **the bucket was probably a trophy or humiliation symbol attached to a serious war, while the “war over a bucket” is a later literary and civic simplification.** “Probably” must remain visible until the critical medieval passages and object provenance are checked.

## Confidence summary

- **High:** a Bologna–Modena conflict culminated in a battle at Zappolino in 1325; Modena won; the episode belongs to wider territorial and factional struggles; Tassoni’s poem was first published in 1622 and made the bucket central to later memory.
- **Medium-high:** 15 November as the modern date; pursuit toward Bologna’s walls; a 1326 peace associated with the aftermath; the bucket tradition as a reported trophy.
- **Medium / unresolved:** the exact sequence around Monteveglio; army sizes and casualty totals; the precise roles of all named commanders; whether the bucket was taken during the battle or afterward; whether the current Modena object is the same bucket.
- **Low / not claimable:** “Bologna went to war to recover its only bucket”; a direct 1325 order or declaration naming the bucket as casus belli; an authenticated chain of custody for the surviving object.
