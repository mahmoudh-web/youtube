## Provisional assessment

The located record firmly documents a sudden Lake Nyos gas disaster, but it does not justify a single neat “lake exploded” explanation. Geological, chemical and medical evidence strongly supports a large CO₂ release from deep, stratified lake water; isotopic evidence links the gas to magmatic sources; the medical record supports asphyxiation as the primary cause of death. The USGS/Science record found no significant direct volcanic activity and called the initiating mechanism undetermined. The Italian technical mission instead interpreted the outburst as a phreatic explosion beneath the lake. That disagreement is part of the historical record and must remain visible.

The safest research framing is therefore: **a volcanic lake had accumulated a dangerous load of magmatic CO₂, released it into the valleys on 21 August 1986, and left scientists debating what actually pulled the trigger.** This is a provisional research frame, not approved narration or a title/thumbnail recommendation.

## What the pack can currently establish

- Lake Nyos in north-west Cameroon experienced a catastrophic gas release on 21 August 1986.
- At least 1,700 people died according to the USGS report; Cameroon’s 2016 ministry account gives 1,746, while the medical evaluation estimates 1,700–2,000. Thousands of livestock also died.
- CO₂-rich gas flowed into low-lying valleys and displaced breathable air; the located medical record supports asphyxia as the primary lethal process.
- Chemical, isotopic and geological evidence supports a deep magmatic source for the CO₂ and substantial pre-event storage in the lake’s bottom water.
- The lake’s density/chemical stratification was central to the storage model.
- A water wave, fountain or surge was reported, but that observation does not by itself establish a conventional flood or direct volcanic eruption.
- US investigators reported no significant direct volcanic activity in the event evidence; the Italian technical mission published a competing phreatic-explosion interpretation.
- The exact trigger remains unresolved. Landslide, earthquake, storm, seasonal/thermal instability, buoyancy reversal and direct phreatic activity are hypotheses with different evidentiary bases.
- Survivor medical findings require careful wording: many lesions were later associated with coma/immobility, but other toxic factors were not entirely excluded.
- A weak natural dam represented a separate future flood hazard, and artificial degassing/warning/dam-reinforcement measures were later developed.

## Editorial interpretation, clearly labelled

The strongest interpretation is not that a hidden bomb went off beneath the lake. It is that a volcanic system gradually loaded a stratified lake with dissolved CO₂, creating a hazard that could remain visually quiet until a rapid physical disturbance caused gas to come out of solution. The extraordinary part is the gap between a firmly documented gas/asphyxia outcome and an initiating trigger that remains unresolved in the core record. The Italian mission’s phreatic explanation should be presented as part of that scientific conflict, while the later mitigation history shows that prevention could proceed even without perfect historical certainty.

This interpretation does not prove that officials ignored a warning, that a particular landslide or earthquake caused the event, or that every source used the same meaning for “explosion.” It also does not make local communities passive scenery; the human record needs additional Cameroonian and survivor-focused sourcing before social-impact narration.
