## Why the number was controversial

The primary report itself records skepticism about a wave reaching such an extreme elevation. Later summaries note that some observers argued the upper trees and soil might have slid into the bay during the earthquake rather than being removed by water. Miller's counter-observations were driftwood and rocks on the upper slope and felled trees oriented in the direction of wave travel. That supports a water-driven trimline interpretation, but it does not turn the trimline into a direct measurement of a vertical wave crest.

**Source trace:** Miller, *Giant waves*, printed pp. 63–64 / PDF pp. 22–23 and the field-evidence discussion at printed pp. 60–62 / PDF pp. 13–15; Alaska Earthquake Center lines 75–80.

## Measurement distinctions that must survive editing

| Quantity | Evidence or result | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| About 1,800 ft | Early flight/altimeter reading beside the fresh upper trimline. | Not a final surveyed wave height. |
| About 1,740 ft | Miller's summary description of water surging over the opposite wall of Gilbert Inlet. | Not identical to the mapped 1,720-ft trimline and not a direct open-water crest measurement. |
| About 1,720 ft / 524 m a.s.l. | Maximum mapped trimline/run-up elevation on the spur southwest of Gilbert Inlet. | Not a 1,720-ft vertical wall moving through the bay or ocean. |
| 94–162 m in several studies | Modeled maximum impulse-wave height range summarized by Franco et al. | Not a single agreed measured wave height. |
| 208 m | Maximum amplitude in the 2020 model's impact-area output. | Not the 524-m run-up elevation. |
| 4–10 m | Modeled flow depth at the point where the 524-m run-up was reached in the 2020 model. | Not the total upslope run-up elevation. |

**Source trace:** [Franco et al. 2020](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/20/2255/2020/), lines 313–325 and 456–470.

## Eyewitness limits

- Ulrich's account is powerful evidence about timing and source location, but he explicitly described the high water as a splash and not a wave travelling intact to 1,800 ft.
- Swanson's account documents a boat lifted over La Chaussee Spit and a view down toward treetops, but it is local to the outer bay and does not determine the head-of-bay maximum.
- Later synthesis reports an eyewitness estimate of 15–30 m amplitude near Cenotaph Island, while other descriptions concern the boat's ride and lower, later chop. These are not interchangeable measurements.

**Source trace:** [Miller PDF](https://dggs.alaska.gov/webpubs/usgs/p/text/p0354c.pdf), printed pp. 57–59 / PDF pp. 10–12; [Franco et al.](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/20/2255/2020/), lines 317–318; [Alaska Earthquake Center](https://earthquake.alaska.edu/60-years-ago-1958-earthquake-and-lituya-bay-megatsunami), lines 65–71.

## Competing source-mechanism explanations

### Single rapid rockslide

Miller's interpretation and later scale-model work treat the earthquake-triggered rockslide into Gilbert Inlet as the principal source. Wiegel's model required a large mass to fall rapidly and found that a sheet of water could run up the opposite slope to several times the water depth. Fritz and Hager later reproduced the initial run-up in a smaller physical model.

### Possible double slide

Ward and Day argued that a rockslide-only source may not explain the destruction farther down the bay or the inferred sediment deposit. Their possible sequence adds a larger, slower submarine slide of glacial sediment after the initial rockfall. The Alaska Earthquake Center summary states that testing this would require surveying and dating the bottom sediments. This is an important competing explanation, not a settled fact.

**Source trace:** [Miller PDF](https://dggs.alaska.gov/webpubs/usgs/p/text/p0354c.pdf), printed pp. 63–65 / PDF pp. 22–24; [Alaska Earthquake Center](https://earthquake.alaska.edu/60-years-ago-1958-earthquake-and-lituya-bay-megatsunami), lines 80–84; [Ward & Day DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793431110000893).

## Model and data limitations

The 2020 numerical study notes that no high-resolution pre- and post-event bathymetry/topography is available for the exact pre-1958 configuration. It reports sensitivity to grid resolution, topography, roughness and treatment of the slide material. Different studies therefore produce different amplitudes and run-up values while still testing the same observed trimline.

**Source trace:** [Franco et al. 2020](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/20/2255/2020/), lines 503–518.

## Editorial implication

The strongest defensible story is not “scientists measured a 1,700-ft wave in the water.” It is: field evidence established that water stripped the slope to a maximum mapped elevation of about 1,720 ft; eyewitnesses and geological context identified a landslide impulse source; models tested how that run-up could occur and continue to disagree about the complete source sequence.
