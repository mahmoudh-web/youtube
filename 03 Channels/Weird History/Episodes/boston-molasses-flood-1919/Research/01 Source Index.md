## Research question

What happened when the Commercial Street molasses tank failed in Boston on 15 January 1919, why did USIA allege sabotage, and what did the criminal inquiry, engineering evidence and later civil proceeding actually establish?

## Working answer and scope

The located record supports a narrow answer: a large tank operated by Purity Distilling and owned by United States Industrial Alcohol (USIA) ruptured, killing 21 people and injuring many more. USIA alleged that anarchists had planted a bomb. The February 1919 criminal inquiry did not produce a manslaughter indictment. In the later consolidated civil proceeding, court-appointed auditor Hugh W. Ogden rejected the sabotage explanation and assigned liability to USIA, emphasizing structural weakness and ignored safety problems. Because the claims were settled after the report, this pack does not describe a final jury verdict.

The pack prioritizes the state death-certificate collection, the Suffolk County case and auditor’s report, contemporary engineering and fire reports, and contemporary newspaper coverage. Secondary and later technical sources are used to triangulate, not replace, the primary record.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or official record:** death certificate, court/auditor record, contemporary engineering or fire report, archival catalogue, or government record.
- **B — institutional or scholarly synthesis:** Boston Archives, Massachusetts Humanities, or a published technical review that identifies its evidence base.
- **C — reputable later reporting:** useful for context and discovery; not sufficient alone for a disputed material claim.
- **D — transcription/reproduction mirror:** useful for locating a primary passage, but the original scan or holding institution must be checked before quotation.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use “USIA alleged” or “USIA’s defense argued” for the sabotage theory. Never state that anarchists planted a bomb as fact.
2. Use “the February 1919 grand jury returned no manslaughter indictment” only for the criminal inquiry; do not treat it as a civil liability ruling.
3. Use “Ogden, the court-appointed auditor, found/recommended” rather than “the jury found” or “the court convicted.”
4. Attribute structural mechanisms to Spofford, Brown, Colby or Mayville and preserve disagreement where the source record does not settle the mechanism.
5. Use the Massachusetts Archives casualty index as the starting list, then verify every person-level detail against the certificate image and court damage record.
6. Treat all photographs, newspaper pages and engineering diagrams as rights-controlled until the holding institution’s terms are checked.
