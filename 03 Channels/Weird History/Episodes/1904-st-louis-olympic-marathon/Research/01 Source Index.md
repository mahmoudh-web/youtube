## Research question

What does the surviving record establish about the 30 August 1904 St. Louis marathon's heat, dust, water access, vehicles, medical treatment and disputed anecdotes—and where does the famous “survival experiment” framing go beyond the evidence?

## Episode boundary

This pack reconstructs the race and its immediate reception from the 1905 Lucas account, period newspaper leads and later scholarship. It tests the familiar stories about deliberate dehydration, Fred Lorz's car ride, Thomas Hicks's strychnine and brandy, William Garcia's hemorrhage, Felix Carvajal's food and nap, the South African runners and the finish. It does not draft narration, diagnose historical athletes, or treat a later database as a substitute for the period record.

## Source-quality key

- **A — period primary or near-primary:** a 1905 race account, 1909 handbook, or digitised period newspaper. These sources still contain observer bias, promotional language and inconsistent counting.
- **B — archival/institutional record:** a library, Olympic archive, weather service or historical collection record. Strong for provenance and results, but not automatically proof of every anecdote.
- **C — scholarly secondary:** peer-reviewed or specialist historical analysis that should be used to triangulate, not silently replace, primary wording.
- **D — reputable explanatory reporting:** useful for locating sources and describing reception; not sole support for a disputed material claim.
- **E — orientation only:** non-specialist retellings. Do not use as sole support.

## Key people and entities

- **Thomas J. Hicks:** English-born American runner; recognized winner in the surviving result tradition. His handlers' account is the source for the oral strychnine, egg, brandy and warm-sponging details.
- **Charles J. P. Lucas and Hugh C. McGrath:** Hicks's handlers. Lucas wrote the central report and drove behind Hicks; their proximity gives useful detail but creates a clear conflict of perspective.
- **Fred Lorz:** Mohawk Athletic Club runner who crossed first, admitted or was reported to have taken a car after withdrawing, and was disqualified. The distance, motive and sanction vary by source.
- **Albert J. Corey and A. L. Newton:** second and third in the Lucas result; the contemporary classification of Corey's nationality/club affiliation requires care.
- **Felix Carvajal:** Cuban runner who finished fourth. Lucas supports a stop to ask for/steal peaches; the familiar apples, nap and travel story is not established by the core account.
- **William R. Garcia:** San Francisco runner found with a severe stomach hemorrhage. Lucas attributed the injury to dust; that is a period report and not a modern medical finding.
- **Len Taunyane and Jan Mashiani:** South African runners who finished ninth and twelfth in Lucas's list. Use the period racial label only as quoted historical language, never as neutral description.
- **James E. Sullivan:** chief Department of Physical Culture official. The claim that he designed the race as a purposeful-dehydration experiment is a later interpretation that needs primary correspondence or a direct report.
- **David R. Francis / Francis Field:** Exposition president who started the race; the stadium was the start and finish point.

## Source-handling rules

1. State the event date, accepted result and 14 finishers with attribution to the period report/database; keep 29/31/32 starter counts visible until the start list is reconciled.
2. Use “about 90°F” only as a period-report statement. The NWS/aggregated 1904 weather data conflict is a verification note, not a reason to silently choose a number.
3. Keep Lucas's “one place” at 12 miles separate from later claims of a water tower at six miles plus a well at 12 miles.
4. Describe Hicks as receiving two reported oral doses of one-sixtieth grain strychnine in Lucas; label the injection/one-milligram version as a later conflicting account.
5. Describe Lorz's car ride and disqualification with source attribution. Avoid calling his motive a joke or fraud unless the wording is tied to the relevant report.
6. Keep Carvajal's peaches, the apples/nap story, Tau's dog detour and any claim that Hicks was literally carried over the line in separate evidence categories.
7. Reproduce historical racist labels only when necessary to explain the source, and never as editorial voice.
