## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections.** The pack’s central, evidence-bounded conclusion is supported: a CIA animal-research effort involving a cat and listening equipment is documented; the surviving 1967 evaluation says cats could be trained to move short distances but that the technique was impractical for specialized intelligence needs; the CIA currently says the effort ended in 1967 without operational use. The pack is appropriately cautious about the taxi story, target, cost, device layout and animal welfare.

It is not narration-ready as written. The largest required controls are to attribute all CIA retrospective and participant claims, keep the taxi death explicitly contested, correct the source-quality labels for archival commentary/catalogue pages, and downgrade the “no formal investigation” statement to an unresolved search result. No script, narration, title, thumbnail, visual concept, production file or publication recommendation is created here.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong evidence

- **C00021897** is the load-bearing primary document. The surviving text supports short-distance training, a scientific-achievement assessment and practical failure under foreign environmental/security conditions. It is heavily redacted and hosted by The Black Vault rather than directly by CIA in the checked URL.
- **C00021892** is a primary declassified status report. Its surviving text supports decision options around modifying systems, simulated operations, termination and transfer, but redactions prevent assigning each option to Acoustic Kitty specifically.
- **C00021906** is a primary declassified broader animal-program document. Its surviving text supports canine development and continued low-level feline laboratory testbed work in late 1967, not Acoustic Kitty deployment.
- **S1**, the CIA’s 2024 public account, directly supports the Agency’s present position on 1964, the broad apparatus, limited field tests, 1967 termination, humane treatment and the taxi-story correction. It is retrospective institutional self-description, not neutral independent confirmation.
- **S2/S12**, National Security Archive material, is a valuable archival release and quotation source. It should be classified as curated secondary commentary when used for interpretation; the scanned memo itself remains the primary evidence.

### Indirect or access-limited evidence

- **S9**, the *Guardian*, is useful contemporary declassification-era reporting but repeats the taxi story rather than independently proving it.
- **S10**, the Courrier International page, is a 2003 republication/translation of *Daily Telegraph* reporting. It is useful for tracing the cost and 1966 chronology claims, but not independent confirmation.
- **S11**, *Smithsonian*, is the strongest located source for Bailey and Wallace’s later testimony, but it is an oral-history-style retrospective and reports destroyed/private records and a Glomar response.
- **S13**, National Security Archive commentary, documents the existence and wording of the reported Glomar response, while also arguing against the CIA’s position. It is evidence of the FOIA dispute, not proof that additional records exist.
- **S4** and **S14** are archive/catalogue or archive-summary pages, not primary CIA releases. Downgrade S4 from “A/D” to **D** for catalogue metadata and S14 to **D** for disputed narrative details.
- **S7/S8** are official FOIA records, but the direct CIA PDF URLs redirected to the generic reading room in this check. The official search-result text exposed the request scope and F-2019-01006 log entry; the actual response files and final dispositions remain unchecked.
- **S15**, the OXYGAS document, is adjacent dolphin-program context only and was not usable as evidence about Acoustic Kitty. Do not merge species or projects.

### Coverage gaps

The pack is strong on the surviving evaluation memo, the CIA’s current position and the existence of conflicting later accounts. It is not complete enough to settle:

1. C00021897’s exact date, addressee, redacted title/use field and release package.
2. The number of cats, prototype stages, procedures, veterinary records, equipment removal and final disposition.
3. The exact field-test location, target, audio result, mission approval or operational order.
4. The provenance and firsthand status of Marchetti’s account and the CIA’s claimed 1979 joke admission.
5. The final dispositions and responsive records for F-2002-01252 and F-2019-01006.
6. Any formal CIA, congressional, inspector-general or intelligence-committee review dedicated to the cat project.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory items

- The taxi death is not supported by the surviving memo. It is a later Marchetti account, contradicted by Wallace and the current CIA account. It must never be narrated as settled fact.
- The exact cost is not established. The checked later figures conflict: S10 reports more than £10 million, S12 reports $15 million, and S14 reports more than $25 million.
- “1966 first experiment,” “five years of preparation,” Soviet embassy/Kremlin targeting and the exact location outside Washington come from later press/archive retellings and are not established by the open primary record.
- The exact device layout is contradictory across CIA, Bailey and Marchetti accounts. Use broad “implanted listening equipment” wording and attribute anatomical detail.
- “The cat was unharmed” and “low-risk microsurgery” are CIA institutional claims. No veterinary notes, welfare assessment or independent disposition record was located.
- “No formal investigation existed” is unsupported. The correct finding is that no dedicated investigation was located in the preliminary search.
- The broader 1967–69 animal-program records cannot be treated as proof that Acoustic Kitty itself continued, succeeded or became operational.

7. Keep any cost, target, 1966 date or five-year duration out of approved wording unless a primary record is found.
