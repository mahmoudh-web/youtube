## Wording gate

Current wording that may advance only after human verification:

> Beginning on 25 August 1835, New York’s penny newspaper *The Sun* ran six installments presented as John Herschel’s astronomical discoveries at the Cape of Good Hope. The reports described lunar landscapes, animals and winged humanoids, but the Moon was not being observed: the serial was fabricated and later attributed to *Sun* writer Richard Adams Locke. The story travelled through reprints and debate, while the paper’s 16 September response was more evasive than a clean correction. The safest account is about how a false scientific report borrowed real authority—not about a whole world believing in bat-men.

This wording intentionally omits a settled circulation number, a universal belief claim, a definitive account of Locke’s motives, and any claim that Herschel publicly debunked the story immediately.
