## Executive verdict

### PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — not approval

The load-bearing conclusion is supported: the 1958 event followed an earthquake and a rockslide into Gilbert Inlet; eyewitnesses locate the disturbance and establish its timing and violence; Miller's field observations, altimetry, aerial photographs, map transfer and photogrammetry document a maximum trimline/run-up elevation of about 1,720 feet (about 524 m) above mean sea level; and later models test, rather than directly measure, how that run-up could occur.

The pack must receive the corrections below before any narration claim is treated as ready. The source record is not strong enough to call this an independently approved episode. In particular, the original PDF's OCR is error-prone, the Tocher and Miller article itself was not accessible through the cited DOI during this check, and the full Ward and Day paper was not accessible beyond its publisher abstract. The original report pages, maps and plates therefore remain a human verification requirement.

## Source quality and coverage

- **S1 — Miller, USGS Professional Paper 354-C:** strongest source and correct primary anchor. It is a contemporaneous field report containing eyewitness records, field observations, photographs, maps, altimeter method and photogrammetric discussion. Its OCR has substantial scanning errors; the human must check the printed pages and plates before quoting technical wording.
- **S2 — Tocher and Miller, official USGS record:** useful for bibliographic identity, authors, DOI, date and the catalogue abstract's wording about fault displacement and a wave above 1,700 ft. It is a catalogue/abstract record, not the full article and not a substitute for opening the article. The DOI returned an access/cache failure during validation. The source index's quality “A” is too strong for the exact claim unless the article itself is obtained.
- **S3 — Alaska DGGS record:** authoritative provenance and stable access path for S1, but catalogue evidence only. It independently confirms the title, author, year, report number, page range and archived PDF; it does not verify the event claims.
- **S4 — Alaska Earthquake Center:** clear institutional synthesis with useful cross-checks for chronology, eyewitnesses, trimlines, models and the possible double slide. It is secondary and occasionally uses vivid or superlative language; S1 or peer-reviewed work must carry the load-bearing claims.
- **S5 — Franco et al., NHESS 2020:** peer-reviewed, accessible model study and the best source for separating amplitude, run-up, flow depth and model sensitivity. It is not a direct observation of 1958 and explicitly documents missing high-resolution pre/post-event data, grid effects and trimline discrepancies.
- **S6 — Ward and Day 2010:** peer-reviewed model paper and an important independent competing interpretation. Only the publisher abstract was accessible in this check; obtain the full paper (pp. 285–319) before relying on precise sediment volumes, slide sequence or causal details.
- **S7 — Roberts, Naval Institute Proceedings:** contemporary and useful for reception, eyewitness retelling and the existence of survey disagreement, but a magazine account with rhetorical language. It should not outrank S1 or serve as the sole basis for a numerical claim.

Coverage is good for the central run-up distinction: C06–C13 are anchored in S1 and cross-checked with S4/S5. The pack also preserves both the single-slide interpretation and Ward–Day's possible double-slide interpretation. The main coverage gaps are the inaccessible full S2 and S6 papers, the uninspected original map/plate wording, and direct checks of the underlying Brazee/Jordan/Davis objections summarized by Miller rather than separately sourced in the pack.

One contextual number needs care if it is ever used: S1's abstract says two people were killed in Lituya Bay, while S4 says all five deaths associated with the wider earthquake occurred in water. These are different scopes, not a demonstrated contradiction. The pack's conclusion does not use a fatality count; do not introduce one without stating the geographic scope and source.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory or missing-source items

1. **“Kenneth Loken's altimeter” is over-specific.** The cited primary wording supports an airplane altimeter during the Miller–Loken reconnaissance, not ownership or custody by Loken.
2. **“Late 1958–1959” is not supported by the cited method locator.** The method is supported, but the date range needs a dated source or a less precise label.
3. **S2 is mis-ranked if treated as primary full-text evidence.** The official web page is a catalogue/abstract record; the article and DOI could not be opened here.
4. **S6 is only abstract-checked.** The double-slide proposal is supported as a proposal, but precise mechanism, timing, sediment volume and bathymetric interpretation remain unresolved until the full paper and underlying charts are checked.
5. **S7's “highest wave” and photogrammetrist language are rhetorical/contextual.** They should not be presented as an independent consensus measurement.
6. **Any “highest ever documented/recorded” superlative needs scope and attribution.** S4 and S5 use that wording, but it is not necessary to answer the research question and should not be stated unqualified as a global fact.
