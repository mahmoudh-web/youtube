## Research question and episode promise

Why did the 1969 El Salvador–Honduras conflict become known as the “Football War” when the evidence points to land, migration, border, economic and political pressures? The research question is not whether football mattered—it clearly supplied a highly visible arena for nationalist hostility—but whether the matches caused the state decision to use force.

Working distinction: **football was an accelerant, symbol and media frame; structural disputes and domestic political pressures supplied the combustible material and the decision pathway.** This is an evidence-bounded interpretation, not a settled causal measurement.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary/official:** treaty, intergovernmental record, archival diplomatic document, contemporary institutional record, or official football record.
- **B — contemporary or first-party perspective:** contemporary journalism, participant/state statement, or institutional account with a clear viewpoint.
- **C — scholarly secondary:** peer-reviewed article, academic book/article, or research synthesis requiring source criticism.
- **D — orientation only:** retrospective popular, heritage, or unspecialised account; never sole support for a material claim.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use S5 and S17 to test the football-trigger story, but preserve the narrower claim that football matches intensified public hostility and provided media-ready symbols.
2. Treat S1–S3 as direct evidence of contemporaneous rights abuses and official investigations, not as a complete battlefield ledger.
3. Keep state claims (“aggression”, “genocide”, “legitimate defence”) attributed to the issuing government or source. Do not collapse them into narrator voice.
4. Do not use the 300,000 Salvadoran population estimate, 1,900 deaths, 12,000 wounded, or any larger popular figure without showing the source and its caveat; these numbers are not yet reconciled.
5. The match date conflict—15 versus 16 June for the second-match violence, and 26 versus 27 June for the playoff—must remain visible until original football and diplomatic records are checked.
6. The 1980 treaty and 1992 judgment are aftermath/context only. They do not prove that the 1969 conflict was primarily territorial, nor do they validate a football-causation claim.
