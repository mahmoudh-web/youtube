## Research question

What can the surviving British, Zanzibari, German and newspaper records safely establish about the succession crisis and bombardment of 27 August 1896, and why do published accounts give 38, 40, 43, 46, 50 minutes, or about an hour?

## Conflict and episode promise

The documented event is a British-directed bombardment in a Zanzibar succession crisis inside an already narrowed protectorate relationship. The research tension is between the memorable “38-minute war” label and records that measure different endpoints, round times differently, or report through delayed telegraphy. The pack also keeps the colonial power imbalance and reported Zanzibari deaths in view.

## Source-quality key

- **A — official or primary:** treaty text, official dispatch, archival catalogue, or a contemporaneous eyewitness/newspaper report.
- **B — institutional/archival synthesis:** a museum, archive or scholarly catalogue that identifies primary material.
- **C — scholarly interpretation:** peer-reviewed or academic work used to frame significance and challenge triumphalist summaries.
- **D — later popular synthesis:** useful for reception history only; not sufficient for a material factual claim.

## Primary-source retrieval queue

1. Retrieve TNA **FO 107/54** and **FO 107/57**, especially Basil Cave’s telegrams dated 25–28 August 1896.
2. Search Admiralty correspondence and reports of proceedings for *St George*, *Racoon/Raccoon*, *Thrush*, *Sparrow* and *Philomel*; check whether **ADM 123/128** contains relevant Zanzibar material.
3. Inspect the *Gazette for Zanzibar and East Africa* 2 September 1896 supplement, likely in ZNA **BA 104/05** or the BOA digitised collection.
4. Compare the original newspaper scans with OCR before quoting numbers, names, timings or descriptions of the dead.
