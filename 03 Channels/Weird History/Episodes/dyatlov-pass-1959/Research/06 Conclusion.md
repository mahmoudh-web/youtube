## Provisional answer to the episode question

The surviving 1959 file does not say “an avalanche killed nine hikers.” It records a tent abandoned without bodies, substantial clothing and equipment left behind, two cuts described as made from inside, 8–9 tracks leading away, bodies found in separate locations, exposure deaths, severe trauma in three ravine victims, beta contamination on selected clothing, and no recorded traces of struggle or other people. Its closure language is that the group succumbed to an “overwhelming force” they could not overcome.

The 2018–20 prosecutor review later selected a more specific natural reconstruction: a slab/avalanche event forced the group out; severe cold, wind and poor visibility prevented a return; later snow collapse in a ravine caused the major injuries. That is an important official investigative finding, but the accessible evidence here is a public account of the review, not the complete underlying file.

The 2021 Gaume–Puzrin paper strengthens the physical plausibility of one part of that reconstruction. Its model shows how a local cut in the snow, weak-layer geometry and wind-driven deposition could produce a delayed slab release after roughly 7.5–13.5 hours, and how a small slab could cause severe but non-fatal thorax/skull injuries. The paper does not demonstrate that the slab released on 1–2 February 1959, explain every movement after the exit, identify the contamination source, or solve the whole case.

## Evidence-bounded conclusion

The narrowest defensible conclusion is: **the file supports a sudden environmental emergency followed by exposure and traumatic deaths; a delayed slab avalanche/snow-collapse sequence is the strongest located natural hypothesis and is physically plausible under modern modelling, but the complete historical sequence is not proven.**

That conclusion is stronger than “nothing can be said” and narrower than “the mystery is solved.” It preserves the difference between a primary record, an official later interpretation, a scientific model, and an editorial inference.

## Safe future wording after validation

- “The 1959 record documents an abrupt evacuation, not a named avalanche.”
- “Russia’s later prosecutor review attributed the deaths to a snow-slab/avalanche event and severe weather.”
- “A 2021 peer-reviewed study showed that a delayed slab mechanism was physically plausible under specific assumptions.”
- “The model explains some of the scene and injuries; it does not prove every step of what happened.”
- “The case file’s radiation test found beta contamination on clothing but did not identify a nuclear cause.”

## Wording to reject

- “The case is solved.”
- “The official cause was proven to be an avalanche.”
- “The hikers were murdered by the Mansi / Soviet military / a secret unit.”
- “Radiation proves a weapons test.”
- “Violent death proves homicide.”
- “The avalanche carried the bodies” unless an independently validated reconstruction supports that exact claim.
