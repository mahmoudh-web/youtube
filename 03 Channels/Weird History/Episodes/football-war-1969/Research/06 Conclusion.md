## Answer to the research question

The evidence supports a distinction between what made the crisis combustible and what made it visible. El Salvador and Honduras entered June 1969 with unresolved migration, land, labour, border and economic-integration tensions. Honduran agrarian policy and anti-Salvadoran pressure affected a large migrant population; Salvadoran political and military actors faced pressure to respond; both states and their media used national grievance to mobilise public opinion. The football qualifiers did not create those conditions.

The matches nevertheless mattered. They brought the two national publics into direct confrontation, supplied emotionally powerful symbols and headlines, and helped accelerate a crisis that already involved diplomatic rupture and reported violence. The record currently supports describing football as an **accelerant and political/media symbol**, not as the structural cause of the war and not as irrelevant background.

The final step—from crisis to armed hostilities—belongs to governments and military institutions. The US diplomatic record reports Salvadoran attacks beginning on 14 July, while the parties’ records contest responsibility and justification. The OAS then constrained the conflict through cease-fire, withdrawal, civilian-protection and media-de-escalation measures. The IACHR’s work shows that civilian rights violations and displacement were not a footnote to the football story. Later peace and judicial processes demonstrate that border questions outlasted the 14–18 July fighting.

## What can be said safely

- “Football did not, by itself, start a war.”
- “The qualifiers intensified public hostility and helped give the crisis its enduring name.”
- “Land, migration, border, economic and political pressures formed the structural context.”
- “The precise weight of football, media, military lobbying and government strategy remains an interpretation, not a measured fact.”
- “Death and displacement totals remain disputed and must be attributed or omitted.”

## What should not be said without validation

- “They went to war because of a match.”
- “The war was purely about land” or “purely about migration.”
- “One side committed genocide” as a narrator-owned legal conclusion.
- “The OAS alone ended the war.”
- “The war killed exactly [single number]” or displaced exactly [single number] without a reconciled source definition.
- “Ryszard Kapuściński coined the term” without checking earliest contemporary usage.

## Key entities and voices

- **El Salvador’s government and armed forces:** responsibility for policy, diplomatic claims and the reported 14 July attacks; source language must remain attributed.
- **Honduras’ government, agrarian institutions and armed forces:** agrarian policy, migrant treatment, sovereignty claims and military response; distinguish official policy from local actors.
- **Salvadoran migrants and Honduran peasants:** civilians affected by land, labour, expulsion and displacement pressures; avoid treating either population as homogeneous.
- **Football teams, supporters and football authorities:** public symbols and witnesses to match-day violence; not decision-makers in the interstate war.
- **IACHR and OAS:** human-rights investigation, mediation, cease-fire and regional diplomacy; powerful institutional records with procedural limits.
- **US State Department/NSC/CIA and UN:** contemporary outside records, useful for chronology and policy but not neutral omniscience.
