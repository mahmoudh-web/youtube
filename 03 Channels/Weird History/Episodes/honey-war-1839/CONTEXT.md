## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional Weird History episode “The Honey War: When Bees Triggered a Border Mobilisation.” It is a research pack, not an approved script or production brief.

## Research question

In 1839, how much of the colourful story about rival Iowa/Missouri militia mobilisations, a jailed sheriff and chopped bee trees is supported by contemporary records, and how much is later retelling?

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a source index, dated chronology, one-claim-per-row ledger, counterevidence note, uncertainty/human-verification gate and provisional conclusion.
- Separate the legal boundary dispute from later “war” language and from local folklore about bees, improvised weapons and mock funerals.
- Distinguish established facts, partisan allegations or official claims, interpretation, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Record URLs, source type, exact page/section/line locators where available, access dates, confidence and caveats.
- Stop before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication until a human verifies the primary records.

## Key decisions

- Use `honey-war-1839` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “Honey War” as a retrospective/source-attributed label. The securely documented event is a Missouri–Iowa boundary and jurisdiction crisis with threatened or mobilised force, not a conventional battle.
- Treat the bee-tree episode as a later-attested narrative until the original 1839 newspaper and court material are inspected. The located 1839 federal message contains the proclamations and boundary acts but does not itself mention bees.
- Treat militia numbers as mobilisation or requisition figures, not battlefield strength. Sources range from roughly 600 to 2,500 Missouri men, and from 500 present at an Iowa camp to a nominal Iowa force of about 1,200.
- Treat the 1849 Supreme Court decision and 1850–51 marking work as separate endpoints: legal resolution in 1849, physical resurvey/acceptance in 1851.

## Constraints and invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not call the conflict definitively “bloodless” unless the wording is explicitly attributed. The primary proclamations anticipated possible bloodshed but do not establish a complete casualty ledger.
- Do not state that exactly three or exactly four bee trees were cut; the sources conflict.
- Do not state a fixed militia total or that all called-up men reached the border.
- Do not infer that the bee-tree cutter was identified, that cutting was definitely ordered by Missouri officials, or that the incident alone caused mobilisation.
- Preserve the slavery/free-territory context as a contextual interpretation, not as a sole proven motive for every participant.
- Indigenous land title and the 1816 Osage boundary context require explicit human review before any narration frames the disputed land as an empty prize.
- Maps, markers, newspaper scans and archival images are research leads only; rights, provenance and legibility need a separate production decision.
