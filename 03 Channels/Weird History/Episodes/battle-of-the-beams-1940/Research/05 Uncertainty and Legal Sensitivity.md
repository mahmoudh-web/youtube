## Fact / attribution / interpretation / uncertainty controls

- **Fact:** The official-history account records a 21 June 1940 Anson search that detected a signal and describes the Knickebein guidance principle.
- **Attribution:** The NSA account says the Y Service intercepted a Knickebein/Kleve reference and that Churchill approved continued investigation; Price reports later German aircrew testimony and his review of raid reports.
- **Interpretation:** The “battle” is best understood as an adaptive contest in which intelligence changed engineering and engineering changed enemy procedure.
- **Uncertainty:** The located pack does not settle code-name usage, the exact operational reach of Bromide, or the causal share of jamming in the decline of German precision bombing.

Do not turn “the official account says” into “the records prove” where the underlying file has not been inspected. Do not turn Price’s later interpretation into a wartime consensus.

## Legal, ethical and editorial sensitivity

### Historical responsibility and blame

The principal actors are deceased wartime personnel and institutions. Ordinary living-person defamation risk is low, but the episode could still falsely attribute incompetence, betrayal, deliberate civilian targeting or sole responsibility. Use institutional and operational descriptions, not moral or criminal labels unsupported by the record.

### Civilian harm

The beams were connected to bombing operations that caused civilian deaths and destruction, particularly in the Blitz. Avoid making the technical contest a consequence-free puzzle or treating Coventry’s casualties as a neat experiment. Human review must approve casualty wording and ensure sources represent civilian experience, not only elite technical history.

### Intelligence secrecy

Ultra, Y Service and technical-intelligence stories can encourage retroactive certainty. Avoid revealing or dramatizing alleged source methods beyond what the cited public record supports. Do not imply a public 1940 audience knew the system’s details.

### Copyright and archival material

Public access to a PDF, catalogue page or archive transcription does not grant a YouTube reuse licence. Record the rights status separately for RAF Museum publications, TNA documents, NSA material, maps, photographs, technical diagrams and any audio. A research screenshot is not an approved production asset.

### Technical simplification

Diagrams can mislead if they show a beam as a visible line or confuse radio guidance with radar. Any visual should label it as a conceptual reconstruction, identify the source basis and show uncertainty where code words or geometry are contested.

## Human-verification stop gate

The research stops here. No narration, script, title/thumbnail package, visual-generation brief, edit plan or publication asset should be made until a human:

1. checks the official-history text and NSA account against the underlying RAF/Air Ministry intelligence trail;
2. inspects AIR 2/5238 and the AIR 20/AIR 41/HW archive leads;
3. reconciles “Ruffian,” “Bromide” and “Aspirin” dates and system mappings;
4. checks the primary raid reports behind the Coventry and four-effective-jamming claims;
5. approves a qualified explanation of “beam bending” and the non-radar distinction; and
6. clears rights and a non-gratuitous treatment of bombing and civilian harm.
