## Operational Timeline

| Date | Development | Evidence and significance |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Jan 1944 | The 23rd Headquarters Special Troops was activated at Camp Forrest. | The unit combined visual, sonic, radio, and security functions. This is the organizational starting point, not proof that every later deception worked. |
| 14 Jun 1944 | Task Force Mason, a 603rd visual-deception platoon, went to Normandy. | The Army University Press account says the unit mimicked the 980th Field Artillery for about 28 days; fake guns drew shelling while the real unit moved relatively unscathed. |
| 9–12 Aug 1944 | Brittany operation sought to make the Germans think U.S. forces were weakening the main front to turn toward Brittany. | Toomey’s Army study calls the outcome marginally successful because German forces did not withdraw from Mortain and were badly damaged at Falaise. |
| 20–27 Aug 1944 (date to verify) | Brest used all three principal deception signatures together: visual, sonic, and radio. | Official account reports German guns shifted toward the dummy armor and fake artillery drew counter-battery fire, but the wider surrender objective failed. Five real 709th tanks were lost after a coordination failure. |
| 15–22 Sep 1944 | Bettembourg placed a fictitious 6th Armored formation and division headquarters opposite German observers. | About 500 men acted as an apparent 8,000; the page cites German G-2 and 3rd Cavalry reporting that the ruse increased German forces facing the sector. |
| Dec 1944 | Kodak used radio-only deception during the Battle of the Bulge. | Official history says the pace of operations makes the effect difficult to appraise. Treat delay/confusion as a hypothesis, not an established outcome. |
| 18–24 Mar 1945 | Viersen fabricated the location and strength of formations before the Rhine crossing. | The reported captured German overlay put the 79th Division where the deception showed it and omitted the 30th; POW reporting allegedly expected the main attack north, not west. Eldridge identifies German records that complicate a total-success reading. |
| 29 Mar 1945 | Ninth Army commendation praised the special project attached to the Rhine crossing. | Evidence of command appreciation, not a causal measurement of lives saved. |

## Timeline Interpretation

The sequence moves from local protection and tactical misdirection toward a large operational deception. The strongest evidence is not that every German observer believed every dummy, but that particular combinations of visual, sonic, radio, and human-signature cues produced identifiable shifts in fire, force allocation, or expected attack direction. The record also shows failure, friendly risk, and competing German intelligence cues.
