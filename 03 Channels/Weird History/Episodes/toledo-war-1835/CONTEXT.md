## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Toledo War: The Border Fight That Cost Michigan a Coast,” about the 1835–36 Ohio–Michigan boundary dispute and its connection to Michigan statehood. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty note and provisional conclusion.
- Separate the boundary dispute, rival surveys, militia mobilisations, arrests and the Wood–Stickney stabbing from later “war” language.
- Distinguish the text of the 1836 and 1837 federal acts from later descriptions of the Upper Peninsula as “compensation.”
- Record source quality, exact page/section/line/image locators, access dates, confidence and unresolved caveats.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `toledo-war-1835` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “Toledo War” only as a source-attributed or retrospective label. The provisional account should call the underlying event a boundary dispute with armed mobilisation and enforcement actions, not a conventional battle.
- Treat Joseph Wood’s July 1835 stabbing as the best-supported casualty lead, while keeping the “sole casualty,” wound severity and exact date open until the underlying affidavit, court and medical records are checked.
- Treat the Upper Peninsula “trade” or “compensation” as shorthand for the 1836 congressional boundary/statehood settlement. The statutory text establishes boundaries and a condition for admission; it does not itself use “compensation.”
- Preserve that Michigan’s 1835 statehood proposal already included part of the eastern Upper Peninsula. “Michigan got the Upper Peninsula for Toledo” is therefore not precise enough without qualification.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not state that nobody was injured, that Wood was mortally wounded, or that a conventional battle occurred. The located sources conflict or use different definitions of casualty and war.
- Do not present Ohio as the simple legal “winner” or Michigan as the simple loser without distinguishing de facto control, the federal settlement, statehood and later resource hindsight.
- Do not state an exact militia total, exact disputed-area size, or exact Upper Peninsula fraction as settled unless the cited source and wording are named. Located sources use 250, 600 and 1,000-person figures and about 450–468 square miles.
- Avoid implying that the settlement was a cash payment. No monetary compensation claim is established by the sources in this pack.
- Indigenous land title, treaty history and the effects of territorial/state boundary-making require a dedicated human review pass before publication.
- Maps and archival documents are research leads, not approved production assets; provenance, rights and legibility require a separate human decision.
