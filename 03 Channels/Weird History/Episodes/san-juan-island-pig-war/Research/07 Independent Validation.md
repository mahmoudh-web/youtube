## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections.** The central explanation is supported: the pig shooting was an immediate trigger inside an unresolved treaty, sovereignty, settlement, and jurisdiction dispute; Harney's local military action escalated it; Scott and Douglas created a joint occupation; arbitration settled the channel question. No claim requires a fail verdict, but the pack is not ready for narration without the corrections below.

The most important controls are:

1. Use “about a company” for the first U.S. landing unless the relevant muster/order is checked. The NPS public page says 64 men; the official document later lists Company D as 66, while the administrative history describes the initial force as 60.
2. Treat 15 June 1859 as secure for the shooting, but do not present the exact position of the pig, compensation amount, or arrest threat as uncontested. The primary U.S. compilation prints the name as “Cutler”; current NPS pages use “Cutlar.” Preserve the document spelling in quotations and have a human settle the preferred identification.
3. Say “late November 1872” for the British withdrawal until the discrepancy is resolved: the current NPS page says 25 November, while the NPS administrative history says 23 November.
4. Retain Coast Salish homeland and colonial context, but add Indigenous- or community-authored/reviewed scholarship before final narration. The current evidence is principally NPS institutional interpretation.

## Chronology, names, numbers, and quotations

- **Pig date:** 15 June 1859 is supported by Griffin's same-day journal and the later affidavits.
- **Harney/Pickett dates:** the NPS administrative history dates the written protection petition and assignment to 11 July, while the pack's timeline separately states a 16 July order. The primary document inspected contains Harney's 19 July report describing the order and a 30 August force report; the exact 16 July order itself still needs direct page-image verification.
- **Landing date:** NPS sources use 27 July. The FRUS compilation's summary says 26 July, while a contemporary affidavit has a plainly inconsistent “27th of June” reference. Treat 26 July as a possible embarkation/reporting date and 27 July as the reported landing date until the order/log sequence is checked.
- **Name:** the official 1860 Senate document prints **Lyman A. Cutler** in the affidavit on printed pp. 49–50. Current NPS pages use **Lyman Cutlar**. A human should inspect the underlying affidavit/manuscript and choose a house style; quotations must retain the printed form.
- **Initial force:** S3's 30 August table lists Company D, 9th Infantry as 66; S4 says Pickett had 60; S7 says 64. The research pack's “about a company” control is correct.
- **Joint-occupation limit:** S3, printed pp. 64–65, explicitly limits each national detachment to 100 men and says the arrangement is without prejudice to either sovereignty claim. This is a safe exact number when attributed to the agreement.
- **British withdrawal:** S7 says 25 November 1872; S4 says 23 November. Use “late November” pending an original British post/transport record.
- **Short quotation:** Griffin's journal entry is directly available in the NPS digitised Bellevue Journal, p. 20: “An American shot one of my pigs for trespassing!!!” It is an interested contemporaneous observation of the shooting, not proof of the subsequent legal narrative.
- **Causal language:** Harney's own 19 July report calls San Juan “the most commanding position” and describes a possible naval-station location. This supports saying strategic considerations were present in his written justification, but not asserting a single hidden motive.

- **S2** is the U.S. Office of the Historian's edited FRUS compilation; strong diplomatic control, though retrospective compilation and containing the apparent 1858/1859 landing-date error noted above.

- **S9** is the treaty text reproduced by the U.S. Office of the Historian; strong legal record for the arbitration mechanism.
- **S10** is a contemporaneous U.S. presidential message; strong for the U.S. government's report of the award and troop-status outcome, but not neutral on the merits.

### Limited or interested sources

- **S4** and **S7** are useful NPS institutional syntheses and provide the causal chronology, but they should not replace the underlying British, HBC, military, or Indigenous records for disputed motive, threats, force totals, or harm.
- **S8** is current NPS interpretation and successfully prevents an Anglo-American-only frame, but it is not Indigenous-authored.
- **S11** is a Washington Territory legislative memorial. Its claims about jurisdiction, banishment, and deprivation are evidence of an American political grievance, not neutral proof of each alleged event.
- **S14** is a contemporary newspaper item. It records public framing and rumour; its “transported,” threat, and troop descriptions are not reliable standalone evidence.
- **S12** is an NPS environmental history with important Indigenous-use and displacement material, but the cited live URL was inaccessible on this audit and the current pack does not provide an Indigenous/community-authored counterweight.
- **S13** is a finding aid only. It supports the proposed archival route, not event claims.

Coverage is strong for the 1846 treaty, rival channel claims, HBC presence, the 1854–55 jurisdiction conflicts, Harney's escalation, Scott's compromise, and the arbitration outcome. Coverage is incomplete for British Admiralty orders, the full HBC journal/Griffin–Douglas correspondence set, the original 1859 petition and orders, the full German award and maps, Indigenous/community perspectives, civilian and resident experience, and a complete harm/casualty record.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory, or missing-source items

- **Not cleared:** “British authorities threatened to arrest Cutlar and evict every American” as an unqualified fact. The claim is present in NPS public history and American affidavits, but Dallas denied the threat and the accounts are interested and conflicting.
- **Not cleared:** “Harney invaded for strategic reasons” as a settled motive. His 19 July report explicitly described San Juan's strategic value, but the record also gives protection and jurisdictional justifications; motive should remain plural and attributed.
- **Not cleared:** exact initial troop and gun totals. Later U.S. force totals and artillery are documented, but the first landing count varies.
- **Contradictory date:** British withdrawal is 23 November in S4 versus 25 November in S7. Resolve or use “late November.”
- **Contradictory name form:** “Cutler” in the 1860 official document versus “Cutlar” in current NPS usage. Resolve from the underlying identity/record and preserve exact historical spelling in quotations.
- **Potentially overstrong:** “face-saving” and “imperial competition” are defensible interpretations, but should be presented as analysis grounded in the legal and colonial record, not as direct statements by the actors.
- **Missing perspective:** the pack acknowledges Coast Salish homeland but lacks an Indigenous- or community-authored/reviewed account specific to the occupation and its effects.
- **Missing harm record:** “only casualty” cannot be expanded into “nobody was harmed.” S12's indexed passage and the NPS context indicate Indigenous displacement, military exclusion, and colonial effects requiring further checking.
