## Narrowest defensible conclusion

On 17 October 1814, a large wooden porter vat at Meux & Co.'s Horse Shoe Brewery in St Giles ruptured after an iron hoop had fallen away. The period inquest report says the vessel was 22 feet high, held 3,555 barrels of mature porter, and burst without warning; pressure then damaged other casks and a neighbouring vat. Masonry and beer entered nearby New Street, George Street and Great Russell Street properties, where cellars and densely packed dwellings made escape difficult.

The subsequent inquest at St Giles Workhouse before Middlesex coroner George Hodgson named eight dead and recorded the verdict “Died by Casualty, accidentally, and by misfortune.” The strongest human-scale evidence concerns the named victims, the Tavistock Arms yard, the New Street houses, inhabited cellars, and the recovery testimony. The neighbourhood was a bounded part of St Giles with strong Irish and Catholic connections, but it should not be reduced to a stereotype or treated as socially uniform.

The volume and mechanics are less settled than the core event. The failed vat's 3,555 barrels must be separated from reported combined losses of 7,664 or 8,000–9,000 barrels and later conversions above 320,000 gallons or about 1.5 million litres. The “700-pound hoop,” exact clock time, modern pressure explanation, ninth death, and crowd-drinking stories remain source-dependent or disputed.

## Confidence summary

- **High:** the date, brewery, rupture, porter release, affected streets, eight inquest-listed deaths, coroner, witness names, and accidental-misfortune verdict.
- **High with attribution:** the 22-foot height, 3,555-barrel failed-vat content, fallen hoop, damaged rear wall/roof, adjacent-vessel cascade, and inhabited cellars.
- **Medium:** the exact minute of rupture; the precise second-vat volume; total beer lost; exact house-flow sequence; the full wake grouping; the legal meaning of the verdict.
- **Low / unresolved:** one definitive litre/gallon total; hoop mass; a modern engineering diagnosis; a ninth alcohol-poisoning death; mass drunkenness or hospital-riot stories; the mother's fate in the Banfield household until the conflicting reports are checked.
