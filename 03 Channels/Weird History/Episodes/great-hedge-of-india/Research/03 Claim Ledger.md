## Ledger conventions

One row contains one material claim. “Confidence” describes how safely the claim can be used after human verification of the cited record; it is not confidence in a colonial government’s characterization of its own conduct. A claim about what an official said is not automatically a claim that the official’s explanation was true.

## Interpretations that must remain labelled

- The line can be interpreted as imperial infrastructure that converted regional tax differences into a controlled internal border; this is grounded in S1, S3, S9 and S11 but is an interpretation.
- The barrier can be framed as coercive because the Acts authorized seizure, destruction, apprehension and searches, and because officials discussed subjects’ ability to obtain salt; keep the legal power and lived effect analytically distinct.
- The system may have displaced or reorganized local salt production and transit; S3–S7 support the administrative intent, but local economic outcomes need regional records.

## Not yet claimable

- That a continuous living hedge ran 2,500 miles.
- That one person, especially Allan Octavian Hume, personally designed or built the entire line.
- That all hedge labour was forced, unpaid or performed by villagers under a single labour regime.
- A single percentage for how much of a household’s income the salt tax consumed.
- That the tax caused a quantified number of deaths, diseases or famine outcomes without a specific demographic/medical source.
- That every traveller was stopped at every point, or that no one crossed outside official checkpoints.
- That the barrier was abolished everywhere on one date without a surviving notice for each segment.
