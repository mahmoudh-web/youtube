## Religious sensitivity

- This subject concerns the desecration and reburial of a deceased Catholic pope. Describe the historical actors and institutions precisely; do not turn the event into a present-day judgment on Catholic believers, Catholic doctrine, or the sanctity of modern religious practice.
- “Church,” “papacy,” “Roman clergy,” and “the faction around Stephen” are not interchangeable. Attribute actions to the specific late-ninth-century people or council for whom the evidence exists.
- Avoid comic treatment of the corpse, relics, burial, or sacramental objects. The historical strangeness can be acknowledged without ridicule.
- Explain that medieval actors were arguing about episcopal translation, ordination, papal legitimacy, burial, and political authority—not conducting a modern criminal trial in the modern legal sense.

## Partisan and medieval-source risks

### Geographical distance and selective annals

The *Annales Fuldenses* and related German annals are valuable near-contemporary evidence, but they were produced outside Rome and compress events into year entries. Absence from an annal is not proof that an event did not happen. S3/S4/S10 must be read as selective records, not transcripts.

### Later hostile or theatrical narration

Liudprand of Cremona wrote roughly six decades after the event and used a strongly rhetorical narrative. His account is unusually detailed but confuses Stephen VI with Sergius III (S9/S10, Ch. 1, pp. 1–4). Treat direct speech, “blood,” and the most theatrical sequence as reported narrative, not eyewitness fact.

### Pro-Formosan polemic

Auxilius and related writers defend Formosus and his ordinations (S12). They are essential counterevidence to the anti-Formosan settlement but have their own agenda. “Pro-Formosan” does not mean “neutral,” and “anti-Formosan” does not mean “false.”

### Composite and misclassified council records

The 898 Ravenna dossier survives in multiple manuscript forms. Older editions treated parts as separate councils; modern MGH and manuscript work revise that arrangement (S2, S6–S10). Do not cite a canon as a direct act of the 897 Cadaver Synod until a Latin specialist checks the critical edition and manuscript context.

### Translation risk

The Fulda phrase *per advocatum suae responsionis* is easy to flatten into modern legal language. “An advocate was used to make a response on his behalf” is safer than asserting a formal defence procedure. Latin terms for deposition, translation between sees, ordination, and burial carry period-specific legal and ecclesiastical meanings.

## Open questions requiring human verification

1. Was the trial held in January 897, in December 896, or at another point within the late 896/early 897 window?
2. Which surviving Ravenna capitula reflect the Cadaver Synod, which reflect Theodore’s or John IX’s rehabilitation process, and which belong to an imperial capitulary?
3. Was the Tiber episode a second exhumation after reburial, and who ordered or carried it out?
4. Were two or three fingers removed? Were they the fingers associated with blessing, consecration, or a later explanatory tradition?
5. Was a deacon formally appointed to answer for Formosus, or is that detail a later annalistic expansion? Compare the *Annales Fuldenses*, Alamannici, Laubacenses, and the critical Ravenna material.
6. What did Lambert and Ageltrude know or command, if anything? No direct order has been located in this pack.
7. Did John IX hold a distinct Roman synod, or has later scholarship separated a Ravenna dossier into councils that were not independently documented?
8. How should the later Sergius III revival of anti-Formosan policy be separated from the 897 trial in a chronological account?
9. Can the Latin editions and manuscript images be checked by a specialist in ninth-/tenth-century ecclesiastical Latin before any quotation is approved?

## Legal and ethical handling

- The principal subjects are long-dead historical figures, so ordinary modern defamation risk is low; the ethical risk is misrepresenting religious history or attributing unsupported wrongdoing to named people.
- Use “alleged,” “reported,” “the annal says,” or “the later polemic claims” where the ledger marks a source-dependent detail.
- Do not state that the modern Catholic Church “put a corpse on trial.” The evidence concerns a specific Roman papacy and clergy in 897, while later Catholic records repudiated the proceedings.
- Do not infer modern doctrinal continuity from the event. The rehabilitation of Formosus and the later ordination controversy belong to a contested period of medieval canon law and papal politics.
