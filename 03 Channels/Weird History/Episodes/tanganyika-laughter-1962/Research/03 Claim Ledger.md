## Wording gate

Current wording that may advance only after human verification:

> In January 1962, a medical report from Tanganyika described recurrent attacks of laughing and crying that began at a girls’ school near Bukoba and later appeared in villages and other schools. The report recorded restlessness, occasional violence when people were restrained, normal findings in the tests it performed and no reported fatalities. Its authors tentatively suggested “mass hysteria” and called for cultural study. A later scholar proposed motor-variant mass psychogenic illness, but that is an interpretation—not a diagnosis of the pupils, and not proof that laughter itself was contagious.

This wording intentionally omits a triggering joke, continuous laughter, a national outbreak, a fixed final case count, a definitive duration, a biological virus, a toxin, a diagnosis of individual people and a claim that independence caused the event.
