## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Exploding Whale: Why Oregon Used Dynamite on a Beach,” about the November 1970 disposal of a beached sperm whale near Florence, Oregon. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/legal-sensitivity note and provisional conclusion.
- Reconstruct the decision chain without collapsing the Oregon State Highway Division, the local district office, George Thornton, the U.S. Navy consultation and later ODOT retellings into one undifferentiated “government” actor.
- Separate established fact, allegation, interpretation and uncertainty.
- Record the contemporary alternatives that were considered or reported as impractical, and distinguish them from later recollections.
- Preserve the provenance and rights uncertainty of the KATU footage held by the Oregon Historical Society.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key decisions

- Use `exploding-whale-1970` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat “Oregon used dynamite” as a shorthand for a local Highway Division disposal decision. The current record does not establish a governor-level, headquarters-level or formal written authorization above Assistant District Highway Engineer George Thornton.
- Treat burial, rendering, cutting and burning as documented at different source layers. Towing/haul-out is excluded from the current alternatives claim because no traceable source was supplied.
- Treat the KATU news report as an archival source with identifiable creators and institutional custody, not as a cleared production asset. OHS confirms the original 16mm film was donated by KATU and transferred at 4K, while the OHS catalogue states that copyright for the broadcasts is held by KATU-TV and marks the material In Copyright.
- Avoid graphic stills and avoid “blunder,” “negligence,” “reckless,” or “only casualty” as narrator voice. Attribute criticism and preserve the distinction between no reported human injury and the documented property damage and exposure to debris.

## Constraints and invariants

- Access dates in the initial pack were 2026-09-14; this correction pass was made 2026-09-15.
- The carcass was initially called a Pacific gray whale in the 11 November newspaper and KATU narration; later identification as a sperm whale is supported by the teeth/jaw observation and institutional corrections. Do not present the initial identification as settled fact.
- The 1970 newspaper account says officials expected scavengers to finish the remains; the result was a partial failure followed by burial of larger pieces. Do not imply the explosion cleanly disposed of the carcass.
- “No one was hurt” is a bounded report about the blast; it does not mean the event was harmless. A large piece damaged a car, debris passed over spectators and people were covered in smaller particles.
- No script, title/thumbnail package, visual brief or production asset is authorized by this pack. Human verification must record the decision chain, exact source scans, approval/Navy records, footage rights and graphic-content treatment first.
