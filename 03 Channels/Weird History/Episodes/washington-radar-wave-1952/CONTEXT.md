## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “When Washington’s Radar Screens Filled with Unidentified Objects,” about the radar-visual sightings around Washington National Airport and Andrews Air Force Base on 19–20 and 26–27 July 1952. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty note and provisional conclusion.
- Keep the two July weekends separate and distinguish radar returns, visual reports, official explanations and later interpretations.
- Test temperature inversion/anomalous propagation, ordinary misidentification or radar artefact, secret-technology and extraterrestrial hypotheses without treating any as proven by “unidentified.”
- Stop for explicit human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `washington-radar-wave-1952` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat Project Blue Book Case 1661 as a contemporaneous Air Force record reproduced from National Archives material, while recording that the file itself says it was incomplete.
- Treat the CAA Technical Development Report No. 180 as a primary technical explanation for a class of unidentified radar targets, not as a complete adjudication of every July 1952 visual or radar report.
- Use “unidentified targets,” “reported lights” and “official hypothesis” as evidence-level language. Do not turn the case into evidence of extraterrestrial craft.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for the initial pack was 2026-09-14; this validator-response revision was accessed on 2026-09-15.
- The July 19–20 and July 26–27 episodes must not be collapsed into one continuous incident.
- The Air Force case file states that two interceptor flights’ official reports had not arrived and that commercial and CAA pilots had not yet been contacted. Missing records are part of the evidence state.
- The 1953 CAA report includes July entries in Table I and correlates the tabulated records with weather data, but its formal tracked-target motion conclusions are explicitly about Washington MEW observations beginning 13 and 15 August 1952. It should not be presented either as a complete July verdict or as having no July analysis.
- “Temperature inversion” explains a mechanism for anomalous radar propagation; it does not, by itself, establish what any witness saw visually.
- The record contains historical military, aviation and UFO terminology. Quote sparingly, identify the speaker or document, and do not use “UFO” as a synonym for extraterrestrial vehicle.
- NARA catalogue metadata confirms the relevant Blue Book case-file, Air Intelligence Report, and motion-picture series, but no item-level F-94 mission report or complete pilot file was verified in the accessible catalogue during this revision. Archival scans, newspaper clippings and government video are research leads only. Rights, provenance, legibility and any future reuse require a separate human decision.
