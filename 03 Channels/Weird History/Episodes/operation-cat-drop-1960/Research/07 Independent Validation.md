## Scope and method

I read all six research files: `01 Source Index`, `02 Timeline`, `03 Claim Ledger`, `04 Counterevidence`, `05 Uncertainty and Human Verification`, and `06 Conclusion`. I audited the material claims C01–C18, the timeline’s additional dated claims, the conclusion against the counterevidence, source quality, chronology, geographic boundaries, causal wording, and the stated legal, ethical, medical, animal-welfare and cultural sensitivities.

The cited sources were checked independently on 16 September 2026. Where a primary page was inaccessible, the result is marked partial or unresolved rather than treated as verified. No researcher file was edited.

## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections; suitable to send to human verification only.** The pack’s central caution is sound: a small March 1960 RAF delivery to Bario is strongly supported by a reproduced RAF Operations Record Book (ORB) and a contemporary newspaper trail, while the DDT-to-cat-deaths-to-rats causal chain is not established. The pack correctly separates Sarawak from North Borneo/Sabah and rejects the 14,000-cat version as unsupported by the located records.

The pack is not ready for narration or production because the load-bearing RAF ORB page and the four newspaper page images were not directly inspected. The 2024 National Library Board (NLB) retrospective also turns 17 March, the *Singapore Free Press* publication date, into the apparent flight date; this conflicts with the reproduced ORB date of 13 March. Exact count, altitude, five test runs, requestor, aircraft detail, landing account and outcome therefore remain source-attributed pending primary-page checks.

## Additional timeline validation

- **1950:** PubMed verifies J. McArthur’s title, author, journal, date and scope across Borneo, but reports no abstract. The timeline’s “established context” label is reasonable; the full article was not inspected. [McArthur record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24538665/)
- **1952–1955:** S1 and S2 support the Sarawak pilot, its location and control results. The S2 OCR has a visible “1934” error; use the scan and S1 rather than repeating that OCR text.
- **1954 RAF DDT supply airdrop:** O’Shaughnessy lines 152–153 says the 1956 WHO paper mentions an RAF airdrop replenishing DDT. This is a secondary account of the primary paper and should remain “reported” until the full paper is read.
- **1955 World Health Assembly:** O’Shaughnessy lines 75–80 supports the May 1955 global programme context and Sarawak insecticide chronology. It does not support a Bario-specific WHO order.
- **1955 dieldrin:** O’Shaughnessy lines 77–80 supports use of dieldrin in Sarawak and its reported discontinuation, but this is a scholarly synthesis. Do not assign dieldrin to the Bario event without a local primary record.
- **1956 publication and 1959 Sarawak transition:** S1 bibliographic data and S2 lines 54–56 support the publication and the transition toward eradication. No source checked connects the project transition to the cat delivery.
- **9, 17 and 22 March 1960; 18 April 1960:** the NLB retrospective and endnotes support these publication dates and report contents, but the four NLB page images were inaccessible. The 17 March date is a publication date; it must not replace the reproduced ORB’s 13 March working flight date.
- **1965 Harrisson:** S4 supports the existence and content of the cited *Animals* account, but not its operational or causal truth. The original is required to determine whether 1965 is a mistake, a separate event or a retrospective dating.
- **1968 Gordon Harrison and Conway:** S4 lines 141–146 and 166 support their role in later popularisation and their dependence on prior accounts. Their original publications were not independently inspected; classify their material as later interpretation/retelling.
- **1971 Jukes:** S4 lines 162–162 supports the dose calculation as reported by O’Shaughnessy, but the article’s endnote 58 is bibliographically incomplete (“Thomas Jukes” without a complete title/journal locator). Obtain the original before quoting the calculation.
- **2005:** S4 endnote 64 and the searchable newsletter reproduction support reception history; direct WHO PDF access failed. Do not treat it as a WHO operational report.
- **2008:** The peer-reviewed article’s metadata and full text are accessible; its conclusion and evidence limits are accurately represented. [O’Shaughnessy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636426/)
- **2024:** The NLB article is a dated official retrospective and useful source-discovery aid. It is not a substitute for its cited 1960 newspaper images or the RAF ORB.

## Source quality and coverage

1. **Strongest checked evidence:** the 1956 WHO-linked scientific report’s PubMed abstract (S1), the official Wellcome scan/OCR of the Sarawak annual report (S2), and the verbatim/near-verbatim discussion of the RAF ORB in the peer-reviewed O’Shaughnessy review (S4).
2. **Primary but not directly inspected:** the RAF ORB (S5), 1959 North Borneo annual report (S3), four NLB newspaper pages (S7–S10), WHO newsletter PDF (S11), and Cheng’s full 1963 WHO article (S14). The direct blockers were, respectively, absent original scan in the pack, oversized PDF fetch, NLB JavaScript/robot challenge, WHO PDF fetch error, and PMC access challenge.
3. **Secondary or retrospective:** O’Shaughnessy (S4) is a peer-reviewed historical synthesis; the NLB BiblioAsia article (S6) is an official retrospective; the WHO former-staff newsletter (S11) is institutional reception history, not an operational record.
4. **Catalogue/finding aids only:** the Cornell Harrisson collection guide (S12) and Sarawak State Library catalogue (S13) identify possible archival routes but do not establish event facts.
5. **Coverage gap:** no checked source provides a Bario-specific veterinary, toxicological or ecological survey proving cat deaths, DDT biomagnification, rat-population change or long-term success. No checked source establishes WHO authorization.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory claims to hold back

- The **17 March flight date** is an NLB retrospective’s conflation of event and publication chronology. Working evidence favors 13 March from the reproduced ORB; 17 March should remain the *Singapore Free Press* publication date until reconciled.
- **23 cats, 400 feet, five test runs, wicker-basket method, hundreds of spectators, John Seal’s exact wording and the “all landed” details** are supported in the NLB retrospective, but the cited newspaper page image and original ORB were not inspected. Use attribution, not unqualified fact.
- **“McSporran,” “MacSporran” and “McSpomran”** vary across later reproductions. The name, role, and whether the speaker was the person on the ground in the ORB require the original page and the cited Heimann/Morrison sources.
- **WHO ordered, planned or carried out the drop** is unsupported by the checked sources. The ORB reproduction identifies RAF action and a Bario message; O’Shaughnessy explicitly notes no WHO operational report acknowledging involvement.
- **DDT killed Bario’s cats; spraying caused Bario’s rat crisis; roofs collapsed in Bario; geckos/lizards transmitted DDT to cats; plague or typhus followed; cats solved the crisis** are unsupported or geographically conflated. Keep them as hypotheses or later retellings only.
- **14,000 cats** is a later variant not supported by the located ORB/newspaper trail. Retain only as a myth-history discrepancy.
- **All cats survived long-term** is not established by the immediate “all cats safe” message.

## Required corrections and additional primary-source checks

1. Amend the working chronology in any future derivative document to distinguish 13 March (ORB-reported flight) from 17 March (newspaper publication).
2. Obtain and inspect the original No. 48 Squadron March 1960 ORB page. Record aircraft, crew, departure, destination, full load, cat wording, named recipient, message text, and whether the record says “over 20” or another number.
3. Inspect the 9 March, 17 March, 22 March and 18 April NLB page images. Record exact headline, dateline, byline/requestor, count, altitude, packing method, landing/survival wording and whether each statement is a quotation, telegram or reporter’s description.
4. Obtain the complete 1959 North Borneo annual-report scan and verify p. 64, including the insecticide named in the surrounding passage and the 42,000-rat figure.
5. Read the complete S1 paper and the Cheng paper (S14), and inspect the Wellcome scan rather than relying on OCR. Keep all roof, cat-death and insecticide claims geographically specific.
6. Obtain Colbourne, Huehne and LaChance, “The Sarawak Anti-Malaria Project” (1960), the Sarawak State Library DOL/12/1 files and adjacent 1960 eradication records. Search specifically for Bario, Kelabit, cats, rats, DDT, dieldrin and supply drops.
7. Obtain Harrisson’s original 1965 *Animals* article, the Heimann and Morrison passages, and Conway/Harrison originals. Label firsthand testimony, hearsay, interpretation and literary embellishment separately.
8. Resolve the spelling and role of McSporran/MacSporran/McSpomran from a primary or archival source.
9. Seek veterinary, toxicological or public-health records for Sarawak-specific cat deaths. Do not infer biomagnification from spraying, regional cat deaths or later ecological diagrams.
10. Preserve the pack’s current community and animal-welfare safeguards. Confirm that any future framing names the Kelabit community specifically, avoids undifferentiated labels, does not make colonial or institutional blame claims without records, and does not invent animal suffering or survival scenes.
