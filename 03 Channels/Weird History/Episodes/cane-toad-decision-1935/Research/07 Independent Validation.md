## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS.** The pack's central, evidence-bounded account is substantially supported: BSES and R. W. Mungomery are documented as the operational actors in importing, breeding and releasing the toads; overseas evidence was used to justify the proposal; a period account explicitly recorded that Queensland greyback beetles behaved differently from Puerto Rican beetles; the Commonwealth prohibited further distribution; and later sources document limited target-pest control and species-specific ecological impacts.

The pack is not fully verified yet. Correct the egg-count/date claim (C9), keep the 100/101/102 animal discrepancy explicit, retain the distinction between a period warning and the later Froggatt publication, and do not turn later accounts of the 1936 lobbying/reversal into a settled formal approval chain. The “no pre-release testing” conclusion must remain an evidence-bounded finding (“not located in the checked record”), because the period sources did contain some diet, habitat and pest-risk discussion even though they do not show a systematic Australian effectiveness or ecological-risk assessment.

The pack is **safe to send to human verification with these corrections flagged**. It is not narration-ready, and this validation does not approve the episode or move it beyond In Review.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strengths

- **S1, S3 and S14, QAJ:** period departmental publications. They are the strongest evidence for what BSES published, believed and reported in 1935–41. The relevant PDF text was accessible through archive search extraction, but original page-by-page scan inspection remains necessary before quotation or exact-number use.
- **S2, State Library of Queensland:** useful institutional reproduction of Courier-Mail, Rockhampton Morning Bulletin, Queenslander and Sydney Morning Herald material. It directly supports the local-beetle caveat and the broad warning/ban chronology, but the original Trove images should be inspected before treating the reproduction as a final quotation.
- **S4, official Queensland history:** strong retrospective synthesis that exposes the BSES operational chain, the Commonwealth prohibition, the September 1936 date and later release locations. It is not an authorisation instrument and contains the internally problematic C9 date/citation.
- **S5, Queensland Government fact sheet:** adequate official synthesis for the limited-control explanation and current spread context; it is not a substitute for the 1941 QAJ or BSES records.
- **S6, National Museum of Australia:** clear institutional synthesis for the broad chronology and explicit evidence-gap wording. Its “no studies” sentence should remain bounded and should not be expanded into an absolute historical negative.
- **S11–S12, peer-reviewed ecological literature:** appropriate for later impact mechanisms and named species/site findings, not for 1935 responsibility or intent.

### Weaknesses and blocked or incomplete checks

- The original BSES import, quarantine, breeding and release records remain absent. This is the largest gap for exact counts, dates, sites, life stages and authorisation.
- The Commonwealth prohibition notice/file, statutory basis, scope and exact date remain unlocated. “Late 1935” is supportable; a precise legal description is not.
- The September 1936 reversal is supported by retrospective official and institutional accounts, but the correspondence between BSES, Bulcock, Forgan Smith, Lyons and the Health Department was not independently inspected.
- S10, the Griggs article, was not independently opened in full. Treat it as a secondary lead; do not use its wording as if it were a primary record.
- The Froggatt quotation is independently reproduced by NMA and SLQ and bibliographically corroborated by later scholarly references, but the original 1936 *Australian Naturalist* issue still needs inspection before using the wider argument or exact publication chronology.
- The 2019 parliamentary page is useful modern context, but the page warns that parts of the inquiry relied on draft hearing transcripts. It should not be used for a 1935 decision claim or a quotation without checking final records.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory items

1. **C9's egg-count date is not safe.** The official history's August 1935 citation cannot establish a measurement “by 17 November 1935,” and later QAJ text uses 17 November 1935 as the starting point for a later cumulative count. Remove or correct it after inspecting the original journal pages.
2. **The import count must remain approximate.** The pack's 100/101 caveat is appropriate, but independently checked later summaries also report 102 captured and one lost in transit.
3. **The first-release exact date and site list remain secondary.** “About 2,400 in August around Gordonvale” is supportable; 19 August and a definitive list of sites need the BSES release record.
4. **The evidence-gap wording is good but must stay bounded.** Period sources considered diet, beneficial insects, habitat and possible harm. The defensible negative is “no completed Australian effectiveness test or systematic environmental assessment has been located,” not “no one considered risk.”
5. **The 1936 reversal is not yet a complete responsibility finding.** Keep the BSES/industry/Queensland-government/federal sequence, but do not assign a sole authoriser or a direct Lyons order without administrative correspondence.
6. **The 1950 problem-species date/legal phrasing is only institutionally reported here.** Verify the relevant declaration before using it as a legal fact.
7. **Later ecological claims are correctly cautious.** Preserve species, site, mechanism and variation. Do not use “the toads killed Australia's wildlife” or an ecosystem-wide absolute.

## Counterevidence and competing interpretations

The pack's counterevidence is materially sound and should remain in the handoff:

- The period case was not evidence-free: Bell, Puerto Rican observations, stomach-content work and Hawaii experience supplied a contemporary rationale.
- The contemporary local-beetle caveat is stronger than a hindsight-only criticism, but it does not prove that every decision-maker understood or rejected it.
- The period press also said that potential harm had been considered and no evidence then showed the toad would be harmful. This supports a conclusion of incomplete/overconfident assessment, not proof of deliberate disregard.
- The 1941 QAJ report records some target-beetle feeding even while later government material describes control as limited.
- Modern ecological science supports major, species-specific toxic-ingestion effects while also documenting spatial/temporal variation and adaptation. The pack correctly avoids universal wildlife-collapse language.

- retrieve the Commonwealth Health Department prohibition notice/file, including date, legal basis, scope and wording;

- verify the 1950 declaration if the episode uses that exact legal wording;
- retain the pack's legal, ethical and animal-welfare cautions, including no unsupported personal negligence, bad faith or universal ecological causation claims.
