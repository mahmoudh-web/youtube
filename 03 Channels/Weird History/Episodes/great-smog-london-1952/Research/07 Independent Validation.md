## Scope and method

I checked all 20 claim-ledger rows, the timeline’s material dates and statistics, the counterevidence controls, the uncertainty register and the conclusion wording. I opened the cited web records where available and recorded the exact URL and locator below. I did not edit any researcher file.

The audit distinguishes a source’s support for a claim from the strength of the source itself. A catalogue record, abstract, transcript or later institutional summary can corroborate a lead, but it cannot substitute for inspecting the underlying report or table where the claim is load-bearing.

## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — suitable to send to human verification, not suitable for approval or production.**

The pack’s central account is evidence-bounded and unusually careful about the competing mortality figures, geography, event-window definitions, influenza question and policy causation. The principal correction is material: the exact Scott sulphur-dioxide value is not safe to quote as `1.339 ppm` from the accessible OCR. The source renders it as `1,339 parts per million`; the decimal placement and table image must be checked against the original before any exact concentration is used. Several other claims need narrower wording or explicit attribution, especially source shares, the “about 4,000” estimate, public-service disruption and the role of influenza.

The result is not a human approval, scientific sign-off, legal conclusion or publication recommendation. No script, narration, title, thumbnail, visual concept, production file, upload metadata or creative recommendation is authorised by this validation.

## Timeline and conclusion checks

- The 5–8 versus 5–9 December distinction is a genuine source-definition difference, not a contradiction requiring one date to be deleted. Scott’s article, S2 PDF p. 1 / article p. 474, calls the intense fog 5–8 December; the Met Office, National Archives transcript and POSTnote use 5–9 December. The pack handles this correctly when it calls 5–9 the public event window and 5–8 a medical/technical phase.
- The mortality figures are not interchangeable. S2 records 2,484 in the administrative county; S3 records 4,703 in Greater London; S6 describes at least 4,000 above normal during the smog and following weeks; S5 estimates about 12,000 from December through February. The pack’s separation is correct. No source checked here resolves the boundary/registration definitions sufficiently to combine C6 and C7.
- The timeline’s 25 November 1954 Beaver-report publication date is supported by S10: on 24 November the Minister said the final report would be published “tomorrow.” The Wellcome catalogue S9, https://wellcomecollection.org/works/nu77fcsw, confirms the 1954 Cmd. 9322 report is 80 pages and available as a public-domain scan, but the scan was not reviewed page by page in this pass.
- The timeline’s 5 July 1954 City of London measure is supported by the parliamentary explanatory notes at S13, https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/bills/lbill/58-03/005/5803005en03.htm, paragraph 4, and by the Act scan at https://cleanair.london/app/uploads/City-of-London_VP_Act-1954.pdf, title/date and section 4. It was a local measure prohibiting smoke emission in the City, not the UK-wide 1956 Act.
- The conclusion’s systems explanation is supported. The Ministry statement itself says the chemical increases were initially better treated as a measure of fuel consumption, principally coal, than as proof of one specially adverse constituent. That is an important reason to retain the pack’s mixed-source and non-exclusive wording.
- The conclusion’s phrase “the death signal that followed” is supportable as a temporal description, but “caused” should remain qualified because cold weather, pre-existing disease, pollutant mixture, influenza and baseline choice are part of the unresolved causal analysis.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory or missing-source items

1. **Exact SO₂ concentration (C5) is the material correction.** The accessible Scott text says `1,339 parts per million`; the pack’s `1.339 ppm` is an interpretation of a damaged/OCR-rendered number. It may be the intended decimal value, but that inference is not evidence. Do not publish the number until the original table is inspected.
2. **Episode-specific source shares are not established.** Domestic, industrial, power-generation, transport and other sources appear in the records, but the pack does not have a checked source-apportionment table. “Major” should be removed or qualified.
3. **The “about 4,000” figure has a scope problem even when the number is supported.** It is an above-normal estimate covering the smog and following weeks in S6, while C6 is a registered Greater London total for one week and C9 is a later extended-period model. The pack generally controls this, but the claim-ledger label “established immediate public-health estimate” is too strong.
4. **“Public life” and service anecdotes are under-sourced.** Broad travel disruption is supported; individual claims about buses, ambulances, railways, theatres, cinemas, livestock, crime and people becoming lost need a dated contemporary source each.
5. **The Ministry report and Beaver report remain uninspected load-bearing primary sources.** Their catalogues prove existence, not the detailed claims assigned to them in the research question or future narration.
6. **Influenza remains an evidence conflict, not a settled fact.** Bell and Davis reject influenza alone as a complete explanation; the original Ministry report’s full interpretation is still missing from the audit. State whose analysis is being reported.
7. **The policy sequence is supported; exclusive causation is not.** Hansard shows public framing and recommendations, while the statute shows legal content. Neither proves the smog single-handedly caused the 1956 Act or all later clean-air regulation.
8. **Logan and detailed Wilkins claims are not independently checked.** Their bibliographic/abstract records are useful leads only. Any exact Logan estimate, Wilkins table, source share, chemical claim or spatial comparison requires the full paper.

- change C3 to a mixed-source formulation unless the original Beaver/Wilkins records provide checked episode-specific source apportionment;

- inspect the complete 1954 Ministry of Health report, especially methods, tables, the 4,000 scope, post-fog mortality and influenza discussion;
- inspect the complete 1954 Beaver report, including recommendations, source evidence, medical conclusions and implementation/cost language;
- obtain and read Scott, Logan and Wilkins in full, recording geography, units, sites, baselines, lag periods and causal wording;
- inspect the original MH 58/398 item and compare it with the National Archives transcript/audio;
- locate original Meteorological Office observations to resolve event-phase wording without erasing the 5–8 versus 5–9 distinction;
- inspect the City of London (Various Powers) Act 1954 and commencement material separately from the 1956 Act;
- check contemporary London newspapers, London County Council transport/health records, Emergency Bed Service records and hospital records before using exact disruption claims;
- read Bell and Davis’s full methods and sensitivity analysis before treating the 12,000 estimate or influenza conclusion as anything more than a reported later interpretation.
