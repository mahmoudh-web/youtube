## Research question

How did a dispute over the legal status of spiny lobsters and the scope of Brazil’s continental-shelf resource rights escalate from fishing incidents into naval deployments, and what can the record safely establish about the later settlement?

## Conflict and episode promise

The popular version is a “war” caused by an argument over whether lobsters swim or jump. The traceable record is more precise: French vessels sought to fish off Brazil’s northeastern coast; Brazil treated the lobster as a resource associated with its submarine platform and required authorization; France challenged that position using the unsettled law of the continental shelf and sought arbitration; both governments then used naval presence to support their positions. The episode’s unanswered angle is how a technical legal category became a test of sovereignty, local fishing economies and coercive diplomacy during the Cold War without turning into open combat.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or official:** decree, official legal opinion, diplomatic/archive record, parliamentary record or institutional Navy record.
- **B — contemporaneous evidence:** 1963 journalistic or specialist account close to the dispute, used with source and viewpoint caveats.
- **C — scholarly/institutional secondary:** academic law/history, government repository or later research synthesis that identifies and interprets primary material.
- **D — later popular/retrospective:** useful for reception history and leads, not sufficient alone for a material claim.

## Key entities and voices

- **João Goulart:** Brazilian president during the 1962–63 escalation and the issuer of the temporary authorization described by later sources.
- **Charles de Gaulle:** French president whose government sent *Tartu* to protect French fishing interests; the scope of any larger force movement requires verification.
- **Paulo de Castro Moreira da Silva:** Brazilian naval officer/oceanographer associated in later accounts with the “kangaroo” analogy; exact provenance of the quotation remains open.
- **Haroldo Valladão:** Brazilian Consultor Jurídico whose official 1962 and 1963 opinions set out Brazil’s legal position and response to French arbitration proposals.
- **Brazilian Navy and northeastern jangadeiros:** The state institution and coastal fishing communities invoked in Brazil’s sovereignty/resource argument; their interests should not be treated as identical without local economic records.
- **Breton lobster fishers and armateurs:** French commercial actors whose declining traditional catches and economic dependence are described in later legal reporting; verify with French fisheries records.
- **Ships most often named:** Brazilian *Ipiranga*, *Purus*, *Forte de Coimbra*, *Paraná*, *Pará*, *Pernambuco*, *Barroso* and other task-force units; French *Tartu*, *Paul Goffeny*, *Clemenceau* and fishing vessels including *Cassiopée*, *Lonk Ael*, *Plomarc’h* and *Françoise Christine*.
