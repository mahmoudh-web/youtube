## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional Weird History episode “The CIA’s Cat Spy: Project or Public Myth?” It examines the 1960s CIA animal-eavesdropping work later associated with Acoustic Kitty, separating the documented research program from the taxi-death and “million-dollar cat” legend.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, chronology, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification gate and evidence-bounded conclusion.
- Distinguish CIA self-description, declassified internal records, former-participant testimony, contemporary reporting and later retellings.
- Establish what was tested, what the available record shows was judged impractical, and what is not established as an operational mission.
- Treat animal experimentation, secrecy, redaction and intelligence methods as ethical and editorial-risk areas requiring human review.
- Stop for explicit human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `cia-cat-spy` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Call the project “Acoustic Kitty” only as the later/publicly used label unless a human reviewer verifies the redacted 1967 title and codename chain.
- The safest factual description is a CIA Directorate of Science and Technology research effort that implanted listening equipment in at least one cat, conducted training/field tests, and ended without operational deployment according to the CIA’s current account.
- Treat the taxi story, exact device configuration, exact target, five-year cost and the identity of every participant as unresolved unless independently documented.
- Do not conflate cat research with adjacent CIA animal programs involving dogs, dolphins, birds or later robotic animals.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- The central declassified memo is heavily redacted. Its surviving language supports short-distance training and a conclusion that the technique was not practical for specialized intelligence needs; it does not provide a complete operational narrative.
- The CIA’s 2024 public account says the cat was unharmed and that the taxi/bus death story came from a former official who later said it was a joke. This is an attributed CIA account, not independently verified veterinary or field documentation.
- No located source establishes a successful intelligence collection mission, a formal operational approval, an exact dollar cost or an official congressional/inspector-general investigation devoted to Acoustic Kitty.
- Do not state “the CIA spent $15/20/25 million” as fact. Those figures are later estimates or retellings and are not supported by the located official record.
- Avoid graphic description of surgery. Describe implanted equipment only as needed to explain the research question, and foreground the evidence gap around procedure, care and outcome.
- Do not present the 2001 press account as a contemporary 1960s record. It is contemporary to the declassification/publicity event and repeats a former-officer account.
