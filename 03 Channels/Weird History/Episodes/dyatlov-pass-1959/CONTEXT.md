## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “Dyatlov Pass: What the File Actually Says about Nine Deaths (Urals, 1959).” It is a research pack, not a script, creative brief, visual plan, or production package.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable six-file research pack under `Research/`: source index, dated timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/legal-sensitivity note, and evidence-bounded conclusion.
- Reconstruct the expedition, scene discovery, body recovery, forensic record, 1959 closure, 2018–20 prosecutor review, and 2021 avalanche model from primary records and clearly attributed later sources.
- Keep the original file’s wording separate from later prosecutor interpretation, scientific modelling, popular theories, and unresolved questions.
- Stop for independent validation before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC, or publication.

## Key decisions

- Use `dyatlov-pass-1959` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat the 1959 case-file scans/transcriptions as the evidentiary anchor. The archive host says the Dyatlov Foundation received an official copy from the State Archive of the Sverdlovsk Region; the host and its translations are not themselves a state archival finding.
- Preserve the 1959 closure wording (“overwhelming force” in the available English transcription) rather than silently translating it into “avalanche.”
- Treat the 2020 prosecutor conclusion as a later official investigative interpretation: a snow slab/avalanche plus severe weather, reported through the prosecutor’s press account. It is not a judicial verdict or a time-stamped reconstruction.
- Treat Gaume and Puzrin’s 2021 peer-reviewed paper as a physical plausibility model. It addresses delayed slab release and impact injuries under specified assumptions; it explicitly does not solve every historical detail.
- Treat beta contamination on selected clothing as a documented 1959 laboratory result. Do not convert it into evidence of a nuclear incident without a source that identifies the radionuclide, pathway, and context.

## Constraints and invariants

- Pack access date: 2026-09-19.
- Do not use “solved,” “murder,” “secret weapon,” “Mansi attack,” “UFO,” or “nuclear test” as factual descriptions. The located record does not establish those claims.
- “Violent death” in the translated autopsy conclusions describes traumatic death; it is not, by itself, a finding of homicide.
- The three relevant geometries must remain visible: the 1959 tent protocol says 30°; another 1959 case-file summary says about 18–20°; the 2021 paper models a locally steeper area around 28°. Resolve the measurement basis before a map or narration asserts one angle.
- The 1959 weather note is explicitly described by its host as material found with the case documents, not an official case-file response; the nearest station was 47 miles away. Do not present its readings as a direct measurement at the tent.
- Avoid graphic autopsy detail. Use only what is necessary to explain the evidentiary dispute, with respectful wording and no post-mortem imagery in this research pack.
