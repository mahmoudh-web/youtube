## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections.** The pack is unusually careful about separating documented events, official conclusions, private claims and unresolved interpretation. The core chronology and the Air Force/DOE account are supported by multiple official records. The pack correctly refuses to turn the object into a confirmed live hydrogen bomb, a known seabed coordinate, a proven recovery, or a present-day safety conclusion.

It is not ready for script or production approval without the corrections below. Most importantly, the original 1966 W.J. Howard document remains uninspected, the original 1958 search records and complete 2004–05 survey dataset remain missing, and the AL-569 receipt supports the custody restriction more directly than it proves the full later configuration conclusion.

## Chronology, names, numbers and wording controls

- **Time:** retain the unresolved S1 “approximately 2:00 a.m.” versus S2/S5 “3:30 a.m.” discrepancy. It is not safe to silently select one.
- **Search duration:** S1 says nine-plus weeks, S4 says ten weeks and S9 says a 90-day search. These may reflect rounding or different counting conventions. Use “weeks-long” or cite the specific source’s figure.
- **Survey-team size:** S4 says 20 people; S3 says 23. The pack should not state one fixed number without explaining whether the figures refer to different teams or counting dates.
- **Weight and speed:** the official report gives approximately 7,600 pounds and about 200 knots; S2/S5 give 7,200 feet and 180–190 knots. S4/S9 use rounded 7,500/7,600-pound figures. Keep “approximately” and do not harmonize the speeds into a false precision.
- **Names:** S4 identifies Derek Duke; S8 identifies Col. Howard Richardson and Rep. Jack Kingston; S10 also reports Howard H. Dixon. These are attributed accounts. Do not add biographies, awards or allegations without separate primary checks.
- **Location:** S1/S2/S5 support “Wassaw Sound area off Tybee Beach” and an unknown impact point. The more specific “south side of Little Tybee” appears in S8 as a reported account and must not replace the official area wording.
- **Configuration:** S6 visually verifies Mk 15 Mod 0, serial 47782, dated 4 February 1958, plus the restriction that no active capsule was to be inserted during the temporary custody. It does not by itself resolve whether “complete weapon” in Howard’s 1966 table had a different administrative meaning.
- **Causal language:** “permission was granted” is supported by S1/S4; “to avoid the possibility of conventional-explosive detonation” is supported. Avoid implying that the crew knew the weapon was incapable of nuclear detonation unless the source is explicitly attributed.
- **No detonation versus no recovery:** the records support no observed detonation and failure of the documented search. They do not prove destruction, movement, secret recovery or continued presence.

## Unsupported, overstated or missing-source claims

1. The original 1966 Howard letter is the decisive missing source. Reporting is sufficient to record an apparent conflict, not to interpret “complete weapon” as a loaded nuclear device.
2. The exact AL-569 wording has now been visually checked, but the receipt should not be treated as a standalone proof of all configuration facts. Its direct evidence is the Mk 15 Mod 0 identification and custody prohibition on active-capsule insertion.
3. S1’s claims about the specific weapon’s maintenance history, July 1956 maintenance window, December 1957 conversion-kit availability and March 1958 conversion start are reproduced from the report but not independently checked against the cited maintenance/AEC records.
4. The six-file pack has no original 1958 accident report, mission log, search log, search-area chart or 16 April termination announcement. This leaves the exact event time, search limits and “three attempts” semantics incompletely checked.
5. The complete 2004–05 interagency/laboratory report is missing. S3 validates the public summary, not the sample design, analytical detection limits or what areas/results were excluded.
6. The sentence “a weapon lost and never recovered” in the conclusion is rhetorically stronger than the evidence. Replace it downstream with “a weapon not recovered in the documented searches; no credible recovery evidence was located.”
7. “Not worth searching for” should be replaced with “the Air Force recommended against a new search” or “the Navy assessment found a very low probability of success,” both of which track S1’s actual recommendation.

## Required corrections and additional primary-source checks

- Read and preserve the original Howard letter, including the surrounding table, addressee, date, document number/release metadata and the meaning of “complete.”
- Preserve a page image and human transcription of AL-569 in the project’s evidence record; do not infer more from it than the scan says.
- Retrieve the maintenance records and AEC production/conversion records cited by S1; verify whether the report’s “conclusive” Mod 0 conclusion is supported by those records.
- Retrieve the original accident/mission record to resolve or transparently document the 2:00/3:30 a.m. discrepancy, aircraft identities, permission chain and landing-attempt wording.
- Retrieve the 1958 Air Force/Navy search logs, equipment records, area chart and 16 April termination record.
- Locate the full 2004–05 survey and laboratory report and record sample locations, methods, detection limits and scope before repeating the natural-mineral explanation.
- Keep the official area description approximate and separate from later private coordinates or Little Tybee claims.
- Keep “hydrogen bomb,” “fully armed,” “nuclear detonation,” “conventional explosive,” “uranium/heavy metals,” “radiation readings” and “irretrievably lost” in separate, attributed evidentiary lanes.
