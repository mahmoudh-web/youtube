## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “The Molasses Wave That Went to Court,” about the 15 January 1919 tank failure in Boston’s North End and the civil litigation that followed. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification gate and evidence-bounded conclusion.
- Reconstruct the event through the Massachusetts death-certificate collection, Suffolk County court records, engineering reports and contemporary newspaper coverage.
- Keep USIA’s sabotage allegation separate from the findings of the 1919 criminal inquiry, the court-appointed auditor’s 1925 report and the later settlement.
- Distinguish a report by a court-appointed auditor from a final jury verdict or judgment.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `boston-molasses-flood-1919` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Describe the event as a tank rupture or failure unless quoting a contemporary source that calls it an explosion. The physical cause remains a subject of engineering interpretation.
- Treat “anarchists bombed the tank” as USIA’s defense allegation. The located record contains no proof that sabotage occurred.
- Treat Hugh W. Ogden’s April 1925 report as a court-appointed auditor’s finding that USIA was liable and that structural weakness caused the failure. Do not call it a jury verdict; the consolidated civil claims were settled afterward.
- Use the official Massachusetts Archives collection as the casualty anchor. The index contains 21 certificates, including delayed deaths; do not infer that every certificate date is 15 January.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access dates in this pack are 2026-09-14.
- Do not state that the court “proved” one precise physical mechanism. Ogden’s report, C. M. Spofford’s examination and modern engineering analyses converge on structural weakness, but fermentation, temperature, material properties, rivet holes and fracture mechanics should be attributed to the relevant source.
- Do not say the criminal case convicted or acquitted USIA. The February 1919 grand jury returned no manslaughter indictment; that is distinct from the later civil proceeding.
- Do not use “hot molasses” as an unqualified fact. Contemporary accounts describe the disaster and rescue conditions; modern analysis says temperature and viscosity affected the flow and rescue difficulty.
- The casualty records are sensitive historical documents. Avoid gratuitous graphic detail and verify names, ages, occupations and causes directly against the scans before narration.
- No script, title/thumbnail package, visual brief or production asset is authorized by this pack. Human verification must record the court-record access, source scans, legal wording and image rights first.
