## Purpose

This folder holds the source-led research pack for the provisional episode “Operation Vegetarian: Britain’s Anthrax Plan That Was Never Used.” It examines the wartime British plan to prepare contaminated cattle feed, the Gruinard Island trials associated with the wider biological-weapons programme, the plan’s non-deployment, and the official record of the island’s later decontamination. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, dated timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/human-verification note and provisional conclusion.
- Distinguish the cattle-feed plan from separate anthrax-bomb work and from the broader British biological-weapons programme.
- Separate established fact, source-attributed allegation or contemporary rationale, later interpretation and unresolved uncertainty.
- Keep the German retaliation premise qualified: the located review says evidence of a German foot-and-mouth-disease weapons programme was limited; this pack does not assert that Germany used biological weapons.
- Treat Gruinard as a documented wartime test site and long-term remediation case, not as evidence that Operation Vegetarian was used in Germany.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key decisions

- Use `operation-vegetarian-1942` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “prepared but never deployed” as the central factual frame. Do not call the plan a battlefield attack or imply that German civilians or livestock were exposed to it.
- Use the PBS institutional timeline as a secondary chronology for the 1941–42 authorisation trail, while requiring inspection of the underlying War Cabinet/Defence Committee records before stronger claims about Churchill or a single authoriser.
- Use Dstl, IWM, Hansard and the UK Parliament answer as the primary institutional trail for Porton Down, Gruinard and decontamination. Use the WOAH review, Nature and the Guardian report to triangulate, not to replace archive inspection.
- Describe testing only at a high level. Do not reproduce agent strains, doses, recipes, filling methods, release configurations or other details that could make the history operationally useful.

## Constraints and invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Biological-weapons sensitivity: no actionable cultivation, weaponisation, dispersal, dosing or decontamination instructions; no procedural reconstruction beyond what is necessary to identify the historical record.
- No graphic descriptions or imagery of animal deaths. The IWM film record is an evidence lead, not an approved visual asset.
- Do not claim that Operation Vegetarian was used in Germany, that it caused human deaths, or that it would certainly have killed “millions.” Those are counterfactual or sensational claims not established by this pack.
- Do not collapse the January 1942 production decision, Gruinard testing, later anthrax-bomb orders and the 1944 abandonment into one authorisation event.
- “Decontaminated in 1986” is supported by Dstl’s current summary, but the official record also shows continued review, an independent advisory group, agricultural-use demonstration and public confirmation of safety in 1990. Preserve that sequence.
