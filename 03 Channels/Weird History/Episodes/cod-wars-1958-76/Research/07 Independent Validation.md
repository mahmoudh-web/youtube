## Scope and method

I independently read the episode `CONTEXT.md` and the complete research files `Research/01 Source Index.md` through `Research/06 Conclusion.md`. I checked every material row in the Claim Ledger (C01–C26) against the cited source, opening the cited treaty, court, parliamentary, institutional, archival-catalogue, retrospective and secondary pages where available. I also cross-checked load-bearing dates, legal holdings, settlement terms, wire-cutting, gunfire, diplomatic involvement and the end-state against independent official or scholarly sources.

Exact URLs and locators are listed in the source key below. Access date for all checks: 2026-09-15. A source being official or primary establishes what that record says; it does not make an interested government’s characterization neutral.

## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS.** The pack’s central, evidence-bounded answer is supportable: the disputes ended in stages through negotiated arrangements, culminating in the 1 June 1976 bilateral exchange of notes, which limited British fishing effort while preserving the parties’ legal positions. The pack is appropriately cautious about “war,” quotas, casualties and NATO causation.

It is not ready for unqualified narration. C18 is unresolved and high-risk; C10, C14, C15, C17, C19, C20 and C26 need bounded wording; and the source index contains metadata and quality-classification errors. The pack must retain its human-verification gate. No script, narration, creative treatment, visual plan or production use is approved by this document.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strengths

- **Primary legal records:** S2 (1961 UNTS), S11 (ICJ), and S12 (1976 UNTS) directly support the legal chronology and the settlement terms. S12 is especially load-bearing and was checked against the agreement text and annexes, not only the index.
- **Contemporary parliamentary records:** S3, supplemental H58, S10 and S13–S14 are strong evidence for what UK ministers and Parliament said, decided and reported. They remain one-sided accounts of incidents and legal positions.
- **Contemporary international record:** S5 directly records the 1958 Icelandic delegate’s figures and argument, which is useful for the Icelandic position but not an independent economic audit.
- **Archival lead:** S8 establishes the existence and catalogue description of IWM film ADM 4675 and identifies filmed collisions, vessels and a chain-towing sequence. The actual footage, captions, provenance and rights were not independently reviewed.
- **Cross-checks:** S6 gives a scholarly retrospective chronology; S15 provides a US government retrospective and exposes the 1976 date/expectation distinction; supplemental IS75 and the official Icelandic material corroborate the 1975 date sequence.

### Limitations and corrections

- Reclassify **S1** from “A/C” to **C**: it is a House of Commons Library synthesis, not a primary record. Its indexed date “2023” is wrong; the page/PDF identify 2012. Its sentence that the UK “finally accepted Iceland’s 200 mile limit in 1976” is an over-compressed political summary and conflicts with the express reservation of legal views in S12.
- Treat **S4** as **C**, not primary evidence for incident-level claims: it is an Icelandic Ministry retrospective with national framing. It is useful for chronology and official narrative.
- Treat **S7** as a NATO institutional retrospective. Its wording that Britain “capitulated” and that Luns “prevailed” is perspective/causal framing, not a neutral finding.
- Treat **S16** as **C/D** and **S17** as **D**. They are useful leads but cannot carry casualty totals, exact first-cut dates, “no cannon shots,” mechanisms or final victory claims without primary corroboration.
- **S9** was not directly accessible in this pass (403 in the research pack’s access attempt). It remains a catalogue route to CAB/ADM files, not evidence that those files were inspected.
- The cited **S5** URL returned an access error in this pass, but the same official UN volume was independently located at `https://legal.un.org/diplomaticconferences/1958_los/docs/english/vol_5.pdf`; the 11th-meeting record supports the indexed figures. Record this alternate URL in the source index before later use.
- The original **S18** HTML URL returned an access error; the official UN Convention PDF at `https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/unclos_e.pdf?external_link=true` was checked instead.
- The original **S11** case page and several old API Hansard URLs intermittently returned access errors, but official ICJ case/judgment pages and the current Hansard URLs were independently opened. Preserve the current canonical URLs below.

### Coverage gaps that prevent a full pass

The pack does not yet contain ship logs, Coast Guard logs, collision reports, casualty/death records, complete 1973 exchange notes, Cabinet minutes, bilingual originals of the Icelandic regulations, or the diplomatic correspondence establishing the exact February 1976 break and post-1 December implementation. British trawler crews, Icelandic small-boat fishermen and affected ports are also underrepresented. These are not cosmetic gaps: they affect C10, C14, C15, C18 and C19 and limit the confidence of C26.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory items

1. **Unsupported as a settled fact:** C18’s reported 1973 fatal engineer accident. Keep unresolved until Icelandic death, Coast Guard and vessel records agree.
2. **Overstated source wording:** S1’s “UK finally accepted Iceland’s 200 mile limit” and S7’s “capitulated/gave in.” Replace with the bilateral, limited-access wording supported by S12.
3. **Contradictory gunfire accounts:** S13 records three shots in a named December 1975 incident; S16 says no cannon shots were exchanged. Resolve by incident and weapon terminology; do not average the accounts.
4. **Unverified mechanism/date details:** the 5 September 1972 and 16 November 1975 first-cut dates and the equipment labels are carried by NAT. Treat them as leads only.
5. **Metadata error:** S1 is from 2012, not 2023.
6. **Legal terminology risk:** “territorial waters,” “fishery zone,” “exclusive fishery jurisdiction” and later “EEZ” are not interchangeable. Preserve each instrument’s wording and explain UNCLOS only as later context.
7. **Quota risk:** 130,000 tons (1973) is an estimated annual package, 30,000 metric tons (1976) is an expectation reported by NOAA, and the 1976 treaty’s 24/29 figures are effort limits. None should be presented as the same kind of quota.
