## Regional and institutional perspective

- **Mexican official memory:** S1 and S3 foreground sovereignty, internal instability, customs revenue, military coercion and the public absurdity of the pastry label.
- **French diplomatic perspective:** S3, S5 and S9 point to a long list of claims for harm and commercial restrictions. These establish what French officials argued, not that each allegation was legally proved.
- **Press and popular memory:** S6 and S7 show how a small or disputed food-business story could become a satirical shorthand for a humiliating intervention and unequal commercial relations.
- **British perspective:** S3 and S8 show merchant losses and mediation pressure, useful for avoiding a two-country-only narrative.

## What would change the conclusion?

1. A primary French or Mexican claim file could show that the pastry incident was absent, minor, or materially different from later retellings.
2. The full ultimatum could show that the 600,000 pesos were itemised in a way that materially changes the role of Remontel’s claim.
3. French diplomatic correspondence could show a strategic, commercial or legal motive that is not visible in the Mexican synthesis.
4. Payment records could show whether the three drafts were paid in full, redirected, disputed or later renegotiated.
5. Contemporary naval and Mexican military reports could alter the dates, scale or casualty framing of the Veracruz action.
6. Earliest newspaper evidence could change whether the “Pastry War” label began as contemporary satire, later historiography or both.
