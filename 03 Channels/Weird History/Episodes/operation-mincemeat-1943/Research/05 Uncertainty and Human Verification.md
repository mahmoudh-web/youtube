## Source and archive verification

Before any script, title, thumbnail, narration, visual brief or production asset is made, a human researcher should:

1. Inspect the relevant pages and metadata in **WO 106/5921**, **ADM 223/794** and the relevant **CAB 154** files at The National Archives, rather than relying on catalogue references or a memoir transcription.
2. Compare Montagu’s printed operational orders and *Seraph* report with the archival copies, recording exact page/folio references and any redactions, later annotations or differences in wording.
3. Locate the German appreciation and 15 May report in the British file and, if possible, compare their translation with an independent German-language copy or specialist transcription.
4. Inspect Spanish records for the recovery, naval/judicial custody, post-mortem, death certificate, burial and briefcase transfer. Do not infer the full Spanish institutional chain from one reported conversation.
5. Verify the 9/10 July date convention for the Sicily landings and use an official campaign history for any force-movement or casualty claim.
6. Check the released-record basis for the Glyndwr Michael identification and the CWGC Huelva grave inscription; record the exact document, date and wording.
7. Read Smyth’s full identity appendix alongside the primary records and separately document the evidentiary basis for the HMS *Dasher* theory. Do not treat a memorial ceremony as dispositive.

## Open factual questions

- What was the exact approval path through the Twenty Committee, Naval Intelligence and other authorities?
- Who first proposed the body/document mechanism, and how should credit be distributed between Cholmondeley, Montagu, Fleming, Godfrey and the wider team?
- What was the exact release position and what happened between release, discovery and burial?
- Which Spanish officials handled the body and documents, and what can be shown about their motives rather than inferred from nationality or regime?
- Which German intelligence and operational records were produced from the planted papers, and which later deployments can be tied to them without circular reasoning?
- Does the archive establish Glyndwr Michael conclusively, or only as the best-supported identification? How should the *Dasher* claim be described after the human review?
- What is the exact provenance of the post-mortem and the claim that the document seals were restored after copying?

## Causal and wording controls

- Say **“designed to misdirect German planning about the next Allied assault”**, not “guaranteed an easy invasion.”
- Say **“German intelligence reports reproduced in the British record treated the papers as genuine”**, not “the Nazis all believed every detail.”
- Say **“the operation appears to have influenced defensive planning”**, not “it alone moved the German army.”
- Say **“released records and later scholarship identify the body as Glyndwr Michael”**, not “the identity is beyond dispute.”
- Say **“the Spanish custody and German-access sequence reported in the sources”**, not “Spain betrayed the Allies” or another blanket national motive.
- Use **“the dead man,” “the body used in the operation,”** or the supported name; avoid repeated dehumanising labels such as “a tramp” or “corpse prop.”
- Treat “Mincemeat swallowed whole” as an internal British success signal, not independent proof of strategic effect.

## Dignity, privacy and legal/ethical sensitivity

- The principal subject is deceased, so modern defamation risk is low; the principal risks are misidentification, dehumanisation, religious/memorial disrespect and false attribution of state or individual wrongdoing.
- Glyndwr Michael’s poverty, mental-health history and death circumstances are relevant only to the identity record and should not be used as spectacle. Confirm the wording in a reliable biographical/archival source before publication.
- Do not imply that a deceased person consented to the use of his body or that the operation had no ethical controversy. The pack does not establish a complete consent or legal-authorisation chain.
- Avoid graphic body imagery. If archival images are necessary, use the least graphic, most respectful image that establishes the evidence and include provenance, rights and caption review.
- Do not show forged correspondence in a way that strips the “fictional” status from the documents or reproduces sensitive personal details unnecessarily.
- Do not use the Huelva grave as a punchline. A memorial image should be treated as a record of later remembrance and identity attribution.
- Keep the Spanish people and modern Spanish institutions distinct from specific wartime officials and services named in the evidence.

## Visual and rights gate

No visual is cleared. Human review must record:

- archive/object reference and direct source URL;
- photographer/custodian and licence or permission status;
- whether the image is a contemporary record, later reconstruction or AI-generated illustration;
- exact caption, date, identity and what the frame does **not** prove;
- graphic/sensitivity assessment and a non-graphic alternative;
- whether use of the fictional papers risks reproducing copyrighted or sensitive material.

## Research stop condition

The pack stops here. It is **not narration-ready** until the archive references, German/Spanish handling, identity evidence, causal wording and visual treatment have been checked and approved by a human editor with appropriate historical expertise.
