## Research question and promise

What was learned by releasing biological simulants over a populated seaport, and what can the record actually show about the unusual infections reported at Stanford Hospital soon afterward? The pack must separate the documented test, the military's contemporaneous safety rationale, the later allegations, the medical association and the absence of a proven strain-level causal link.

## Source-quality key

- **A** — primary report, official hearing, court opinion, or government record/catalogue.
- **B** — official institutional or technical retrospective with named provenance.
- **C** — peer-reviewed or reputable historical synthesis used for triangulation.
- **D** — reporting or tertiary material used only for disclosure history or competing accounts.

## Entities and voices

- U.S. Army Chemical Corps / Camp Detrick: report authorship, biological research and vulnerability-test programme.
- U.S. Navy: vessel and maritime delivery role in the San Francisco trials, as described in later records; exact ship identity requires primary-file confirmation.
- John S. McNulty: author named in the technical-report catalogue.
- Edward A. Miller, Brig. Gen. William S. Augerson and Lt. Col. George A. Carruth: Army witnesses in the 1977 Senate hearing.
- Richard P. Wheat, Anne Zuckerman and Lowell A. Rantz: Stanford authors of the 1951 medical case report.
- Edward J. Nevin and his family: patient/family at the centre of the later wrongful-death allegation; do not turn the allegation into a settled causal fact.
- General Anthony McAuliffe: official legal record identifies him as the Army official who approved the test site and strain; treat rank and role as legal-record facts, not proof of personal negligence.
- San Francisco residents and Stanford patients: affected civilians, not a faceless “population” only; avoid unnecessary personal detail and graphic medical description.

## Research-source opportunities and gaps

- Locate the original Special Report 142 scan and any appendices, maps, sampling sheets, ship logs and release records in NARA, Army Chemical Corps or Dugway holdings.
- Obtain the full Wheat article and Stanford hospital records, including dates, clinical routes and strain preservation status.
- Obtain the complete Farmer et al. Lancet paper and underlying CDC typing methods/results.
- Read the original district-court Nevin findings, trial exhibits and official Ninth Circuit reporter copy.
- Verify the 1977 Army report's exact agency language: the hearing distinguishes the Army's records from a Navy test about which the witness lacked direct knowledge.
- Do not treat the 1979 article's 117-square-mile, 800,000-person or six-day claims as final until they are reconciled with Special Report 142's six trials, 20–27 September and 43 stations.
