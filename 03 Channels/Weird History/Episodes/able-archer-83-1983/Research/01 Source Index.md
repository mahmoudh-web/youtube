## Research question

Did Able Archer 83 make a NATO nuclear first strike look plausible to the Soviet system, and what can the surviving record actually show about the danger of miscalculation?

## Working answer and scope

The record establishes that Able Archer 83 was a NATO command-post exercise held 7–11 November 1983, designed to rehearse the transition from conventional to chemical and nuclear operations. NATO’s own later historical summary says it involved headquarters and response cells rather than national leaders or troops on the ground. U.S. intelligence recorded unusual Soviet collection and air-force readiness during the exercise, while later PFIAB review judged that at least some Soviet forces may have prepared for a NATO attack under cover of the exercise.

That evidence does not establish that the Politburo ordered a nuclear launch, that Soviet leaders uniformly believed an attack was imminent, or that the world was “hours” or “minutes” from nuclear war. The strongest historical disagreement is about the meaning of unusual military and intelligence activity: genuine fear and dangerous miscalculation are plausible; an imminent Soviet attack remains unproven. The pack keeps the maximalist PFIAB/National Security Archive interpretation beside the more sceptical CIA and Simon Miles readings.

This is a source pack, not a script, title/thumbnail package, visual brief or production approval.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or official record:** declassified intelligence, after-action report, official NATO historical summary, presidential record or archival document.
- **A/S — primary record accessed through an institutional reproduction:** the document is primary, but the host’s transcription, OCR or redactions require human checking against the scan.
- **B — scholarly secondary analysis:** a published historian’s argument based on archival material; useful for historiographical disagreement, not a substitute for the documents.
- **C — research archive synthesis:** expert archival curation that links and contextualises documents; use alongside the underlying record.

## Source-handling rules

1. Use **Able Archer 83** for the 7–11 November command-post exercise; explain **Autumn Forge 83** and **Reforger 83** separately when discussing the wider live exercises and troop airlift.
2. “Soviet reaction” must be plural and qualified: intelligence collection, air-unit alerts and official warnings do not reveal a single Politburo intention.
3. Do not convert PFIAB’s “may have” and “appears” into “was about to launch.”
4. Treat Gordievsky’s reporting as important but not self-sufficient; record the CIA’s contrary recollections and Miles’s archival critique.
5. Use the Reagan diary only for Reagan’s recorded perception. Its causal connection to Able Archer is unresolved.
