## Research question

What do the surviving radar, pilot, Air Force, CAA and press records establish about the Washington sightings on 19–20 and 26–27 July 1952, and how far can the competing explanations be taken without converting “unidentified” into “extraterrestrial”?

## Conflict and editorial promise

The records show experienced civilian and military personnel reporting unusual radar returns and lights over the capital during a Cold War air-defence period. The central conflict is between a real record of observations and an uncertain object-level explanation: the Air Force and CAA developed an anomalous-propagation explanation, while some witnesses and later researchers argued that it did not account for every radar-visual detail. The pack can document the evidence and the limits of each hypothesis; it cannot identify the objects from the current record.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or near-primary:** contemporaneous report, official technical report, press-conference record, archive catalogue or original newspaper/clipping.
- **B — institutional historical synthesis:** National Archives or CIA historical page, transportation-library record or reputable retrospective using named records.
- **C — researched secondary finding aid:** useful for locating primary files or comparing later interpretations; not a substitute for the original record.
- **D — popular or advocacy synthesis:** lead-finding only unless independently corroborated.

## Verification outcome for the validator findings

- **NARA:** the public NARA guides confirm the relevant series and identifiers, but not a synchronized, item-level copy of the July case or the missing interceptor/pilot reports. This remains an archival retrieval gate, not evidence that the reports do not exist.
- **Official press record/film:** NARA catalogue record 25738 confirms a 31 July Samford film record. The 29 July transcript remains a secondary transcription pending comparison with official minutes or a complete record.
- **Original newspapers:** the Blue Book clipping index supplies dated titles and page leads; secondary reproductions supply additional retrieval leads. Original newspaper pages were not independently checked, so headlines and reception claims remain limited to circulation/coverage, not belief.

## Primary-source retrieval queue

2. Read CAA Report 180 Table I separately from its August track-analysis sections; record the July tabulations and weather correlations without presenting the August motion conclusions as a complete July verdict.
3. Obtain the complete 29 July press-conference minutes and review the 31 July film record; compare the inversion wording, the limits of the explanation and the distinction between radar and visual reports.
4. Retrieve the 1952 *Washington Daily News*, *Washington Post*, *New York Daily News* and other contemporaneous clippings cited in S2/S12. Classify each as report, scepticism, satire, official statement or later recollection.
5. Check weather-station/radiosonde records for the two July weekends and compare them with the CAA report’s method; do not infer that a recorded inversion explains every target.
6. Request or inspect the two F-94 mission reports, air-ground communications and commercial/CAA pilot reports identified as missing in S2. No item-level copy was located in the accessible catalogue during this revision.
