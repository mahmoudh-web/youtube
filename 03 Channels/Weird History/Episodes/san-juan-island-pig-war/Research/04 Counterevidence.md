## Competing causal models

### Model A: livestock incident as primary cause

This is the popular label. It is supported only for the narrow proposition that the pig shooting preceded the immediate military escalation. It fails to explain the treaty ambiguity, prior confrontations, and the long arbitration.

### Model B: treaty ambiguity plus local jurisdictional conflict

This model fits the legal record: two channels, two sovereignty claims, an HBC presence, American settlement, and local officials acting before the governments could settle the issue. The pig supplied a vivid incident through which the larger conflict became actionable.

### Model C: deliberate strategic provocation by Harney

This is plausible but not fully established. The NPS administrative history says Harney's move functioned as a message to Britain and notes competing speculation about his motives. The claim must stay labelled interpretation unless the primary orders show intent.

### Working assessment

Model B is the strongest explanation. Model C may explain why the 1859 dispute became a military crisis so quickly, but it should not replace the documented structural causes. Model A is a shorthand for the trigger, not the cause of the twelve-year crisis.
