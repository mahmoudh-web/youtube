## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional Weird History episode “The Pastry War Wasn’t About Pastries.” It is an evidence pack, not an approved script, title, thumbnail or production brief.

## Research question

What do the Mexican, French and later scholarly records show about the 1838–39 French blockade and attack on Mexico? In particular, was the Tacubaya pastry-shop grievance a documented cause, one item in a wider indemnity dispute, or a later satirical label for a conflict driven by diplomacy, trade and power?

## Responsibilities

- Maintain a source index, dated chronology, one-claim-per-row ledger, counterevidence note, uncertainty/human-verification gate and provisional conclusion.
- Preserve the distinction between the alleged Remontel shop incident, the aggregate French claims, the commercial and forced-loan demands, and the military events at Veracruz.
- Attribute claims to French diplomats, Mexican officials, later newspapers, archival institutions or modern historians; do not convert a claim or settlement into proof of liability.
- Record URLs, source type, exact page/section/line locators where available, access dates, confidence and caveats.
- Stop before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication until a human verifies the underlying records.

## Key decisions

- Use `pastry-war-1838` as the episode-folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Treat “Pastry War” / “Guerra de los Pasteles” as a later or popular label for the first French intervention in Mexico, not as a neutral description of causation.
- Treat the pastry-shop episode as a later-attested grievance whose details remain disputed. The strongest sources located for this pass do not establish that France attacked Mexico solely, or even principally, over pastries.
- Treat the 600,000 pesos in the 9 March 1839 convention as an aggregate settlement for French claims predating 26 November 1838. It is not an itemised judicial finding that every claim was valid, nor evidence that the pastry claim itself was worth 600,000 pesos.
- Keep the SRE account’s 800-peso Remontel figure beside later 30,000/60,000 retellings. Do not silently choose one number.

## Constraints and invariants

- Access date for this pack is 2026-09-14.
- Do not say that Mexico and France “fought over pastries” without immediately qualifying the wording with the wider claims and the uncertainty around Remontel’s account.
- Do not state that Remontel definitely owned a pastry shop, that soldiers merely ate unpaid cakes, that they looted the premises, or that the claim was exactly 30,000 or 60,000 pesos until the underlying diplomatic or press record is inspected.
- Do not describe the 600,000-peso convention as a debt that Mexico admitted in full. The treaty language settles French claims; the Mexican position had disputed state responsibility for losses during internal disturbances.
- Distinguish the 21 March ultimatum, 16 April blockade, late-November assault on San Juan de Ulúa/Veracruz, the 1 December expulsion law, and the 9 March 1839 peace and claims convention. Source dates differ for the assault and must remain attributed.
- Treat claims of French commercial imperialism, Mexican sovereignty, xenophobia, civil war and British mediation as contextual interpretations supported by specific sources, not as a single proven motive.
- No living-person defamation issue is apparent, but historical actors and national communities must not be assigned criminal intent beyond the record. Human review is required for colonial, nationalist and casualty framing.
