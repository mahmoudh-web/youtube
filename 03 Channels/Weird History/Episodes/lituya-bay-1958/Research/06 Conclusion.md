## Answer to the research question

The reported “1,700-foot wave” is best understood as a **localized run-up elevation**. After the 9 July 1958 earthquake, a rockslide entered Gilbert Inlet and produced an impulse wave. Survivors' accounts helped investigators place the disturbance at the head of the bay and establish its timing and movement. Miller's field team then combined the sharp upper boundary of forest destruction with altimeter readings, aerial photographs, map transfer and photogrammetric measurements. The maximum mapped 1958 trimline reached about **1,720 feet above mean sea level** on the spur southwest of Gilbert Inlet.

That number is not the wave's height in open water. Miller's own summary distinguishes a water surge described at about 1,740 feet from a forest-destruction trimline at about 1,720 feet. Modern modeling makes the distinction even clearer: a 2020 study reports a maximum modeled amplitude of about 208 m in the impact area but a run-up reaching 524 m a.s.l.; at the run-up point, its modeled flow depth was only 4–10 m. Run-up is the elevation reached by water moving up a slope, not a vertical wall of water of the same height.

## Evidence chain

1. **Trigger and source:** the earthquake and fresh scar support an earthquake-triggered landslide into Gilbert Inlet.
2. **Eyewitness testimony:** Ulrich and Swanson establish the sequence, source direction and extreme local flow, while also showing that witness estimates are position-specific.
3. **Physical field evidence:** the trimline, stripped vegetation, bare bedrock, transported logs/rocks and damaged boats show the destructive reach of the water.
4. **Measurement:** altimeter readings, aerial photographs and photogrammetry turn the upper trimline into an elevation estimate relative to mean sea level.
5. **Model testing:** scale and numerical models test whether a rapid slide can generate the observed run-up, but outputs vary by model assumptions.
6. **Open mechanism:** a single rapid rockslide explains much of the evidence; a possible second submarine slide remains a live interpretation for outer-bay effects and sediment evidence.

## Safe editorial formulation for human approval

> In 1958, an earthquake-triggered landslide in Alaska's Lituya Bay generated a localized impulse wave whose run-up stripped trees to a measured elevation of about 1,720 feet above mean sea level; that figure describes run-up, not the wave's height in open water.

This is a proposed evidence-bounded sentence, not approved narration. The editor should verify “earthquake-triggered,” the 1,720-ft datum and whether “measured elevation” or “mapped trimline elevation” is the preferred final wording.
