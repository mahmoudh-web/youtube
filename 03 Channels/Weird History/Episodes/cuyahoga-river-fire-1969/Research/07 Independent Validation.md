## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — not approved for narration or production.**

The pack is unusually careful about the main historical traps: June 22 rather than June 29, the 1969 fire not being the first or largest, the river surface rather than liquid water burning, and the EPA/Clean Water Act being a multi-cause policy sequence. The legal sequence is supported by primary or official records.

The pack cannot yet claim the image correction as fully settled. WOSU identifies the famous image as the 1952 fire, but the current online Time article displays an image captioned “March 11, 1970.” The original 1 August 1969 magazine page and item-level image provenance must be checked before any image or definitive caption is used. The exact ignition mechanism, local newspaper chronology, 13-fire register, 1969 Congressional Record scan, Stokes/Klaric source trail and fire-specific legislative influence also remain partly unresolved.

This file is a validation report, not approval. Human verification remains the publication gate.

## Additional timeline and source-index checks

- **1881 quotation:** EPA Journal lines 5294–5298 prints “an open sewer through the center of the city,” but this is a 1993 historical synthesis. The original municipal/newspaper source was not opened. Treat the wording as attributed, not as a cleared verbatim quotation.
- **16 April 1969 pre-fire Congressional Record:** the pack’s use is plausible and the GovInfo search record identifies the cited document, but the PDF was not rendered in this check. Human must inspect page 9279 onward before quoting the bond or pre-fire debate.
- **24 June editorial and 30 June Congressional Record:** the GovInfo search result for p. 17881 reproduces the relevant Feighan passage and $50,000/two-bridge wording, but the large PDF itself did not render in the browser. Inspect the scan before quoting the Plain Dealer editorial or treating a Member’s remarks as a neutral incident report.
- **NEPA:** the timeline’s description is substantively correct, but the source index’s GovInfo summary parenthetically says “January 1, 1970 (established EPA),” which is wrong or misleading. The primary statute, [83 Stat. 852](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-83/pdf/STATUTE-83-Pg852.pdf), p. 852, §§2 and 202, establishes national environmental policy and the Council on Environmental Quality; it does not establish EPA. EPA’s [NEPA summary](https://www.epa.gov/laws-regulations/summary-national-environmental-policy-act), lines 4–15, confirms the EIS framework.
- **Cleveland Press collection quantity:** the source index says “hundreds of thousands of photographs and about one million clippings.” The current Cleveland Memory finding aid says “hundreds of thousands of clippings and photographs” ([collection page](https://web.ulib.csuohio.edu/press/), lines 34–37). Remove the unsupported “about one million” figure unless an archival source is found. This does not change the episode thesis.
- **Current river status:** the current EPA AOC page supports ongoing degradation and restoration, but its wording and listed work are dynamic. The pack should not present an old BUI list as the current status without an access date.
- **Human harm:** no source checked here supplies a comprehensive injury/exposure audit for the 1969 fire. “No one was hurt,” “harmless” and “no casualties” remain unsupported and should stay excluded.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strongly supported areas

- **Date and physical outline:** multiple independent institutional sources, a contemporaneous Congressional Record passage and the EPA Journal agree on June 22, a short oil/debris fire and bridge damage.
- **Legal chronology:** the 1970 reorganization plan, the NEPA statute and EPA/Clean Water Act histories provide a coherent primary/official sequence. They support the pack’s refusal to make the Cuyahoga fire a sole legal cause.
- **Counterevidence:** the pack correctly tests first/largest/only-river myths against the Cleveland State record, CWRU and WOSU.
- **Media and local politics:** NPS supplies a coherent Stokes/Klaric account, but it remains an institutional retrospective until the original Cleveland Press items are checked.

### Weak or incomplete areas

- The original 22–24 June 1969 Cleveland Press/Plain Dealer reports, fire response report and image metadata are not yet in the pack at item level.
- The “13 fires” figure is corroborated but the underlying register and inclusion criteria are not reconstructed.
- Ignition is not settled: the EPA Journal says unknown source, while later accounts propose train-associated spark/flare.
- Damage totals differ by source and later Congressional remarks. The safe bounded wording is stronger than any attempt to select one total.
- The image provenance is a material conflict, not a minor caption detail: WOSU says 1952, while the current Time web page labels its displayed image March 11, 1970.
- The fire-specific effect on CWA drafting, votes and EPA organization has not been demonstrated by committee reports or floor debates. Official summaries establish contribution/context, not quantified causation.
- Archival availability is not rights clearance. Cleveland State, Time and government pages require item-level copyright, licence and attribution review.

Source hierarchy used: primary statutes and contemporary Congressional Record/Time material first; official EPA/NPS/Ohio EPA histories second; CWRU and WOSU institutional/public-media synthesis third; archive finding aids and item records for provenance leads, not as substitutes for the item itself.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradicted or unresolved claims

Do not carry these into narration without new evidence or a safer formulation:

- “The fire created the EPA” or “the fire caused the Clean Water Act.”
- “The train definitely dropped a flare,” or any named industrial culprit without an incident investigation.
- “Time published a photograph of the 1969 flames.” The available evidence conflicts and requires the original issue/page.
- “The river itself burned.”
- “First,” “worst,” “only burning river” or “the only event behind the reforms.”
- Exact damage, height, duration or fire-count figures presented without source attribution.
- “No one was harmed,” “there were no casualties” or “the fire was harmless.”
- “Cleveland officials or industry staged the fire for publicity.” No source supports that motive.
- “The river was fully restored.” Current EPA material says improvement and continuing work.
