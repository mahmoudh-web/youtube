## Fact / attribution / interpretation / uncertainty controls

- **Fact:** The USGS report documents a sudden Lake Nyos gas disaster, valley deaths, a water wave/surge and a gas-rich lake system.
- **Medical conclusion:** The located medical studies find asphyxia compatible with CO₂ exposure and do not support treating all skin lesions as acid burns.
- **Attribution:** The Italian technical mission attributed the outburst to a phreatic explosion; later US investigators attributed the stored gas to magmatic sources while finding no significant direct volcanic activity.
- **Interpretation:** The most defensible explanatory frame is that a gas-loaded, stratified lake released CO₂ rapidly, while the initiating disturbance remains unknown.
- **Uncertainty:** The pack does not identify the exact trigger, settle the terminology, reconcile every casualty count or supply a complete Cameroonian community record.

Do not convert “magmatic source” into “volcanic eruption,” “plausible trigger” into “cause,” or “official 2016 account” into an independently verified 1986 finding.

## Legal, ethical and editorial sensitivity

### Human dignity and graphic material

The event killed people and animals in their homes and fields. Contemporary images of victims, livestock or damaged communities may be disturbing and may have been circulated without meaningful consent. A human editor must decide whether any archival still is necessary, avoid graphic repetition and consult local/community context before publication.

### Medical claims

Do not diagnose named individuals or imply that survivor symptoms were imaginary or universally identical. Medical statements should stay at the study level, use “the study reported/found,” and avoid sensational descriptions of lesions.

### Cultural and geographic context

The disaster affected Cameroonian communities, not an abstract “African lake.” Do not frame local beliefs, village names or resettlement as exotic colour. Verify spellings, translations and community sources with a qualified human reviewer.

### Scientific overstatement

“Invisible cloud,” “lake explosion,” “limnic eruption,” “volcanic gas” and “no warning” are rhetorically strong phrases with different technical implications. Use only after defining them and attaching the relevant source. Avoid presenting a single trigger as settled when the core scientific record says it is undetermined.

### Archival rights

USGS, Smithsonian, journal, Cameroon ministry and university-hosted PDFs have separate copyright, licence and reproduction conditions. Public access to a PDF or figure is not production clearance. Treat all maps, photographs, diagrams and scans as research references until a human checks rights, attribution and any licence restrictions.

## Required primary-source checks

1. Inspect the full USGS report PDF, including field observations, chemistry, sediment, isotopes, medical findings and executive-summary qualifications.
2. Retrieve the complete Italian technical mission paper and record its direct observations, sample basis and exact language of attribution.
3. Read the full witness-account paper and compare its chronology with the USGS introduction; preserve disagreements rather than averaging them away.
4. Verify the medical studies’ abstracts/full text, sample sizes and wording about asphyxia, lesions, acid gases and excluded volatiles.
5. Inspect the original Cameroon ministry PDF and, where possible, locate the 1987 conference proceedings and original Cameroonian administrative records.
6. Verify affected village names, human/livestock counts and resettlement consequences through local or archival sources before expanding the social-impact section.
7. Confirm rights and attribution for every proposed map, figure, photograph, scan or archival clip.
