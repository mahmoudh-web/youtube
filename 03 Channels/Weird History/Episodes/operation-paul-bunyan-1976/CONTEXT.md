## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research pack for *The Most Heavily Armed Tree-Cutting Mission in History*. It is an evidence file, not a script, creative treatment, thumbnail brief, shot list, or other production material.

Central question: after two United Nations Command officers were killed at Panmunjom on 18 August 1976, how did the United States and South Korea signal that the attack would not be tolerated while avoiding a second clash?

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty note and provisional conclusion.
- Separate established fact, attributed position, allegation, interpretation and uncertainty.
- Keep U.S., South Korean, North Korean, United Nations and retrospective accounts distinct.
- Leave all material awaiting human verification before narration, packaging or production.

## Key Decisions

- Use the slug `operation-paul-bunyan-1976` under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “Axe Murder Incident” or “18 August incident” for the attack and “Operation Paul Bunyan” for the 21 August tree-cutting response; the title’s superlative is a quoted/repackaged Army description, not a measured historical fact.
- Treat the central finding as provisional: visible military coercion was paired with restraint, including no executed strike on North Korean forces during the tree-cutting operation, despite contingency planning for possible follow-on action.
- Do not state that the operation alone “prevented war.” The evidence supports a sequence of choices and a later negotiated de-escalation, but not a counterfactual proof.
- Do not repeat the allegation that Kim Jong-il ordered the attack as fact; record it as an unresolved attribution requiring stronger evidence.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Research accessed 2026-09-14; links and locators are recorded in `Research/01 Source Index.md`.
- The incident involved deaths and serious military danger. Do not make it comic, call it bloodless, or use the tree as a joke prop.
- Force totals, operation duration, the exact number of wounded, and the command-failure sequence vary by source. Preserve the discrepancies.
- “North Korea backed down” is a U.S. diplomatic interpretation. North Korean sources described the tree-cutting as provocation and its later message as regret without accepting responsibility.
- U.S. intelligence judged the incident deliberate but also recorded uncertainty about whether the killings themselves were planned. Do not collapse that distinction.
- The packet includes sources with redactions, OCR errors, partisan framing and retrospective simplification. Human verification is required.
