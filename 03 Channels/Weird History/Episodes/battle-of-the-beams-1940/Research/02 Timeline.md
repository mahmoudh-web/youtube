## Operational reconstruction

The strongest current sequence is: intelligence intercepts and technical examination → Jones’s hypothesis about German radio navigation → Cabinet/ministerial approval to continue the search → airborne signal detection and plotting → No. 80 Wing and TRE countermeasure development → German adaptation from Knickebein toward X-Gerät/Y-Gerät → British interference, deception, meaconing, defence and transmitter-attack plans → iterative assessment through later operational reports.

The “battle” is therefore a useful metaphor for a feedback loop, not evidence of one decisive encounter. The record currently supports a continuing contest in which each side changed equipment, procedure and interpretation.

## Timeline items not cleared for narration

- The exact first operational date and target of Bromide.
- Whether “Ruffian” means X-Gerät consistently, or whether British and later institutional sources apply the word more broadly.
- Whether the June 21 Anson and the NSA-described next-day flight are one sequence or two differently narrated events.
- The degree to which corrected Bromide altered later KGr 100 outcomes versus weather, moonlight, target characteristics, limited pathfinder capacity, ordinary air defence and German redeployment.
