## Ledger conventions

One material claim per row. Confidence means the safest currently supportable wording, not certainty that a source is impartial. “Established fact” means the record establishes that an act, document or reported position occurred; it does not mean a partisan allegation within that record is true. ## Not yet claimable

- “France invaded Mexico because soldiers ate 60,000 pesos’ worth of cakes.”
- “Remontel’s shop was definitely a bakery/pastry shop” without the original claim or a contemporaneous corroboration.
- “The pastry claim was exactly 800, 30,000 or 60,000 pesos” without explaining which source, date and currency/translation produced the figure.
- “Mexico admitted the 600,000-peso debt” without distinguishing a negotiated aggregate claims convention from an adjudicated admission of liability.
- “The 600,000 pesos went to Remontel” or “France paid Remontel directly”; the treaty names French claims collectively and directs payment to the French government through Veracruz customs drafts.
- “The French demands were fabricated” or “all French claims were legitimate”; the located sources show competing positions and a bundle of allegations, not a complete claims tribunal record.
- Any exact blockade casualty, ship-loss, troop, damage or Santa Anna injury figure until French, Mexican or British contemporary reports are checked.
