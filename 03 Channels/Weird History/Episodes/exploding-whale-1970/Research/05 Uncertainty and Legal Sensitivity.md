## Fact / allegation / interpretation / uncertainty controls

- **Fact:** The newspaper says officials considered burial and a rendering plant; it reports a planned blast and later car damage.
- **Attribution:** The later ODOT account says Thornton was assigned the task by default and that Navy officials were consulted.
- **Interpretation:** The episode is a maintenance decision made under institutional memory failure and disposal constraints, then transformed by unusually vivid footage into a legend.
- **Uncertainty:** The located sources do not show a formal written authorization above Thornton or the Navy’s exact advice.

Do not convert an attributed account into an institutional fact by changing “ODOT later said” to “ODOT ordered.” Do not convert the interpretation into a motive claim such as “officials wanted publicity” or “Thornton ignored safety.”

## Legal, ethical and editorial sensitivity

### Defamation and blame

The main actors are historical figures, and George Thornton died in 2013, so ordinary living-person defamation risk is limited. The stronger risk is false attribution of negligence, incompetence, bad faith or criminal conduct. Do not call the act negligent or reckless without a qualified engineering/legal source. Attribute Umenhofer’s criticism and the later media label “debacle” rather than adopting them as narrator fact.

### Graphic material

The original film contains a dead animal, explosive dismemberment and debris falling near spectators. The event is often treated as a joke, but the material can be disturbing. A human editor must decide whether any archival frame is necessary, whether to use non-graphic setup/aftermath stills, and how to avoid gratuitous replay or thumbnail sensationalism.

### Property and privacy

The 1970 reports identify Umenhofer and describe his car and insurance claim. The historical claim is relevant to the outcome, but modern packaging should avoid unnecessary address details or identifiable private information from the newspaper. Do not reproduce the full address present in one transcription.

### Copyright and footage reuse

The official [OHS KATU news footage catalogue](https://digitalcollections.ohs.org/katu-news-footage) says the collection is open for research but that copyright for the broadcasts is held by KATU-TV and the material is In Copyright. OHS’s public presentation and 4K transfer therefore do not by themselves grant a YouTube reuse licence. The original footage, audio, KATU broadcast edit, OHS scan and later remaster may involve different rights. Treat all downloads, screenshots and clips as research references until a human secures written permission and records the licence, attribution and any restrictions.

### Environmental/animal context

Avoid implying that a whale carcass is disposable comic material or that explosive disposal was an accepted universal practice. The 1970 decision should be described in its operational context, while current wildlife-management practice requires separate current sourcing and should not be projected backward without evidence.

## Human-verification stop gate

The research stops here. No narration, script, title/thumbnail package, visual-generation brief, edit plan or publication asset should be made until a human:

1. checks the original 11–13 November newspaper scans against the mirror/transcription pages;
2. searches for the Highway Division district authorization and any Navy/munitions consultation record;
3. verifies the exact OHS catalogue entry and KATU-TV rights path;
4. reconciles the 1979 comparison account;
5. approves non-graphic treatment and safe wording for “no one was hurt”; and
6. records whether the episode promise will be “who authorized it?” or the narrower, supportable promise that the record identifies the field decision-maker but not a complete higher-level approval chain.
