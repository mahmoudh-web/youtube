## Why the simple “live H-bomb in the mud” story is not yet supported

The evidence is not one clean narrative. The Air Force/DOE assessment, DOE-hosted historical summary and 2005 official survey all support a non-nuclear training configuration and an unresolved location. A later-reported congressional document and private search claims point in another direction. The disagreements must remain visible.

| Question | Official/strong record | Counterclaim or competing evidence | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Was it nuclear-capable? | S1 says the Mk 15 Mod 0 had no nuclear capsule; S3 and S4 repeat that no nuclear detonation was possible. S6 is a custody receipt that may corroborate the “simulated” configuration. | S8, S10 and S12 report that a 1966 W.J. Howard letter listed the weapon as a “complete weapon.” Former personnel and popular accounts also challenged the capsule-removal account. | Apparent documentary conflict, not a resolved fact. The original Howard letter and S6 scan must be read together with the 2001 report’s maintenance/conversion evidence. |

Do not repeat treasure-hunt stories as possibilities with equal weight. |
| Is it still in Wassaw Sound? | S1’s DOE modeling says “if intact,” it probably rests 5–15 feet below seabed; S3 says its position is unknown and recommends leaving it. | Tidal movement, unknown impact point, breakup, corrosion, or an unrecorded recovery could all defeat a “still at the drop point” assumption. | Location remains unresolved. “Irretrievably lost” is not the same as “located beneath a known patch of mud.” |
| Did the private team find radiation from the bomb? | S3 reports more than 20,000 readings and 12 seabed samples, no evidence of man-made enriched uranium, and officials’ attribution to natural mineral deposits. | S9 records the private team’s claim of high readings and its belief that the bomb was buried in the surveyed area. | Private readings are part of the 2004 search history, not proof of a weapon. | Was the object a dangerous hydrogen bomb? | S1 identifies a Mk 15 Mod 0, no capsule, about 400 lb conventional explosive and uranium/heavy metal; no nuclear explosion was possible under its conclusion. | Public and media shorthand calls it a hydrogen bomb, and the Howard letter reportedly used “complete weapon.” | Use “Mk 15 bomb/nuclear weapon” with the configuration dispute explained. ## Rejected or unverified claims

- **“Canadian divers found and removed the bomb.”** No credible source located; this is a later internet hoax/rumor and must be excluded.
- **“The bomb is definitely under Little Tybee.”** The official record gives an area and conditional model, not a confirmed coordinate.
- **“A Russian submarine recovered it.”** No evidence located; exclude as folklore.
- **“It would destroy Savannah if it went off.”** S1’s technical assessment rejects a nuclear-explosion scenario and distinguishes localized conventional-explosive hazards from a nuclear yield. Do not use cinematic blast estimates.
- **“The search proved the weapon was destroyed.”** It did not. The search failed to find it; the status is unresolved/irretrievably lost.
- **“The radiation survey proved the bomb is there.”** The official survey found no evidence of man-made enriched uranium and did not confirm the bomb’s presence.

## Counterevidence control

The pack should not resolve the dispute by counting sources. S1, S2, S3 and S5 are institutionally related and repeat an official conclusion; S6 and the original Howard letter are the documents needed to test that conclusion. S8–S10 are useful reports of the dispute, not independent proof of the underlying weapon configuration.
