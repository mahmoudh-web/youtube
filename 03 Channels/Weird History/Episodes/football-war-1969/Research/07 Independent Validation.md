## Executive verdict

**PASS WITH CORRECTIONS — safe to send to human verification, not safe for scripting or production.**

The pack’s central distinction is soundly bounded: the football matches were part of the public and media escalation, while migration, land, labour, border, economic and domestic political pressures formed the wider crisis and state institutions made the decision to use force. The primary record supports the chronology of hostilities, the OAS cease-fire architecture, and the IACHR finding of serious rights violations.

The pack must not yet state a final death toll, final displacement total, an uncontested first-attack narrative, a settled match date for the deciding game, or that football caused or definitively did not cause the war. The direct OAS operative record, national government/military records, FCO file cited for 19 June, and underlying casualty/displacement records remain incomplete. Human bilingual and editorial review is required.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strengths

- The IACHR’s Spanish report is a contemporary inter-American primary/institutional record. It documents both governments’ complaints, field visits, testimonies, media concerns and serious rights violations, while making clear that allegations and remedies were procedurally limited.
- FRUS provides accessible contemporary U.S. diplomatic and intelligence records with dates, document status and source notes. It is strong evidence for what U.S. officials reported and believed, not neutral omniscience or a substitute for national records.
- The UN Treaty Series and ICJ materials are authoritative for the 1980 and 1992 legal aftermath.
- The 1969 Vieytez article is valuable contemporaneous Spanish-language evidence for migration and structural economic pressures, and it explicitly warns that statistical data are inadequate.
- The FIFA retrospective, UPI archive, RSSSF and the Honduran federation list provide mutually useful football/reception evidence, but they are not equivalent to an original FIFA match file.

### Limitations and source conflicts

- The UN S/9342 record and PDF were located, but direct retrieval was blocked during this pass. FRUS d647 and indexed UN metadata support the bounded OAS summary; the operative bilingual text still needs direct inspection.
- Bar-On & Lewis (S17) is a relevant 2025 peer-reviewed secondary source and its abstract/ indexed text states the anti-trigger conclusion, but the full article was not reliably retrievable. It must not be the sole basis for a causal conclusion.
- Vincent Cable’s 1969 article is useful for context and contemporary estimates, but it contains a material football chronology error in the passage on p. 662: it gives the deciding match as 22 June and 4–3, whereas the independent football records give 26 or 27 June and 3–2 after extra time. Cable must not be used for the match date or score.
- The match date remains conflicted: the Honduran federation page says 27 June; RSSSF says 26 June; UPI’s independently verified item is dated 28 June but does not itself resolve whether the match was played on 26 or 27 June. The result is stable; the date needs an original competition record or contemporaneous newspaper comparison.
- The source index does not separately index FRUS d642, d645, d648, the UPI archive or RSSSF even though the timeline uses them. This weakens reproducibility and must be corrected in the research pack by the researcher or coordinator; this validation file does not rewrite those files.
- The timeline’s 19 June entry depends on S17’s citation to an FCO/National Archives item that was not directly opened. The FCO file and the underlying protest should be retrieved before the date or wording is used.
- The 300,000 resident estimate, 20,000 crossing report, 75,000 wartime-displaced estimate, 25,000 pre-war estimate and popular 100,000/300,000 figures are not interchangeable. No final casualty/displacement ledger was established.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory or missing-source items

1. **Unsupported as a settled causal fact:** “The match started the war” and its inverse, “football had no causal influence.” The records support only bounded, attributed wording about chronology, public symbolism and the absence of demonstrated state-level causation.
2. **Unsupported as final quantities:** 1,900 dead, 12,000 wounded and any single displacement total. These are attributed estimates requiring definitions and primary/archival reconciliation.
3. **Contradictory date:** deciding qualifier listed as 26 June by RSSSF and 27 June by the Honduran federation and García. Do not silently normalize.
4. **Contradictory secondary chronology:** Cable’s p. 662 account of a 22 June, 4–3 playoff is contradicted by the football-result record and UPI’s 28 June report; treat Cable’s match passage as erroneous.
5. **Attribution risk:** “genocide,” “aggression,” “legitimate defence,” “invasion,” and “aggressor” occur as state or partisan language. None is a narrator-owned legal conclusion established by this pack.
6. **Primary-source gaps:** direct OAS operative resolutions; Salvadoran and Honduran foreign-ministry notes; military operational logs and communiqués; Honduran agrarian law/INA records; the 1967 immigration arrangement; FCO 7/1210; original Spanish and Honduran press; and national/humanitarian casualty and displacement records.
7. **Quotation/translation risk:** Spanish legal and human-rights terms (*genocidio*, *agresión*, *expulsión*, *refugiado*, *alto el fuego*) require fluent human review before quotation or translation.

## Required corrections and additional primary checks

### Required before any script or narration

- Keep the executive thesis as an interpretation: structural disputes and state decisions supplied the causal pathway; football and media supplied a visible arena and may have accelerated hostility.
- Replace any unqualified exact casualty or displacement number with an attributed range or omit it until definitions and primary records are reconciled.
- Use “hostilities began 14 July; OAS cease-fire from 18 July; withdrawal and negotiations continued afterward.” Do not imply the 18 July cease-fire instantly resolved every military or diplomatic issue.
- Use “the U.S. memorandum reported Salvadoran air attacks beginning around 1800 on 14 July” and preserve the memorandum’s uncertainty; do not assert uncontested first attack or legal responsibility.
- Resolve or transparently preserve the 26/27 June deciding-match date conflict. Do not use Cable’s 22 June/4–3 account.
- Treat the 19 June protest as open pending direct FCO/National Archives retrieval.
- Add the missing direct-use sources (FRUS d642/d645/d648, UPI and RSSSF) to the source index in the researcher’s next revision.
