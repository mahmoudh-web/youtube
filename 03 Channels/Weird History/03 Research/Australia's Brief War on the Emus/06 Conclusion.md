## Narrowest defensible conclusion

In 1932, Western Australian soldier settlers facing drought, low wheat prices, crop damage and damaged rabbit-proof fences asked Defence Minister George Pearce for machine-gun assistance. The parliamentary record says Lewis guns and personnel were made available under a State/settler cost arrangement; the guns were then returned to Perth, while contemporary newspapers reported a resumed attack. The National Archives’ later summary says the project was abandoned with fewer than 1,000 emus killed.

The secure historical core is therefore a short, real, government-supported cull—not a formally declared war. “Australia lost a war to emus” is a later compression of limited operational effect, withdrawal and press humour. It may be a useful attributed framing for an episode, but it must not replace the record’s uncertainty about phases, totals, costs, human casualties and the famous medal anecdote.

## Confidence summary

- **High:** a 1932 Western Australian cull involved Defence Minister George Pearce, soldier-settler requests, machine guns/Lewis guns and a return of guns to Perth; the NAA records fewer than 1,000 emus killed.
- **Medium-high:** drought, low wheat prices, crop/fence damage and the cost-sharing arrangement; contemporary press described a resumed machine-gun attack.
- **Medium / unresolved:** exact number of guns and personnel; exact dates of each phase; final kill count; ammunition expenditure; whether “abandoned” means the last deployment or the overall policy response; whether a later bounty system should be treated as the practical successor.
- **Low / not claimable:** formal declaration of war; military “surrender”; exact 986/9,860 figures; zero human casualties; 20,000 as a verified count; exact Hansard wording for the medals joke; any claim about a specific AWM object.
