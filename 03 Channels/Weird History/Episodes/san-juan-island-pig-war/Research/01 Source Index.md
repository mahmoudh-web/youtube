## Research question

Why did the shooting of one Hudson's Bay Company pig become a twelve-year joint military occupation of San Juan Island, and what can the surviving record actually establish about the dispute?

## Scope and source handling

The working hypothesis is that the pig was a trigger, not the underlying cause. The evidence pack therefore separates the 1846 treaty's ambiguous water boundary, competing British and American land claims, local authority and tax disputes, Harney's unilateral military decision, the British response, and the eventual arbitration. It does not treat the episode as a declared war.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary/legal or contemporaneous official record:** treaty, award, diplomatic dispatch, military correspondence, colonial correspondence, or archival journal.
- **B — official archival synthesis:** National Park Service administrative or historical study that identifies and quotes underlying records.
- **C — current institutional history:** useful for orientation and public interpretation; not sufficient alone for a disputed material claim.
- **D — partisan primary:** political memorial, claim, or public advocacy document; evidence of a position, not neutral proof.
- **E — contemporary press:** evidence of public framing and rumour; not a substitute for official records.

## Source-handling rules

1. Do not say the pig caused the sovereignty dispute. Say the shooting triggered an already active dispute over the treaty boundary, land, jurisdiction, and settlement.
2. Treat Griffin, Cutlar, Dallas, Hubbs, Douglas, and Harney's accounts as interested or contested accounts. Where they conflict, preserve the conflict rather than selecting a dramatic version.
3. Distinguish the U.S. and British claims from the 1872 legal result. The award settled the channel interpretation and resulting sovereignty, but it did not make the earlier claims irrational or the local occupation peaceful for every resident.
4. Do not use “war” to imply a declared war or human battlefield casualties. The conventional label refers to the 1859 crisis and subsequent joint occupation.
5. Keep Coast Salish history visible. The Anglo-American dispute was over land and resources inhabited and shaped by Indigenous peoples; the pack is not complete until that context is human-verified with appropriate scholarship and, where possible, Indigenous perspectives.
