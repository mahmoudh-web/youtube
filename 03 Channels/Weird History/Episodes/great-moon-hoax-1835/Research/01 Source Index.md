## Research question

What did *The Sun* actually publish in its six-part lunar-discovery series, how did the story travel beyond the paper, and when—and how clearly—did readers learn that the report was fabricated?

## Conflict and episode promise

The newspaper presented an invented report as a scientific scoop attached to a real astronomer, a real telescope expedition and a supposedly defunct scientific journal. The evidence can show the text, the machinery of credibility and documented reactions; it cannot support the blanket claim that every reader believed it or that one neat confession ended the story. The central editorial tension is between the fictional Moon and the very real history of newspapers, science, circulation and verification.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or near-primary:** the 1835 text, a contemporary image/newspaper object, original correspondence or an archival record created close to the event.
- **B — institutional record or archival synthesis:** library, museum, observatory or archive description that identifies and contextualises primary material.
- **C — researched secondary account:** useful for reception, authorship debates and historiography; not a substitute for the newspaper or correspondence.
- **D — popular synthesis:** lead-finding only unless independently corroborated.

## Primary-source retrieval queue

1. Inspect the original *Sun* issues for 21 August, 25–29 August, 31 August and 16 September 1835 in a newspaper archive; record issue/page/column locators and compare with S1.
2. Read S1 page by page and transcribe only short passages needed to establish what each installment claimed; label every creature, landscape and instrument as fictional content of the hoax.
3. Retrieve the original 1836 Margaret Herschel letter and 1837 John Herschel letter identified by S6; confirm wording, recipient, date and whether either was public at the time.
4. Inspect the 1847 Cape-results volume and the RAS Cape notebooks to establish what the real expedition recorded and to avoid using “Herschel’s telescope” as a generic prop.
5. Retrieve the contemporary *Journal of Commerce*, *New York Herald*, *New York Times* and *New Yorker* items quoted or summarised in S11 and the 1859 appendix; distinguish contemporaneous belief, scepticism, satire and later recollection.
6. Verify Yale delegation, Springfield missionary-fund and circulation figures from contemporary sources before using them; currently treat them as reported anecdotes, not universal proof of belief.
