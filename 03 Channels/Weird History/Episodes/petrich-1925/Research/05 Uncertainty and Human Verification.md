## Unresolved factual questions

- What does the full League Commission report (C.727.M.270.1925.VII / Annex 815) say about the first crossing, first shot, Captain Vassiliadis, the white flag and the dog story?
- Does the report identify one incident on 19 October, a sequence through 22 October, or multiple linked frontier incidents?
- What did the Greek and Bulgarian representatives say in the 27 October Council meeting, and how did the Council distinguish their allegations from its adopted conclusion?
- Which Bulgarian and Greek units occupied which posts, villages or town areas, and was Petrich itself ever captured or only threatened?
- What were the verified military and civilian casualties, and which losses were included in the Commission’s compensation calculation?
- Did the Council adopt 30 million leva, another amount, or a formula that later English-language accounts converted to approximately £45,000? Was payment made, when and to whom?
- Which preventive measures were adopted: foreign officers for frontier guards, a conciliation commission, communications/transport arrangements or other machinery?
- Where does the stray-dog story first appear in a dated primary or near-primary source? Is it from an official Bulgarian account, a memoir, a newspaper wire, a later historian or oral tradition?
- How did the 18/19/22 October dates arise, and which calendar/translation or endpoint issue explains the disagreement?

## Required primary-source checks

1. **League Commission:** inspect S1’s full scan and record page-level quotations, findings and recommendations; compare with S2 Annex 815 pp. 196–210.
2. **Council decision:** inspect S2 pp. 172–177 and the 27 October Council pages around 1701–1706; record exact operative language and the Council’s treatment of both sides’ claims.
3. **League appeal/telegrams:** retrieve the Bulgarian 22 October appeal, Briand’s 23 October telegram and the 28 October acceptance/withdrawal messages.
4. **Field observation:** locate the British, French and Italian military-attaché reports and any maps showing the withdrawal line and occupied areas.
5. **Diplomatic and military records:** inspect Greek and Bulgarian notes, military orders, casualty lists and the record of Captain Vassiliadis.
6. **Settlement/payment:** locate C.826.1925.VII, the 14 December decision’s financial clauses and any 1926 payment/implementation record; do not infer payment from a later summary.
7. **Dog provenance:** retrieve the earliest traceable appearance of the dog explanation and record author, date, language, source basis and whether it is eyewitness, official claim or retrospective folklore.

## Language and translation checks

- Preserve Demir-Kapu/Demir Kapu, Petrich/Petritch and Belasitsa/Belesh spellings as the cited source uses them; record a normalised form only in metadata.
- Have a bilingual reviewer check Greek and Bulgarian terms for frontier post, sentry, officer under a white flag, occupation, withdrawal and compensation.
- Do not translate a government’s “aggression” or “provocation” into a narrator’s finding without the Council’s exact wording.
- Check whether “Captain Vassiliadis” is rendered consistently across Greek, Bulgarian, French and English records.

## Legal, ethical and editorial-risk flags

| Risk | Why it matters | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Dog-story certainty | A vivid anecdote can become a false single cause. | Present it only as a disputed/later account until provenance is found. |
| Nationalist blame | Greek and Bulgarian records frame the first shot and territorial movement differently. | Put both accounts beside the Commission/Council record and label each voice. |
| “War” inflation | The label can imply a formal declaration, stable start/end and settled casualties. | Use “frontier crisis,” “fighting” or “Greek incursion” with source attribution; reserve “War of the Stray Dog” for the label. |
| Legal overstatement | The Council’s Covenant finding is specific to occupation; it is not a complete judgment on every preceding shot or political motive. | Quote/attribute the adopted conclusion and do not generalise it. |
| Currency/compensation error | 30 million leva and £45,000 circulate together with unclear conversion and scope. | Verify the original decision and payment record before using either figure as final. |
| Casualty inflation | Contemporary and later counts may combine killed, wounded, missing, prisoners and civilians. | Do not state a final count without returns and definitions. |
| Archival rights | Official scans, maps and newspaper images have separate access and reuse conditions. | No asset is cleared for production by this research note. |
