## Executive verdict

**FAIL for clean validation clearance; conditionally safe to send to human verification with corrections.**

The central correction is well supported: a real execution took place at Luna Park/Coney Island on 4 January 1903; Edison Manufacturing recorded it; the film and copyright record carry Edison’s name; and the available Edison Papers review does not find evidence that Thomas Edison personally ordered the execution, attended it or directed the filming. The 1880s–early-1890s current-war context is chronologically distinct from the 1903 event.

The pack cannot receive an unqualified pass because:

1. **High severity — primary evidence remains uninspected.** The original 5 January newspaper pages, the 1902–03 newspaper sequence, the original copyright catalogue page, and the ASPCA report/correspondence were not all available as page-level scans in this pass. Several citations are reproductions, catalogue records or OCR metadata.
2. **Medium-high severity — the ASPCA/SPCA intervention is not independently established.** Rutgers states that the SPCA objected to hanging and approved the combined method, but the cited ASPCA item exposes only catalogue metadata and warns that its OCR is uncorrected; the “Topsy” no-hit is not evidence of silence.
3. **Medium severity — the utility attribution needs primary-source cleanup.** BPL prints “P. D. Sharkley” and a plant two blocks away; Rutgers prints “P. D. Sharkey” and describes power from General Electric AC generators in Bay Ridge. The core local-utility distinction is strong, but the person’s spelling, corporate name, location and transmission details remain unresolved.
4. **Medium severity — the reception-history claim is under-sourced.** The pack reasonably infers later conflation from the Edison name appearing in several corporate roles, but it does not yet provide a dated chain showing when and how the anti-AC retelling spread.

## Timeline and counterevidence audit

- **1892 corporate chronology:** supported by Rutgers’ Current Wars essay (lines 51–62) and the [1892 chronology](https://edison.rutgers.edu/edit-chronology/chronology-details/1891---1900/1892), lines 2–8: GE was organized on 15 April and Edison entered a later contractual research relation. This weakens, but does not by itself disprove, a claim that every Edison-named utility was under Edison’s personal control.
- **Topsy’s biography and 1902 incidents:** the BPL essay supports one reported 1902 death involving J. Fielding Blount and a nearly empty whiskey glass (lines 163–169), while explicitly warning that other alleged killings cannot be confirmed from multiple sources. The 30 October and 6 December timeline entries, exact handler incidents, early life, import date and larger death count remain unresolved without the cited original newspaper scans.
- **13 December 1902 publicity:** BPL supports a December announcement of electrocution (lines 169–173), but the exact *Brooklyn Daily Eagle* headline/date and the 1 January hanging/admission plan were not checked as original pages. Keep those as reported plans, not established execution arrangements.
- **4–5 January 1903 event and press:** the AFI catalogue, film and Rutgers/BPL summaries support the event date, Coney Island/Luna Park setting, Edison Manufacturing record and 6,600-volt catalogue description. The newspaper method details, 6,000-versus-6,600 voltage discrepancy, poison unit, crowd size and physiological order remain source conflicts. The *New York Times*, *Commercial Advertiser* reproduction and BPL-linked newspaper image were not all directly accessible in this pass.
- **Film timebase:** the named Internet Archive MP4’s 95.261929-second duration and modern cards were directly confirmed. AFI/Silent Era’s 70-foot metadata and the scholarly 74-second period-display description are not the same timebase. The timeline’s approximate 0:51 smoke and 0:52 collapse are usable only for that exact file and require editorial re-audit.
- **12/17 January film records:** AFI and Silent Era support the 12 January H26890 catalogue entry and cite the *New York Clipper* of 17 January 1903, p. 1052. The original copyright page and *Clipper* advertisement were not inspected; do not quote release language as primary evidence yet.
- **ASPCA/SPCA and method:** the pack correctly keeps this as unresolved. Rutgers is a strong institutional secondary source, but the ASPCA digital item is currently only a metadata/OCR lead. No page-level 1903 ASPCA evidence was found.
- **Edison correspondence and current-war chronology:** Rutgers’ bounded negative finding and Current Wars chronology support the pack’s correction, subject to re-opening the Topsy essay and documenting its correspondence-search method. The conclusion must not become “Edison had nothing to do with any aspect”; company presidency/copyright association is documented.
- **Film preservation:** LOC supports the paper-print mechanism and identifies the elephant film in the collection, but the exact item-level record and relationship between that print and the Internet Archive derivative remain to be established.
- **2014–2026 reception:** BPL is directly verified as a 2014 corrective essay. The broad 2017–2026 statement that popular treatments continued to repeat the Edison/AC story is plausible and visible in modern secondary retellings, but the pack does not yet provide a documented, dated spread chain. Keep “later conflation” as an inference until such examples are logged.

The counterevidence section is directionally sound: it separates corporate association from personal direction, 1903 disposal/publicity from 1880s electrocution experiments, period “humane” claims from present ethical judgment, reported deaths from confirmed newspaper content, and visible collapse from medically established death. Those distinctions should be retained after the corrections below.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strong sources checked

- **AFI Catalog:** authoritative film catalogue with production, distribution, alternate-title, date, length and copyright fields, plus reproduced Edison/LOC descriptions. It is not itself the original catalogue or newspaper record and repeats period promotional language.
- **Library of Congress guide:** authoritative preservation context and explicit identification of the Edison elephant film. It does not supply the item-level Topsy shelf/catalogue record or clear every derivative.
- **Rutgers Edison Papers:** authoritative institutional research and chronology; the Topsy page’s direct endpoint timed out during this pass, but its indexed text was available. Its claims about absence of evidence remain a retrospective archival assessment requiring scope/method confirmation.
- **Internet Archive derivative:** the audiovisual object was directly downloaded and inspected. The item is uploaded by an unknown account, and its opening/end cards are modern; it is evidence for what this derivative shows, not automatically for the original release or rights status.
- **YouTube Help:** current first-party policy, directly opened, suitable for platform-risk wording but not a legal or publication guarantee.

### Limited or blocked sources

- **S1 Railway Bridge:** newspaper reproduction could not be opened because of host/certificate/access failures. Treat its exact quotation and units as unverified until the original scan is obtained.
- **S2 *New York Times*:** cited page was not accessible. Locate a library/newspaper archive scan and record page/column before quoting.
- **S3/S5 BPL:** useful library synthesis with a quoted *Brooklyn Daily Eagle* passage, but still a secondary transcription; inspect the linked newspaper image and original pages.
- **S4 Wikimedia Commons newspaper image:** page fetch returned a cache miss; provenance is useful but the scan itself was not checked here.
- **S9 copyright transcription:** the cited Readingroo.ms page was not directly fetchable. AFI and a searchable mirror corroborate the entry, but a catalogue transcription is not the same as a scanned legal record.
- **S15 NC State ASPCA item:** genuine archive metadata, but the page explicitly says OCR is automatic/unreviewed and not all physical materials were scanned. A no-hit search cannot establish absence.
- **S16 Fiumara lead:** the cited URL is an eScholarship dissertation/PDF quoting Fiumara’s 2016 article, not the article itself. It is useful secondary corroboration for film sequence and spectator interpretation; obtain the published article for direct scholarship citation.
