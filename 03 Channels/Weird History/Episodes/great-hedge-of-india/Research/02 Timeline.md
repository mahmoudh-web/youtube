## Timeline interpretation

The chronology points to a moving administrative system rather than a one-time wall. The line expanded, was realigned, used multiple barriers, lost eastern sections to railways and equalization, and was finally replaced by production-point control. The hedge’s “peak” cannot be assigned safely without separating the line’s total length from the mileage of live, good, inferior or dry barriers.

## Open chronological questions

- When and where did the first live hedge take root, as distinct from cut-and-laid dry thorn?
- Which 2,500-mile measurement includes which Indus, Mooltan, Berar or Bombay sections?
- What exact notice made the 1874 partial abandonment effective, and what did “abandoned” mean on the ground?
- Did the 1879 decision end maintenance immediately along every live section, or did local barriers persist as remnants?
- How did the 1878 equalization change prices and consumption in specific districts rather than in the aggregate?
