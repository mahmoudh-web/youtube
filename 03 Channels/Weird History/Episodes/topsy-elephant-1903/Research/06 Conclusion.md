## Provisional conclusion

Topsy’s death is documented as a Luna Park execution on 4 January 1903, technically supplied by a local Edison-named electric utility and recorded by the Edison Manufacturing Company. The film and its copyright record made the Edison name permanently visible beside the death. That is enough to explain why later audiences connected Topsy with Thomas Edison.

It is not enough to say that Thomas Edison personally killed Topsy, staged the event to defeat alternating current or ordered the camera crew to attend. The Edison Papers’ dedicated review finds no Edison mention in the contemporaneous newspaper accounts, no Topsy reference in the located extant correspondence, and no direction to the Edison Manufacturing Company. Rutgers also places the active Edison/Westinghouse current dispute in the late 1880s–early 1890s and Edison’s exit from the lighting business in 1892. The anti-AC story is therefore best treated as a later conflation of earlier Edison electrocution experiments, a Brooklyn utility carrying the Edison name, a film company bearing Edison’s name, and a sensational surviving film.

The strongest episode promise is not “Edison fried an elephant to win the war of currents.” It is: “A real animal execution was filmed by an Edison company, and that corporate association later became a story about Edison’s personal technology war.” Even that framing must keep “later became” as a reception-history inference until the spread of the retelling is documented with dated examples.

## Safe research-stage wording

> On 4 January 1903, Luna Park’s Topsy was killed in a publicised Coney Island spectacle. A local Edison electric plant supplied the current, and Edison Manufacturing filmed the execution. The surviving Edison Papers record does not show Thomas Edison ordering the killing or the film, so the familiar anti-AC version is a later association—not an established motive.

This is a research formulation, not an approved script. Verify the ASPCA intervention, original newspaper wording, copyright record and film timebase before narration.
