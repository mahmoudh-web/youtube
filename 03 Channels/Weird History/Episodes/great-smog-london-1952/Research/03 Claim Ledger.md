## Wording gate

The safest central claim is: **In December 1952, cold, calm weather and an inversion trapped coal smoke and other pollution over London for several days. Death registrations rose sharply afterward, especially from respiratory and circulatory causes. The event helped drive official inquiry and a later clean-air legislative sequence, but its death toll depends on the period and method counted.**

## Human approval rules

- Approve numbers only with their geography, time window, baseline and source method attached.
- Keep C6 and C7 separate until the original records explain their boundary/definition difference.
- Keep C8 and C9 separate: the former is the immediate official estimate, the latter a later extended-period model estimate.
- Do not use “killed by the fog” for a named individual without a primary medical record and human approval.
- Do not assign sole responsibility to households, power stations, factories, coal suppliers or one pollutant.
- Do not state that the Clean Air Act was an automatic or exclusive consequence of this one event.
