## Research conclusion

The most defensible conclusion is narrower than the popular version but still extraordinary: in Rome, near the beginning of 897, Pope Stephen VI presided over proceedings in which the corpse of his predecessor Formosus was removed from its grave and posthumously condemned or deposed. The near-contemporary Fulda account supports the grave removal, deposition through an advocate, and deliberately downgraded burial. Related annals and later polemical narratives add the throne, deacon, mutilation, dragging, and Tiber sequence, but those details are not preserved in one uncontested contemporary transcript.

The episode was not an isolated piece of macabre theatre detached from politics. Formosus’s earlier conflict with John VIII, his translation from Porto to Rome, the 896 Arnulf coronation, competing claims to imperial authority, and the status of ordinations all supplied legal and political material. Yet the usual claim that Lambert and Ageltrude simply ordered the trial as revenge remains an interpretation, not a demonstrated fact. Regino confirms the coronation without repeating the invitation story; records associated with the Ravenna rehabilitation present coercion/necessity and show that the anti-Formosan settlement was quickly contested.

The aftermath is also part of the evidence. Stephen was soon imprisoned and killed; Theodore II is associated with an initial reversal and recovery of the body; John IX’s Ravenna synod in 898 rehabilitated Formosus and addressed the ordination controversy. The surviving 898 dossier is itself composite and transmitted in variant manuscripts, which means it can corroborate the existence and memory of the conflict without automatically serving as a verbatim record of the 897 council.

## Confidence summary

- **High confidence:** Formosus’s dates; Arnulf’s 896 coronation by Formosus; a posthumous Roman proceeding under Stephen VI; exhumation and an altered burial/deposition; rapid reversal and later rehabilitation as a historical process.
- **Medium confidence:** throne and deacon; mutilation; a second removal to the Tiber; Stephen’s imprisonment and strangulation; Theodore’s precise role.
- **Low or unresolved:** exact day of the trial; whether Lambert/Ageltrude ordered it; who ordered the Tiber episode; whether two or three fingers were removed; whether a separate Roman synod of John IX is evidenced; literal blood/miracle details.
