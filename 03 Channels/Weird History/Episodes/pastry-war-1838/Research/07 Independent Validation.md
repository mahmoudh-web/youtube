## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections.** The pack's main conclusion is evidence-led: the 1839 settlement and the SRE account support a wider dispute over accumulated indemnity claims, commercial access, forced loans, official conduct and sovereignty, not a pastry-only war. The pack correctly preserves the 800/30,000/60,000 discrepancy, distinguishes claims from proof, and keeps narration and production out of scope.

It is suitable to send to **human verification/in-review**, but not to scripting, packaging or production. The human verifier must still inspect the complete 1838 ultimatum and Mexican reply, the original Remontel/Gros record, French diplomatic correspondence and contemporary naval/Mexican/British reports. No approval is given by this document.

## Timeline-only validation and corrections

The timeline is consistent with the ledger except for the following points that require explicit handling:

- **1826 Parián/Seguin background:** S9 p. 169 / web text lines 45–46 confirms later scholarship's account of 1828 Parián losses; S6 is the other cited secondary source. Individual amounts and state responsibility remain unverified. Treat this as reported background, not established liability.
- **1827 declarations:** S3 p. 51 / PDF lines 1091–1104 supports May 1827 “Declarations” concerning bilateral commerce, reciprocity and most-favoured-nation treatment. Their legal status and the later 1832 treaty negotiations need the underlying texts. “Did not settle all questions into a durable treaty” is a fair cautious summary, not a direct treaty holding.
- **27 May 1837 memorandum and 1837 indemnity bill:** S3 footnote 9 and pp. 67–69 identify the memorandum and summarize Mexico's position; the memorandum, bill and debate were not independently inspected. Keep them as source-led context and do not quote their legal wording.
- **21 March 1838 ultimatum:** S3 footnote 10 and S4's LOC catalogue corroborate the date, author and existence of the printed Mexican response. The SRE account identifies Deffaudis; the AGN summary S1 incorrectly says the ultimatum was carried by Charles Baudin. Correct any such attribution to **Deffaudis** and treat Baudin's October 1838 role separately.
- **15 April 1838 deadline:** S8 p. 91 / article extract states that the 21 March ultimatum expired on 15 April; S4 supplies the contemporary Mexican publication route. The exact deadline language still needs the scan.
- **16 April 1838 blockade:** S3 PDF lines 1556–1559 directly supports the date and Veracruz blockade. Do not extend this to “all Mexican ports” without a blockade notice or naval report.
- **27–28 November 1838:** S1 uses 27 November for the Veracruz action; S3 uses 28 November for Ulúa's capture and then describes the Veracruz action. Preserve the discrepancy as a source/sequence issue.
- **28 November–1 December response:** S7's peer-reviewed article supports the 1 December law and documents implementation/variation. The timeline should not imply that the law, war declaration and capitulation response were one instrument or one date.
- **Late January 1839 British role:** S3 explicitly calls Pakenham's role “mediador oficioso” and says Mexico later accepted British mediation. This is stronger and more precise than an unqualified “British-brokered peace.”
- **9 March, 21 March and 6 July 1839:** S2 supports signature on 9 March, Mexican ratification by Santa Anna on 21 March and French ratification by Louis-Philippe on 6 July, but the web page is a later reproduction from Dublán and Lozano. Verify against official ratification records before treating it as the original instrument.
- **30 November 1839 ordinance:** S7 p. 77 / indexed PDF text associates the claims process with the ordinance of 30 November 1839. Retrieve the ordinance before asserting its exact operation, distribution formula or awards.

### Coverage verdict

The pack has good coverage for the treaty, aggregate settlement, demand bundle, blockade date and broad diplomatic sequence. Coverage is incomplete for the load-bearing details that make the episode memorable: the original Remontel claim, exact business and incident, amount, earliest label, full ultimatum, French-side file, payment performance, exact November sequence, casualties and expulsion enforcement. The pack itself identifies these gaps; they must remain visible.

## Unsupported, overstated, contradictory or missing-source items

1. **Unsupported as settled fact:** that Remontel definitely owned a pastry shop; that soldiers only ate unpaid cakes; that the claim was 30,000 or 60,000 pesos; that the claim caused the intervention; that the 600,000 pesos went to Remontel; and that Mexico admitted every French allegation.
2. **Source contradiction:** S1 attributes the ultimatum to Baudin, while S3, S4 and S8 identify Deffaudis. Use Deffaudis for 21 March 1838 and Baudin for the late-October 1838 expedition/negotiations.
3. **Date contradiction:** S1 gives 27 November; S3 gives 28 November for the capture of Ulúa and then the Veracruz attack. Do not force one date until naval, Mexican and British reports are compared.
4. **Unit contradiction:** S8's accessible opening extract says “600,000 dollars”; S2's treaty text says “600,000 pesos fuertes,” and S3 says pesos. Use pesos fuertes and flag the S8 wording as a secondary translation/error unless its original context is checked.
5. **Causal overstatement risk:** “France went to war over pastries,” “France invaded because of a 60,000-peso cake bill,” “purely economic imperialism,” and “British-brokered peace” all exceed the verified record or need explicit attribution and qualification.
6. **Missing primary sources:** full S4 scan transcription; Deffaudis's original ultimatum and Mexican reply; 27 May 1837 memorandum; Remontel/Gros file; earliest Mexican and French newspaper reports; French Foreign Ministry political correspondence; official treaty/ratification scan; naval, military and British dispatches; 1 December expulsion law and enforcement records; 30 November 1839 ordinance and payment records.

## Required corrections and additional checks

- Correct S1-derived ultimatum authorship to Deffaudis and preserve S1 as a fallible modern summary.
- Keep the 27/28 November discrepancy in the timeline and any later work until primary operational reports resolve the sequence.
- Use “600,000 pesos fuertes” / “aggregate French claims settlement,” never “600,000 dollars,” “debt for pastries” or “money paid to Remontel.”
- Keep 800, 30,000 and 60,000 as attributed, source-specific figures. The only directly verified SRE figure in this audit is 800; 30,000 is documented as a later press/Fossey tradition; 60,000 remains a later popular/official retelling.
- Read S4 image by image and record page/image locators for the demand schedule, deadline and Mexican reply.
- Retrieve the original Remontel/Gros correspondence and earliest press reports; record occupation, business type, alleged actors, alleged loss, amount, date and wording without harmonising variants.
- Consult French, Mexican and British primary records for the blockade, Ulúa/Veracruz sequence, mediation, casualties and commercial effects.
- Read the expulsion law and 1839 ordinance before making claims about scope, enforcement, compensation or payment distribution.
