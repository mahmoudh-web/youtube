## Narrowest defensible conclusion

On 30 August 1904, the St. Louis Olympic marathon began in the afternoon and sent a small field over a hilly, dusty road course. Charles Lucas's accepted period report described the temperature as 90°F in the shade, roads filled with dust, automobiles around the runners and only one place along the road where fresh water could be obtained, at a 12-mile well. Later reporting describes a six-mile water tower as well, so restricted water access is secure while the exact layout is not.

The race produced severe distress. Lucas recorded William Garcia's stomach hemorrhage and emergency removal, multiple vomiting and cramp withdrawals, and Thomas Hicks's late collapse. Lucas—who was one of Hicks's handlers—reported giving Hicks two oral doses of one-sixtieth grain strychnine sulphate, egg, brandy and warm sponging. Hicks finished in 3:28:53 after Fred Lorz had crossed first and been disqualified following a reported automobile ride. Lucas's accepted list contains 14 finishers.

The story becomes less certain at the edges. Starter counts vary between 29, 31 and 32; the weather number conflicts with later station summaries; the six-mile water point is not reconciled with Lucas's one-place wording; a later injection account conflicts with Lucas's oral-dose account; and Carvajal's apples and nap, the exact dog detour, barefoot claims, Lorz's motive and sanction, and a literal carry over the finish line are not secure from the core source. “Survival experiment” is a useful editorial thesis about the combination of conditions and later interpretation, but Sullivan's intent must remain attributed or unresolved until a primary statement is found.

## Confidence summary

- **High:** date, afternoon start, dusty/hilly course, Lorz's reported car ride and disqualification, Hicks's recognized result and time, 14 finishers in the accepted period list, recorded medical distress, and Lucas's account of oral strychnine/egg/brandy/warm sponging.
- **High with attribution:** 90°F in the shade; one 12-mile well; Garcia's dust-related mechanism; the cars around Hicks; the protest against Hicks; the period report's claim that drugs and warm sponging were useful.
- **Medium:** exact starter count, exact route distance, exact start/finish time, number and placement of water points, Lorz's mileage and motive, dog interference details, official rules and sanction.
- **Low / unresolved:** Sullivan's deliberate dehydration intent, a one-milligram injection, “rat poison” as the precise treatment description, Carvajal's rotten apples and nap, barefoot running, a literal carry across the line, and any modern diagnosis of Hicks or Garcia.
