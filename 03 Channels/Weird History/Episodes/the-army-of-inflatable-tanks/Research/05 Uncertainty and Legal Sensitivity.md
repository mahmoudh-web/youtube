## Classification Controls

- **Fact:** what a dated record says happened, with provenance named.
- **Assessment:** what a commander, G-2, veteran, or later historian concluded; never silently convert it to fact.
- **Enemy evidence:** identify whether it is a direct German document, captured document reported by a U.S. source, POW statement, or postwar recollection.
- **Interpretation:** mark as provisional and include the strongest counterevidence.
- **Uncertainty:** state what is missing and what conclusion cannot be drawn.

## Legal, Ethical, and Editorial Sensitivity

- The people and military units are historical subjects, but accuracy and attribution still matter. Do not accuse named people of incompetence, deception beyond their orders, or criminal conduct without a source that supports the specific allegation.
- Do not generalize from “German” records to all German personnel or imply that every German commander held the same belief. Distinguish German Army echelons, captured records, POW statements, and postwar testimony.
- The Nazi-era setting requires precise historical language and no celebratory or comedic treatment of atrocities, prisoners, civilians, or deaths. The episode’s subject is deception operations, not a license for dehumanizing imagery.
- Friendly losses caused by coordination failure should be described soberly and with the source’s level of certainty. Avoid graphic detail.
- Archive access does not grant reuse rights. Confirm permissions for NARA scans, GALP photographs/maps, LOC Veterans History Project audio/video, and any reproduced German documents before publication.
- Do not use a copyrighted web transcription as a substitute for obtaining rights or checking the original.
