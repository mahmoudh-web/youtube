## Research question

What does the League of Nations’ official record establish about the Demir-Kapu frontier incident, the subsequent Greek incursion into Bulgaria and the December 1925 settlement—and does it support the popular dog story?

## Conflict and episode promise

The popular version says a Greek soldier crossed the border while chasing a stray dog and the resulting shooting nearly produced a war. The traceable record is more cautious: a frontier incident near Demir-Kapu led to reciprocal claims about who crossed first and who fired, troop movements and Greek occupation of Bulgarian frontier districts; Bulgaria appealed to the League, which imposed an immediate withdrawal mechanism, sent officers to observe it and later appointed a commission of enquiry. The episode’s unanswered angle is the gap between the memorable dog anecdote and the League’s documentary sequence.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or official:** League report, Official Journal, official League publication, diplomatic record or official archival reproduction.
- **B — contemporaneous/near-primary:** diplomatic dispatch, contemporary press or institutional archive reproducing a contemporaneous report.
- **C — scholarly/institutional secondary:** legal history, academic study or later institutional synthesis that identifies and interprets primary material.
- **D — later popular/retrospective:** lead-finding only; not sufficient alone for a material claim.
