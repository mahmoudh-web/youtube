## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research area for the provisional episode “China’s War on Sparrows,” covering the Four Pests campaign’s sparrow target from 1956 through the 1960 policy reversal and its possible relationship to the 1959–61 famine. It is a research pack, not a script or production brief.

## Responsibilities

- Preserve a traceable source index, timeline, claim ledger, counterevidence record, uncertainty/sensitivity note and provisional conclusion.
- Keep Chinese-language primary material, translated summaries and academic analysis visibly separate.
- Distinguish the campaign’s documented aims and local results from claims about national ecological effects or famine mortality.
- Treat famine causation as contested and multi-factor; do not present sparrow killing as the sole or settled cause.
- Stop for human verification before narration, packaging, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.

## Key Decisions

- Use `china-war-on-sparrows-1958-60` as the episode folder slug under the authoritative Weird History channel folder.
- Use “Four Pests campaign” for the wider programme and “sparrow campaign” for the specific target; retain the Chinese terms 除四害 (chú sì hài) and 麻雀 (máquè) for source searching.
- Treat the 1958 central directive and contemporary People’s Daily reports as evidence of official policy and mobilisation, not neutral measures of success.
- Treat the 1959 Chinese Academy of Sciences report and March 1960 instruction as the strongest located evidence for the policy reversal; the exact internal decision chain still needs archival verification.
- State that sparrow eradication may have worsened pest pressure and food insecurity in some places, while keeping its national contribution to the famine explicitly unresolved.

## Constraints and Invariants

- Access dates in this pack are 2026-09-14.
- Official kill totals are incomplete campaign statistics and may reflect reporting incentives; they are not a reliable national population estimate.
- Zheng Zuoxin’s 1956 article argued that sparrows damaged crops and supplied control methods; later research and the 1959 CAS report stressed seasonal and regional variation. Do not simplify this into “scientists warned and were ignored” without qualification.
- The famine is conventionally dated 1959–61, but the harvest and policy sequence begins earlier. Do not imply that a single 1958 campaign mechanically caused all famine deaths.
- The 2026 Frank et al. paper is a working paper, not a settled consensus. Its nearly two-million excess-death estimate is model-dependent and must be presented as a finding under specified assumptions.
- Avoid graphic images of mass animal killing and famine suffering unless a human editor approves necessity, context, rights and sensitive treatment.
