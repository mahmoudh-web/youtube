## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections; not narration- or production-ready.**

The central event is well supported: two same-night Chicago broadcast interruptions occurred on 22 November 1987; WGN’s interruption was brief, WTTW’s was longer; contemporary reporting described a signal-path/microwave explanation and WGN’s frequency change; later WTTW reporting preserves an attributed technical assessment; and no public identification is established by the sources checked.

The pack must be corrected before human approval because it currently understates the FBI FOIA trail, uses the wrong official title for the *Doctor Who* serial in several places, calls an off-screen person an “accomplice” without evidence of that relationship, and attributes viewer-tape reporting to the *Tribune* locator where the opened *Tribune* text does not say that. The exact FCC response letter, original station records, recording provenance/timing, and legal analysis remain unresolved.

This validation is not approval. The episode remains awaiting human verification and must not advance to script, packaging, visuals, editing, QC, rights clearance or publication on the strength of this file alone.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strongly useful sources

- The official WTTW retrospective is directly accessible and supports the broad chronology, approximate durations, the WTTW response, named retrospective witnesses and the attributed technical assessment. It is a 2017 station retrospective, not a contemporaneous engineering log or FCC finding.
- The *Chicago Tribune* archive page identifies John Camper, 24 Nov 1987, section 2 page 1, and exposes a linked two-page scan. Its transcription supports the principal contemporary timing, station, signal-path, engineering and content claims. The archive itself warns that its line citations are mechanically parsed; image-level confirmation is still required before exact quotations or names are published.
- The UPI archive/search result is contemporaneous and supports the approximate WGN/WTTW durations, viewer-tape reporting and Wally Wright’s attributed microwave hypothesis. The direct UPI page returned an internal access error during this check, so the original page or a contemporaneous syndication scan should be preserved.
- The official FBI log PDFs directly support request numbers, opened dates and subject lines. They do not disclose request text, dispositions, released records, an original 1987 case, or the truth of any requester theory.
- The official Doctor Who page resolves the serial-title correction to **Horror of Fang Rock**.

### Weak, indirect or incomplete sources

- The *Electronic Media* PDF is a valuable contemporary trade source and its indexed extraction supports the WTTW time/duration and local-feed distinction, but the PDF page image and exact quotations still need human scan inspection.
- The FCC FY2017–FY2020 log is an access copy hosted by GovernmentAttic, not an FCC-hosted page. Its search extraction supports FCC-2020-000497, submitted 24 May 2020 and closed 8 Jul 2020, but the FCC original should be checked before treating it as definitive.
- MuckRock is a public request-record mirror. It exposes the FCC tracking number, dates, status and response metadata, but the attached 12 July response letter could not be independently opened here. “No responsive documents” must remain bounded to that request.
- Archive.org MP4 links are circulating copies with no established custody, generation, frame boundaries or licence. They cannot support exact durations, original provenance, or production reuse without further work.

### Coverage finding

The pack’s overall source mix is appropriate and unusually careful about the difference between event evidence, retrospective testimony and later FOIA metadata. The principal coverage defect is the incomplete FBI request inventory. The official logs contain at least eight plausibly related entries, not only the three highlighted in the pack. This does not prove FBI investigative involvement; it does mean the current “three later requests” description is incomplete and should not be used as an exhaustive FOIA trail.

## Unsupported, overstated or contradictory items

1. **Wrong serial title:** “Horror at Fang Rock” appears in the WTTW retrospective, but the official programme title is **Horror of Fang Rock**. Correct the research record’s neutral title wherever it is presented as a programme fact.
2. **“Off-screen accomplice”:** the sources show an off-screen person who appeared to wield a fly swatter. They do not establish accomplice status, identity, gender with certainty, or relationship to the performer.
3. **Incomplete FBI trail:** the three named log entries are real, but the three official volumes also contain at least five other plausibly related subject lines. Do not call the current three-entry list complete.
4. **Viewer tapes locator:** the *Tribune* text opened here supports an FCC search and serious treatment, but the viewer-tape detail is supported by UPI, not by the cited *Tribune* lines. Correct the citation and use “offered” rather than “received” unless a primary record proves receipt.
5. **FCC FOIA date/attachment:** the public record distinguishes 4 July requester submission, 5 July FCC confirmation and 12 July response. The response attachment itself remains unchecked; do not quote its search scope from MuckRock metadata alone.
6. **Exact recording details:** the pack correctly flags this, but the validation confirms that no frame-level duration, start/end boundary, custody, generation or rights claim can yet be made from the cited Archive.org file URLs.
7. **Technical causality:** contemporary engineers’ statements support a possible strong-signal/microwave-link explanation and WGN’s reported recovery action. They do not prove the route, equipment, power, launch location, operator skill profile, or common method across both stations.
8. **Legal limitation:** the 1992 date remains unverified and legally under-specified. It must stay out of narration and conclusion language pending offence-specific counsel review.
