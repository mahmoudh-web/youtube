## Executive verdict

**Pass with corrections.** The pack’s central account is responsibly bounded and its strongest claims are supported: the 1958 directive named sparrows, contemporary reports document mass mobilisation, the 1959–60 scientific/political record supports a policy reversal, and the famine must not be reduced to a single sparrow explanation. It is not narration-ready without the corrections below.

The largest correction is that S12 is a **2025** working paper (BFI Working Paper 2025-98, July 2025 / dated 20 June 2025), not a 2026 working paper. The paper’s estimates are directly visible, but they remain model-dependent and not peer-reviewed consensus. The pack also relies on institutional retrospective accounts for the CAS report and policy chain, while the original archival files and several provincial agricultural records remain unchecked.

## Source-quality and coverage assessment

### Strongly usable evidence

- S1–S7 are contemporaneous People’s Daily material. They are strong primary evidence for policy language, mobilisation, methods and what authorities publicly claimed. The host is a digital reproduction rather than a directly inspected archive scan, so page provenance, image quality and reuse rights still need human checking. Their celebratory language and reporting incentives make their kill totals weak as independent population estimates.
- S18 is a contemporaneous People’s Daily publication of the 10 April 1960 National People’s Congress resolution. It directly confirms the formal wording that replaced sparrows with bedbugs.
- S13’s Columbia-hosted PDF is the published 2015 *Review of Economic Studies* article and exposes both its 32–43% estimate and its measurement/counterfactual qualifications.
- S14 is a published *Journal of Political Economy* article; the publisher’s abstract supports the 61% output-decline estimate, but full methods were not accessible in this pass.

### Useful but indirect or access-limited evidence

- S8–S10 are institutional retrospectives. S8 is valuable because it identifies Chinese Academy archive numbers 63-19 and A024-489-001/006, and it provides the most coherent policy/science chronology located here. It is still a secondary account; the archival files, full translations and provenance of cited transcripts were not opened.
- S10 is an institutional retrospective from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Zoology. Indexed page text supports the 848-specimen research lead and the seasonal/regional account, but direct page retrieval was timeout-limited. Do not use it as a substitute for the 1957 primary article.
- S11 is a later official party resolution. It is primary evidence of the state’s retrospective explanation, not an impartial adjudication of famine causation.
- S12 is a current working paper, not peer-reviewed consensus. The direct PDF supports the reported estimates, but the paper itself identifies proxy exposure, assumptions and an empirical model; a qualified human reviewer should read its robustness tables and appendix before approving numerical narration.
- S15’s cited ScienceDirect page was access-limited. The Monash institutional record at `https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/three-parts-natural-seven-parts-man-made-bayesian-analysis-of-chi/` independently exposes the article metadata and abstract: policy was more important overall than weather, while weather mattered especially in excessively wet provinces.
- S17’s cited ScienceDirect page was access-limited. The RePEc record at `https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/deveco/v146y2020ics0304387820300882.html` exposes the published metadata and abstract: county-panel estimates find exports increased death rates and that production decline, procurement and progressive/inflexible procurement collectively increased excess deaths, with no single factor dominating. The full article and numerical tables still need human access.

### Coverage gaps

The pack is well covered on central policy, contemporary reporting, source criticism and the broad multi-causal famine literature. It is not yet covered well enough for strong ecological or national-causal claims. Still required:

1. Direct inspection and translation of CAS archive 63-19, the attached scientific materials and A024-489-001/006 meeting records.
2. The original 1957 *Acta Zoologica Sinica* food-analysis article and contemporary critiques.
3. Provincial crop-protection, locust/planthopper, yield, weather and local campaign records for 1958–60, including areas with different sparrow suitability.
4. The original Mao March instruction and the April agricultural-outline documents, with distribution and local-compliance evidence.
5. Full-method review of S12, S15 and S17 before using causal percentages, mortality estimates or claims about relative importance.

## Unsupported, overstated or unresolved claims

- No cited primary source independently establishes a precise national sparrow population loss. The 2.1 billion figure is an official 1959 report; the paper’s “local extinction” account is a working-paper interpretation supported by modelling and historical sources.
- “All sparrows were wiped out across China,” “the campaign caused the famine,” “scientists unanimously warned Mao,” “locusts exploded everywhere,” and any single settled famine death toll remain blocked, correctly, by the pack’s own controls.
- The exact CAS decision chain is plausible and well documented by the institutional account, but the underlying archives remain uninspected.
- “The 1960 reversal immediately restored the ecosystem” is unsupported. The cited evidence establishes policy change, not immediate ecological recovery or uniform compliance.
- Any future use of the reported 250,000 sparrows imported from the USSR requires a separate check. In the S12 PDF, pp. 4 and 10, the authors themselves say formal validation of that purchase is difficult and cite a later book/popular source rather than a primary import record.

## Required corrections before human approval

1. Correct S12’s year from 2026 to 2025 everywhere in the pack and preserve “working paper.”
2. Replace direct-sounding descriptions of the CAS report with “the institutional account reports…” until the archive is read.
3. Keep the 400,116 versus 450,000 Beijing totals as conflicting contemporary reports and retain their incomplete-statistics caveat.
4. Label every national kill total as an official reported figure, never as an audited count or population estimate.
5. Use the precise reversal sequence: 15 March discussion, 16 March instruction, and 10 April formal resolution published 11 April 1960.
6. Keep causal language at “may have contributed” or “the paper estimates”; do not convert association/model output into sole causation.
7. Add the full primary and provincial checks listed under coverage gaps before approving any nationwide pest or famine-impact sentence.
