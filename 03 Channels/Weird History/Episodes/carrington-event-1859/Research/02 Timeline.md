## Evidence sequence

1. **Solar layer:** Carrington and Hodgson independently record a short-lived white-light outburst.
2. **Observatory layer:** Kew’s photographic magnetic record shows a short disturbance near the solar observation and a larger storm beginning on 2 September; later digitisation preserves the traces.
3. **Aurora layer:** contemporary observers report unusually bright aurorae at low geomagnetic latitudes.
4. **Telegraph layer:** line-specific reports document induced currents, interruptions, shocks, sparks, heating, fires and in some cases battery-free operation.
5. **Interpretation layer:** later physics explains the coupled solar-terrestrial system and modern risk studies simulate effects on infrastructure that did not exist in 1859.

## Date and time cautions

- “Carrington Event” is a later umbrella label for a sequence of late-August/early-September disturbances, not a single timestamped instrument reading.
- Carrington’s 1 September solar observation, Kew’s near-simultaneous short disturbance and the major 2 September geomagnetic storm should remain separate rows.
- Kew, Greenwich, local telegraph and newspaper times use different conventions. Do not normalise them silently.
- A modern CME arrival interval or “17 hours” estimate is an inference from later reconstruction; it is not an 1859 spacecraft measurement.

## What the chronology does not establish

- It does not establish that every telegraph station failed or burned.
- It does not establish a global internet outage, modern grid collapse, exact transformer losses or a fixed repair duration.
- It does not make the word “CME” a contemporary observation; it is a modern physical interpretation of the solar-terrestrial sequence.
