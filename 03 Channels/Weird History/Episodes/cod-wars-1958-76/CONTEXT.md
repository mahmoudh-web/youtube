## Purpose

This folder is the source-led research pack for *The Cod War That Was Fought with Wire Cutters*. It is an evidence file, not a script, treatment, thumbnail brief, shot list, or other production material.

Central question: how did the Iceland–United Kingdom fisheries confrontations of 1958–76 move from naval protection and gear cutting to negotiated settlements?

The pack treats “Cod Wars” as a political and media label for three linked confrontations—1958–61, 1972–73, and 1975–76—not as a conventional declared war.

## Responsibilities

- Separate established facts, attributed positions, allegations, interpretation, and uncertainty.
- Prefer treaties, parliamentary records, court records, official archival catalogues, and contemporary primary evidence.
- Keep vessel-level incidents, injuries, deaths, quotations, and legal conclusions open until primary records are checked.
- Leave the material awaiting human verification before any editorial or production use.

## Key decisions

- Use the slug `cod-wars-1958-76`.
- Keep the 1961, 1973, and 1976 settlements distinct; “it ended in 1976” is only true of the final phase.
- Treat wire/net cutters as a later-confrontation development, not a claim about the 1958 phase.
- Describe the June 1976 outcome as a bilateral, de facto access regime whose text preserved the parties’ legal positions—not unconditional British recognition of a 200-mile zone.
- Use “war” only as the historical label and foreground fisheries confrontation, coercive diplomacy, and dangerous maritime encounters.

## Constraints and invariants

- Research accessed 2026-09-14; links and locators are recorded in `Research/01 Source Index.md`.
- The pack does not claim that the confrontations were bloodless: a December 1975 Hansard statement records three shots, while an Icelandic retrospective says no cannon shots were exchanged. Casualty claims remain verification items.
- Official Icelandic and UK claims are attributed rather than silently presented as neutral fact.
- The 1982 UNCLOS framework is aftermath/context, not proof that its final rules governed the disputes in 1958, 1972, or 1975.
- “NATO caused the settlement” is not established. NATO Secretary-General Joseph Luns and Norwegian Foreign Minister Knut Frydenlund are documented as diplomatic participants; causal weight remains an interpretation.
- No final narration, creative treatment, visual plan, edit plan, or publication asset belongs in this folder.
