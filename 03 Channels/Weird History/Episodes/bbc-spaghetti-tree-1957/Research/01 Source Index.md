## Research question

How did a trusted BBC current-affairs format make a fictional Swiss spaghetti harvest look like an ordinary report, what can the surviving record establish about the broadcast and response, and where does the popular “Britain believed it” story outrun the evidence?

## Conflict and episode promise

The report used real places, a familiar current-affairs format, documentary images and Richard Dimbleby’s authoritative narration to present an impossible crop. The evidence can show how the item was listed, made and received by documented respondents; it cannot support a precise national belief rate or the claim that everyone was fooled. The editorial tension is between the BBC’s public-service authority and the audience’s incomplete knowledge of spaghetti in 1950s Britain.

## Source-quality key

- **A — primary or near-primary:** the programme record, surviving broadcast/audio, contemporary newspaper copy, or a firsthand production account.
- **B — institutional record or archival synthesis:** BBC, local tourism authority or archive description that identifies and contextualises primary material.
- **C — researched secondary account:** useful for triangulation and leads, not a substitute for production files or audience records.
- **D — popular synthesis:** lead-finding only unless independently corroborated.

## Primary-source retrieval queue

1. Retrieve the original BBC *Panorama* production file, script, running order and correspondence for 1 April 1957 from the BBC Written Archives Centre or another authoritative archive.
2. Preserve the surviving film/audio with timecode notes: opening, narration, harvest images, drying/meal sequence, sign-off and the 1 April calendar cue.
3. Locate the contemporary newspaper issue reproduced by S5 and record title, date, page, column and the exact BBC spokesman wording.
4. Recover the complete 2004 BBC Wheeler interview and the 2014 BBC Peacock interview/transcript rather than relying on secondary summaries.
5. Verify the “eight million,” “hundreds,” “two hours,” and “very large number” figures against audience research, telephone logs, letters, memos or contemporary press. Do not combine them as if they measured the same thing.
6. Resolve the filming location and crew-role variants (Ticino/Lake Lugano; Castagnola/Castiglione; de Jaeger, Wheeler, Cawston, Peacock) from production paperwork.
7. Check BBC and rights-holder terms for the film, audio, stills, script, and newspaper facsimile before any visual use.
