## Unresolved factual questions

1. What are the authoritative Icelandic texts and translations for the 1958, 1972 and 1975 regulations? Confirm the 15 October 1975 effective date from the original instrument.
2. Which Coast Guard vessels cut which trawl wires, on what dates, using what equipment? Check logs for Ægir, Þór/Thor, Óðinn and Týr and the named British vessels before naming them in any final copy.
3. How many cuts and collisions occurred in each phase? Do not use NAT’s claim of 82 cut trawls as a total without a primary incident ledger.
4. What exactly happened in the 1973 collisions, including the reported fatal engineer accident? Retrieve Icelandic death, Coast Guard, RN and vessel records; handle any confirmed death with dignity.
5. What is the complete legal and operational sequence of the 1972 ICJ provisional measures, 1973 jurisdiction judgment, and 1974 merits judgment? Read operative orders, not only summaries.
6. What were the full 1973 exchange notes, annexes, maps and enforcement procedures, and how were they implemented in practice?
7. What were the exact UK naval, RAF and civilian-tug deployments, repair costs, and casualty/injury figures? The April 1976 Hansard list of eleven damaged frigates needs vessel-level corroboration.
8. What diplomatic correspondence records the February 1976 break and restoration/normalisation of relations?
9. Did the June 1976 arrangement have a direct successor after its six-month term, and what precisely happened to British fishing after 1 December?
10. What part did Luns and Frydenlund play in negotiations, as opposed to later institutional credit? Seek minutes or correspondence rather than inferring causation.
11. How did British trawlermen, Icelandic small-boat fishermen, and affected ports describe the stakes? Add local/industry sources without replacing the official record.
12. When and where did “Cod War” and “wire cutters” become the dominant public labels? Check contemporary British and Icelandic press usage.

## Required primary-source checks

- **United Kingdom archives:** inspect CAB 128 (Cabinet conclusions), CAB 129 (Cabinet memoranda), CAB 148 (defence/overseas policy), ADM 306 and ADM 330 (Cod War/Royal Navy routes). Candidate catalogue leads include `CAB 128/50`, `CAB 128/53`, `CAB 128/57`, `CAB 128/58`, `CAB 129/163`, and `CAB 129/170`; catalogue and document contents must be verified.
- **Operational records:** retrieve RN, civilian tug and trawler logs, reports of proceedings, collision damage reports, and radio/telegraph records. The National Archives guide in S9 was not directly accessible during this pass.
- **Icelandic records:** retrieve Coast Guard logs and incident reports, Althingi debates, the original regulations, Ministry fisheries statistics, and death/injury records. Have a bilingual reviewer check names, dates and legal terms.
- **International records:** read the complete 1961 and 1976 UNTS agreements, the registered 1973 notes, and all relevant ICJ orders/judgments/correspondence.
- **Archive film:** review IWM ADM 4675 for date, captions, provenance and exact actions. The catalogue is a research lead; no image/film rights are cleared.
- **Statistics and communities:** verify catch, stock and fleet figures through contemporary fisheries data and port/industry records, identifying who produced each number.

## Language and naming checks

Retain Icelandic diacritics where verified: Þór, Ægir, Óðinn and Týr. Confirm transliterations such as Thor/Thór and the difference between `fiskveiðilandhelgi` (fisheries jurisdiction/zone), `landhelgisgæsla` (Coast Guard), and terms for trawl wire/net cutters. Do not translate a fisheries zone as territorial sea or EEZ without a legal reason.

## Legal, ethical and editorial flags

- “War” can inflate the record; use it as a label, not a legal classification.
- Unilateral legality, customary rights, and “illegal fishing” claims must be attributed to the issuing government or court.
- Do not sensationalise any confirmed death, injury, collision, or gunfire.
- National sources are valuable but interested; show the dispute between narratives.
- Do not infer that Cold War geography made the fisheries dispute a proxy war. Strategic context is documented; proxy causation is not.
- Do not state NATO “brokered” the final treaty unless a primary record supports that wording; the treaty was a bilateral exchange of notes.
- Do not claim unconditional UK recognition of Iceland’s 200-mile jurisdiction from the 1976 text.
- IWM and National Archives materials need provenance, access and rights checks before any future production use.
- Preserve the experiences of fishing communities and crews, not only state and naval voices.
