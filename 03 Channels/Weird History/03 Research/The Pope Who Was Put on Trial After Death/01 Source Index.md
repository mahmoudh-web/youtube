## Research question

What can be documented about the late-ninth-century posthumous proceedings against Pope Formosus in Rome, and which details belong to later or partisan retellings?

## Source quality key

- **A — primary/critical:** surviving medieval text or a modern critical edition/catalogue of the manuscript witnesses.
- **B — near-contemporary primary:** annal or record close to the event, but still selective and geographically or politically situated.
- **C — later primary/polemical:** medieval narrative or tract written after the event and shaped by a known argumentative purpose.
- **D — modern scholarly secondary:** useful for interpretation, source criticism, and bibliography; not a substitute for the medieval text.
- **E — institutional/older tertiary:** useful for dates or reception, but not adequate as the sole support for a disputed claim.

## Source handling rules

1. Use S3, S4, S5, S6, and S7 to separate what is attested from what is inferred.
2. Treat Liudprand’s detailed account as a later narrative: it names the wrong pope (Sergius rather than Stephen) in the trial episode, so its vivid details cannot be copied into a fact list without qualification.
3. Do not present “Lambert and Ageltrude ordered revenge” as established. It is a traditional interpretation challenged by S9/S10 and not demonstrated by a surviving direct order.
4. Preserve the distinction between the event in 897, the rehabilitation process in late 897/898, and later Formosan polemics under Sergius III.
