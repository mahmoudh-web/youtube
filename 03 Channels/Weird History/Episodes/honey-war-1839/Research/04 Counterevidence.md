## Purpose

This note stress-tests the episode’s most tempting claims. A repeated story is not treated as corroborated merely because two modern summaries repeat it; sources are compared by date, provenance, jurisdictional perspective and exact wording.

## Regional memory and source bias

- **Iowa framing:** S1’s Lucas documents and S7 emphasise Iowa’s continuous administration to the Sullivan line, the danger of foreign jurisdiction, and the improvised Iowa force. These establish the position Iowa authorities took, not automatic legal truth.
- **Missouri framing:** S1’s Boggs document, S4–S6 and S14 emphasise Missouri’s state boundary, the Brown line, bee trees, the $1.50 fine, militia and humorous aftermath. These establish Missouri memory and official claims, not automatic legal truth.
- **Later comic framing:** S5–S9 use “bloodless,” “comic opera,” deer effigies and improvised weapons to make the story memorable. These details may be valuable colour but should not displace the contemporary legal record.
- **Federal/legal framing:** S2, S10 and S11 prioritise survey evidence, boundary definitions and the eventual practical line. They are strongest for legal and technical claims but do not answer every local question about the bee-tree incident.

## What would change the conclusion?

The conclusion should be revised if primary retrieval shows any of the following:

1. A dated 1839 newspaper or court record names the bee-tree cutter, gives a consistent count and records a tax-seizure motive.
2. A casualty, shooting or physical confrontation is documented in the county records, contradicting the broad “no battle/no injury found” wording.
3. Muster rolls or payment records establish a materially different number of troops present at the border.
4. The complete Supreme Court opinion or commissioner reports alter the distinction between the 1849 holding and the 1850–51 marking work.
5. Treaty or Indigenous-history sources show that the disputed land was described or governed in ways that make the state-centred framing misleading.
