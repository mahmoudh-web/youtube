## The 1958 event and first observations

| Date / local time | Evidence event | What it contributes | Source / confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 July 1958, about 10:16 p.m. local | A major earthquake struck along the Fairweather fault. | Starts the causal chain; the USGS publication title uses 10 July because of its reporting/UTC convention. | S1 printed pp. 57–59 / PDF p. 10; S2 abstract; high. |
| Within roughly 1–3 minutes | Howard Ulrich heard a crash and saw what he described as an explosion or glacier sluff beginning in Gilbert Inlet. | Eyewitness evidence places the initiating disturbance at the head of the bay and supports a landslide-generated impulse wave. | S1 printed p. 59 / PDF p. 10; S4 lines 64–66; high as testimony, not as a direct height measurement. |
| Same interval | Bill and Vivian Swanson's boat was lifted and carried across La Chaussee Spit; the Edrie survived and the Sunmore disappeared. | Confirms violent propagation into the outer bay and the wave's ability to move a fishing boat; it does not measure the 1,720-ft run-up. | S1 printed pp. 58–59 / PDF pp. 11–12; S4 lines 67–71; high. |
| 10 July, morning | Miller flew over the bay and observed logs, ice, stripped slopes, a vanished delta and a large landslide scar. | Adds immediate post-event physical observations before later regrowth or erosion could obscure them. | S1 printed pp. 59–60 / PDF pp. 12–13; S4 lines 73–75; high. |

## How the 1,700-foot figure was assembled

| Date / period | Evidence event | What it contributes | Source / confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 July onward | Pilot Kenneth Loken's altimeter read about 1,800 ft beside the new trimline. | Early reconnaissance estimate; it is an elevation reading associated with the stripped slope, not proof of a wave crest 1,800 ft high. | S1 eyewitness/observation section, printed pp. 59–60 / PDF pp. 10–13; S4 lines 73–76; high as an initial reading, lower precision. |
| August–early September 1958 | Miller and collaborators made ground and aerial observations and photographs. | Provides the field basis for mapping the upper destruction boundary and interpreting the source scar. | S1 printed pp. 53–54 / PDF pp. 6–7; high. |
| 29 August 1958 | U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey obtained nine-lens and vertical aerial photographs. | Supplies the photogrammetric material used with field observations to map the trimline. | S1 printed p. 54 / PDF p. 6; high. |
| Late 1958–1959 | Altimeter readings were tied to mean sea level; the trimline trace was transferred from oblique and vertical photographs, with additional photogrammetric altitude determinations. | Explains the measurement chain behind the maximum trimline figure rather than treating it as eyewitness spectacle. | S1 printed pp. 60–61 / PDF p. 13; high. |
| 1959 | Tocher and Miller published a contemporary summary reporting more than 1,700 ft and the Fairweather fault displacement. | Shows how the early public/scientific formulation entered the literature. | S2 abstract; high for what the paper reported. |
| 1960 | Miller's Professional Paper 354-C summarized a surge to about 1,740 ft and a maximum forest-destruction trimline of about 1,720 ft. | Separates the water-surge interpretation from the mapped trimline and preserves the two nearby but non-identical numbers. | S1 printed pp. 79–80 / PDF p. 40; high for the report's conclusion. |

## Later testing and unresolved interpretation

| Date / period | Evidence event | What it contributes | Source / confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1964 | Wiegel's 1:1,000 physical model showed rapid slide entry could produce run-up roughly three times water depth in the opposite slope. | Early physical plausibility test for the landslide mechanism. | S1 printed pp. 63–64 / PDF pp. 22–23; S5 lines 321–323; high as a model result. |
| 2001 | Fritz and Hager used a 1:675 scale model to reproduce the initial 1,720-ft run-up in Gilbert Inlet. | Later experimental support that a rapid slide can generate the observed run-up through strong impact/air-cavity effects. | S4 lines 81–82; S5 lines 322–325; medium-high as model support, not direct observation. |
| 2010 | Ward and Day proposed a possible double-slide source because one slide did not explain all outer-bay destruction and sediment evidence. | Keeps source-mechanism uncertainty open. | S6 abstract; S4 lines 83–84; medium. |
| 2020 | Franco et al. modeled 208 m maximum amplitude in the impact area and 524 m a.s.l. run-up at the opposite slope; the model reached the run-up with a shallow 4–10 m flow depth there. | Directly demonstrates why run-up elevation cannot be reported as wave height. | S5 lines 456–470; high for the model output, not for an unqualified real-world measurement. |

## Dating note

The local event occurred on **9 July 1958 at about 10:16 p.m.** The USGS 1959 article title says **10 July**, consistent with the UTC/reporting convention noted in Miller's report. Use both only with the time-zone distinction intact.
