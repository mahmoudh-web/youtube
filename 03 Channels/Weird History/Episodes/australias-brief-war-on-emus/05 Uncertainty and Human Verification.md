## Stop condition

This is a source-led research pack, not a script. No narration, title, thumbnail, visual treatment or production asset should be built until a human editor verifies the primary records, locations, numbers and the line between press humour and documented outcome.

## Source and archival risks

- Inspect **NAA A461, D305/1/9** and related Defence/Prime Minister correspondence. The NAA blog identifies a telegram protesting the cull but does not reproduce the full file.
- Verify the 9 Nov Hansard answer against the current ParlInfo display/download, including the exact speaker attribution, page number, and wording of the cost-sharing arrangement.
- Resolve the 8 Nov “Central Australia” wording against the 9 Nov Western Australia clarification. Do not silently correct the parliamentary record in narration.
- Inspect the Trove page images for S5–S9 and S13, not just search-result OCR. Record newspaper, page, article title, date, and whether a number is an estimate, interim report, headline or editorial joke.
- Search the complete 1932 House and Senate Hansard for “emus,” “destruction of emus,” “machine guns,” “medal,” “farce,” “Lewis guns” and “Western Australia.” The located fragments do not establish the famous medal joke.
- Ask an AWM/RecordSearch specialist whether any relevant operational record, Meredith report, Defence file or later collection item exists. AWM’s public guides say many records require the Research Centre and RecordSearch.

## Number and outcome sensitivity

- **Safe official bound located:** fewer than 1,000 emus killed, per NAA’s 2022 summary.
- **Not yet safe:** 986; 9,860 rounds; 20,000 emus; 300 in the first phase; 50–200 in the first week; or any exact ammunition-to-kill ratio.
- **Do not state “zero human casualties.”** No complete casualty report was located. If Meredith’s later report says his men had no casualties, attribute and verify it rather than generalising.
- **Do not say “the army surrendered.”** The record supports return/withdrawal of guns, later resumption, and eventual abandonment; “surrender” is a later metaphor.
- **Do not say “the emus won” as a bare fact.** It can be used only as an attributed metaphor for limited cull effectiveness.

## Language and ethical handling

- The event concerns a native species and a government-supported lethal control programme. Avoid celebrating animal suffering or treating the affected settlers as foolish caricatures.
- “Invading army,” “enemy,” “commander,” “victory,” “defeat” and “surrender” are metaphors unless clearly labelled as press/later language.
- Keep the soldier-settlement, drought and low-wheat-price context visible. The episode should not imply that farmers invented a crisis merely to obtain military spectacle.
- Do not extend the 1932 story into claims about modern Australian environmental policy without new sources.
- If using images, verify copyright and captions through Trove/NAA/AWM. Do not treat an undated photograph as proof of a particular phase or location.

1. Retrieve and read NAA **A461, D305/1/9** and any linked Defence/Prime Minister files; record item-level identifiers and exact passages.
2. Verify the 8–9 Nov 1932 House of Representatives entries in current ParlInfo, especially the “Central Australia”/Western Australia discrepancy and the cost wording.
3. Locate the complete parliamentary source for the “medals should go to the emus” anecdote, or remove it from the pack’s claimable facts.
4. Inspect the Trove page scans for the initial phase, withdrawal, resumption, tactical reports and all kill/ammunition numbers.
5. Locate Major G. P. W. Meredith’s operational report or a direct archival copy. Confirm whether exact figures are confirmed kills, estimates, claims or later calculations.
6. Establish a defensible phase chronology and end date. Keep the first return to Perth distinct from later resumption and final abandonment.
7. Query AWM Collection/RecordSearch for Meredith, the relevant Royal Australian Artillery unit, Defence correspondence and any images/film; document both hits and meaningful non-hits.
8. Have an Australian military/environmental historian review the wording “military-assisted cull,” “operation,” “withdrawal,” “abandonment,” “war” and “lost.”
9. Keep the episode in research until every material number, quote, image caption and outcome statement has a human-approved source label and caveat.

Until those checks are complete, this pack is **not narration-ready** and must not advance to scriptwriting, creative development, visual generation, editing, QC or publication.
